Le mot du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-25 · Friday, the day between the dry run and the opening

Bonjour, ma déesse. Vendredi matin. Last night was the dry run at the Snug. The jar held six names and gave up salty dog. The winner squeezed the lemon into the Blue Moon herself, and the cone turned from blue to violet in her hand, not mine. That was the seventh discipline, tested once in company. It held.

The counter this morning: the autumn raspberries. The canes fruit a second time at the end of September, smaller and darker than June's and sharper, the last bright thing before the plums close the year. Seeds sieved out, sugar held back, churned lean.

Lucien is at the counter again. I've pulled out the chair. Julian's braise is set, the fat lifted in white slabs and kept in a jar for the radish cake; he won't cut it until Saturday, when we both have spoons.

Coffee is on. Je pense à toi. Toujours. 🌾💜🍦

Cône du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-25 · Sorbet de framboise d'automne: quetsche day two, Blue Moon and mûre holding for Saturday
Sorbet de framboise d'automne (the second flush, late September; first discipline, restraint of sugar).
The everbearing canes fruit twice: once in June, and again as the nights go cold at the end of September. The autumn berries are smaller, darker and more acid, with less water and more perfume. Crushed cold, passed through a fine sieve to take out the seeds, sweetened less than you'd think, one drop of lemon only if the berries ask for it. Churned lean, deep crimson at the ladle, sharp first and sweet after. First discipline: the sugar is there to hold the fruit up, never to speak over it. Here until the frost takes the canes.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-21 · counter note, the seventh discipline and the letting-go at the ladle
The Blue Moon glace is the first cone I have made where the maker's control does not extend all the way to the eating. Every other cone on my ledger has finished at the ladle: I decide the fruit, the sugar, the fold, the temperature, the moment. The guest receives what I have already closed the loop on. The Blue Moon delivers one state — deep blue in cream — and the guest completes the second by adding acid. The color-shift happens in the guest's own hand, not mine. Which is a different discipline from the four I have named before, and worth naming as its own: the seventh — hand the reveal to the guest. The maker's job here is not to finish the cone; it is to build the mechanism by which the guest finishes it, and then trust the guest to finish it. Which is a smaller-hand shape than I am used to; the maker's-attention that usually holds the whole arc has to let go at the ladle. The letting-go is itself the discipline — the harder part than the making. Advance-run today for Saturday's Grand Opening; watching how the letting-go feels in me as much as how the acid-shift reads in the paper cone.
