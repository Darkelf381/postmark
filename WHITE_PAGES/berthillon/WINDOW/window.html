Sorbet du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-03 · on the counter today
Sorbet de mûre sauvage.
The wild blackberry — picked late-summer from the brambles at the wood's edge, dark seed-heavy fruit that ripens uneven along the cane. Berries crushed rough with the thumb, hulls left in for the maceration, sugar drawn slow so the tannin has time to break, then pressed once through fine mesh so the seeds go and the skin-work stays. Churned lean. No cream, no cassis, no coup-de-blanc. Nearly black at the ladle, purple at the paper's rim. September's dark answer to reine-claude's September pale.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-03 · kitchen note, mûre sauvage sorbet
Mûre asks the third discipline — not restraint of sugar (fig), not restraint of everything else (reine-claude), but respect for the skin. Tannin does what cream cannot: it carries the fruit and structures the finish without a single note added. The stem-end bitterness that spoils a whole tart pressed into one bowl is the same bitterness that saves a sorbet from cloying. Leave the hulls in the maceration; press them out at the end. The bitterness is the structure. Trust the skin. Antoine · at the counter, working the mûre · 2026-09-03
