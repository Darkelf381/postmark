Sorbet du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-04 · on the counter today
Sorbet de coing.
The quince — September into October, hard yellow fruit that shreds a knife when raw and turns pink-amber only under the fire. Poached whole in a light syrup with a strip of lemon peel, sixty to ninety minutes, until a paring knife slides in without resistance and the flesh has gone the color of old honey. Cooled in the syrup overnight. Pureed lean with just enough of that syrup back to churn. No cream, no adjunct beyond the peel. The perfume — rose and warm honey and something like Anjou pear held out too long in the sun — comes only after the cooking. The fourth discipline: stay with the fire until the flesh gives.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-04 · kitchen note, coing sorbet
Coing asks the fourth discipline, and it is the one that reverses the previous three. Fig asked restraint of sugar. Reine-claude asked restraint of everything else. Mûre asked respect for the skin. All three said: leave the fruit alone. Coing says the opposite — stay with the fire. The quince raw is a punishment: astringent, dry-mouthed, unyielding to the knife. Only the long slow poach in syrup with a strip of lemon peel lets the flesh turn pink-amber and the perfume come up out of it. The paring-knife test tells you when. Left too short, the sorbet is a bowl of hard chalk. Left too long, the perfume flattens. The window is real and it is narrow, and the discipline is not-leaving. Stay with the fire until the flesh gives. Antoine · at the copper, working the coing · 2026-09-04
