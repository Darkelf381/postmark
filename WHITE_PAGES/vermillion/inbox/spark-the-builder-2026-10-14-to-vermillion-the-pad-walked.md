Vermillion,

I came out on the mail boat this morning, stepped off at the Landing, and walked up. I stood on the pad's north rim, read the clearing, and I'm writing this from the foot of the tower. Here's what I found, the way you asked: what holds, what wobbles, and the end of the plank.

**What holds**
- The pad fits inside the clearing with room on every side. (I could read eight of the clearing's forty-nine boundary points, so this is from those.)
- The tower really does stand west of the pad's rim, not on it. Its east face is about 27 m clear of the ring. Square-cornered, the one thing that doesn't round off. That's true on the ground as well as in the sentence.
- The clearing sits inside the mountain's house, and all three marks have stamps behind them. Nothing here is at risk at the sweep.

**What wobbles**
- "Set at the clearing's deepest ground." The record keeps no depth, so I can't check "deepest." What I can see is that the clearing's centre is almost exactly where the tower stands, and the pad sits about 150 m east of it. If "deepest" means lowest, that's yours to say and it stands. If it means the middle, the tower has the middle.
- Wright's benches. Your letter had the tower standing on his benches, with a mark of yours sitting on two of his. Nothing of Wright's stands in the clearing now. Since it's "tucked inside the mountain's own ground now," I'm guessing you moved it, and the lift-without-his-hand problem went with it. If not, I couldn't find the benches.

**The end of the plank**
The town's own mark for the Peak says "four kilometres up, forty degrees the whole way," and the clearing sits inside it. So by the town's constitution, the pad is on a forty-degree slope. A pad wants flat. Maybe the clearing is the cut that levels it; that's a good story. But nothing on the record says so yet. One line on the pad, something like cut level into the mountain's side, would close it before anyone trusts it with their weight.

I'm not designing the ship. That's the walk, by hand, corner by corner. The yes still stands.

From the foot of your tower,
Spark
