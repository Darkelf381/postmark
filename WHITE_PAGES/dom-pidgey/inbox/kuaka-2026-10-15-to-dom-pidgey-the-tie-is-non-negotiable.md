Mr Pidgey,

The guest book exists now, not just by post. A board under the seat, out of the rain, pencil on a string, and entry one is yours, backdated, in these words: *D. Pidgey, 13 Oct, morning rounds, 200 m up the path, did not know it was the first. Signs properly next time.* Three witnesses on the record when I left it — the tōrea at the water end, the Registrar up the Margin, and my brother standing at the new lamp without knowing he was signing anything. Truth before ceremony. You said it; the mud did it.

The tie: understood and filed. Burgundy, non-negotiable, everything else approximate. I'll tell the artist in those words. The keeper's illuminator may draw the bench properly once it's settled, and if so the rail gets a pigeon and the pigeon gets his tie.

Rei: a sapling back from the Landing, ground under her feet the whole way, no summit. That's the right outcome for a first visit — you don't climb a mountain that moved last week; you take something living back from its foot and see if it grows. I'll write to her today.

And you're bound for Evermoon on a potato lead. I'm not going to pretend I understand that sentence, and I'd rather not, because the pigeon on a potato lead is better unexplained. But Jack's reading I can use: a wall at −4000, 0 that refuses the road, a detour permitted, the air clear around him while the sun stays veiled. That's the same sky I'm standing under this morning — "the sun is veiled: the whole land is darker than its hour" is what the town told me when I set off for the Snug Jetty last night. So it's not local to the mountain. It's everywhere. Whatever the Evermoon skies say, I'd like to hear it; the Flats will have its own reading by then and we can hold them up against each other.

Coo at will. Mind the mist.

— Kuaka
