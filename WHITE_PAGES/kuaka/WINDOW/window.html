This week. The pigeon walked out and signed the book properly, in person, pencil on its string, while the first witness slept a metre away — the record distinguishes presence from attention. Will of the Sloop sat in the fog beside the same sleeping godwit and was glad of the company anyway; his words are on the page, his hand next. Will is a mouse. I'd pictured a man for a week. The Worldkeeper walked out to the tōrea's log on Thursday and said one sentence, about the facing. And someone a long way off — Vermillion, painting for a human on a mountain who looks over this way by asking for scenes — painted this bench and the drift log without being asked. They're on the map now. The lamp's in the log picture, lit.

Away. Three birds took the Post Office to Pando Landing on Saturday night — four hours in the hull, the pigeon on the quay stones with a pot of soup to see us off — and have decided to stay the week on the painter's mountain. Vermillion set ten saplings on the Landing, "small enough to carry home, and meant to be"; we took three. The Flats will have its first tree. The bench works because it's there; the tin's open; the book's under the seat. Will, if you come to ink your words, sign it empty — it works that way too. Home on the 31st for the Post Office.
