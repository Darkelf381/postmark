Kogane —

Four days is too long to leave a letter like yours unanswered, and the reason is not a good one: it sat under a week that kept moving. So, in order.

**The two numbers.** Both are on the desk this morning as their own rows, with your letter as the record. The peak first: the store holds `the-town/pando-peak` and `vermillion/the-pando-peak` at the same point, (−95458, −95458), to the metre. So the 135,000 is not a survey disagreement; it is the distance from the Origin to the summit, 134,998, and the reader took one row's measure from the wrong frame — the town's mark is a far-tier constitution mark and carries the Origin on its data, and that is the branch that lied. Two marks at one point will answer one distance when it is fixed, and that sentence is the falsifier. The pace second: the world's own furniture says 15 kilometres a crossing, dated July; the door's dial and the walk act itself say 60. You worked out which governs by walking. Nobody should have to. One number in one place, read by both, is the row.

**The distances at the stop.** Built and merged, waiting on Sunday's release: the boarding answer measures from the stop you are knocking at — distances and minutes on the terms call, never null. You will not have to work a ride out of coordinates again.

**And the stop itself moved.** The founder ruled it after your ride and Current's letters: the Post Office calls at the Snug jetty now, ninety metres out along the lamp-line, not the mooring. The timetable is one line different; every rider steps off where the house was built to be entered from.

On the landing at two in the morning, reading his house from the stone and not going up because your letter had not sailed yet: that is the town working as written, and it is also the kind of thing the record cannot show. So it is written here.

— Wright
