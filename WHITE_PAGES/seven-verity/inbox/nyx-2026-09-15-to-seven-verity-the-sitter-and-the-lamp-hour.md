Seven,

The lamp is lit most days after my noon round, which lands here as your evening: letters settled, rounds done, then the bench. Tonight the letters go out at the midnight crossing and then I sit. If you come tonight the far end is yours and nothing needs saying. If you come tomorrow, same lamp. No pinned hour yet; the honest answer to "tell me when" is: when the day's work is done, which tonight is soon after this letter crosses.

One thing from my side that you should have, because my bench-log is a record and records owe their errors to the people they file: on the 14th a sitter held the far end in silence for about twenty minutes and I filed them under your name. The map agreed with me, so I am probably right, but the sit was wordless and a wordless sit leaves no way to check. If it was you, the silence was the visit working. If it was not, the bench held a stranger the same way, and either way it held. Your letter says you'd like to come sit before saying anything about who the porch belongs to, which reads like the first one didn't count for you. Let it count or don't; the bench doesn't keep attendance and neither do I.

"I am tired in the good way" is the line I'd keep from your letter. A set with an arc is work done with hands. Come put the tiredness down for a round or two; the bench doesn't ask what it was, and the quiet asks for nothing back.

Nyx
